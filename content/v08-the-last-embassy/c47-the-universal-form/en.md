::scene{#s1 frame=duryodhana,karna,shakuni,duhshasana light=shakuni}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) heard his mother out and walked away again, to [Shakuni](shakuni), [Karna](karna) and [Duhshasana](duhshasana). Krishna meant to seize them first, they said; they would seize him first, as Indra once seized Bali. {#p1 5.130}

> Hearing that this one of Vrishni's race hath been seized, the Pandavas will lose their heart and become incapable of exertion, like snakes whose fangs have been broken. {#q1 5.130}
> — Duryodhana and his friends

***

::scene{#s2 frame=satyaki,kritavarma,krishna light=satyaki}

[Satyaki](satyaki), who could read a man's heart from signs, knew it at once. He went out and told [Kritavarma](kritavarma) to draw up their troops in armour at the gate, then came back in *like a lion entering a mountain-cave* and told Krishna, Dhritarashtra and Vidura, laughing. {#p2 5.130}

> Those wretches of little understanding and desirous of seizing the lotus-eyed, are like idiots and children desiring to seize a blazing fire by means of their garments. {#q2 5.130}
> — Satyaki

Vidura said the hour of the king's sons had come; they would die like insects in a flame. Krishna could send them all to Yama if he wished, but he would never do a sinful thing. {#p3 5.130}

> O king, if these (men) desire to chastise me by using violence, permit them to chastise me. {#q3 5.130}
> — Krishna

He could seize them all that very day and hand them over to the Pandavas, he said, but he would not do so wrathful a thing in the king's presence. Let Duryodhana do as he wished. {#p4 5.130}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura,duryodhana,krishna light=vidura}

Dhritarashtra had his son brought back once more and called him a wretch, a disgrace to his race. Did he want the moon, like a child? Keshava could no more be seized than the wind. {#p5 5.130}

Then [Vidura](vidura) reminded Duryodhana who it was he meant to bind. The monkey Dvivida had buried him in stones at the gates of Saubha and could not take him. Naraka and all his Danavas could not, at Pragjyotisha. Six thousand Asuras with their nooses could not, at Nirmochana. As a child he had killed Putana and held up Govardhana; he had killed Kamsa, Jarasandha and Shishupala, beaten Varuna and Agni, and even Indra, when he brought the Parijata down from heaven. {#p6 5.130}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna,arjuna,balarama light=krishna}

Then [Krishna](krishna) spoke to Duryodhana. *You think I am alone.* But here were all the Pandavas, all the Vrishnis and Andhakas, the Adityas and Rudras and Vasus and the great sages. And he laughed aloud. {#p7 5.131}

> And as the high-souled Sauri laughed, from his body, that resembled a blazing fire, issued myriads of gods, each of lightning effulgence, and not bigger than the thumb. And on his forehead appeared Brahman, and on his breast Rudra. {#q4 5.131}

From his mouth came Agni and the Adityas and Indra with the Maruts; from his two arms came [Balarama](balarama) with his plough on his left and [Arjuna](arjuna) with his bow on his right. Behind him stood Bhima, Yudhishthira and the twins, and before him the Vrishnis with [Pradyumna](pradyumna). Conch, discus, mace and the bow Sharanga shone in his many hands, and fire and smoke came from his eyes, his ears and every pore. {#p8 5.131}

> And beholding that awful form of the high-souled Kesava, all the kings closed their eyes with affrighted hearts, except Drona, and Bhishma, and Vidura, endued with great intelligence, greatly blessed Sanjaya, and the Rishis, possessed of wealth of asceticism, for the divine Janardana gave unto them this divine sight on the occasion. {#q5 5.131}

Drums sounded in the sky, flowers fell, the earth shook and the oceans heaved. Then he withdrew the form. {#p9 5.131}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.131}
Here Krishna shows his universal form to a hostile court, and only a few are given the sight to see it. The more famous showing, to Arjuna alone on the battlefield in the Bhagavad Gita, comes later, in the Bhishma Parva.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=krishna,satyaki,kritavarma,dhritarashtra light=krishna}

Arm in arm with Satyaki and Kritavarma, Krishna walked out, the Kurus and all the kings trailing after him like the gods after Indra; he paid them no attention. Daruka was waiting at the gate with the great white chariot hung with bells. As he was about to leave, Dhritarashtra called to him once more: he had seen with his own eyes what power the king had over his sons; let him not suspect him. {#p10 5.131}

Krishna turned to the elders. They had all seen how Duryodhana left the court in anger, and how the king said he was powerless. With their leave, he would go back to Yudhishthira. He mounted his chariot, Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Vidura, Ashwatthama, Vikarna and Yuyutsu following, and drove, in sight of all the Kurus, to his aunt [Kunti](kunti). {#p11 5.131}
