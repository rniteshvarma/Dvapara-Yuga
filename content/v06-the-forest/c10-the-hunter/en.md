::scene{#s1 frame=shiva,uma,arjuna light=shiva}

[Shiva](shiva) came down the mountain disguised as a Kirata, a hunter of the hills, golden and huge as a second Meru, with a bow and arrows like poisonous snakes. [Uma](uma) came with him as a hunter's wife, and crowds of spirits and hill women followed. A deep stillness fell over the mountain; the streams and the birds went quiet. {#p1 3.39}

Just then a demon named Muka, in the shape of a boar, came to kill [Arjuna](arjuna). Arjuna strung the Gandiva. The hunter called out that the boar was his, he had aimed at it first. Arjuna ignored him and shot, and the hunter shot at the same instant. Both arrows struck together, and Muka died, turning back into a Rakshasa. {#p2 3.39}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,shiva light=arjuna,shiva}

*Who art thou, wandering in these woods with women?* Arjuna demanded. That was my boar, and you have broken the rules of the hunt, and I will kill you. The hunter smiled. The forest is our home, he said; the boar was mine, and the fault is yours. *Stay thou: I will shoot at thee.* {#p3 3.39}

Arjuna rained arrows on him. The hunter stood like a hill and took them all, crying, *Shoot thou best arrows capable of piercing into the very vitals.* Arjuna shot until his inexhaustible quivers, Agni's gift, were empty. *Who is this person that swalloweth my arrows?* {#p4 3.39}

He struck at the hunter with the end of his bow, and the hunter took the Gandiva from his hands. He swung his sword at the hunter's head, and the sword broke into pieces. He threw trees and rocks; the hunter bore them patiently. They fought with fists and then chest to chest, until smoke rose from their bodies like burning charcoal, and the god pressed Arjuna until he fell senseless. {#p5 3.39}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,shiva light=arjuna}

When Arjuna came to, bloodied, he made an image of Shiva out of clay and worshipped it with a garland of flowers. And he saw his garland on the hunter's head. {#p6 3.39}

He fell at the god's feet. {#p7 3.39}

> Let not this rashness of mine be regarded as a fault—this combat in which I was engaged with thee from ignorance. O Sankara, I seek thy protection. Pardon me all I have done. {#q1 3.39}
> — Arjuna

Shiva took his hands, smiling. *I have pardoned thee.* {#p8 3.39}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=shiva,arjuna,krishna light=shiva,arjuna}

Shiva told him who he was: in a former life Arjuna was Nara, the friend of Narayana, and had done austerities at Badari for thousands of years. He gave back the Gandiva and the quivers. {#p9 3.40}

> This couple of quivers, fit for thee, will again be inexhaustible, O son of Pritha! {#q2 3.40}
> — Shiva

*Ask thou of me the boon that thou desirest.* Arjuna asked for Shiva's own weapon, the one that ends the world, so that he could face Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and Karna. Shiva gave him the Pashupata, which not even Indra or Yama knew, and taught him how to loose it and how to call it back. {#p10 3.40}

> This weapon should not be hurled without adequate cause; for if hurled at any foe of little might it may destroy the whole universe. {#q3 3.40}
> — Shiva

The earth shook, and the weapon itself, in bodily form, came and stood beside Arjuna. Shiva touched him and every pain left his body. *Go thou into heaven.* {#p11 3.40}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.39,3.40}
**The hunter and the archer.** This fight is one of the best-loved scenes in Indian art and poetry. Bharavi's Sanskrit poem *Kiratarjuniya*, the Kirata and Arjuna, retells it at length, and it is carved on temple walls across India. The epic's version is short, and its turn is the garland: Arjuna sees his own flowers on the stranger's head.
::
