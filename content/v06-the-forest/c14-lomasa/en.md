::scene{#s1 frame=indra,arjuna,krishna light=indra}

One day the wandering sage Lomasa came to Indra's hall and was startled to see a Kshatriya sitting on half of Indra's throne. What had this man done to deserve it? [Indra](indra) read his thoughts and smiled. {#p1 3.47}

This was no mortal, Indra told him, but his own son by Kunti. Long ago there had been two sages, Nara and Narayana, at Badari by the source of the Ganga; they were Arjuna and [Krishna](krishna), born on earth to lighten its burden. And there was work for Arjuna in heaven too: demons called the Nivatakavachas, made proud by a boon, were plotting against the gods, and the gods together could not beat them. {#p2 3.47}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna light=yudhishthira}

Go down to earth, Indra told Lomasa, to Yudhishthira in the Kamyaka forest. Tell him not to worry about Arjuna, who will come back a master of weapons, and of heavenly music and dance as well. Tell him to go on pilgrimage to the sacred waters with his brothers, to wash away his sins and cool his heart. And protect him on the way from the Rakshasas of the mountains. {#p3 3.47}

[Arjuna](arjuna) added his own request. *Protect thou ever the son of Pandu.* {#p4 3.47}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,sanjaya,arjuna light=dhritarashtra}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) heard about Arjuna in heaven from Vyasa, and was afraid. Who could stand before Arjuna's arrows? he asked [Sanjaya](sanjaya). Karna was kind and forgetful, Drona was old; *the slayer of Arjuna, however, existeth not*. {#p5 3.48}

Sanjaya agreed. Arjuna had fought Shiva himself, and the guardians of the world had come to him. And the Pandavas would not forgive what had been done to Draupadi. {#p6 3.49}

> O charioteer, what mischief hath been done by Karna uttering those cruel words, to the sons of Pandu! {#q1 3.49}
> — Dhritarashtra

His son thought him a fool because he was blind, he said, and would not listen. {#p7 3.49}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

Meanwhile, in the forest, the Pandavas hunted for their food. Each day they killed deer with clean arrows, Yudhishthira to the east, Bhima to the south, the twins to the west and north, and gave the first share to the ten thousand Brahmanas who lived with them. No one in Yudhishthira's company was pale or thin or afraid. {#p8 3.50}

> And Draupadi of pure fame fed her husbands and the Brahmanas, as if she was their mother; and last of all took her food herself. {#q2 3.50}
> — the narrator

So they lived five years in the Kamyaka woods, anxious for Arjuna, studying, praying and sacrificing. {#p9 3.50}
