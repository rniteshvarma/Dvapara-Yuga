::scene{#s1 frame=bhishma,duryodhana light=bhishma}

Having taken command, [Bhishma](bhishma) told [Duryodhana](duryodhana) to let the fever leave his heart: he knew every kind of battle array, of gods and Gandharvas and men, as well as Brihaspati did. Duryodhana said he feared nothing with Bhishma and Drona on his side. But he wanted to know who, on both sides, were the *Rathas* and the *Atirathas*, the chariot-warriors and the great chariot-warriors. {#p1 5.166}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,kritavarma,shalya,bhurishravas,jayadratha light=bhishma}

Bhishma began with his own side. Duryodhana and his brothers were all excellent Rathas, pupils of Drona and Kripa. Of himself he would not speak. [Kritavarma](kritavarma) was an Atiratha; so was [Shalya](shalya), who had left his own sister's sons for Duryodhana and boasted himself Vasudeva's equal. [Bhurishravas](bhurishravas) was a leader of leaders. [Jayadratha](jayadratha) was equal to two Rathas, and would fight remembering the humiliation he suffered over Draupadi. {#p2 5.166}

Sudakshina of Kamboja, Nila of Mahishmati in his blue mail, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, the five Trigarta brothers, Duryodhana's son Lakshmana and Duhshasana's son, Brihadbala of Kosala: all Rathas. Kripa was a leader of leaders, born in a clump of reeds, invincible like Kartikeya. {#p3 5.167}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=ashwatthama,drona,bhagadatta light=ashwatthama}

Shakuni was one Ratha. Then [Ashwatthama](ashwatthama): his arrows flew in an unbroken line like Arjuna's; he could burn the three worlds if he wished; and yet Bhishma would not call him a Ratha or a Maharatha. {#p4 5.168}

> This regenerate man is exceedingly fond of living, life being very dear to him. {#q1 5.168}
> — Bhishma

[Drona](drona), old as he was, would burn the Pandava army like dry grass, but he would never kill Arjuna, whom he loved more than his own son. Karna's son Vrishasena was a Maharatha; Bahlika was an Atiratha who never turned back; the Rakshasa Alambusha remembered old enmities; and [Bhagadatta](bhagadatta) of Pragjyotisha fought from his elephant like Indra on Airavata. {#p5 5.168}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,karna,drona light=karna}

Then he came to [Karna](karna). This braggart who always urged Duryodhana to war, this vain counsellor, had lost his natural mail and his earrings, and lay under the curse of his teacher Parashurama and of a Brahmana. {#p6 5.169}

> In consequence of the curse of Rama (his preceptor in arms) as also of the words of a Brahmana (who cursed him on another occasion), owing also to his deprivation of the accoutrements of battle, he, in my judgment, is only half a Ratha. {#q2 5.169}
> — Bhishma

Drona agreed: Karna boasted before every battle and retreated from every one. {#p7 5.169}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.169}
The two curses Bhishma refers to, Parashurama's and the Brahmana's whose cow Karna killed, are told later in the epic, in the Shanti Parva.
::

Karna's eyes widened with rage. Bhishma had always hated him and wounded him with words; he bore it only for Duryodhana's sake. A Kshatriya's rank came from strength, not from years or grey hair. Let Duryodhana cast this wicked old man aside. {#p8 5.169}

> It is true the scriptures teach that the words of the old should be listened to. That, however, doth not refer to those that are very old, for these, in my judgment, become children again. {#q3 5.169}
> — Karna

He alone would scatter the Pandavas like oxen before a tiger, but the fame would go to the commander. So he would not fight while Bhishma lived. {#p9 5.169}

Bhishma answered that only the coming war kept him from crushing Karna's hope of life. Parashurama himself, Karna's teacher, had not hurt him; he had carried off the daughters of Kashi from all the kings of the earth on a single chariot. Let Karna go and fight the Partha he was always challenging; Bhishma would like to see him come back alive. {#p10 5.169}

Duryodhana begged them both: great business was at hand. Let Bhishma tell him now about the enemy. {#p11 5.169}
