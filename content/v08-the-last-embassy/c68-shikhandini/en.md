::scene{#s1 frame=duryodhana,bhishma,drupada,shiva light=bhishma}

How, [Duryodhana](duryodhana) asked, had Shikhandi been born a daughter and become a man? [Bhishma](bhishma) went on. [Drupada](drupada)'s chief queen had long been childless, and Drupada did fierce penance to [Shiva](shiva) for a son: *a son, not a daughter, to be revenged on Bhishma*. {#p1 5.191}

> Thou shalt have a child who will be a female and male. Desist, O king, it will not be otherwise. {#q1 5.191}
> — Shiva

***

::scene{#s2 frame=drupada,shikhandi light=drupada}

In time the queen bore a beautiful daughter, and gave out that it was a son. Drupada, trusting the god's word, kept the secret with her: he had every rite for a boy performed and named the child Shikhandi. No one else in the city knew. Only Bhishma knew, he said, through his spies and from Narada, and because he knew of the god's words and of Amba's penance. {#p2 5.191}

The child learned writing, painting and every art, and studied arms under Drona. When she came of age, her mother urged Drupada to find her a wife, trusting that the god's word would come true. So they married her to the daughter of Hiranyavarman, the powerful king of the Dasharnas. {#p3 5.192}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drupada light=drupada}

The bride soon discovered that her husband was a woman like herself, and in shame told her nurses, and the nurses told her father. Hiranyavarman sent a messenger to Drupada in private. {#p4 5.192}

> Thou hadst, from folly, solicited my daughter for thy daughter! {#q2 5.192}
> — Hiranyavarman

He would kill Drupada with all his kin. Drupada, *like a thief caught in the act*, could not answer; he sent soft-spoken envoys to say it was not so. But Hiranyavarman, sure now, gathered his allies: if Shikhandi was a daughter, they would bind Drupada, drag him from his city, set another king over the Panchalas, and kill Drupada and Shikhandi both. {#p5 5.193}

> King Drupada was not naturally courageous. {#q3 5.193}

Frightened, he went to his queen and asked her before others what the truth was, *to proclaim his own innocence*, though he knew it all along. {#p6 5.193}
