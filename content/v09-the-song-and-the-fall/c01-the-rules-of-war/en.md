::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

The Pandavas came onto the sacred plain of Kurukshetra and took their stand on its western side, facing east. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) set up thousands of tents beyond Samantapanchaka. Men of every race had come from the whole of Jambudvipa, and their camps spread over districts, rivers, hills and woods for many yojanas. He gave them food, and watchwords, and badges, so that in battle each side would know its own. {#p1 6.1}

> The whole earth was empty, having only the children and the old left (at home), in consequence of that large army mustered by the Kauravas. {#q1 6.1}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,krishna,arjuna light=krishna}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana), under a white umbrella among a thousand elephants and his hundred brothers, drew up his ranks. The Panchalas blew their conchs for joy at the sight of him; then [Krishna](krishna) and [Arjuna](arjuna), standing in one chariot, blew theirs, and the other army shrank as animals do at a lion's roar. A dust rose that hid the sun, a black cloud rained flesh and blood, and a wind drove stones across the field. Still the two armies stood glad and ready, like two oceans heaving at the end of an age. {#p2 6.1}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=6.1}
Ganguli names the two conchs here *Gigantea* and *Theodotes*, the Latin names the German scholar Schlegel gave them, as one of Ganguli's own later footnotes says. They are Krishna's Panchajanya and Arjuna's Devadatta.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,duryodhana light=yudhishthira}

Then the Kurus, the Pandavas and the Somakas agreed on rules for the fighting. Equals would meet equals, fairly; those who fought with words would be answered with words; anyone who left the ranks would be spared. Chariot would face chariot, elephant elephant, horse horse, foot-soldier foot-soldier, and a man would warn another before striking. {#p3 6.1}

> No one should strike another that is unprepared or panic-struck. {#q2 6.1}

No one was to strike a man already engaged with another, or one asking for quarter, or one retreating, or one whose weapon was broken, or one without armour. {#p4 6.1}

> Car-drivers, animals (yoked to cars or carrying weapons) men engaged in the transport of weapons, players on drums and blowers of conches should never be struck. {#q3 6.1}

Having made these covenants, the two sides looked at each other in wonder, and their joy showed in their faces. {#p5 6.1}
