At the Shami tree, [Arjuna](arjuna) told the prince to climb up and bring down the bows tied there. Uttara's own bows could not bear his strength. In this tree, he said, were the bows and arrows, banners and mail of the sons of Pandu, and the Gandiva, *which singly is equal to many thousands of other bows*. {#p1 4.40}

[Uttara](uttar) hung back. Everyone said there was a corpse in that tree. {#p2 4.41}

> How can I, therefore, being a prince by birth, touch it with my hands? {#q1 4.41}
> — Uttara

There were only bows in the tree, Arjuna told him, no corpses; he would not make a king's son do anything unclean. So Uttara climbed, cut away the wrappings and then the cords, and found the Gandiva and four other bows. Unwrapped together, they shone *like unto that of the planets about the time of their rising*. They looked to him like hissing snakes, and his hair stood on end. {#p3 4.41}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=uttar light=uttar}

He touched them and asked whose they were. Whose was the bow with a hundred golden bosses? The one with golden elephants on the staff, the one with threescore golden Indragopaka insects, the one with three blazing suns, the one with golden insects set with gems? Whose were the thousand gold-headed arrows, the iron shafts with vulture feathers, the quiver painted with five tigers, the seven hundred crescent-headed arrows? Whose the sword marked with a toad, the sword in a tiger-skin sheath hung with bells, the sword in goat-skin, the one in cow-skin made by the Nishadas? {#p4 4.42}

> Asked by me, O Vrihannala, do thou answer me truly. Great is my wonder at the sight of all these excellent objects. {#q2 4.42}
> — Uttara

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,yudhishthira,bhima,nakula,sahadeva light=arjuna}

The first, said Brihannala, was the Gandiva, Arjuna's bow, worth a hundred thousand weapons. It had passed from hand to hand among the gods. {#p5 4.43}

> Shiva held it first for a thousand years. Afterwards Prajapati held it for five hundred and three years. After that Sakra, for five and eighty years. And then Soma held it for five hundred years. And after that Varuna held it for a hundred years. And finally Partha, surnamed Swetavahana, hath held it for five and sixty years. {#q3 4.43}
> — Arjuna

Partha had it from Varuna. The bow with golden elephants was [Bhima](bhima)'s, with which he conquered the east; the one with the Indragopakas was [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira)'s; the one with the three suns, [Nakula](nakula)'s; the gem-set one with golden insects, [Sahadeva](sahadeva)'s. The thousand razor arrows that never ran out in battle were Arjuna's; the crescent shafts, Bhima's; the tiger quiver, Nakula's, which had conquered the west. And the swords, one by one: the toad-marked sword, Arjuna's; the tiger-skin one, Bhima's; the gold-hilted one, Yudhishthira's; the goat-skin one, Nakula's; the great scimitar in cow-skin, Sahadeva's. {#p6 4.43}
