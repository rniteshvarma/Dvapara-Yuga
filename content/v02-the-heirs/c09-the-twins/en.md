::scene{#s1 frame=madri,pandu light=madri}

When Kunti had borne her sons, and Gandhari her hundred, [Madri](madri) came to [Pandu](pandu) privately. {#p1 1.124}

She did not complain, she said, that he showed her little favour, nor that she, though of higher birth than Kunti, had the lower place. She did not grieve that Gandhari had a hundred sons. Her one grief was this: she and Kunti were equal, and yet Kunti alone had children by him. If Kunti would help her to have children, it would be a great kindness to her and to him. But Kunti was her rival, and she could not bring herself to ask. Would he ask for her? {#p2 1.124}

> O Madri, I do revolve this matter often in my own mind, but I have hitherto hesitated to tell thee anything, not knowing how thou wouldst receive it. {#q1 1.124}
> — Pandu

***

::scene{#s2 frame=pandu,kunti,madri light=kunti,madri}

So Pandu spoke to [Kunti](kunti) alone. He asked her to give him more sons, for the sake of his line and the offerings to his ancestors and hers, and to win undying fame by doing something difficult. *Rescue this Madri as by a raft,* he said, and make her a mother. {#p3 1.124}

Kunti agreed at once. She told Madri to think, without delay, of some god, and she would have a child like him. {#p4 1.124}

::scene{#s3 frame=madri,ashvins,nakula,sahadeva light=ashvins,nakula,sahadeva}

Madri thought for a moment, and called on the twin [Ashvins](ashvins), the physicians of the gods. They came to her quickly, and she bore twin sons, [Nakula](nakula) and [Sahadeva](sahadeva), more beautiful than anyone on earth. As soon as they were born a voice said: *In energy and beauty these twins shall transcend even the twin Aswins themselves.* {#p5 1.124}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva}

The sages of the mountain blessed the children, performed their birth rites, and gave them their names: Kunti's sons were Yudhishthira, Bhimasena and Arjuna, and Madri's, Nakula and Sahadeva. They were born a year apart, the text says, and *looked like an embodied period of five years*. Pandu looked at his sons, beautiful as gods and full of strength, and was overjoyed; and they became the darlings of the sages and their wives. {#p6 1.124}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=pandu,kunti,madri light=kunti}

Some time later Pandu asked Kunti again, on Madri's behalf. This time Kunti said no. {#p7 1.124}

> Having given her the formula of invocation only once, she hath, O king, managed to obtain two sons. Have I not been thus deceived by her, I fear, O king, that she will soon surpass me in the number of her children. {#q2 1.124}
> — Kunti

She had not known, she said, that calling twin gods would bring twin sons. She begged him not to ask her again. {#p8 1.124}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.124}
**Kunti, without varnish.** The epic does not tidy Kunti up. She is generous and quick to help, and she is also jealous, and says so. Both are part of the woman who will raise all five boys as her own.
::

So five sons were born to Pandu, fathered by the gods, every one of them marked with good fortune, handsome as the moon, proud as a lion and skilled with the bow. On that snowy, holy mountain they grew, and far away in Hastinapura the hundred sons of Dhritarashtra grew too: all of them together, the text says, *like a cluster of lotuses in a lake.* {#p9 1.124}
