In the morning the kings came into the great white hall with its golden floor: [Bhishma](bhishma), [Drona](drona), Kripa, Shalya, Kritavarma, Jayadratha, Ashwatthama, Vidura and the rest with [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) at their head; [Duryodhana](duryodhana) with his brothers, Shakuni and [Karna](karna). The hall looked *like a mountain-cave filled with lions*. Then [Sanjaya](sanjaya) came in from his chariot and gave the Pandavas' greetings to each by age. {#p1 5.47}

What did Arjuna say? asked the king. {#p2 5.48}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,krishna light=arjuna}

Arjuna had spoken in front of Krishna, with Yudhishthira's leave, Sanjaya said, and wanted every word heard by Duryodhana and his counsellors, and by *that Suta's son, of foul tongue and wicked soul*. {#p3 5.48}

> If Dhritarashtra's son doth not surrender to king Yudhishthira of the Ajamida race, his kingdom, then (it is evident) there must be some sinful act committed by the sons of Dhritarashtra, whose consequences are yet unreaped by them. {#q1 5.48}
> — Arjuna

Let there be war, if Duryodhana wanted it. And let the hard bed Yudhishthira had slept on in the forest become Duryodhana's last bed, on the bare earth. {#p4 5.48}

Then, again and again, the same refrain. When Yudhishthira let loose the anger he had stored up for years; when Duryodhana saw Bhima in the van, mace in hand, *vomiting the venom of his wrath*; when the elephants fell with their heads broken like casks; when Nakula and Sahadeva and Draupadi's young sons came on; when Satyaki rained arrows like a cloud: *then will the son of Dhritarashtra repent for this war*. {#p5 5.48}

> When he will also behold my terrible car, endued with the effulgence of gold and bright gems, drawn by white steeds and furnished with the banner bearing the device of the Ape and guided by Kesava himself, then will that wretch of uncontrolled passions repent. {#q2 5.48}
> — Arjuna

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,krishna light=krishna}

A Brahmana had once told him, Arjuna said, that either Indra would walk before him in battle, or Krishna would guard him from behind. He had chosen Krishna over Indra. {#p6 5.48}

> The person whose success is only wished for by Krishna, without the latter's actually taking up arms in his behalf, is certain to prevail over all enemies, even if those be the celestials with Indra at their head. {#q3 5.48}
> — Arjuna

He listed Krishna's deeds: Rukmini carried off, Kamsa killed, Naraka and Mura slain at Pragjyotisha. And Duryodhana dreamed of imprisoning him. {#p7 5.48}

Arjuna would bow to Bhishma, Drona and his son, and Kripa, and then fight for the kingdom. The omens were already moving. {#p8 5.48}

> My bow, Gandiva, yawneth without being handled; my bow-string trembleth without being stretched; and arrows also, issuing from my quiver's mouth, are again and again seeking to fly. {#q4 5.48}
> — Arjuna

*But so let it be even as the aged Bhishma, the son of Santanu, and Kripa, and Drona with his son, and Vidura endued with great wisdom, are saying, 'May the Kauravas all live long!'* {#p9 5.48}
