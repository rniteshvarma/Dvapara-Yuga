::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,draupadi,nakula,sahadeva light=yudhishthira}

At last, after many sacred places, they turned north into the high mountains, toward the Gandhamadana, where Arjuna was to come back to them. Lomasa warned them that the way was guarded by invisible spirits. {#p1 3.139 3.140}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) wanted to send [Bhima](bhima) back with Draupadi and the servants to wait at the source of the Ganga, and go on alone. Bhima would not hear of it. Draupadi would not stay behind without them, and neither would Sahadeva. {#p2 3.140}

> Trouble thyself not, O king, I shall carry Panchala's daughter wherever she will be incapable of walking. {#q1 3.140}
> — Bhima

***

::scene{#s2 frame=draupadi,nakula,yudhishthira,dhaumya light=draupadi}

They had gone only two miles when a storm of wind and hail came down on the mountain, and [Draupadi](draupadi), who had never walked such roads, sank to the ground, trembling like a plantain tree. [Nakula](nakula) ran and caught her. {#p3 3.142 3.143}

Yudhishthira took her on his lap and grieved. Her feet and her lotus face had turned blue with cold. {#p4 3.143}

> Fool that I am, having been addicted to dice, I have been wandering in the forest full of wild beasts, taking Krishna in my company. {#q2 3.143}
> — Yudhishthira

[Dhaumya](dhaumya) and the Brahmanas chanted mantras, the brothers fanned her and stroked her with their hands, and slowly she came to. The twins pressed her feet with hands scarred by the bowstring. {#p5 3.143}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,ghatotkacha,draupadi light=bhima,ghatotkacha}

*There are many mountains yet*, said Yudhishthira. *How will Krishna cross them?* Bhima said he would carry them all, or else his son could. He thought of [Ghatotkacha](ghatotkacha), Hidimbi's son, and the young Rakshasa was there at once, with joined hands. *Command me.* Bhima hugged him to his chest. {#p6 3.143}

*Carry thy mother*, said Bhima, *not too high, so that thou mayst not render her uneasy.* Ghatotkacha said he could carry all of them alone; but he called hundreds of Rakshasas who could fly, and they carried the Pandavas and the Brahmanas across the sky, over forests and rivers and snow, to the great jujube tree at Badari, the hermitage of Nara and Narayana. {#p7 3.144}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=draupadi,bhima light=draupadi}

They waited there six nights for Arjuna. Then a wind came from the north-east and dropped at Draupadi's feet a lotus of a thousand petals, bright as the sun, with a fragrance not of this world. {#p8 3.145}

She was delighted. She would give this one to Yudhishthira, she told Bhima. *If, O Pritha's son, I have found grace with thee, do thou then procure others of this species in large numbers.* {#p9 3.145}

So Bhima took his bow and set out into the wind, toward where the flower had come from. {#p10 3.145}
