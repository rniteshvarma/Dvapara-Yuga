::scene{#s1 frame=duryodhana,krishna light=duryodhana}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) answered [Krishna](krishna). Why did everyone blame him alone? He could find no fault in himself, not the smallest. The Pandavas had accepted the game of dice gladly, and Shakuni had won; he had even ordered their wealth returned. If they lost again and went to the woods, how was that his fault? {#p1 5.127}

He would not bow from fear, not to Indra himself. Even the gods could not beat Bhishma, Kripa, Drona and Karna. And if he fell in battle, that was heaven. {#p2 5.127}

> Even this, O Janardana, is our highest duty as Kshatriyas, viz., that we should lay ourselves down on the field of battle on a bed of arrows. {#q1 5.127}
> — Duryodhana

A Kshatriya should keep himself erect, he said, quoting the sage Matanga; *one should rather break at the knots than bend*. What his father had given away when he was a child, from ignorance or fear, would never be given again. {#p3 5.127}

> At present, O Kesava of mighty arms, as long as I live, even that much of our land which may be covered by the point of a sharp needle shall not, O Madhava, be given by us unto the Pandavas. {#q2 5.127}
> — Duryodhana

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,duryodhana,draupadi light=krishna}

Krishna thought for a moment, his eyes red with anger. {#p4 5.128}

> Wishest thou for a bed of heroes? Verily, thou shalt have it, with thy consellors. Wait (for a short while), a great slaughter will ensue. {#q3 5.128}
> — Krishna

No offence against the Pandavas? Let the kings judge. He had planned the dice with Shakuni. He had dragged his brother's wife into the assembly and spoken to her as he did. He had tried to burn the Pandavas alive at Varanavata when they were children, and tried poison, and snakes, and ropes. And now he put on a different face, and refused them even their father's share. {#p5 5.128}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duhshasana,duryodhana,karna light=duhshasana}

Then [Duhshasana](duhshasana) leaned to his brother. If he did not make peace of his own will, the Kurus would bind him and hand him over, and Karna and Duhshasana with him; Bhishma, Drona and their own father would do it. {#p6 5.128}

Duryodhana rose, breathing like a great snake, and walked out of the court, ignoring Vidura and his father, Bahlika, Kripa, Somadatta, Bhishma, Drona and Krishna. His brothers, his counsellors and all the kings went after him. {#p7 5.128}

> I see also, O Janardana, that the hour of all those Kshatriyas is arrived, for all those kings, from delusion, have with their counsellors followed Duryodhana. {#q4 5.128}
> — Bhishma

***

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna,kamsa,ugrasena light=krishna}

Then Krishna turned to the elders who were left. They were at fault, he said, for not seizing this king and binding him. He had done it himself once: [Kamsa](kamsa) had seized the throne in his father's lifetime, and Krishna had killed him and set old [Ugrasena](ugrasena) back on it, and the Yadavas had given up one man for the sake of their whole race and prospered. So too Brahma once had the Daityas bound and given to Varuna, to save the worlds a war. {#p8 5.128}

> For the sake of a family, an individual may be sacrificed. For a village, a family may be sacrificed. For the sake of a province, a village may be sacrificed. {#q5 5.128}
> — Krishna

Bind Duryodhana, Karna, Shakuni and Duhshasana, he said, and hand them to the Pandavas, and let not the whole Kshatriya race be slaughtered for one man. {#p9 5.128}
