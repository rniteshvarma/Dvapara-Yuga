::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,arjuna,duryodhana light=krishna}

[Arjuna](arjuna) set out for Dwaraka himself. [Duryodhana](duryodhana)'s spies told him, and he raced there with fast horses and a few troops, and both arrived on the same day. [Krishna](krishna) was asleep. {#p1 5.7}

> And as Krishna was sleeping, Duryodhana entered the room, and sat down on a fine seat at the head of the bed. And after him entered that wearer of the diadem the magnanimous Arjuna. And stood at the back of the bed, bowing and joining his hands. {#q1 5.7}

When Krishna woke, he saw Arjuna first. {#p2 5.7}

Duryodhana spoke. They were both his friends, related to him equally, and he had come first. *Right-minded persons take up the cause of him who comes first to them.* Krishna did not doubt that Duryodhana had come first; but he had seen Arjuna first. So he would help both. And the younger should choose first. {#p3 5.7}

> These soldiers, irresistible in battle, shall be sent to one of you and I alone, resolved not to fight on the field, and laying down my arms, will go to the other. {#q2 5.7}
> — Krishna

On one side, the Narayanas, ten crores of cowherd warriors as strong as he. On the other, Krishna himself, unarmed, who would not fight. {#p4 5.7}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,duryodhana,balarama,kritavarma light=duryodhana}

Arjuna chose Krishna. Duryodhana took the army, and was delighted, though he knew Krishna was not on his side. Then he went to [Balarama](balarama), who reminded him of what he had said at Virata's court. He could not go against Krishna, he said, and so he would fight for neither side. Duryodhana embraced him and thought Arjuna as good as beaten. [Kritavarma](kritavarma) gave him an akshauhini of troops. {#p5 5.7}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,arjuna light=arjuna}

When Duryodhana had gone, Krishna, in his yellow silk, asked Arjuna why he had chosen a man who would not fight at all. {#p6 5.7}

> It hath been always my desire to have you for driving my car. I, therefore, ask you to fulfil my desire cherished for a long time. {#q3 5.7}
> — Arjuna

> It beseems thee well, O Kunti's son, that thou measurest thyself with me. I will act as thy charioteer; let thy wish be fulfilled. {#q4 5.7}
> — Krishna

***

::scene{#s4 frame=shalya,duryodhana light=shalya}

[Shalya](shalya), king of the Madras and the twins' uncle, was marching with an akshauhini to join the Pandavas. Duryodhana had pavilions built all along his road, full of garlands, meat, drink, wells and tanks, and his servants waited on Shalya like a god, until Shalya thought less of Indra than of himself. He asked to see the men of Yudhishthira who had done all this, to reward them. Then Duryodhana, who had been hiding, stepped out. Shalya embraced him and told him to ask for anything. {#p7 5.8}

> O thou auspicious one, let thy word be true, grant me a boon. I ask thee to be the leader of all my army. {#q5 5.8}
> — Duryodhana

***

::scene{#s5 frame=shalya,yudhishthira,karna light=yudhishthira}

Shalya went on to Upaplavya all the same, embraced his nephews, and told [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) what he had promised. Yudhishthira said he had done well to keep his word. But he asked one improper thing. When Karna and Arjuna met, Shalya would surely drive Karna's chariot. {#p8 5.8}

> O king, thou must likewise so act that the Suta's son Karna may be dispirited and the victory may be ours. {#q6 5.8}
> — Yudhishthira

Shalya promised. He would speak words to [Karna](karna) on the field that would rob him of his pride and his courage. And all Draupadi's sufferings, at the dice, from Jatasura, from Kichaka, would end in joy, as Damayanti's had. Even the gods have suffered, he said: even Indra, with his wife. {#p9 5.8}
