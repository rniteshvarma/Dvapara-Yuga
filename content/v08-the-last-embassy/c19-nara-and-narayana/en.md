[Bhishma](bhishma) spoke to [Duryodhana](duryodhana) with a story. Once the gods were gathered round Brahma when two ancient Rishis rose and left, drawing every mind after them, without bowing to him. Who were they? asked Brihaspati. Nara and Narayana, Brahma said, who exist to destroy the Asuras. And Indra went to them for help in his war with the Daityas, and with their help he won it. {#p1 5.49}

> That Narayana is Krishna, and that Nara is Falguna. Indeed, they are one Soul born in twain. {#q1 5.49}
> — Bhishma

When Duryodhana saw them on one chariot, Krishna with conch and discus and Arjuna with his bow, he would remember these words. He was alone in his opinion, said Bhishma, except for three: Shakuni, his own sinful brother Duhshasana, and *Karna, a low-born Suta's son cursed by Rama*. {#p2 5.49}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=karna,bhishma light=karna}

> It behoveth thee not, O blessed grandsire, to use such words towards me, for I have adopted the duties of the Kshatriya order without falling off from those of my own. {#q2 5.49}
> — Karna

What wickedness was in him? He had never harmed Duryodhana; he would kill all the Pandavas. {#p3 5.49}

[Bhishma](bhishma) turned to the king. This man boasted of killing the Pandavas, but was not worth a sixteenth of them. The coming disaster was his doing. Where had he been when his brother was killed at Virata's city, or when Arjuna took the Kurus' clothes, or when Duryodhana was carried off by the Gandharvas? {#p4 5.49}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drona,dhritarashtra light=drona}

[Drona](drona) agreed with Bhishma: make peace before the war, for there was no bowman like Arjuna in the three worlds. But [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) did not answer them; he turned back to Sanjaya and asked about the Pandavas. {#p5 5.49}

> From that moment, when the king returned not a proper answer to Bhishma and Drona, the Kauravas gave up all hopes of life. {#q3 5.49}
