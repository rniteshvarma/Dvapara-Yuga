[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) said he did not grieve so much for himself or his brothers or his kingdom as for [Draupadi](draupadi). When they were lost at dice, she had saved them; and now she had been carried off. *Hast thou even seen or heard of any chaste and exalted lady that resembleth this daughter of Drupada?* {#p1 3.291}

So Markandeya told him of Savitri. {#p2 3.291}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=narada light=narada}

Savitri was the only child of Ashvapati, king of the Madras, born of a goddess's boon. No prince dared ask for her, so her father sent her out to find a husband herself. She came back and said she had chosen Satyavan, son of Dyumatsena, a blind king driven from his throne and living in the forest. {#p3 3.291 3.292}

[Narada](narada), who was sitting with the king, said Satyavan had every virtue, and only one defect: within a year from that day he would die. Choose another, said her father. {#p4 3.292}

> The death can fall but once; a daughter can be given away but one; and once only can a person say, I give away! {#q1 3.292}
> — Savitri

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

So she married Satyavan and lived with him in the forest, counting the days. Three days before the end she began to fast, and on the last day she went with him into the woods to cut fuel. His head began to ache, and he lay down with it in her lap. {#p5 3.294}

Then Yama came, dark and red-eyed, with a noose in his hand, and drew out of Satyavan's body a being the measure of a thumb, and went south with it. And Savitri followed him. {#p6 3.295}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

Go back, said Yama. She would not. She followed him, speaking to him of dharma and the friendship of the good, and each time she spoke Yama was pleased and gave her a boon, anything but her husband's life. She asked for her father-in-law's sight, then for his kingdom, then for a hundred sons for her father. Then she asked for a hundred sons of her own, by Satyavan. {#p7 3.295}

Yama granted it, and then saw what he had promised. He untied the noose. {#p8 3.295}

Satyavan woke as if from a long sleep. They walked home through the dark forest, and found his parents frantic and his father able to see. {#p9 3.295 3.296}

> And he granted me five boons, of which do ye hear from me! {#q2 3.296}
> — Savitri

And so Dyumatsena got back his kingdom, and Ashvapati had a hundred sons, and Satyavan lived four hundred years. {#p10 3.296 3.297}

> And like that gentle lady Savitri, the auspicious daughter of Drupada, endued with excellent character, will rescue you all. {#q3 3.297}
> — Markandeya

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.291,3.297}
**Why this story.** Markandeya tells Savitri's story to answer Yudhishthira's question about Draupadi: a wife who brings her family back from ruin. A companion volume will tell it in full.
