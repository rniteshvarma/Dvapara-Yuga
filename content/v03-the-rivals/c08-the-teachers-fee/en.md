::scene{#s1 frame=drona,drupada light=drona}

When [Drona](drona) saw that the sons of Pandu and of Dhritarashtra were masters of arms, he decided the time had come to ask for his fee. He called his pupils together. {#p1 1.140}

> Seize Drupada, the king of Panchala in battle and bring him unto me. That shall be the most acceptable fee. {#q1 1.140}
> — Drona

*So be it,* they said, and mounted their chariots and marched out, with Drona going with them. {#p2 1.140}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.140,1.134}
**The purpose, named at last.** Years before, Drona had asked his pupils to promise him a *particular purpose*, and only Arjuna had promised. Now it has a name.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,karna,yuyutsu,duhshasana,vikarna,arjuna light=duryodhana,karna}

They fought their way through the Panchala country and surrounded [Drupada](drupada)'s capital. [Duryodhana](duryodhana), [Karna](karna), [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu), [Duhshasana](duhshasana), [Vikarna](vikarna) and many other princes raced each other to be first into the attack. {#p3 1.140}

[Arjuna](arjuna) held back. He told Drona that they would act after these others had shown what they could do, because none of them would ever take the king of the Panchalas. And he waited with his brothers a mile outside the town. {#p4 1.140}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drupada,duryodhana,vikarna,duhshasana,karna light=drupada}

Drupada came out on his white chariot and met them alone. He moved so fast across the field that the Kurus, in their panic, thought there were many Drupadas. Conches, trumpets and drums sounded from every house in the city, and the Panchalas roared like lions. Drupada wheeled over the field like a ring of fire and struck down Duryodhana, Vikarna, even Karna, and many others. The citizens, young and old, rained weapons on the Kurus from the roofs, and the Kurus broke and fled, wailing, back towards the Pandavas. {#p5 1.140}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna,bhima,yudhishthira,nakula,sahadeva light=arjuna,bhima}

The Pandavas bowed to Drona and mounted their chariots. Arjuna told Yudhishthira to stay out of the fight, set [Nakula](nakula) and [Sahadeva](sahadeva) to guard his chariot wheels, and drove in, with [Bhima](bhima) running ahead, mace in hand. {#p6 1.140}

Bhima went straight for the elephants, and broke their heads with his mace, and they fell like cliffs struck by lightning. He drove the chariots and the elephants before him as a herdsman drives cattle with his staff. {#p7 1.140}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=arjuna,drupada,satyajit light=arjuna,drupada}

Arjuna fought towards the king. Those who watched him could see no gap between his setting an arrow to the string and letting it go. Drupada's general, [Satyajit](satyajit), came at him; Arjuna cut his bow in two, and when he took another, killed his horses and his charioteer, until Satyajit gave up the fight. {#p8 1.140}

Then Drupada himself came at Arjuna. Arjuna cut his bow and his flagstaff, struck his horses and his charioteer, threw aside his own bow, drew his sword, and with a great shout leapt from his own chariot onto Drupada's. He seized the king as Garuda seizes a great snake. The Panchala army fled. {#p9 1.140}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=arjuna,bhima,drupada light=arjuna}

As Arjuna came back with his prisoner, the other princes began to sack Drupada's city. Arjuna stopped them. {#p10 1.140}

> This best of monarchs, Drupada, is a relative of the Kuru heroes. Therefore, O Bhima, slay not his soldiers. Let us only give unto our preceptor his fee. {#q2 1.140}
> — Arjuna

***

::scene{#s7 frame=drona,drupada light=drona,drupada}

They brought Drupada to Drona, with his friends and counsellors, beaten and stripped of everything. Drona remembered the old insult. {#p11 1.140}

> Thy kingdom and capital have been laid waste by me. But fear not for thy life, though it dependeth now on the will of thy foe. Dost thou now desire to revive thy friendship (with me)? {#q3 1.140}
> — Drona

He smiled a little. Brahmanas forgive, he said, and his love for Drupada had grown up with him since they played together as children in the hermitage. So he asked for his friendship again. {#p12 1.140}

> Thou toldest me before that none who was not a king could be a king's friend. Therefore is it, O Yajnasena, that I retain half thy kingdom. {#q4 1.140}
> — Drona

Drupada would rule the lands south of the Ganga, and Drona the lands to the north. Now they were equals, and could be friends. {#p13 1.140}

*Thou art of noble soul and great prowess,* said Drupada. *I desire thy eternal friendship.* {#p14 1.140}

***

::scene{#s8 frame=drupada,drona,dhrishtadyumna,draupadi light=drupada}

Drona released him and gave him half the kingdom, and Drupada went to live, sorrowing, in Kampilya, ruling the southern Panchalas as far as the river Charmanvati. Drona lived in Ahicchatra, the northern country that Arjuna had won for him. {#p15 1.140}

But Drupada had learnt something. He could not beat Drona with a warrior's strength alone; Drona's power as a Brahmana was greater than his. So he began to wander the whole earth, looking for a way to have a son who would one day overcome his Brahmana enemy. {#p16 1.140}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.140}
**A seed for later.** Drupada's search will bring him a son born from fire, [Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna), and a daughter, [Draupadi](draupadi). The quarrel between two boyhood friends will run all the way to Kurukshetra.
::

***

::scene{#s9 frame=dhritarashtra,yudhishthira,bhima,balarama,arjuna,drona light=yudhishthira}

A year later [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), moved by kindness for the people, made [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) heir to the kingdom, for his firmness, patience, kindness, frankness and honesty; and soon Yudhishthira's conduct outshone even his father's. Bhima went on learning the sword, the mace and the chariot from [Balarama](balarama). {#p17 1.141}

One day, before all the princes, Drona spoke to Arjuna. He had a fee of his own to ask. {#p18 1.141}

> O sinless one, thou must fight with me when I fight with thee. {#q5 1.141}
> — Drona

Arjuna gave his word, touched his teacher's feet, and went away. {#p19 1.141}

::aside{#a3 kind=note src=1.141}
**The second fee.** It is a strange thing to ask, and Drona does not explain it. The reader of the whole epic knows where it leads: to the war, where Drona will command the Kaurava army against his best pupil.
::

***

::scene{#s10 frame=arjuna,bhima,sahadeva,nakula,dhritarashtra light=arjuna,dhritarashtra}

Word went round the world that there was no bowman like Arjuna. Sahadeva learnt the whole science of duty and morality; Nakula, the favourite of his brothers, became a great chariot-warrior. The Pandavas went out to war and brought kings under the Kurus: a Sauvira king, the king of the Yavanas whom even Pandu had not subdued, the kings of the east, the whole of the south. They sent great spoils home to Hastinapura, and widened the kingdom's borders. {#p20 1.141}

And Dhritarashtra, seeing how strong his brother's sons had grown, began to be afraid of them. From that day, the epic says, he could hardly sleep. {#p21 1.141}
