::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura light=dhritarashtra}

In Hastinapura, [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) could not rest. He asked [Vidura](vidura) what he should do now, so that the people would not destroy them all. {#p1 3.4}

Vidura told him plainly. Give the Pandavas back what was theirs. Disgrace Shakuni. Let Duryodhana, Shakuni and Karna serve the Pandavas, and let Duhshasana ask forgiveness of Bhima and Draupadi in open court. Or else abandon Duryodhana, as Vidura had told him to do on the day he was born, and put Yudhishthira on the throne. {#p2 3.4}

> How can I abandon my son for the sake of the sons of Pandu? Doubtless they are my sons, but Duryodhana is sprung from my body. {#q1 3.4}
> — Dhritarashtra

Vidura, he said, was no friend to him. *Stay or go as thou likest.* And he rose and went into his inner rooms. *This race is doomed*, said Vidura, and he left for the forest. {#p3 3.4}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vidura,yudhishthira,bhima light=vidura,yudhishthira}

The Pandavas had travelled west from Kurukshetra, bathing in the Sarasvati and the Drishadvati and the Yamuna, to the Kamyaka forest on the Sarasvati, a favourite haunt of sages. {#p4 3.5}

When [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) saw Vidura's chariot coming, his first thought was fear. Had Shakuni sent for them again, to win their weapons this time? He could not refuse if challenged. {#p5 3.5}

But Vidura had come with advice, not a summons. {#p6 3.5}

> That wise man who bearing all the gross wrong heaped upon him by his enemies, patiently bideth his time, and multiplieth his resources even as men by degrees turn a small fire into a large one, ruleth alone this entire earth. {#q2 3.5}
> — Vidura

Share your food with your followers, he said, and never boast before them. Yudhishthira promised to do as he said. {#p7 3.5}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,sanjaya,vidura light=dhritarashtra}

As soon as Vidura had gone, Dhritarashtra was sorry. He fainted at the door of his hall in front of the kings, and when he came to, he sent [Sanjaya](sanjaya) to find his brother. Bring him back, he said, or I will lay down my life. {#p8 3.6}

Vidura came back. Dhritarashtra took him on his lap and smelt his head. *Forgive me, O sinless one, the words in which thou wert addressed by me!* Vidura said he had forgiven him already. {#p9 3.6}

> All virtuous men, O tiger among men, are (instinctively) partial towards those that are distressed! {#q3 3.6}
> — Vidura

Dhritarashtra's sons were as dear to him as Pandu's, he said; it was only that Pandu's were the ones in trouble. {#p10 3.6}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=duryodhana,shakuni,karna,duhshasana,vyasa light=karna}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) heard of it and burned. If the Pandavas were brought back, he would starve himself or take poison. [Shakuni](shakuni) told him not to worry: the Pandavas kept their word and would never come back before their time. {#p11 3.7}

[Karna](karna) had a quicker plan. {#p12 3.7}

> Let us now, encased in mail and armed with our weapons, mount our cars and go in a body to slay the Pandavas now living in the forest! {#q4 3.7}
> — Karna

While they were poor and alone, he said, the Kauravas were a match for them. Everyone cheered, and they mounted their chariots and set off. But [Vyasa](vyasa) saw them go with his inner sight. He appeared before them and ordered them back. Then he went to see the king. {#p13 3.7}
