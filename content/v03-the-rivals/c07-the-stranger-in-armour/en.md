::scene{#s1 frame=karna,surya,kunti light=karna,surya}

The crowd made way, and he came in: [Karna](karna), with the armour he was born with and his face bright with his ear-rings, bow in hand and sword at his side, like a walking cliff. {#p1 1.138}

The epic stops to tell us who he is, though no one in the arena knows. He was born to Pritha before her marriage, a portion of the [Sun](surya). He was tall as a golden palm tree, splendid as the sun, lovely as the moon, fierce as fire. {#p2 1.138}

He looked round the arena and bowed to Drona and Kripa, without much care. The whole assembly sat still, staring. *Who is he?* {#p3 1.138}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=karna,arjuna,duryodhana light=karna,arjuna}

Then, in a voice like thunder, he spoke to his brother, though neither of them knew it. {#p4 1.138}

> O Partha, I shall perform feats before this gazing multitude; excelling all thou hast performed! Beholding them, thou shall be amazed. {#q1 1.138}
> — Karna

The crowd rose to its feet as one. [Duryodhana](duryodhana) was delighted; [Arjuna](arjuna) was at once ashamed and angry. With Drona's leave, Karna did everything Arjuna had done. {#p5 1.138}

Duryodhana and his brothers embraced him. *Welcome,* said Duryodhana. *Live as thou pleasest, and command me, and the kingdom of the Kurus.* {#p6 1.138}

> I only long for thy friendship. And, O lord, my wish is even for a single combat with Arjuna. {#q2 1.138}
> — Karna

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,karna light=arjuna,karna}

Arjuna, feeling himself disgraced, spoke to Karna, who stood among the hundred brothers like a cliff. {#p7 1.138}

> That path which the unwelcome intruder and the uninvited talker cometh to, shall be thine, O Karna, for thou shall be slain by me. {#q3 1.138}
> — Arjuna

> This arena is meant for all, not for thee alone, O Phalguna! {#q4 1.138}
> — Karna

Kings are those who are stronger, Karna said, and a Kshatriya respects only strength. Why quarrel, which is what the weak do? *Speak then in arrows.* {#p8 1.138}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna,karna,indra,surya,kunti,vidura light=indra,surya,kunti}

Arjuna's brothers embraced him, and he came forward with Drona's leave; Duryodhana's brothers embraced Karna, and he took up his bow. {#p9 1.138}

Then the sky filled with clouds and lightning, and Indra's bow, the rainbow, appeared, with white cranes flying across it. [Indra](indra) was watching over his son; and the Sun, seeing it, drove the clouds away from his own. Arjuna stood in the shadow of the clouds, and Karna in the full light of the sun. {#p10 1.138}

Duryodhana stood by Karna, and Drona, Kripa and Bhishma by Arjuna. The crowd was divided, and so were the women. And [Kunti](kunti), who knew, fainted. [Vidura](vidura) and her women brought her round with sandal paste and water, and she looked at her two sons, both in armour, and was afraid, and could do nothing. {#p11 1.138}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=kripa,karna,duryodhana light=kripa,karna}

[Kripa](kripa), who knew the rules of single combat, spoke to Karna. Arjuna was the youngest son of Kunti, of the Kuru line. Karna should tell them his lineage, his father, his mother and his royal house. Then Arjuna would decide whether to fight. {#p12 1.138}

> Sons of kings never fight with men of inglorious lineage. {#q5 1.138}
> — Kripa

Karna's face, the epic says, became like a pale lotus beaten by the rain. {#p13 1.138}

Duryodhana answered for him. The scriptures say there are three kinds of people who can claim royalty, he said: those of royal blood, heroes, and those who lead armies. *If Phalguna is unwilling to fight with one who is not a king, I will install Karna as king of Anga.* {#p14 1.138}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=karna,duryodhana light=karna,duryodhana}

At that very moment Brahmanas versed in mantras crowned Karna on a golden seat, with parched rice, flowers, water-pots and gold. The royal umbrella was held over him and yak-tail fans waved around him. {#p15 1.138}

> O tiger among monarchs, what shall I give unto thee that may compare with thy gift of a kingdom? {#q6 1.138}
> — Karna

*I eagerly wish for thy friendship,* Duryodhana said. *Be it so,* said Karna, and they embraced. {#p16 1.138}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=adhiratha,karna,bhima light=adhiratha,karna}

Then an old man came into the lists, with his cloth slipping, sweating and trembling, leaning on a staff. It was [Adhiratha](adhiratha), the charioteer. {#p17 1.139}

Karna put down his bow and bowed his head to him, out of a son's love, his head still wet with the water of the coronation. The old man hurriedly covered his feet with the end of his cloth, called him son, embraced him and wept over the newly crowned head. {#p18 1.139}

[Bhima](bhima) saw, and understood that Karna was a charioteer's son, and laughed at him. {#p19 1.139}

> O son of a charioteer, thou dost not deserve death in fight at the hands of Partha. As befits thy race take thou anon the whip. {#q7 1.139}
> — Bhima

He was no more fit to rule Anga, Bhima said, than a dog is fit to eat the butter set before the sacred fire. Karna's lips trembled a little. He sighed deeply and looked up at the sun. {#p20 1.139}

***

::scene{#s8 frame=duryodhana,bhima,drona,kripa,skanda,vishvamitra light=duryodhana}

Duryodhana rose from among his brothers like a maddened elephant rising out of a lotus pool. {#p21 1.139}

> Might is the cardinal virtue of a Kshatriya, and even a Kshatriya of inferior birth deserveth to be fought with. The lineage of heroes, like the sources of a lordly river, is ever unknown. {#q8 1.139}
> — Duryodhana

Fire rises from the waters, he said. The thunderbolt that kills demons was made from the bone of a mortal, [Dadhicha](i2969_dadhicha). No one knows the parentage of the god [Skanda](skanda). Kshatriyas like [Vishvamitra](vishvamitra) have become Brahmanas. Their own teacher Drona was born in a water-pot, and Kripa in a clump of heath. And as for the Pandavas' own births, he said, he knew all about those. {#p22 1.139}

> Can a she-deer bring forth a tiger (like Karna), of the splendour of the Sun, and endued with every auspicious mark, and born also with a natural mail and ear-rings? {#q9 1.139}
> — Duryodhana

Karna deserved to rule the world, not Anga only. If anyone could not bear what Duryodhana had done, let him mount his chariot and bend his bow with his feet. {#p23 1.139}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.139,1.138}
**Duryodhana is right, and does not know why.** The reader has been told what no one in the arena knows: the armour and ear-rings are the Sun's, and Karna is Kunti's eldest son. Duryodhana's guess, that no deer could have borne this tiger, is truer than he can imagine.
::

***

::scene{#s9 frame=karna,duryodhana,kunti,yudhishthira light=kunti,karna}

The crowd murmured its approval. But the sun went down, and Duryodhana took Karna by the hand and led him out of the arena, lit now with countless lamps. The Pandavas went home with Drona, Kripa and Bhishma. The people went home too, some saying the day was Arjuna's, some Karna's, some Duryodhana's. {#p24 1.139}

Kunti had known her son by the marks on his body. Seeing him made king of Anga, she was glad, from a mother's love. {#p25 1.139}

Duryodhana, with Karna beside him, stopped being afraid of Arjuna. And [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) came to believe that there was no warrior on earth like Karna. {#p26 1.139}
