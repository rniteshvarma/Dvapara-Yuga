So [Arjuna](arjuna) drove Indra's chariot to the ocean, its waves heaving like moving cliffs, full of whales and gems, and near it he saw the city of the Nivatakavachas. Matali drove straight down into the earth beneath the sea. The demons heard the rumble of the wheels, thought Indra himself had come, and shut their gates. Arjuna blew Devadatta again and again until the sky echoed. {#p1 3.168}

Then they came out in their thousands, armoured, with javelins and maces and discs. There were thirty million of them, all alike in form and strength. {#p2 3.167 3.168}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

They rained weapons on him, and he cut them down. Then they turned to illusion: showers of rock, then floods of water, then fire and wind, then a darkness so complete that Arjuna and Matali could not see the horses. When Arjuna tired and the horses were hard pressed by falling crags, Matali saw that he was afraid. {#p3 3.169 3.170 3.171}

> O Arjuna, Arjuna! be thou not afraid; send that weapon, the thunder-bolt, O lord of men. {#q1 3.171}
> — Matali

Arjuna loosed Indra's own weapon, the thunderbolt, through the Gandiva, and its arrows went through the illusions and into the demons, and the Nivatakavachas fell in heaps like cliffs. {#p4 3.171}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

On the way home Arjuna saw a city flying through the sky, bright as fire, its trees made of gems: Hiranyapura, the golden city. Long ago, Matali said, two demon women, Puloma and Kalaka, had done austerities for a thousand years, and Brahma gave them a city no god could destroy, for the Paulomas and Kalakanjas. But Brahma had left one opening: they could die at the hands of a mortal. {#p5 3.172}

Arjuna told Matali to drive into it. The demons fought back hard, and the city rose and fell and moved sideways and plunged under water, and Arjuna was afraid. Then he bowed to Shiva and loosed the weapon called Raudra. A being with three heads and six arms, its hair flaming, rose out of it, followed by thousands of forms of beasts, and the golden city came down from the sky in pieces. {#p6 3.172}

The demons' wives came out with their hair loose and wept for their sons, brothers and fathers, *like unto Kurari birds*. {#p7 3.172}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=indra,arjuna,bhishma,drona,karna light=indra}

Back in heaven Indra embraced him and gave him a golden garland, his own armour, the diadem and fine clothes. {#p8 3.173}

> When thou art in the field, Bhishma and Drona and Kripa and Karna and Sakuni together with other Kshatriyas shall not amount unto one-sixteenth part of thee. {#q2 3.173}
> — Indra

*O Arjuna, the time hath come for thy departure; thy brothers have thought of thee.* {#p9 3.173}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna,narada light=yudhishthira}

The next morning Yudhishthira asked to see the weapons. Arjuna purified himself, put on his armour, and began to show them one by one. At the first, the earth shook and the trees trembled, the rivers and the sea boiled, rocks split, the wind stopped, the sun went dark and fire would not burn. The creatures who live under the earth rose up trembling and begged him with joined hands to stop. The sages, the gods and Brahma himself came to watch. {#p10 3.174}

Then [Narada](narada) came. {#p11 3.174}

> O Arjuna, Arjuna, do thou not discharge the celestial weapons. These should never be discharged when there is no object. {#q3 3.174}
> — Narada

Used without cause, he said, they could destroy the three worlds. *O Ajatasatru, thou too wilt behold even these weapons, when Partha will use them for grinding (thy) enemies in battle.* {#p12 3.174}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.174}
**Weapons that must not be shown.** Shiva warned Arjuna that the Pashupata must never be used without cause; now Narada stops even a demonstration. The epic keeps returning to the idea that the greatest weapons are dangerous simply to hold.
::
