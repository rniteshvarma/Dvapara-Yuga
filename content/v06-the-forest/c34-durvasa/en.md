::scene{#s1 frame=durvasa,duryodhana,karna,duhshasana light=durvasa}

Duryodhana heard that the Pandavas were living as happily in the forest as in a city, and it ate at him. Then the irascible sage [Durvasa](durvasa) came to Hastinapura with ten thousand disciples. {#p1 3.260}

Duryodhana served him himself, day and night, like a servant. Durvasa tested him: sometimes he demanded food at once, sometimes he came back late and would not eat, sometimes he woke at midnight, had a meal cooked, and found fault with it. Duryodhana never once lost his temper. At last Durvasa offered him a boon. {#p2 3.260}

Duryodhana had agreed with Karna and Duhshasana in advance what to ask. Go and be Yudhishthira's guest too, he said, with all your disciples. And go at a time when Draupadi has fed the Brahmanas, her husbands and herself, and has lain down to rest. {#p3 3.260}

> The sons of Pandu are now exposed to the fire of Durvasa's wrath. {#q1 3.260}
> — Karna

***

::scene{#s2 frame=durvasa,yudhishthira,draupadi light=durvasa}

So one day, when the Pandavas were sitting at ease and Draupadi was resting after her meal, Durvasa came to the forest with his ten thousand disciples. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) welcomed him and asked him to come back after his bath to eat. The sage and his disciples went down to the river. {#p4 3.261}

[Draupadi](draupadi) was in despair. The sun's vessel fed everyone each day only until she had eaten, and she had eaten. There was no food. She prayed to Krishna. {#p5 3.261}

> As thou didst protect me before from Dussasana, do thou extricate me now from this difficulty. {#q2 3.261}
> — Draupadi

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,draupadi,bhima,rukmini light=krishna}

[Krishna](krishna) heard her, left the bed of [Rukmini](rukmini) where she lay asleep, and came at once. Draupadi told him everything. *I am very much afflicted with hunger*, he said. *Give me some food.* {#p6 3.261}

She was confused. The vessel is empty, she said; I have eaten. *This is no time for jest, O Krishna*, he said. *Bring it.* {#p7 3.261}

He looked into the vessel and found one particle of rice and vegetable stuck to its rim. He ate it. {#p8 3.261}

> May it please the god Hari, the soul of the Universe, and may that god who partaketh at sacrifices, be satiated with this. {#q3 3.261}
> — Krishna

***

::scene{#s4 frame=durvasa,bhima,yudhishthira,krishna light=durvasa}

Krishna told [Bhima](bhima) to go and call the sages to dinner. But down at the river, the sages felt their stomachs full to the throat, and looked at each other. We have spoiled the king's meal, said Durvasa; the Pandavas are devoted to Hari, and if they look at us in anger they will burn us like cotton. *Do ye all run away quickly without seeing them!* And they ran. {#p9 3.261}

Bhima searched every landing-place and could not find them. Yudhishthira feared they would come back at midnight. Then Krishna told them there was nothing to fear: the sage had fled. And he went home. {#p10 3.261}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=3.261}
**Two rescues.** In Ganguli's text Draupadi herself says Krishna saved her from Duhshasana, which is the popular telling of the robe scene. This episode is also one the Critical Edition leaves out of its main text, as a later addition. Both the rescue in the hall and the grain of rice belong to the Krishna of devotion, whom many readers know best.
::
