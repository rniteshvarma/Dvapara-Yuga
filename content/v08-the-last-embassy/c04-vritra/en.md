[Indra](indra) admitted it: Vritra filled the universe now, and he could no longer face him. They went to [Vishnu](vishnu), who had covered the three worlds in three steps and bound Bali. Vishnu gave them a plan. Make peace with Vritra, he said. {#p1 5.10}

> By virtue of my power, victory, ye gods, will be won by Indra, for, remaining invisible, I shall enter into his thunderbolt, that best of weapons. {#q1 5.10}
> — Vishnu

***

::scene{#s2 frame=indra light=indra}

So the Rishis went to Vritra and spoke of friendship: the friendship of the righteous, they said, is formed at a single meeting. How could there be friendship between two enemies? asked Vritra. But he bowed to the Rishis and named his terms. {#p2 5.10}

> Ye lords of the Brahmana race, ordain so that Indra himself or the gods do not kill me by what is dry, or wet; by stone, or by wood; by a weapon fit for close fight, or by a missile; in the day time, or at night. {#q2 5.10}
> — Vritra

*Very good!* said the Rishis, and peace was made. And Indra, always looking for a gap, found Vritra one evening on the seashore, at the awful hour that is neither day nor night. In the sea he saw a mass of foam as big as a hill. {#p3 5.10}

> This is neither dry, nor wet, nor is it a weapon; let me hurl it at Vritra. {#q3 5.10}
> — Indra

He threw the foam, with the thunderbolt in it, and Vishnu, inside the foam, ended Vritra's life. The darkness lifted from the sky, a sweet wind blew, and all beings praised Indra. {#p4 5.10}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=indra light=indra}

But now Indra was overcome: by the falsehood he had used, and by the sin of killing a Brahmana, Tvashtri's three-headed son. He went to the edge of the worlds, lost his senses, and hid in the waters *like a writhing snake*. {#p5 5.10}

With him gone, the earth withered. Trees died, rivers stopped, the rains failed, and the world had no king. The gods and Rishis were afraid. Who would rule heaven? No one wanted to. {#p6 5.10}
