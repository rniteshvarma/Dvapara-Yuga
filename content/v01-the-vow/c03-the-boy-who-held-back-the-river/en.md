[Shantanu](shantanu) ruled from Hastinapura, the capital of the Kurus, and the epic remembers his reign as a golden one. He was truthful and free of guile, slow to anger and without malice. He was handsome as the moon, mighty as an elephant in its fury, and every sign of royalty seemed to dwell in him; even his neck bore three lines, like a conch-shell. The other kings of the earth called him King of kings. {#p1 1.100}

Under him, the text says, the kings of the earth were without fear, and *they all slept in peace, rising from bed every morning after happy dreams.* No deer, boar or bird was killed without need. Shantanu was father to everyone who had no protector, to the poor and the helpless, and to birds and beasts as well. {#p2 1.100}

And far away, the son he had lost was growing up. {#p3 1.100}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=shantanu,bhishma,ganga light=bhishma,shantanu}

One day Shantanu was hunting along the banks of the Ganges, following a deer he had wounded with an arrow. He noticed that the river had grown shallow. The first of rivers no longer ran as it always had, and the king stopped to wonder why. {#p4 1.100}

Then he saw the reason. A young man stood by the water, handsome and strongly built, *like Indra himself*. With his celestial weapon he had checked the whole flow of the Ganga, and held the river back with his arrows. {#p5 1.100}

Shantanu stared in astonishment. He did not know the youth. He had seen his son only once, for a few moments, just after his birth, and could not have recognised him now. But the youth knew his father at once. He did not reveal himself. Instead he clouded the king's sight with his celestial powers of illusion, and vanished before his eyes. {#p6 1.100}

Shantanu had seen enough to guess. He turned to the river and called out to her: *Show me that child.* {#p7 1.100}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=ganga,bhishma light=ganga,bhishma}

Ganga rose from the water in a beautiful form, dressed in white and decked with ornaments, holding the boy on her right arm. Even so, Shantanu did not recognise her at first, though he had once lived with her for years. {#p8 1.100}

> O tiger among men, that eighth son whom thou hadst some time before begat upon me is this. Know that this excellent child is conversant with all weapons, O monarch, take him now. I have reared him with care. {#q1 1.100}
> — Ganga

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,vasishtha,shukra,i1862_brihaspati,parashurama light=vasishtha,shukra,i1862_brihaspati,parashurama}

She had not raised him alone. She named his teachers, and they were teachers of gods and of asuras. With [Vasishtha](vasishtha), the very sage whose curse had sent him to earth, he had studied the Vedas and all their branches. He knew every science that [Usanas](shukra), the teacher of the asuras, knew. He had mastered all the learning of [Brihaspati](i1862_brihaspati), the teacher of the gods. And he knew every weapon known to [Rama, the son of Jamadagni](parashurama), the warrior-sage better known today as Parashurama. {#p9 1.100}

He was a mighty bowman, Ganga said, like Indra in battle, and he understood all the treatises on the duties of a king. Gods and asuras alike looked on him with favour. {#p10 1.100}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.100}
**A teacher to remember.** Rama, the son of Jamadagni, is better known today as Parashurama, "Rama with the axe". Here he is simply named as the master whose weapons the boy had learned. He will come back into this story, and the student and his teacher will one day face each other in battle.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=shantanu,bhishma,pratipa,i6726_mahabhisha light=bhishma}

So Shantanu took his son, who shone like the sun, and brought him home to Hastinapura. He gathered all the Pauravas and, before them, installed the boy as heir to the throne, to protect the kingdom after him. {#p11 1.100}

The prince soon won over everyone, his father, his kinsmen and all the people of the kingdom. Father and son lived happily together. {#p12 1.100}

His names gathered around him: Gangadatta, *given by Ganga*; Gangeya, *son of Ganga*; and Devavrata. He was the Vasu Dyu, born under a curse to live long, to obey his father, and to have no children. No one at Hastinapura knew yet how the last of those would come true. {#p13 1.98 1.99 1.100}

::scene{#s6 frame=shantanu,satyavati light=satyavati}

Four years passed. Then one day the king went walking in the woods along another river, the Yamuna, and caught a sweet scent on the air, coming from somewhere he could not see. {#p14 1.100}
