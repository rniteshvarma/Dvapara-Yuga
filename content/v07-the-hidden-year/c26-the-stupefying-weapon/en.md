::scene{#s1 frame=duryodhana,arjuna,vikarna light=duryodhana}

With Bhishma gone, [Duryodhana](duryodhana) himself came on roaring and put a spear-headed arrow into [Arjuna](arjuna)'s forehead. The blood ran down *like a wreath of golden flowers*, and Arjuna struck back with arrows like poisonous snakes. [Vikarna](vikarna) charged on an elephant huge as a mountain; one iron arrow went into its head up to the feathers and it fell, and Vikarna ran eight hundred paces to Vivimshati's chariot. Another arrow went into Duryodhana's chest, and the king turned his chariot and fled, vomiting blood, to wherever Arjuna was not. {#p1 4.64}

> Lo, I am an obedient servant of Yudhishthira, myself being the third son of Pritha, standing here for battle. Turn back, show me thy face, O son of Dhritarashtra, and bear in thy mind the behaviour of kings. The name Duryodhana bestowed on thee before is hereby rendered meaningless. {#q1 4.64}
> — Arjuna

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,karna,bhishma,drona,kripa,arjuna light=arjuna}

Stung *like a snake that is trampled under foot*, Duryodhana turned back. Karna, Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Vivimshati and Duhshasana all turned with him to guard him, and surrounded Arjuna with celestial weapons. Then Arjuna used the weapon he had from Indra, the Sammohana. He filled every direction with arrows, and with the twang of the Gandiva and the blast of his conch he took their senses away. {#p2 4.65}

> And all of them stood still, their bows, from which they were never separated, dropping down from their hands. {#q2 4.65}

Arjuna remembered the princess's request. Go among them while they are senseless, he told [Uttara](uttar), and bring the white garments of Drona and Kripa, Karna's handsome yellow ones, and the blue ones of the king and Drona's son. But keep Bhishma's horses on your left: he was not stupefied, for he knew how to counter the weapon. Uttara jumped down, took the garments, and drove Arjuna out of the battle. {#p3 4.65}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhishma,duryodhana light=bhishma}

[Bhishma](bhishma) struck after him; Arjuna killed his horses and his driver, put ten arrows in him, and came out *like the sun emerging from the clouds*. Duryodhana woke and saw him standing alone. How had he escaped? Stop him! Bhishma smiled. {#p4 4.65}

> Where had been this sense of thine, and where had been thy prowess too, when thou hadst been in a state of unconsciousness renouncing thy arrows and handsome bow? {#q3 4.65}
> — Bhishma

> Vibhatsu is not addicted to the commission of atrocious deeds; nor is his soul inclined to sin. He renounceth not his principles even for the sake of the three worlds. It is for this only that all of us have not been slain in this battle. {#q4 4.65}
> — Bhishma

Let them go home, he said, and let Partha go with the cattle. Duryodhana sighed deeply and said nothing, and the Kurus turned for their city. {#p5 4.65}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna,bhishma,drona,ashwatthama,kripa,duryodhana light=arjuna}

Arjuna followed them a little way, glad, to honour them. He saluted the grandsire and the teacher, and Drona's son and Kripa, with arrows, and with one more he broke Duryodhana's jewelled crown to pieces. He blew Devadatta and filled the three worlds with the Gandiva's twang. Then he told Uttara to turn the horses: the cattle were won and the enemy gone. The gods went home to their own worlds, talking of what they had seen. {#p6 4.65}
