The night passed in talk with Vidura, too quickly for Vidura. At dawn, bards and conchs woke [Krishna](krishna). He bathed, poured butter on the fire and worshipped the rising sun, and while he was still at his prayers [Duryodhana](duryodhana) and [Shakuni](shakuni) came to say the court was waiting. He gave the Brahmanas gold, robes, cattle and horses, put on the Kaustubha gem, and mounted his chariot. {#p1 5.94}

Vidura followed in his own car, Duryodhana and Shakuni in another, Satyaki, Kritavarma and the Vrishnis behind on cars, horses and elephants, with thousands of soldiers marching ahead. The streets were swept and watered; the women crowded the balconies until the houses seemed ready to fall. At the court gate conchs blew, and the kings' hair stood on end at the rumble of his wheels. {#p2 5.94}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,vidura,satyaki,dhritarashtra,bhishma,drona light=krishna}

> And that illustrious hero entered the court, arm-in-arm with Vidura and Satyaki on either side, and overshadowing with his own the splendour of all the Kurus, like the sun overshadowing the radiance of lesser lights in the firmament. {#q1 5.94}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), [Drona](drona) and [Bhishma](bhishma) rose, and the thousands of kings rose with them. Krishna took the golden seat kept for him and smiled his greetings to each by age. {#p3 5.94}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,narada,bhishma light=narada}

Then, as he sat, he saw in the sky the Rishis he had met on the road, with [Narada](narada) at their head. *If they are not seated, no one here is capable of taking his seat*, he told Bhishma, and seats of gold were brought. Only when the Rishis had sat did Krishna sit again, and the kings with him. {#p4 5.94}

Karna and Duryodhana sat together on one seat, not far from him; Vidura's seat, covered with a white deerskin, almost touched his. The kings gazed at him and could not have enough, *like drinkers of the Amrita*. {#p5 5.94}

> And Janardana attired in yellow robes having the complexion of the Atasi flower, sat in the midst of that assembly like a sapphire mounted on gold. And after Govinda had taken his seat, a perfect silence ensued, for none present there spoke a single word. {#q2 5.94}
