::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,i5309_kanika light=dhritarashtra,i5309_kanika}

Sleepless with worry, [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) sent for [Kanika](i5309_kanika), a Brahmana minister skilled in statecraft, and spoke to him frankly. {#p1 1.142}

> O best of Brahmanas, the Pandavas are daily overshadowing the earth. I am exceedingly jealous of them. Should I have peace or war with them? {#q1 1.142}
> — Dhritarashtra

***

::scene{#s2 frame=i5309_kanika,dhritarashtra light=i5309_kanika}

Kanika asked the king not to be angry with what he was about to hear, and then gave him a long lesson in the hard side of kingship. {#p2 1.142}

A king should always be ready to strike, he said, and hide his own weaknesses like a tortoise hiding its limbs, while watching for the weaknesses of his enemies. A thorn not drawn out entirely will fester. An enemy, however small, is a spark that can burn a whole forest. When an enemy is in your power, destroy him by any means, open or secret, and show him no mercy even if he begs for protection. {#p3 1.142}

> If thy son, friend, brother, father, or even the spiritual preceptor, anyone becometh thy foe, thou shouldst, if desirous of prosperity, slay him without scruples. {#q2 1.142}
> — Kanika

Speak softly before you strike, he said, and while you are striking; and afterwards, grieve for your victim and even weep. Be humble in speech, but let your heart be as sharp as a razor. {#p4 1.142}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=i5309_kanika light=i5309_kanika}

To show how it was done, Kanika told the story of a clever jackal. {#p5 1.142}

The jackal had four friends, a tiger, a mouse, a wolf and a mongoose, and together they wanted to catch a swift young deer. On the jackal's advice, the mouse gnawed the deer's feet while it slept, and the tiger killed it. Then the jackal sent them all to bathe, and dealt with them one by one as they came back. He told the tiger that the mouse was boasting the kill was his, and the tiger left in disgust. He told the mouse that the mongoose meant to eat him, and the mouse fled. He told the wolf that the tiger was angry with him and on his way, and the wolf ran. To the mongoose he simply offered a fight, and the mongoose, hearing that the jackal had beaten the other three, went away. And the jackal ate the deer alone. {#p6 1.142}

Frighten the timid, win over the brave, buy the greedy, and bring equals and inferiors to heel by a show of strength, said Kanika. *Protect thyself from the sons of Pandu,* he told the king, and went home. Dhritarashtra was left thoughtful and unhappy. {#p7 1.142}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.142}
**Kanika's lesson.** The epic gives Kanika's counsel at full length, and does not endorse it. It is advice about how power is held, and it is put into a frightened king's ear at exactly the moment he is looking for permission.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=shakuni,duryodhana,duhshasana,karna,dhritarashtra light=duryodhana,shakuni}

The people of Hastinapura were talking. In the courtyards and the meeting places they said that Dhritarashtra, born blind, had never been given the kingdom, so how could he be king now? Bhishma had given it up long ago and would never take it. They would crown Yudhishthira, young, skilled in battle, learned, truthful and kind, and he would look after Bhishma and Dhritarashtra and his sons. {#p8 1.143}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) heard them and could not bear it. He went to his father alone. {#p9 1.143}

> If Pandu's son now obtaineth the kingdom as his inheritance from Pandu, his son will obtain it after him and that son's son also, and so on will it descend in Pandu's line. {#q3 1.143}
> — Duryodhana

He and his children would be shut out of the royal line, Duryodhana said, and despised by everyone, and would have to live on others for their food. {#p10 1.143}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=dhritarashtra,duryodhana,karna,shakuni,duhshasana,pandu light=dhritarashtra,duryodhana}

Dhritarashtra remembered Kanika's words, and his mind began to waver. Duryodhana, [Karna](karna), [Shakuni](shakuni) and [Duhshasana](duhshasana) talked it over together. Then Duryodhana asked his father to send the Pandavas, by some clever means, to the town of Varanavata. {#p11 1.144}

Dhritarashtra hesitated. [Pandu](pandu) had always been dutiful to him, he said, and had given him everything, even the kingdom. Yudhishthira was as good as his father, and the people loved him. The counsellors and soldiers had all been cared for by Pandu. Would the citizens not kill them all for Yudhishthira's sake? {#p12 1.144}

Duryodhana said they would win the people over with money and honours. The treasury and the ministers were already theirs. Let the Pandavas go to Varanavata, and come back when Duryodhana was king. {#p13 1.144}

> This, O Duryodhana, is the very thought existing in my mind. But from its sinfulness I have never given expression to it. {#q4 1.144}
> — Dhritarashtra

Bhishma, Drona, Vidura and Kripa would never agree, he said; to them the two sets of cousins were the same. {#p14 1.144}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=duryodhana,bhishma,ashwatthama,drona,kripa,vidura light=duryodhana}

Duryodhana had thought of that too. Bhishma, he said, cared no more for one side than the other, and would stay neutral. Ashwatthama was on Duryodhana's side, and where the son went the father would follow; and Kripa would never leave Drona and his own sister's son. Vidura depended on them for his living, though he was secretly with the Pandavas, and alone he could do nothing. {#p15 1.144}

Send them this very day, he begged his father, and put out the fire that was burning in him and robbing him of sleep. {#p16 1.144}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=dhritarashtra,yudhishthira,bhishma,vidura,drona,gandhari light=yudhishthira,dhritarashtra}

Duryodhana and his brothers began winning the people over with gifts and honours. Then some of Dhritarashtra's counsellors, on his instructions, began talking at court about how lovely Varanavata was, and how a great festival of Shiva had begun there, with the finest procession ever seen. When the Pandavas began to want to see it, Dhritarashtra told them to go: to enjoy themselves, give gifts to the Brahmanas, and come back to Hastinapura in time. {#p17 1.145}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) understood exactly why he was being sent. He was weak and without friends; he said, *So be it.* Then he went to Bhishma, Vidura, Drona, Kripa, Ashwatthama, [Gandhari](gandhari) and all the elders, and asked them humbly for their blessing. *Let all the elements bless you along your way,* they said. {#p18 1.145}

***

::scene{#s8 frame=duryodhana,i8787_purochana light=duryodhana,i8787_purochana}

Duryodhana was delighted. He called his counsellor [Purochana](i8787_purochana) in private and took his right hand. The world was his, he said, but it was Purochana's equally; he had no one he trusted more. {#p19 1.146}

He told him to go to Varanavata that very day in a chariot drawn by fast mules, and to build a four-sided house near the arsenal. It was to be built of hemp, resin and everything that would burn, and its walls plastered with earth mixed with butter, oil, fat and a great deal of lac, so that no one, looking closely, would guess. It was to be furnished with the finest seats, carriages and beds, so that Dhritarashtra would have nothing to complain of. {#p20 1.146}

> And assuring thyself that the Pandavas are sleeping within in confidence and without fear, thou must then set fire to that mansion beginning at the outer door. {#q5 1.146}
> — Duryodhana

Everyone would think it an accident. *So be it,* said Purochana, and went, and did everything he had been told. {#p21 1.146}
