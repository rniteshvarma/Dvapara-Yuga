[Krishna](krishna) went to [Kunti](kunti)'s house, bowed at her feet, and told her briefly what had happened in the assembly. Duryodhana had refused everything; his hour had come. He was going back to her sons. What should he tell them from her? {#p1 5.132}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti,yudhishthira light=kunti}

Her message to [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) was not a gentle one. {#p2 5.132}

> Thy virtue, O son, is decreasing greatly. Do not act vainly. {#q1 5.132}
> — Kunti

He was like a man who reads the Vedas without grasping their meaning, she said, and sees only one kind of virtue. Let him look to the duties of his own order. The Creator made the Kshatriya for hard deeds and for the protection of the people, to live by the strength of his own arms. The royal sage Muchukunda, offered the whole earth as a gift by Kubera, refused it: he would rule only what he had won with his own arms. {#p3 5.132}

> Let not this doubt be thine, viz., whether the era is the cause of the king, or the king the cause of the era, for (know this to be certain that) the king is the cause of the era. {#q2 5.132}
> — Kunti

It is the king who brings in the Krita age, or the Treta, or the Dvapara, or the Kali. The weak-hearted king who clings to compassion never earns the merit of protecting his people. Neither Pandu nor she nor his grandfather had ever wished him that kind of understanding; they had prayed for sacrifice, gifts, courage, children and might. {#p4 5.132}

A Brahmana may beg, but not a Kshatriya; nor may he farm. Let him win back his father's share by conciliation, by division, by gifts or by force. {#p5 5.132}

> What can be a matter of greater grief than that I, deprived of friends, should live upon food supplied by others, after having brought thee forth, thou enhancer of the joys of friends? {#q3 5.132}
> — Kunti

Fight, she said, as kings fight. Do not sink your ancestors in shame. {#p6 5.132}
