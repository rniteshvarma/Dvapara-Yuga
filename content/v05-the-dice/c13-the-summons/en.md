::scene{#s1 frame=vidura,dhritarashtra light=vidura}

[Vidura](vidura) bowed at his brother's feet and refused. {#p1 2.56}

> I approve not, O king, of this command of thine. Do not act so. I fear, this will bring about the destruction of our race. {#q1 2.56}
> — Vidura

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) answered that if Fate was not against them, no quarrel would come of it; the whole universe moves at the will of its Creator and is not free. With Bhishma and Drona and Vidura himself beside him, what harm could happen? *Tell me nothing.* Go to Indraprastha, he said, and bring Yudhishthira. {#p2 2.48 2.56}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vidura,yudhishthira light=vidura,yudhishthira}

So Vidura drove to Indraprastha, against his will. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) greeted him and saw at once that his uncle's mind was troubled. {#p3 2.57}

Vidura gave the message: the king invites you and your brothers to see his new hall, to say if it equals your own, and to sit to a friendly match at dice. And then he added what the message did not say: *thou wilt see there those gamblers and cheats* that Dhritarashtra had gathered. {#p4 2.57}

*If we sit to a match at dice, we may quarrel*, said Yudhishthira. *What do you think we should do?* Vidura said he knew gambling was the root of misery, and had tried to stop it; the king had sent him anyway. Yudhishthira asked who would be playing. Shakuni, said Vidura, and Vivimshati, Chitrasena, Satyavrata, Purumitra and Jaya. {#p5 2.57}

> If, however, he challengeth me, I will never refuse. For that, as settled, is my eternal vow. {#q2 2.57}
> — Yudhishthira

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.57,2.46}
**Two vows.** Yudhishthira has two promises binding him here: the old rule of his order that a Kshatriya does not refuse a challenge, and the new vow he made after Vyasa's prophecy, to speak no hard word to any king for thirteen years. Vidura has warned him plainly. He goes anyway.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,draupadi,dhritarashtra,gandhari,duhshala light=yudhishthira}

The next day he set out with his brothers, and with Draupadi and the women of the household. {#p6 2.57}

> Like some brilliant body falling before the eyes, Fate depriveth us of reason, and man, tied as it were with a cord, submitteth to the sway of Providence. {#q3 2.57}
> — Yudhishthira

At Hastinapura he embraced all the Kurus one by one, greeted [Gandhari](gandhari) among her daughters-in-law *like Rohini by the stars*, and Dhritarashtra smelt the heads of all five brothers. [Duhshala](duhshala) and the women of the household came to visit Draupadi, and the Kaurava wives, seeing her beauty, grew jealous and gloomy. That night the Pandavas slept to music, and at dawn they went into the hall. {#p7 2.57}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=shakuni,yudhishthira,duryodhana light=shakuni,yudhishthira}

*The assembly is full*, said [Shakuni](shakuni). *All had been waiting for thee. Let the dice be cast.* {#p8 2.58}

> O Sakuni, vanquish us, not like a wretch, by deceitful means. {#q4 2.58}
> — Yudhishthira

Gambling, Yudhishthira said, had no Kshatriya prowess in it and no virtue. The sage Devala had taught that it was a sin to play deceitfully. Shakuni answered that every contest is a man of skill approaching a man without it, the learned against the unlearned, the strong against the weak, and no one calls that dishonest. *If thou art under any fear, desist then from play.* {#p9 2.58}

*Summoned, I do not withdraw*, said Yudhishthira. *Who will stake against me?* {#p10 2.58}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) said he would put up the wealth, and his uncle would throw the dice for him. Yudhishthira objected that one man playing for another was against the rules. *If, however, thou art still bent on it, let the play begin.* {#p11 2.58}
