::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=yudhishthira}

At Indraprastha [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) ruled, and the people were happy under him. He gave virtue, pleasure and profit each its due, the epic says, as if they were three friends, and he the fourth among them. He never said anything untrue, unkind or unbearable. His brothers brought other kings under his sway, and no enemy troubled their peace. {#p1 1.224}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,krishna,draupadi,subhadra light=arjuna,krishna}

Then summer came, and [Arjuna](arjuna) said to [Krishna](krishna): *Let us go to the banks of the Yamuna.* With Yudhishthira's leave they went, with friends and women of the household, to a lovely place by the river with tall trees and pleasure-houses, food and wine and garlands. The women danced and sang and swam and laughed; [Draupadi](draupadi) and [Subhadra](subhadra), merry with wine, gave away their own robes and ornaments. {#p2 1.224}

Krishna and Arjuna went off a little way, sat down, and talked about deeds of old. And a Brahmana came towards them. {#p3 1.224}

He was as tall as a sal tree, with skin like molten gold and a beard bright yellow tinged with green, matted hair and rags, and he blazed like the morning sun. Krishna and Arjuna rose to their feet. {#p4 1.224}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=i239_agni,krishna,arjuna,takshaka,indra light=i239_agni}

> I am a voracious Brahmana that always eateth much. O thou of the Vrishni race, and O Partha, I solicit you to gratify me by giving me sufficient food. {#q1 1.225}
> — the Brahmana

What food did he want? they asked. {#p5 1.225}

> I do not desire to eat ordinary food. Know that I am Agni! Give me that food which suiteth me. {#q2 1.225}
> — Agni

He wanted the forest of Khandava, he said. But Indra guarded it, because his friend [Takshaka](takshaka), king of the Nagas, lived there with his family, and whenever [Agni](i239_agni) blazed up, [Indra](indra) poured rain on him. If these two great archers would keep off the rain and stop the creatures from escaping, he could eat at last. {#p6 1.225}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=i2892_shvetaki,durvasa,shiva,i239_agni,brahma light=i2892_shvetaki,i239_agni}

Why did Agni want it? The epic goes back to explain. {#p7 1.225}

There was once a king, [Shvetaki](i2892_shvetaki), who loved sacrifices above everything. He sacrificed for so many years that his priests' eyes were ruined by the smoke and they refused to serve him any longer; when he begged them, they told him to go and ask Shiva. So he did, with penance on Kailasa, standing six months with his arms raised. Shiva set him a condition: twelve years of pouring butter into the fire without a break. Shvetaki did it, and Shiva gave him a priest who was a portion of himself, the sage [Durvasa](durvasa), and the great sacrifice was done. {#p8 1.225}

But for twelve years Agni had drunk an unbroken stream of butter. He grew pale and sick and lost his appetite and his strength. He went to [Brahma](brahma), who told him the cure: the forest of Khandava, full of creatures. Eat their fat, and he would be himself again. {#p9 1.225}

Agni went and blazed up. And the creatures of the forest put him out: elephants by the hundred thousand spraying water from their trunks, many-hooded snakes pouring water from their hoods. Seven times he tried; seven times they put him out. {#p10 1.225}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=i239_agni,brahma,krishna,arjuna light=krishna,arjuna}

Agni went back to Brahma, angry and still sick. Brahma told him that the old gods Nara and Narayana were on earth, as Arjuna and Krishna, and were near Khandava now. Ask them. {#p11 1.226}

So Agni had come. And Arjuna answered him. He had celestial weapons enough to fight many wielders of the thunderbolt, he said. But he had no bow strong enough for his arms, no quivers that would never run empty, no chariot that could carry the arrows he needed or horses swift enough, and Krishna had no weapon worthy of him. {#p12 1.226}

> O Pavaka, we are ready to do all that manliness and prowess can do. But, O exalted one, it behoveth thee to give us the adequate means. {#q3 1.226}
> — Arjuna
