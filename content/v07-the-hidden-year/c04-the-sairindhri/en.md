::scene{#s1 frame=draupadi light=draupadi}

[Draupadi](draupadi) bound her long black hair into a knotted braid, threw it over her right shoulder and hid it under her cloth: a single piece of black cloth, dirty though costly. Then, dressed as a sairindhri, a lady's maid, she wandered the city as if in distress. People came up to ask who she was. *I am a king's Sairindhri.* Nobody believed a maid could look like that. {#p1 4.9}

From her terrace, Queen [Sudeshna](sudeshna), Virata's wife and the daughter of the king of Kekaya, saw her and called her up. She went through every mark of beauty she could see on her, and asked whether she was a Yakshi, a goddess, a Gandharvi, an Apsara, a Naga woman, the queen of Indra. {#p2 4.9}

> I am neither a goddess nor a Gandharvi, nor a Yakshi, nor a Rakshasi. I am a maid-servant of the Sairindhri class. {#q1 4.9}
> — Draupadi

She could dress hair, pound fragrant pastes for unguents, and make garlands of jasmine, lotus, blue lily and champaka. She had served Krishna's queen Satyabhama, and Draupadi, the wife of the Pandavas. *Draupadi herself called me Malini.* {#p3 4.9}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=sudeshna,draupadi light=sudeshna}

Sudeshna would have kept her *upon my head itself*, she said, but for one fear: the king would see her and forsake his queen. {#p4 4.9}

> Even as a person that climbs up a tree for compassing his own destruction, even as the crab conceives for her own ruin, I may, O thou of sweet smiles, bring destruction upon myself by harbouring thee. {#q2 4.9}
> — Sudeshna

Draupadi answered that no one could have her. Five young Gandharvas were her husbands, and they guarded her in secret. She would serve only where she was not made to eat another's leftovers or wash anyone's feet, and any man who tried to take her like a common woman would die that same night. Sudeshna agreed to her terms, and took her in. {#p5 4.9}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=sahadeva,virata light=sahadeva}

[Sahadeva](sahadeva) came in a cowherd's dress, speaking the cowherds' dialect, to the king's cow-pens. Virata had him brought, and asked who he was. {#p6 4.10}

> I am a Vaisya, Arishtanemi by name. I was employed as a cowherd in the service of those bulls of the Kuru race, the sons of Pandu. {#q3 4.10}
> — Sahadeva

People had called him Tantripala, he said. He had kept Yudhishthira's herds, divisions of many thousands each, and knew the past, present and future of every cow within ten yojanas, how to make herds multiply and keep them free of disease, and how to pick out the bulls with lucky marks. Virata handed over his hundred thousand cattle and all their keepers. {#p7 4.10}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna,virata,uttara_f light=arjuna}

Then at the gate appeared a person of enormous size and great beauty, dressed in women's ornaments, with large earrings and conch-shell bracelets overlaid with gold, and long hair loose about the neck, walking with an elephant's gait and shaking the ground. Virata stared. {#p8 4.11}

> It seemeth to me that such a person as thou can never be of the neuter sex. {#q4 4.11}
> — Virata

[Arjuna](arjuna) said he could sing, dance and play, and asked to teach the princess [Uttara](uttara_f). How he had come to this form, he would not say: it would only add to his pain. {#p9 4.11}

> Know me, O king of men, to be Vrihannala, a son or daughter without father or mother. {#q5 4.11}
> — Arjuna

The king tested Brihannala in dance and music, had the women of the palace confirm that the condition was permanent, and sent him to the maidens' apartments. There he taught Uttara, her friends and her maids, and they grew fond of him. {#p10 4.11}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=4.11}
The form was Urvashi's curse, which Indra had softened to a single year, the year Arjuna could choose. Volume VI, chapter 13 tells it.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=nakula,virata light=nakula}

Last came [Nakula](nakula), who stopped to look hard at the king's horses. Virata had him brought in: whoever he was, he knew horses. Nakula said he had kept Yudhishthira's horses, and that he could break them, cure them, and correct the vicious ones. {#p11 4.12}

> No animal in my hands becometh weak or ill. {#q6 4.12}
> — Nakula

His name, he said, was Granthika. Virata gave him all the horses and their grooms and charioteers, and said that seeing him was like seeing Yudhishthira himself. So the sons of Pandu lived in the country of the Matsyas, true to their pledge, keeping their composure through their pain. {#p12 4.12}
