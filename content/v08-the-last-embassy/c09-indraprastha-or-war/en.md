> What words from me, O Sanjaya, hast thou heard, indicative of war, that thou apprehendest war? O sire, peace is preferable to war. {#q1 5.26}
> — Yudhishthira

Who would choose war, if he could have what was his without it? But desire is never satisfied by its object; it grows *like unkindled fire when clarified butter is poured upon it*. Dhritarashtra had everything, and still he grieved. That was because he had followed his son and disregarded Vidura, the wisest of the Kurus. Duryodhana's counsellors now were Duhshasana, Shakuni and Karna. Even at the dice game, Yudhishthira had seen the end of the Kurus coming, when Vidura's words won no praise. {#p1 5.26}

Duryodhana believed his object won only because he had not yet heard the Gandiva or seen Bhima angry. Still, for Sanjaya's sake, Yudhishthira would forgive. {#p2 5.26}

> Let me have Indraprastha for my kingdom, Let this be given to me by Duryodhana, the chief of Bharata's race. {#q2 5.26}
> — Yudhishthira

***

::scene{#s2 frame=sanjaya,yudhishthira light=sanjaya}

Sanjaya answered with a sermon. Life is short and liable to infamy. If the Kurus would not give up his share without war, *it is far better for thee to live upon alms in the kingdom of the Andhakas and the Vrishnis than obtain sovereignty by war*. {#p3 5.27}

Why had they spent all those years in the forest, he asked, if they meant to fight now? They had had the army and the allies then. *Why are you now desirous of fighting, having let the proper opportunity slip?* {#p4 5.27}

> Wrath, O mighty king, is a bitter drug, though it has nothing to do with disease; it brings on a disease of the head, robs one of his fair fame, and leads to sinful acts. {#q3 5.27}
> — Sanjaya

What joy would there be in a world where Bhishma lay dead, and Drona and his son, and Kripa, Vikarna, Karna and Duryodhana? {#p5 5.27}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,krishna light=krishna}

Yudhishthira agreed that righteousness came first. But Sanjaya should first make sure which was righteousness and which was not. {#p6 5.28}

> When vice assumes the aspects of virtue and virtue itself wholly seems as vice, and virtue, again, appears in its native form, they that are learned should discriminate it by means of their reason. {#q4 5.28}
> — Yudhishthira

In times of distress, he said, duties change. He would not seek even the world of Brahma by unrighteous means. And here was [Krishna](krishna), who sought the good of both sides. Let Krishna say whether he would be at fault if he gave up the idea of peace, or if he fought. {#p7 5.28}

> Krishna is dear to us, and is the most illustrious of men. I never disregard what Krishna sayeth. {#q5 5.28}
> — Yudhishthira
