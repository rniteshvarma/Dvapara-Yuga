The one who does the work that should be done without caring for its fruit, [Krishna](krishna) said, is the renouncer and the yogi; not the one who lets his sacred fire go out or stops acting. {#p1 6.30}

> One should raise (his ) self by self; one should not degrade (his) self; for one's own self is one's friend, and one's own self is one's enemy. {#q1 6.30}
> — Krishna

To whom a clod, a stone and gold are alike, who looks the same on friends, enemies, strangers, the good and the wicked, he is the best. {#p2 6.30}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna light=krishna}

Let the yogi sit alone in a clean, quiet place, on a seat neither too high nor too low, covered with cloth, deerskin and kusha grass; body, head and neck straight and still, eyes on the tip of his nose, mind calm and fearless, thinking of Krishna. Yoga is not for one who eats too much or not at all, sleeps too much or too little, but for the one who is moderate in food, play, work, sleep and waking. {#p3 6.30}

> As a lamp in a windless spot doth not flicker, even that is the resemblance declared of a devotee whose heart hath been restrained and who applieth his self to abstraction. {#q2 6.30}
> — Krishna

Wherever the restless mind wanders, bring it back to the self. Then the yogi sees the self in all creatures and all creatures in the self, and whoever sees Krishna everywhere and everything in Krishna is never lost to him. {#p4 6.30}

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,krishna light=arjuna}

> O Krishna, the mind is restless, boisterous, perverse, and obstinate. Its restraint I regard to be as difficult of accomplishment as the restraint of the wind. {#q3 6.30}
> — Arjuna

> With practice, however, O son of Kunti, and with the abandonment of desire, it can be controlled. {#q4 6.30}
> — Krishna

And what of the man who has faith but falls away from yoga before he succeeds, Arjuna asked: is he lost, like a cloud torn apart? {#p5 6.30}

> since none, O sire, who performs good (acts) comes by an evil end. {#q5 6.30}
> — Krishna

Such a man lives long in the worlds of the righteous and is born again into a pure and prosperous house, or even a family of yogis, and takes up the work where he left it, and after many births reaches the goal. The yogi is greater than the ascetic, the scholar and the man of rites; so, said Krishna, be a yogi. {#p6 6.30}
