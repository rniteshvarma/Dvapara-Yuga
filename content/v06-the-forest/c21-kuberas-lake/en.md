::scene{#s1 frame=bhima light=bhima}

Near the Kailasa cliff [Bhima](bhima) found the lake the flowers came from: Kubera's own pleasure lake, fed by the mountain's cascades, its water sweet as nectar, its surface covered with golden lotuses on stalks of lapis lazuli and the Saugandhika flowers he had come for. He drank deeply. {#p1 3.152}

Thousands of Rakshasas called the Krodhavashas guarded it for Kubera. *Who art thou?* they asked. An ascetic in deer-skin, but armed? {#p2 3.152}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima,draupadi light=bhima}

Bhima told them who he was and why he had come: Draupadi wanted the flowers. This was Kubera's lake, they said. Ask the lord of the Yakshas first, or you will not so much as look at a lotus. {#p3 3.153}

> Ye Rakshasas, I do not see the lord of wealth here. And even if I did see that mighty king, I would not beseech him. Kshatriyas never beseech (any body). {#q1 3.153}
> — Bhima

And besides, he said, the lake was not dug in Kubera's palace; it came from the mountain's own springs, and so it belonged to all creatures alike. {#p4 3.153}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima light=bhima}

He waded in. They came at him from every side shouting *Seize him! Bind him! We shall cook Bhimasena, and eat him up!* Bhima took up his gold-plated mace and said, *Stay!* He killed more than a hundred of them on the shore, and the rest fled through the air to Kailasa. Then he went back into the lake and gathered the flowers. {#p5 3.153}

The guards ran to Kubera. The lord of wealth only smiled. *Let Bhima take for Krishna as many lotuses as he likes. This is already known to me.* {#p6 3.153}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,draupadi,ghatotkacha,bhima light=yudhishthira}

At the hermitage a sharp wind rose, meteors fell, the sun went dim and the earth shook. *Who is it that will overcome us?* said [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), and looked round, and Bhima was gone. [Draupadi](draupadi) told him about the flower. {#p7 3.154}

They followed, carried by [Ghatotkacha](ghatotkacha) and his Rakshasas, and found Bhima on the shore of the lake among the bodies of the guards, mace raised in both hands like Yama at the end of the world, biting his lip. Yudhishthira embraced him again and again. {#p8 3.154}

> O Kaunteya, what hast thou done? Good betide thee! If thou wishest to do good unto me, thou shouldst never again commit such a rash act, nor offend the gods. {#q2 3.154}
> — Yudhishthira

They took the flowers, and the garden's huge guardians came with rocks in their hands and bowed to Yudhishthira, who made peace with them. With Kubera's knowledge, the Pandavas stayed by the lake a while. {#p9 3.154}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira,dhaumya light=yudhishthira}

They had seen every sacred place now, Yudhishthira said, with Lomasa. How might they reach Kubera's own city? A voice from the sky answered: *Thou wilt not be able to go to that inaccessible spot.* Go back to Badari, then on to the hermitages of Vrishaparva and Arshtishena, and from there you will see it. *This should not be gainsaid*, said [Dhaumya](dhaumya). So they went back to the hermitage of Nara and Narayana. {#p10 3.155}
