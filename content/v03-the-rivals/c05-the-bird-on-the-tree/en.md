::scene{#s1 frame=drona,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,duryodhana light=drona}

When their education was finished, [Drona](drona) wanted to see which of his pupils was the best. He had an artificial bird set up at the top of a tree nearby, and called them all together. {#p1 1.134}

> Take up your bows quickly and stand here aiming at that bird on the tree, with arrows fixed on your bowstrings; shoot and cut off the bird's head, as soon as I give the order. {#q1 1.134}
> — Drona

***

::scene{#s2 frame=drona,yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

He called [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) first. The prince took up his bow and aimed at the bird. *Behold, O prince, that bird on top of the tree,* said Drona. *I do,* said Yudhishthira. {#p2 1.134}

> What dost thou see now, O prince? Seest thou the tree, myself or thy brothers? {#q2 1.134}
> — Drona

*I see the tree, myself, my brothers, and the bird,* said Yudhishthira. Drona asked again, and again, and got the same answer every time. At last he said, vexed: *Stand thou apart. It is not for thee to strike the aim.* {#p3 1.134}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drona,duryodhana,bhima,nakula,sahadeva,karna light=duryodhana,bhima}

He tried [Duryodhana](duryodhana) next, and the other sons of Dhritarashtra one by one; then [Bhima](bhima) and the others, and the princes who had come from other lands. Every one of them gave Yudhishthira's answer: they saw the tree, their teacher, the other pupils, and the bird. Every one of them was told to stand aside. {#p4 1.134}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=drona,arjuna light=arjuna,drona}

When everyone had failed, Drona smiled and called [Arjuna](arjuna). Arjuna stood with his bow bent, aiming. {#p5 1.135}

> Seest thou, O Arjuna, the bird there, the tree, and myself? {#q3 1.135}
> — Drona

*I see the bird only,* Arjuna answered, *not the tree, or thyself.* Drona, well pleased, asked him to describe the bird. {#p6 1.135}

> I see only the head of the vulture, not its body. {#q4 1.135}
> — Arjuna

Drona's hair stood on end with delight. *Shoot,* he said. Arjuna loosed at once, and his sharp arrow struck off the bird's head and brought it to the ground. {#p7 1.135}

Drona held him close. In his mind, the epic says, Drupada and all his friends were already beaten. {#p8 1.135}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=1.135}
**The eye or the head.** In the version most people know today, Arjuna sees only the bird's eye. In Ganguli's text of the epic he sees only its *head*, and that is what he strikes off. The eye belongs to later retellings.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=drona,arjuna light=drona}

Some time later Drona went with all his pupils to bathe in the Ganga. As he waded in, a strong crocodile, as if sent by Death himself, seized him by the thigh. {#p9 1.135}

He could easily have saved himself. Instead he called out, as if in a hurry: *O, kill this monster and rescue me.* The other pupils stood where they were, confounded. Arjuna, before the words were finished, shot five sharp arrows into the water and cut the beast to pieces, and it let go and died. {#p10 1.135}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.135}
**A second test.** The epic says plainly that Drona was *quite capable* of saving himself. It was one more test, and only one pupil passed it.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=drona,arjuna light=arjuna}

Drona now held Arjuna to be the first of all his pupils, and gave him a gift: the weapon called Brahmashira, with the secrets of hurling and recalling it. And with it a warning. {#p11 1.135}

> Thou must not, however, ever use it against any human foe, for if hurled at any foe endued with inferior energy, it might burn the whole universe. {#q5 1.135}
> — Drona

Only against a foe who was not human, he said, might Arjuna use it in battle. Arjuna gave his word and received the weapon with folded hands. {#p12 1.135}

> None else in this world will ever become a superior bowman to thee. Vanquished thou shall never be by any foe, and thy achievements will be great. {#q6 1.135}
> — Drona
