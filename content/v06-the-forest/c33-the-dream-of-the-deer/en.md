When [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) heard of Karna's vow, he was troubled, and thought of Karna's armour. {#p1 3.255}

Then one night in the Dvaita woods the deer came to him in a dream, trembling, with joined hands and voices choked with tears. {#p2 3.256}

> We are, O Bharata, those deer that are still alive after them that had been slaughtered. We shall be exterminated totally. Therefore, do thou change thy residence. {#q1 3.256}
> — the deer

The brothers had thinned the forest, they said; they were all that was left, like seed. Let us increase. Yudhishthira wept for them. *So be it.* {#p3 3.256}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

In the morning he told his brothers. We have fed on them for a year and eight months, he said, and we ought to pity the creatures of the forest. So they went back to the Kamyaka woods, at the edge of the desert near the lake Trinabindu. {#p4 3.256}

They had spent eleven years in the forest now. Yudhishthira lay awake, knowing all their suffering came from his gambling, feeling as if a lance were in his heart, remembering Karna's harsh words. His brothers and Draupadi grieved to look at him, and as the end drew near they grew so lean and hard with anger and hope that their bodies almost changed shape. {#p5 3.257}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=vyasa,yudhishthira light=vyasa}

Then [Vyasa](vyasa) came to see his grandsons, and found them thin. Austerity, he said, is the greatest thing; and charity is the hardest. He told them of the sage Mudgala, who gleaned a drona of grain each fortnight like a pigeon and fed his guests from it, and fed even the irritable Durvasa six times without a frown; and when a messenger of the gods came with a chariot to take him to heaven, Mudgala asked what heaven was like, heard that its joys too would end, and refused to go. {#p6 3.257 3.258 3.259}

> Misery after happiness, and happiness after misery, revolve by turns round a man even like the point of a wheel's circumference round the axle. {#q2 3.259}
> — Vyasa

*After the thirteenth year hath passed away, thou wilt … get back the kingdom.* And Vyasa went back to his hermitage. {#p7 3.259}
