Why would he not take her himself? asked [Virata](virata). {#p1 4.72}

[Arjuna](arjuna) explained. He had lived a whole year in the women's apartments, and the princess had trusted him as a father; she was grown, and people might talk. By taking her as his son's wife he would show that both of them were pure. {#p2 4.72}

> There is no difference between a daughter-in-law and a daughter, as also between a son and son's own-self. {#q1 4.72}
> — Arjuna

And his son was worthy of her: [Abhimanyu](abhimanyu), first in arms, beautiful as a young god, the favourite nephew of Krishna. {#p3 4.72}

> He that hath Arjuna for the father of his son-in-law, hath all his desires gratified. {#q2 4.72}
> — Virata

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,krishna,balarama,drupada,dhrishtadyumna,shikhandi,abhimanyu,subhadra light=krishna}

Yudhishthira agreed, and invitations went out. The thirteenth year was over, and the Pandavas went to live in Upaplavya, one of Virata's towns. Arjuna brought Abhimanyu and Krishna from the Anarta country. The king of Kashi and Shaibya came, each with an akshauhini. [Drupada](drupada) came with another, bringing the sons of Draupadi, [Shikhandi](shikhandi) and [Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna). {#p4 4.72}

Then [Krishna](krishna) came in his garlands, with [Balarama](balarama), [Kritavarma](kritavarma), [Satyaki](satyaki), Akrura, [Samba](samba) and others, bringing Abhimanyu and his mother [Subhadra](subhadra). Indrasena and the Pandavas' servants, who had spent the year at Dwaraka, came with their chariots. Krishna gave each of the Pandavas slave-women, gems and robes. {#p5 4.72}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=abhimanyu,uttara_f,arjuna,yudhishthira,krishna,draupadi,sudeshna,virata light=uttara_f}

Conchs and drums sounded through Virata's palace; bards sang the kings' genealogies. The Matsya ladies, led by [Sudeshna](sudeshna), brought out the princess [Uttara](uttara_f) in every ornament, *resembling the daughter of the great Indra himself*; and among all those women, [Draupadi](draupadi) was the first in beauty and fame. {#p6 4.72}

> And then Dhananjaya, the son of Kunti, accepted Virata's daughter of faultless limbs on behalf of his son by Subhadra. {#q3 4.72}

With Krishna before him, Arjuna had the rites performed for [Abhimanyu](abhimanyu). Virata gave seven thousand horses swift as the wind and two hundred of the best elephants, poured butter on the fire, and offered the Pandavas his kingdom, his army, his treasury and himself. Yudhishthira gave away to the Brahmanas all the wealth Krishna had brought, and cattle, robes, land and gold, and the city of the Matsyas, full of cheerful, well-fed people, shone like a great festival. {#p7 4.72}
