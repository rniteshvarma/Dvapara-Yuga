::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,duryodhana,bhishma light=dhritarashtra}

The news ran through Hastinapura: men, women and children were talking of it in houses and open places. Krishna was coming. [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), his hair standing on end, told [Duryodhana](duryodhana) to honour him: *If worshipped he is sure to bestow happiness; and if not worshipped he is sure to inflict misery.* [Bhishma](bhishma) and the others said *Excellent*, and Duryodhana had pavilions built along the road, full of gems, seats, beautiful girls, scents, robes, food and drink, the finest of all at Vrikasthala. {#p1 5.85}

> Kesava, however, of Dasarha's race, arrived at the capital of the Kurus, without casting a single glance at all those pavilions and all those gems of diverse kinds. {#q1 5.85}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura light=dhritarashtra}

Dhritarashtra told [Vidura](vidura) what he would give Krishna: sixteen golden chariots with four Bahlika horses each, eight elephants always in rut, a hundred golden-skinned maidservants and as many men, eighteen thousand soft woollen blankets from the hills, a thousand deerskins from China, a gem that shone day and night, his own mule-car that ran fourteen yojanas a day. All his sons and grandsons except Duryodhana would go out to meet him, and the girls of the city would go unveiled. Duhshasana's house, finer than Duryodhana's, would be made ready for him. {#p2 5.86}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=vidura,dhritarashtra light=vidura}

Vidura saw through it. Krishna deserved all this and the whole earth besides, he said. {#p3 5.87}

> I truly swear by my own soul that thou dost not wish to give all this unto Krishna either from motives of virtue or for the object of doing what is agreeable to him. {#q2 5.87}
> — Vidura

The Pandavas asked for five villages and the king would not give even those. He was trying to buy Krishna away from Arjuna, and he could not. {#p4 5.87}

> Save only a vessel of water, save only the washing of his feet, save only the (usual) enquiries after the welfare (of those he will see), Janardana will not accept any other hospitality or set his eyes on any other thing. {#q3 5.87}
> — Vidura

Give him what he comes for, said Vidura: peace. *Thou art their father, O king, and the Pandavas are thy sons.* {#p5 5.87}
