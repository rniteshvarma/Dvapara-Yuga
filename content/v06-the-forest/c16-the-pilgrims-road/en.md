::scene{#s1 frame=draupadi,bhima,nakula,sahadeva,yudhishthira light=draupadi}

Without Arjuna, the brothers were like pearls fallen from a broken string. [Draupadi](draupadi) said the forest was no longer beautiful to her; wherever she looked the earth seemed empty. [Bhima](bhima) said that without him even the sky seemed to have no sun. [Nakula](nakula) did not want to stay in Kamyaka any longer. {#p1 3.80}

> Beholding, O king, that Jishnu's bed of grass empty in our asylum, my heart refuses consolation. {#q1 3.80}
> — Sahadeva

***

::scene{#s2 frame=narada,yudhishthira,dhaumya light=narada}

Then [Narada](narada) came, blazing like a fire. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) asked him what merit a man earns by going round the earth to its sacred waters. Narada told him what the sage Pulastya had once told Bhishma: that a pilgrimage to the tirthas, the holy fords and rivers and shrines, is worth more than the greatest sacrifices, and open even to the poor. And he named them one by one, from Pushkara and Kurukshetra to Prayaga, where the Ganga meets the Yamuna. {#p2 3.81 3.82}

[Dhaumya](dhaumya) saw the Pandavas still downcast, and named the sacred places of the four directions himself. {#p3 3.87}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,indra,arjuna,karna light=yudhishthira}

While Dhaumya was speaking, the sage Lomasa arrived from heaven. He had seen Arjuna sitting on Indra's throne, he said. Arjuna had Shiva's weapon now, and the weapons of Yama, Kubera, Varuna and Indra, and had learned the music of the Gandharvas. And Indra had sent a message. {#p4 3.91}

> In battle Karna is not equal unto even a sixteenth part of Pritha's son. {#q2 3.91}
> — Indra, through Lomasa

*And as for the fear of Karna which is in thy heart, O repressor of foes, I shall dispel when Savyasachin will have left heaven.* Let Yudhishthira go to the tirthas with Lomasa, and Lomasa would protect him from the Rakshasas of the mountains. {#p5 3.91 3.92}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.91}
**Indra's promise.** Indra has heard Yudhishthira's sleepless fear of Karna, and promises to remove it himself. He does not say how. Hold on to that.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,vyasa,narada light=yudhishthira}

*Be thou light as regards thy retinue*, Lomasa advised. So Yudhishthira sent back everyone who could not bear hunger, cold and hard roads, and those who lived on sweets and cooked food. Those who had followed him for love he sent to Dhritarashtra, who would pay them, or else to Drupada. Dhritarashtra received them kindly. {#p6 3.92}

Some Brahmanas begged to come anyway; the tirthas were dangerous to reach in small parties. Yudhishthira wept with joy and said yes. Vyasa, Narada and the sage Parvata came to see them off. Purify your hearts before you purify your bodies, they said. {#p7 3.93}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

Before they set out, Yudhishthira asked Lomasa the question that troubled him most. He did not think he was without merit, yet no king had ever suffered as he had. His enemies had no virtue at all. Why did they prosper? {#p8 3.94}

> A man may be seen to prosper by his sins, obtain good therefrom and vanquish his foes. Destruction, however, overtakes him to the roots. {#q3 3.94}
> — Lomasa

So the sons of Pandu set out from place to place, giving away cattle and gold. {#p9 3.95}
