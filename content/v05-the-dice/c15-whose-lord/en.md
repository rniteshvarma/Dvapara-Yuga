::scene{#s1 frame=duryodhana,vidura light=duryodhana,vidura}

*Come, Kshatta*, said [Duryodhana](duryodhana) to [Vidura](vidura). *Bring Draupadi here. Let her sweep the rooms with our serving-women.* {#p1 2.65}

Vidura told him he was hanging over a cliff, a deer provoking tigers, with poisonous snakes coiled over his head. And he gave his ruling. {#p2 2.65}

> In my judgement, slavery does not attach to Krishna, in as much as she was staked by the King after he had lost himself and ceased to be his own master. {#q1 2.65}
> — Vidura

Like the bamboo that fruits only when it is about to die, he said, Duryodhana had won his treasure. Gourds may sink and stones may float, but this foolish king will not listen. {#p3 2.65}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,draupadi light=draupadi}

*Fie on Kshatta!* said Duryodhana, and sent the usher, Pratikamin, instead. The man went to the Pandavas' rooms *like a dog in a lion's den*, and told [Draupadi](draupadi) that Yudhishthira had lost her at dice. {#p4 2.66}

*What prince stakes his wife?* she asked. *Could he find nothing else?* {#p5 2.66}

He had staked everything else first, said the usher: his brothers, then himself, then her. {#p6 2.66}

> O son of the Suta race, go, and ask that gambler present in the assembly, whom he hath lost first, himself, or me. {#q2 2.66}
> — Draupadi

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,duryodhana,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

The usher came back and put her question to [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira): *Whose lord wert thou at the time thou lost me in play?* Yudhishthira sat like a man out of his mind and said nothing at all. {#p7 2.66}

Let her come and ask it herself, said Duryodhana. The usher went back, distressed. *It seemeth that the end of the Kauravas is at hand*, he told her. Draupadi sent him back again: she would do whatever the elders of the assembly, who knew the law, told her was right. But no one in the hall said a word. {#p8 2.66}

Yudhishthira sent her a trusted messenger. She was in her season, wearing a single cloth; he told her to come as she was, weeping, before her father-in-law. {#p9 2.66}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=duhshasana,draupadi,duryodhana light=duhshasana}

The usher was afraid of Draupadi and asked again what he should say. So Duryodhana turned to his brother: *This son of my Suta feareth Vrikodara. Go thou thyself.* {#p10 2.66}

[Duhshasana](duhshasana) went to her rooms, his eyes red. *Come, come, O Krishna, princess of Panchala, thou hast been won by us.* She ran towards the women of Dhritarashtra's household, and he ran after her and seized her by the hair, the long blue hair that had been sprinkled with holy water at the Rajasuya. He dragged her into the hall, trembling like a plantain tree in a storm, while she prayed to Krishna and Vishnu. {#p11 2.66}

*Whether thy season hath come or not*, he said, *whether thou art attired in one piece of cloth or entirely naked*, she was their slave now. {#p12 2.66}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=draupadi,bhishma,drona,vidura,dhritarashtra light=draupadi}

In the hall, with her hair loose and her cloth slipping, Draupadi spoke to the whole assembly. There were men here equal to Indra, she said, men she must respect; she could not stand before them like this. Her husbands would never forgive him, though Indra himself were his ally. {#p13 2.66}

> O fie! Truly hath the virtue of the Bharata gone! {#q3 2.66}
> — Draupadi

Had Drona and Bhishma lost their strength, and Vidura, and the king? Why did they sit and watch? She looked at her husbands, and that look hurt them more than the loss of their kingdom and all their wealth. Duhshasana pulled her harder and laughed, *Slave! Slave!*, and [Karna](karna) and [Shakuni](shakuni) laughed with him. Everyone else in the hall was filled with sorrow. {#p14 2.66}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=bhishma,draupadi,bhima,yudhishthira light=bhishma}

[Bhishma](bhishma) answered her. {#p15 2.66}

> O blessed one, morality is subtle. I therefore am unable to duly decide this point that thou hast put. {#q4 2.66}
> — Bhishma

A man who owns nothing cannot stake what belongs to another, he said; but a wife is always under her husband's command. Yudhishthira would give up the whole world before he gave up dharma, and he himself had said *I am won*. He did not even say that Shakuni had cheated. {#p16 2.66}

Draupadi answered him. Yudhishthira had been summoned, she said, and made to play, a man with no skill against skilled and wicked cheats. {#p17 2.66}

> How can he be said then to have staked voluntarily? {#q5 2.66}
> — Draupadi

Let the Kurus here, who are fathers of sons and fathers-in-law of daughters-in-law, think about it and answer her. {#p18 2.66}

And [Bhima](bhima), looking at her, and then at Yudhishthira, could bear no more. {#p19 2.66}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.65,2.66}
**The question.** Draupadi's question is the hardest legal problem in the epic: once a man has lost himself and become a slave, can he still stake his wife? Vidura says no. Bhishma, the greatest authority in the hall, says he cannot decide. Nobody answers her yet.
::
