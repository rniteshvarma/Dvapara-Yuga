::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

After Draupadi was brought back, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) sat with Markandeya among the grieving sages and asked his question again. Draupadi was born from the sacred fire; she had done nothing wrong; she had served the Brahmanas faithfully; and still she had been carried off by force while her husbands were careless. *Is there any one who is more unfortunate than I am?* {#p1 3.271}

> O bull of the Bharata race, even Rama suffered unparalleled misery, for the evil-minded Ravana, king of the Rakshasas, having recourse to deceit and overpowering the vulture Jatayu, forcibly carried away his wife Sita from his asylum in the woods. {#q1 3.272}
> — Markandeya

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

So Markandeya told him the story of Rama, at length. {#p2 3.272}

How Ravana was born and won his boons; how Rama, prince of Ayodhya, was sent into the forest for fourteen years by his father's promise to Kaikeyi, with Sita and Lakshmana; how Ravana's sister Shurpanakha was disfigured, and Ravana sent the golden deer Maricha, and carried Sita off to Lanka, and killed the vulture Jatayu who tried to stop him; how Rama made friends with Sugriva and Hanuman, built a bridge across the sea, and fought Ravana's armies; how Ravana died, and Sita passed through the fire; and how Rama went home to rule. {#p3 3.273 3.290}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

> It was thus, O mighty-armed one, that Rama of immeasurable energy had suffered of old such excessive calamity in consequence of his exile in the woods! {#q2 3.290}
> — Markandeya

Do not grieve, Markandeya told him. You are a Kshatriya, and you walk the same path as the gods. You have not a particle of sin. {#p4 3.290}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.272,3.290}
**The Ramopakhyana.** Markandeya's telling of the Ramayana fills nineteen sections of the Vana Parva. It differs from Valmiki's in many details, and scholars still argue over which came first. A companion volume will tell it in full; here it matters for why it is told. Rama's wife was carried off in the forest and rescued; so was Draupadi.
::
