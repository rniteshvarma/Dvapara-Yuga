So Dhritarashtra asked [Vidura](vidura), and Vidura told him what the Pandavas and their Brahmanas had told him in the forest. {#p1 3.11}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi,dhaumya light=draupadi}

On the third night after they left Hastinapura, the Pandavas came to the Kamyaka woods just after midnight, the hour when man-eaters hunt and even the cowherds keep away. A Rakshasa stood in their path holding a burning brand: eight teeth jutting out, copper-coloured eyes, his hair standing up and blazing. He roared like a rain-cloud, and birds fell from the trees in fright. {#p2 3.11}

[Draupadi](draupadi) shut her eyes in fear. With her hair still loose from Duhshasana's hand, standing among the five brothers, she looked *like a stream chafing amid five hills*, and the five held on to her. [Dhaumya](dhaumya) chanted mantras and broke the demon's illusions. {#p3 3.11}

*Who art thou?* asked [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira). {#p4 3.11}

> I am the brother of Vaka, the celebrated Kirmira. {#q1 3.11}
> — Kirmira

He lived in these woods, he said, and ate the men he beat in fight. When Yudhishthira gave his name, Kirmira was overjoyed. He had been roaming the earth looking for Bhima, who had killed his brother Vaka in Ekachakra, and his friend Hidimba, and married Hidimba's sister. Now he would drink Bhima's blood and digest him as Agastya digested Vatapi. {#p5 3.11}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,arjuna light=bhima}

Yudhishthira said that could never happen. [Arjuna](arjuna) strung the Gandiva, but [Bhima](bhima) waved him back. He tore up a tree, stripped its leaves, tightened his cloth and went at the Rakshasa. {#p6 3.11}

The tree broke on the demon's head, and Kirmira did not even sway. He threw his burning brand; Bhima kicked it back with his left foot. They fought with trees until the forest around them lay crushed like reeds, then with rocks, then hand to hand like two bulls. Bhima remembered the dice hall, and that Draupadi was watching, and grew stronger. {#p7 3.11}

At last he lifted the Rakshasa, whirled him round like a hurricane shaking a tree, bound him in his arms like a beast with a rope, put a knee in his back, and pressed his neck. {#p8 3.11}

> O sinful wretch, thou wilt no more have to wipe away the tears of Hidimva or Vaka, for thou too art about to go to the mansions of Yama! {#q2 3.11}
> — Bhima

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,draupadi,dhritarashtra,vidura light=yudhishthira}

The brothers praised Bhima, and with Draupadi in front they went on, into a forest now free of its terror. Vidura said he had seen the body himself, lying by the road. {#p9 3.11}

Dhritarashtra heard it all, sighed, and sat lost in thought. {#p10 3.11}
