::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,yudhishthira,virata,drupada,balarama,satyaki light=krishna}

The morning after Abhimanyu's wedding the kings gathered in Virata's council hall: Virata and [Drupada](drupada) in front, [Balarama](balarama) and [Krishna](krishna) with their father, [Satyaki](satyaki), the Pandavas, Pradyumna and Samba, Abhimanyu with Virata's sons, and Draupadi's sons. They talked of many things, then fell silent, looking at Krishna. {#p1 5.1}

Krishna laid it out. Everyone knew how [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) had been cheated at dice by Shakuni, and how he and his brothers had kept the terms, twelve years and the hard thirteenth, unrecognised, in servants' work. {#p2 5.1}

> The virtuous king Yudhishthira would not unrighteously covet even the celestial kingdom. But righteously he would accept the rule even of a single village. {#q1 5.1}
> — Krishna

The Kauravas had tried to kill the Pandavas since they were boys. But no one knew Duryodhana's mind. So let an honest, wary envoy of good birth go and ask them mildly to give Yudhishthira half the kingdom. {#p3 5.1}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=balarama,satyaki light=balarama}

Balarama agreed, and went further. The envoy should salute Bhishma, Dhritarashtra, Drona, Vidura, Kripa, Shakuni and Karna, and speak humbly, for the Kauravas held the kingdom by force. Yudhishthira had lost it himself: he was no dice-player, his friends had begged him not to play, and of all the players there he had chosen to challenge Shakuni. {#p4 5.2}

> For this, no blame can attach to Sakuni. {#q2 5.2}
> — Balarama

Satyaki leapt up. {#p5 5.2}

> Even as a man's heart is, so doth he speak! {#q3 5.3}
> — Satyaki

How could anyone blame Yudhishthira in this assembly? Experts had challenged a man bound by a Kshatriya's code to accept, and won by a trick. And now that the Pandavas had kept every term, were they to beg? The Kauravas even claimed they had been recognised before the year was out. Satyaki would beseech them with sharp arrows. *There is no sin in slaying them that are bent on slaying us: but to be a beggar before foes is both impious and infamous.* {#p6 5.3}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drupada,krishna light=drupada}

Drupada sided with Satyaki. Duryodhana would never give up the kingdom peacefully, and Dhritarashtra would follow his son, Bhishma and Drona out of weakness, Karna and Shakuni out of folly. Mild words would only make him think he had won. Send to every king now, he said, for the right-minded side with whoever asks first: to Shalya, Bhagadatta, the Kekayas, the Kambojas, and a long list more. And let his own priest go to Dhritarashtra. {#p7 5.4}

Krishna agreed it was the politic course. But the Yadavas were related equally to both sides, and had come only for a wedding. Let Drupada, eldest and most learned, send the message. {#p8 5.5}

> If on the other hand, the son of Dhritarashtra should wax haughty and from folly refuse to make peace, then, having summoned others, summon us too. {#q4 5.5}
> — Krishna

So Krishna went home to Dwaraka, and both sides sent word to every king of the earth, and *the goddess Earth with her mountains and forests seemed to tremble beneath their tread*. {#p9 5.5}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=drupada light=drupada}

Drupada sent his priest, old in years and wisdom, with instructions. Speak righteously to Dhritarashtra, he said, and you will win the hearts of his fighting men; Vidura will take up your words and turn Bhishma, Drona and Kripa. While the Kauravas argue among themselves, the Pandavas will gather their army. The priest set out for Hastinapura with his disciples, under the star Pushya. {#p10 5.6}
