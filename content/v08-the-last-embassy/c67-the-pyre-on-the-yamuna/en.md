::scene{#s1 frame=parashurama,amba light=amba}

[Parashurama](parashurama) called [Amba](amba) before them all and told her sorrowfully that he had done everything he could, and could not get the better of Bhishma. Let her seek Bhishma's own protection; she had no other refuge. {#p1 5.189}

> As regards myself, I will not go a second time to Bhishma. {#q1 5.189}
> — Amba

She would go, she said, wherever she might find the means to kill Bhishma in battle herself. Her eyes were wild with anger as she went. {#p2 5.189}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,narada,vyasa light=bhishma}

Bhishma went home and told Satyavati, and set spies to bring him word of the girl every day. When he heard that she had gone into the forest to do penance, he grew melancholy. [Narada](narada) and [Vyasa](vyasa) told him not to grieve: who can thwart destiny by effort? {#p3 5.189}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=amba,ganga light=amba}

For six months she lived on air, unmoving as a post. For a year she stood in the Yamuna without food. For another year she stood on her toes eating one fallen leaf. Twelve years she heated the heavens with her austerities, and her relatives could not turn her. She bathed at holy place after holy place, Prayaga, the hermitages of Vishvamitra and Mandavya, Narada and Chyavana. {#p4 5.189}

One day [Ganga](ganga) rose from the water and asked her why she tormented herself. To destroy Bhishma, she said. Ganga warned her: if she died under that vow, she would be reborn a crooked river, dry eight months of the year, full of crocodiles. And so it happened: by the power of her austerities, half of her became such a river in Vatsabhumi, and the other half remained a maiden. {#p5 5.189}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=amba,shiva light=shiva}

The ascetics of Vatsabhumi asked her what she wanted. Not heaven, she said. Bhishma had robbed her of the life a husband would have given her. {#p6 5.190}

> he for whom I have become neither woman nor man, without slaying in battle that son of Ganga I will not desist, ye that are endued with wealth of asceticism. {#q2 5.190}
> — Amba

She no longer had any desire as a woman, she said; she would win manhood to be revenged. Then [Shiva](shiva), bearing the trident, appeared before her, and she asked for Bhishma's defeat. *You shall kill him*, the god said. But how, she asked, could a woman win in battle? {#p7 5.190}

> Thou shalt slay Bhishma, and even obtain manhood. {#q3 5.190}
> — Shiva

She would remember everything in her new body, he said; she would be born in Drupada's line, a great chariot-warrior, and become a man some time after her birth. Then he vanished. {#p8 5.190}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=amba light=amba}

Amba gathered wood from the forest before the eyes of the sages, built a great pyre on the bank of the Yamuna, set it alight herself, and entered the fire with her heart burning with anger, saying it was *for Bhishma's destruction*. {#p9 5.190}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.189}
Read literally, the text says two things: that half of Amba became a river while she still lived, and that she then died on the pyre to be reborn. The epic does not explain how the two fit together, and we leave them as it gives them.
::
