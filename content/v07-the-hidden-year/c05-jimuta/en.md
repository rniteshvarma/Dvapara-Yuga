::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

So they lived, each looking after the others in secret. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) played dice with Virata and his courtiers, made them *sit together in the dice-hall like a row of birds bound in a string*, and quietly shared what he won among his brothers. [Bhima](bhima) sold him meat and dishes from the king's kitchen. [Arjuna](arjuna) shared what he earned from selling the worn-out clothes of the women's apartments; [Sahadeva](sahadeva) brought milk, curds and ghee; [Nakula](nakula) shared what the king paid him for the horses. And [Draupadi](draupadi), in her own hard state, looked after all of them. {#p1 4.13}

> And thus ministering unto one another's wants, those mighty warriors lived in the capital of Virata as hidden from view, as if they were once more in their mother's womb. {#q1 4.13}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima,virata light=bhima}

In the fourth month came the great festival of Brahma, and wrestlers came to it from every quarter by the thousand, huge men with lions' shoulders and waists, who had won in the lists before many kings. One of them, Jimuta, towered over the rest and challenged them all, and no one would go near him. So [Virata](virata) made him fight his cook. {#p2 4.13}

Bhima did not want to, but could not openly refuse the king. He bowed to Virata, walked into the arena *with the careless steps of a tiger*, and girded himself. {#p3 4.13}

They met like two huge elephants. They struck with fists and flat hands, threw each other far, pinned each other to the ground, caught each other by the legs and whirled each other down, kicked, drove knee and head together *producing the crash of one stone against another*, all without weapons, to the roaring delight of the crowd. At last Bhima, shouting, seized Jimuta by the arms the way a lion seizes an elephant, and lifted him from the ground. {#p4 4.13}

> And having whirled him round and round a hundred times till he was insensible, the strong-armed Vrikodara dashed him to death on the ground. {#q2 4.13}

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,virata,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=draupadi}

Virata rewarded Vallava on the spot, *with the liberality of Kuvera*. After that Bhima killed many wrestlers and strong men, and when no man would face him, the king set him against tigers, lions and elephants, and even made him fight furious lions in the women's apartments for the ladies to watch. Arjuna pleased the women with song and dance; Nakula brought the king swift horses that followed him wherever he went; Sahadeva showed him herds of well-trained bullocks; and the king rewarded them all. {#p5 4.13}

And Draupadi, seeing those warriors suffer, sighed and sighed. {#p6 4.13}
