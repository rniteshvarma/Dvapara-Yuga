When Kripa had been carried off, [Drona](drona) came on behind his red horses with an arrow already on the string. [Arjuna](arjuna) told [Uttara](uttar) to take him to the chariot with the golden altar on its banner: there was the teacher, wise as Ushanas, learned in the four Vedas, master of every celestial weapon and of how to call it back; forgiving, self-controlled, truthful, harmless. *I desire to fight with that highly-blessed one on the field.* {#p1 4.58}

The red horses and the white ones met, and the army trembled. Arjuna, glad to have reached him, saluted his teacher. {#p2 4.58}

> Having completed our exile in the woods, we are now desirous of avenging our wrongs. Even invincible in battle, it doth not behove thee to be angry with us. O sinless one, I will not strike thee unless thou strikest me first. {#q1 4.58}
> — Arjuna

***

::scene{#s2 frame=drona,arjuna light=arjuna}

Drona shot more than twenty arrows; Arjuna cut them all in the air. Drona covered his chariot with a thousand; Arjuna, *desirous of angering* him, covered the teacher's horses. Then they fought in earnest, arrows touching wing to wing across the sky like rows of cranes, until the sun was hidden. The soldiers watched in wonder. {#p3 4.58}

> Indeed, who else save Falguna, is worthy of fighting with Drona in battle? Surely the duties of a Kshatriya are stern, for Arjuna fighteth with even his own preceptor! {#q2 4.58}
> — the soldiers

Drona sent the weapons of Indra, of the Wind and of Fire; Arjuna met each with its own. And seeing his pupil set on striking him, the teacher, out of affection, *began to fight with him playfully by means of smooth and straight arrows*. A voice in the sky praised Drona for daring to fight such a man. {#p4 4.58}

Arjuna drew the Gandiva now with one hand, now with the other, so fast that no one could see the gap between taking an arrow and loosing it, and hundreds and thousands of arrows fell on Drona's chariot until it disappeared. The Kurus cried out, and Indra and the Gandharvas and Apsaras in the sky applauded. {#p5 4.58}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=ashwatthama,drona,arjuna light=ashwatthama}

Then [Ashwatthama](ashwatthama) came at Arjuna with a mass of chariots, angry, though in his heart he admired what he had seen. Arjuna turned his horses towards him, and so gave Drona room to leave the field. The teacher went, wounded, his mail and banner gone, behind his swift horses. {#p6 4.58}
