The queen told the truth: childless, and afraid of her co-wives, she had called her daughter a son, and Drupada had gone along for love of her, and she had let the marriage happen remembering Shiva's word. Drupada fortified his city and prayed to the gods. Pray, his queen said, but act too. {#p1 5.194}

> reliance on the gods, when supported by human exertion, always, O king, leadeth to success. {#q1 5.194}
> — Drupada's queen

***

::scene{#s2 frame=shikhandi light=shikhandi}

Their daughter heard them and was filled with shame. {#p2 5.194}

> It is for me that these two are plunged into grief! {#q2 5.194}
> — Shikhandini

She resolved to die, and went alone into a deep forest that no one entered for fear of a Yaksha named Sthunakarna. In it stood a walled mansion fragrant with the smoke of fried paddy. She went in and stopped eating. The Yaksha, who was kind, appeared to her and asked what she wanted; he served the lord of treasures, he said, and could grant even what cannot be given. Make me a man, she said, until the Dasharna king leaves my father's city. {#p3 5.194}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=shikhandi light=shikhandi}

*It was ordained*, the Yaksha said, *and ordained for my grief*. He would lend her his manhood for a time and bear her womanhood; she must promise to come back. She promised, and they exchanged their sexes. Shikhandi went home a man. Drupada sent word: *my child is male; believe it*. Hiranyavarman sent young women to find out, and they reported, to his joy, that Shikhandi was a man. He stayed a few days happily with Drupada, gave Shikhandi elephants, horses and cattle, scolded his daughter, and went home. {#p4 5.195}

Meanwhile Kubera, travelling over the earth, passed Sthuna's mansion and wondered why its master did not come out to greet him. His Yakshas told him: Sthuna had taken a woman's form and was hiding in shame. Kubera had him brought out, and cursed him. {#p5 5.195}

> therefore from this day, thou shalt remain a woman and she shall remain a man! {#q3 5.195}
> — Kubera

The Yakshas begged him to set a limit, and he did. {#p6 5.195}

> After Sikhandin's death, ye Yakshas, this one will regain his own form! {#q4 5.195}
> — Kubera

When the time came, Shikhandi went back to the forest as promised, without guile. Sthuna was glad of him and told him what had happened: go and live happily among men. Shikhandi went home rejoicing, worshipped Brahmanas, gods, great trees and crossroads, and was given as a pupil to Drona, with Dhrishtadyumna. {#p7 5.195}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,duryodhana,shikhandi,amba light=bhishma}

So, said Bhishma, Shikhandi was born a woman and became a man; and it was Amba, the eldest daughter of the king of Kashi, who was born in Drupada's line as Shikhandi. If he came at Bhishma bow in hand, Bhishma would not look at him even for a moment, nor strike him. His vow was known to the whole world: he would not {#p8 5.195}

> shoot weapons upon a woman, or one that was a woman before or one bearing a feminine name, or one whose form resembleth a woman's. {#q5 5.195}
> — Bhishma

Duryodhana thought for a moment, and decided that this was proper conduct for Bhishma. {#p9 5.195}
