The gods and Rishis chose the king [Nahusha](nahusha), handsome, powerful and virtuous. He said he was too weak; Indra had always been the strong one. So they gave him a boon: whatever being he looked upon, god or Asura, Yaksha or Rishi, he would absorb its power. {#p1 5.11}

He was crowned, and at first ruled well. But with heaven and that boon, he turned to pleasure: Apsaras and celestial women, the Nandana groves, Kailasa, the Himalaya, songs and music. And one day he saw Shachi, Indra's queen. *Let Sachi make haste and visit me at my house.* {#p2 5.11}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=nahusha light=nahusha}

Shachi ran to Brihaspati, the gods' preceptor, who had always told her she would never be a widow. He promised her she would see Indra again. Nahusha raged, and the gods begged him to turn from another man's wife. {#p3 5.11}

> Ahalya of spotless fame, the wife of a saint, was outraged by Indra while her husband was alive. Why did ye not prevent him? {#q1 5.12}
> — Nahusha

The gods gave in, and went to Brihaspati to ask for Shachi. He would not give up a woman who had come to him for shelter. {#p4 5.12}

> He that delivereth up to a foe of a person terrified and asking for protection obtaineth no protection when he himself is in need of it. {#q2 5.12}
> — Brihaspati

Then let her ask Nahusha for time, he said. *Time will send time onward.* So Shachi went to Nahusha, trembling *like a plantain-stalk at a breezy spot*, and asked for time to learn what had become of Indra. If she found no news, she would come to him. Nahusha agreed. {#p5 5.13}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=indra,vishnu light=indra}

The gods went to [Vishnu](vishnu). Let [Indra](indra) offer him a horse sacrifice, Vishnu said, and he would be purified; Nahusha would fall by his own deeds. So the horse sacrifice was performed, and Indra divided his sin among the trees, the rivers, the mountains, the earth and women, and was free. But when he looked at Nahusha, before whom every creature cowered, he trembled, and vanished again. {#p6 5.13}

Shachi grieved. She worshipped the goddess Night and called on the goddess of Divination. *Let truth be verified by truth.* {#p7 5.13}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=indra light=indra}

Divination came and led her: through the heavenly groves, over the Himalaya to its far side, across a sea to an island, to a lake eight hundred miles wide, full of five-coloured lotuses. In the middle stood a great white lotus on a tall stalk. {#p8 5.14}

> And penetrating into the lotus-stalk, along with Sachi, she saw Indra there who had entered into its fibres. {#q3 5.14}

Shachi made herself small, as he was, and praised his deeds of old, and told him about Nahusha. *O thou of powerful arms, slay the terrible Nahusha of vicious soul.* {#p9 5.14}
