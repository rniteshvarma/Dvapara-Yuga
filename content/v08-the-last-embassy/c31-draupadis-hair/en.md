[Draupadi](draupadi) had heard Yudhishthira's peaceful words, and Bhima speaking for peace. Weeping, she turned to [Krishna](krishna). The five villages had been refused. Why go to make a peace that did not give back the kingdom? Those who cannot be won by conciliation or gifts should be treated with severity. {#p1 5.82}

> Persons conversant with the scriptures declare, O Janardana, that sin is incurred in slaying one that deserveth not to be slain. So there is equal sin in not slaying one that deserveth to be slain. {#q1 5.82}
> — Draupadi

> Whatever woman, O Kesava, is there on earth like me? I am the daughter of king Drupada, risen from the sacrificial altar. I am the sister of Dhrishtadyumna, thy dear friend, O Krishna. {#q2 5.82}
> — Draupadi

The daughter-in-law of Pandu, the queen of five men like Indras, the mother of five sons, and she had been dragged by the hair into the hall and treated as a slave, in Krishna's lifetime. *In my heart I called upon thee. O Govinda, saying,—Save me, O save me!* {#p2 5.82}

> Fie to Partha's bowmanship, oh, fie to Bhimasena's might since Duryodhana, O Krishna, liveth for even a moment. {#q3 5.82}
> — Draupadi

***

::scene{#s2 frame=draupadi,krishna,duhshasana light=draupadi}

Then she took her hair in her left hand, deep blue, perfumed, soft and glossy *like a mighty snake*, and came to him. {#p3 5.82}

> Lotus-eyed one that art anxious for peace with the enemy, thou shouldst, in all thy acts, call to thy mind these tresses of mine seized by Dussasana's rude hands! {#q4 5.82}
> — Draupadi

If Bhima and Arjuna wanted peace, her old father and her brothers would avenge her, and her five sons with Abhimanyu. {#p4 5.82}

> What peace can this heart of mine know unless I behold Dussasana's dark arm severed from his trunk and pulverised to atoms? {#q5 5.82}
> — Draupadi

Thirteen years she had hidden her anger *like a smouldering fire*, and now Bhima's words were breaking her heart. She wept aloud, her tears *hot as liquid fire*. {#p5 5.82}

> Soon wilt thou, O Krishna, behold the ladies of Bharata's race weep as thou dost. {#q6 5.82}
> — Krishna

If the Kauravas would not listen, they would lie on the earth as food for dogs and jackals. {#p6 5.82}

> The mountains of Himavat might shift their site, the Earth herself might split into a hundred fragments, the firmament itself with its myriads of stars might fall down, still my words can never be futile. {#q7 5.82}
> — Krishna
