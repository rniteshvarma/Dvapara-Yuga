::scene{#s1 frame=arjuna,yudhishthira light=arjuna}

[Arjuna](arjuna) had his bow, his quivers, his chariot and his hall now. What they needed next, he told [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), was a treasury. He wanted to make the kings of the earth pay tribute, and he would go north, to the quarter ruled by Kubera, the lord of wealth. {#p1 2.25}

So the four brothers set out, each at the head of an army: Arjuna to the north, Bhima to the east, Sahadeva to the south, Nakula to the west. Yudhishthira stayed at Indraprastha among his friends. {#p2 2.25}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,bhagadatta light=arjuna,bhagadatta}

In the north Arjuna met [Bhagadatta](bhagadatta), king of Pragjyotisha, his father's old friend, and fought him. Bhagadatta yielded gracefully. {#p3 2.26}

> O thou who hast Kunti for thy mother, as thou art to me, so is Yudhishthira also. I shall do all this. Tell me, what else I may do for thee. {#q1 2.26}
> — Bhagadatta

Arjuna went on through the hills and mountain kingdoms, through Kashmira and the Trigartas, across the White Mountains, to Lake Manasa and the lands of the Gandharvas, taking tribute everywhere. {#p4 2.27 2.28}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

At last he came to the edge of the land of Harivarsha, beyond which lay the country of the Northern Kurus, and huge guards came out to meet him. {#p5 2.28}

> O son of Pritha, this country can be never conquered by thee. If thou seekest thy good, return hence. He that entereth this region, if human, is sure to perish. {#q2 2.28}
> — the guards of the north

Nothing there could be seen with human eyes, they said, and there could be no war in it. Arjuna smiled. He only wanted Yudhishthira to be emperor, he said; if the land was closed to men, he would not enter it. They gave him celestial cloths, silks, ornaments and skins as tribute, and he turned home. {#p6 2.28}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhima,shishupala,sahadeva_m,karna light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) went east. He came to Chedi, and [Shishupala](shishupala) came out of his city to receive him with honour, offered him his kingdom, and smiling asked what he wanted. Bhima stayed thirty nights as his guest. {#p7 2.29}

He brought Jarasandha's son under the empire by conciliation, and then met [Karna](karna) and subdued him. He went on through Videha and Vanga, to the sea, taking tribute of gems, sandal, cloth and gold. {#p8 2.30}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.29,2.37}
**Shishupala, host.** Remember how warmly the king of Chedi received Bhima. He will not be so friendly at the Rajasuya.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=sahadeva,i7673_nila,i11834_vibhishana light=sahadeva}

[Sahadeva](sahadeva) went south. At Mahishmati he fought King [Nila](i7673_nila), and his army was nearly burned: the fire-god Agni himself fought for Nila, who had given his daughter to the god, and Agni protected the city. {#p9 2.31}

Sahadeva sat down before the fire, purified himself, and praised Agni. Pleased, the god held back, and Nila paid tribute. Then Sahadeva went on to the far south, to the caves of Kishkindha where he fought the monkey-kings Mainda and Dvivida for seven days, and to the sea-shore. From there he sent messengers across the water to [Vibhishana](i11834_vibhishana), king of the Rakshasas in Lanka, who accepted the empire's sway, *regarding it all as the act of Time*, and sent jewels, sandalwood and pearls. {#p10 2.31}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=nakula,shalya,krishna light=nakula}

[Nakula](nakula) went west, through the deserts and the lands of the five rivers, and sent messengers to Krishna, who accepted. In Shakala, city of the Madras, his uncle [Shalya](shalya) received him with affection and paid tribute. Nakula came home with so much treasure that ten thousand camels could hardly carry it. {#p11 2.32}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

Under Yudhishthira the kingdom flourished. There were no droughts or floods, and the rains came as people wished. Even thieves, the epic says, did not lie to one another. His treasury was so full it could not be emptied in a hundred years. And he decided the time had come for the Rajasuya. {#p12 2.33}
