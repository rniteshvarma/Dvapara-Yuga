::scene{#s1 frame=arjuna,uttar light=arjuna}

[Arjuna](arjuna) walked round the Shami tree, took down Uttara's lion banner and left it at the tree's foot. Then he thought of Agni's gift, and his own golden banner fell from the sky onto the chariot: the ape with a lion's tail, an illusion made by Vishvakarman, with its unearthly creatures sitting on it. He walked round it in reverence, put on his lizard-skin finger-guards, took up his bow and set out north, with [Uttara](uttar) driving. {#p1 4.46}

He blew his great conch. The horses dropped to their knees, and Uttara sat down in the chariot in fright. Arjuna took the reins himself, raised the horses, and held the prince. {#p2 4.46}

> Heard have I the blare of many a conch and many a trumpet and the roar of many an elephant stationed in the battle-array, but never have I heard before the blare of such conch. {#q1 4.46}
> — Uttara

> Firmly stand thou on the car, pressing thy feet on it, and tightly catch hold of the bridles, for I will blow the conch again. {#q2 4.46}
> — Arjuna

He blew it again, so that it seemed to split the hills, and Uttara sank down once more, clinging to the chariot. {#p3 4.46}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=drona,duryodhana light=drona}

On the Kuru side, [Drona](drona) knew the sound. The fires would not blaze though fed, the animals howled towards the sun, crows sat on the banners, a jackal ran wailing through the ranks unhurt. *All this is ominous.* Send the cattle ahead, he said, and stand ready in battle order. {#p4 4.46}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana,karna,drona,bhishma,kripa light=duryodhana}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) would not be shaken. The Pandavas had promised a thirteenth year unknown, and that year was not over; if this was Arjuna, they must go back to the forest for twelve more years. Let [Bhishma](bhishma) count the time. They had come for the Matsyas' cattle on behalf of the Trigartas; whoever came, Virata or Arjuna, they must fight. *At present there is nothing better than fighting.* {#p5 4.47}

[Karna](karna) went further. Ignore the teacher, he said: Drona loved Arjuna and praised him at the sound of a horse neighing. {#p6 4.47}

> Horses always neigh, whether walking or standing, the winds blow at all times; and Indra also always showereth rain. {#q3 4.47}
> — Karna

Learned men belonged in palaces and assemblies and sacrifices, not on battlefields. Put the cattle in safety, he said, draw up the army, and post the guards. {#p7 4.47}
