How had Indra and his queen come to suffer? asked [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira). [Shalya](shalya) told him an old story. {#p1 5.9}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.9}
Shalya's story of Indra runs through ten sections of Ganguli (5.9–18). It is told here in four short chapters; a fuller telling belongs in a companion volume of tales.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=indra light=indra}

Tvashtri, lord of creatures, out of hatred for [Indra](indra), made a son with three heads, like the sun, the moon and fire. With one mouth he read the Vedas, with one he drank wine, and with the third he looked about *as if he would absorb all the cardinal points*. He was mild, self-controlled and given to fierce austerities, and Indra feared he would take his place. {#p2 5.9}

So Indra sent the Apsaras to tempt him. They tried every gesture of love; he looked at them and was unmoved, *like the ocean, full to the brim*. They came back with joined hands. *O, that unapproachable being is incapable of being disturbed by us.* {#p3 5.9}

> Even a strong person should not overlook a rising foe, contemptible though he may be. {#q1 5.9}
> — Indra

He hurled his thunderbolt, and the three-headed one fell like a hilltop. But even dead he blazed, and his heads seemed alive, and Indra found no peace. {#p4 5.9}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=indra light=indra}

A carpenter happened by with an axe on his shoulder, and Indra ordered him to cut off the heads. The carpenter would not: the shoulders were too broad for his axe, and it was a deed the righteous condemned. Who was this, anyway? {#p5 5.9}

> O Indra, how is it that thou art not ashamed of this thy inhuman act? How it is that thou hast no dread of the sin of slaying a Brahmana, after having slain this son of a saint? {#q2 5.9}
> — the carpenter

Indra would purify himself later, he said; he was afraid of this enemy even now. And he gave the carpenter a gift: in sacrifices, men would give him the head of the victim as his share. So the carpenter cut off the three heads, and from the mouth that read the Vedas and drank Soma flew partridges; from the mouth that looked about, quails; and from the mouth that drank wine, sparrows and hawks. {#p6 5.9}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=indra light=indra}

When Tvashtri heard, his eyes went red. Indra had killed his son, who had done no wrong. He washed his mouth, made offerings to the fire, and created Vritra. {#p7 5.9}

> 'Risen like the doomsday sun, what am I to do?' 'Kill Indra,' was the reply. {#q3 5.9}

Vritra seized Indra and swallowed him. The gods made Yawning, and as Vritra yawned, Indra shrank himself and slipped out of his open mouth; *and thenceforth the yawn attaches itself to the living breath of animated beings in three worlds*. They fought again, a long while, and Vritra, filled with Tvashtri's power, drove Indra back. The gods gathered on the top of Mandara, and thought of Vishnu. {#p8 5.9}
