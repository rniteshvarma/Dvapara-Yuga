::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,draupadi light=dhritarashtra,draupadi}

Then [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), hoping to save his family, turned to [Draupadi](draupadi) and spoke kindly. {#p1 2.70}

> Ask of me any boon, O princess of Panchala, that thou desirest. {#q1 2.70}
> — Dhritarashtra

She was the first of all his daughters-in-law, he said. {#p2 2.70}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=draupadi,yudhishthira,prativindhya light=draupadi}

She asked for [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira)'s freedom. {#p3 2.70}

> Let not unthinking children call my child Prativindhya endued with great energy of mind as the son of a slave. {#q2 2.70}
> — Draupadi

*Let it be so*, said the king. *Ask a second; you deserve more than one.* So she asked that Bhima, Arjuna and the twins be freed too, *with their cars and bows*. *Let it be so. Ask a third.* {#p4 2.70}

> O best of kings, O illustrious one, covetousness always bringeth about loss of virtue. I do not deserve a third boon. {#q3 2.70}
> — Draupadi

A Vaishya may ask one boon, she said, a Kshatriya woman two, a Kshatriya man three. Her husbands, once free, would win back their fortune by their own deeds. {#p5 2.70}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=karna,bhima,arjuna,yudhishthira light=karna}

[Karna](karna) said what everyone in the hall was thinking. {#p6 2.71}

> The princess of Panchala, becoming as a boat unto the sons of Pandu who were sinking in a boatless ocean of distress, hath brought them in safety to the shore. {#q4 2.71}
> — Karna

To be saved by their wife: [Bhima](bhima) could not bear it. A man's offspring, deeds and learning are the three lights that live on after him, he said to Arjuna, and theirs had been dimmed by this insult to their wife. {#p7 2.71}

[Arjuna](arjuna) said that great men do not dwell on the words of lesser ones. But Bhima turned to Yudhishthira: should he kill them all now, here, or outside the palace? He glared around like a lion among small animals, and smoke and sparks seemed to come from his ears. Yudhishthira put his arms around him. *Be not so. Stay in silence and peace.* {#p8 2.71}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,dhritarashtra,gandhari light=yudhishthira}

Then Yudhishthira went to his uncle with joined hands. *Thou art our master. Command us.* {#p9 2.72}

Dhritarashtra gave them back everything. Go home, he said, and rule your kingdom with your wealth. And forget what was said here. {#p10 2.72}

> The axe falleth upon wood, not upon stone. {#q5 2.72}
> — Dhritarashtra

He meant that Yudhishthira would listen to advice and Duryodhana would not. The good remember only the good deeds of their enemies. Remember Gandhari, he said, and remember me, *old and blind*. He had let the match go on, he claimed, only to see the strength and weakness of his sons. {#p11 2.72}

So the Pandavas went through every courtesy and set out for Indraprastha with Draupadi, in chariots the colour of clouds, with cheerful hearts. {#p12 2.72}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.72,2.80}
**Two versions from one man.** Here Dhritarashtra says he allowed the game *from motives of policy*. Later, alone with Sanjaya, he tells it differently: he offered the boons on Vidura's advice, after the omens, and ignored the rest of that advice out of love for his son.
::
