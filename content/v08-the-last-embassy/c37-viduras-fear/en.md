That night, after Krishna had eaten, [Vidura](vidura) told him he should not have come. Duryodhana was wicked, wrathful, ignorant, ungrateful; he trusted in Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna, Ashwatthama and Jayadratha, and believed Karna alone could beat the Pandavas. He would never make peace. {#p1 5.92}

> Where, O slayer of Madhu, words, good or bad, are of the same effect, no wise man would spend his breath for nothing, like a singer before the deaf. {#q1 5.92}
> — Vidura

And the kings gathered in that hall were enemies Krishna had once stripped of their possessions; they had joined Duryodhana out of fear of him. How could he go alone into their midst? {#p2 5.92}

> O Madhava, the love I bear to thee is equal to that I bear to the sons of Pandu. {#q2 5.92}
> — Vidura

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,vidura light=krishna}

[Krishna](krishna) said Vidura had spoken like a father and mother to him. But he had come knowing all of it. {#p3 5.93}

> If a man striving to the best of his abilities to perform a virtuous act meets with failure, I have not the least doubt that the merit of that act becomes his, notwithstanding such failure. {#q3 5.93}
> — Krishna

A friend should try to stop a friend's wrong, *even to the extent of seizing him by the hair*. And he had come so that no one could say afterwards that Krishna, though able, had not tried. {#p4 5.93}

> If, on the other hand, they seek to injure me, I tell thee that all the kings of the earth; united together, are no match for me, like a herd of deer incapable of standing before an enraged lion. {#q4 5.93}
> — Krishna

Having said this, he lay down on his soft bed and slept. {#p5 5.93}
