::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

On Kubera's mountain there was no difference between night and day, for the place shone by its own light. The Pandavas watched the sun rise and set, recited the Vedas, kept their vows, and waited. Every day and night without Arjuna seemed to them like a year. {#p1 3.163}

Arjuna, meanwhile, had spent five years in Indra's heaven and learned the weapons of Agni, Varuna, Soma, Vayu, Vishnu, Indra, Shiva, Brahma, Yama and Kubera. Then he bowed to his father, walked around him, and came down to the Gandhamadana. {#p2 3.163}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,dhaumya,yudhishthira,draupadi light=arjuna}

One day, as they were thinking of him, Indra's chariot came down through the clouds like a meteor, Matali at the reins, and in it sat [Arjuna](arjuna) in garlands and new ornaments. {#p3 3.164}

He bowed first at [Dhaumya](dhaumya)'s feet, then at Yudhishthira's and Bhima's, and the twins bowed to him. Then he went to [Draupadi](draupadi) and comforted her, and stood humbly before his eldest brother. {#p4 3.164}

They walked around the chariot in which Indra had beaten the demons, and worshipped Matali as if he were Indra himself, and Matali went home. Arjuna gave Draupadi the jewels Indra had given him. And that night he slept beside the sons of Madri. {#p5 3.164}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=indra,yudhishthira,arjuna light=indra}

In the morning there was music in the sky and the rattle of wheels, and [Indra](indra) himself came down in a chariot with his Gandharvas and Apsaras. Yudhishthira worshipped him, and Arjuna stood before his father like a servant. {#p6 3.165}

> Thou shalt rule the earth, O Pandava, Blessed be thou! Do thou, O Kunti's son, again repair unto Kamyaka. {#q1 3.165}
> — Indra

And he went back to heaven. {#p7 3.165}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna,shiva light=yudhishthira}

Yudhishthira smelt Arjuna's head, and his voice shook. How had he lived in heaven? How had he pleased Shiva and Indra? Tell me everything. {#p8 3.166}

So Arjuna told it: the ascetic at Indrakila, the boar and the hunter, the garland on the stranger's head, Shiva's embrace, the guardians of the world on the peaks, the chariot and the city of the gods. And then he told what Indra had asked of him at the end. {#p9 3.166 3.167}

> My enemies, those Danavas, named, Nivata-Kavachas dwell in the womb of the ocean. And they number thirty million and are notorious, and all of equal forms and strength and splendour. Do thou slay them there, O Kunti's son; and that will be thy preceptor's fee. {#q2 3.167}
> — Indra

Indra gave him Matali's chariot, a diadem for his head, armour no weapon could pierce, and a new string for the Gandiva. And the gods gave him a conch born in the deep sea, called Devadatta. {#p10 3.167}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.167}
**Devadatta and the diadem.** This is where Arjuna gets two things he keeps to the end of the war: the conch Devadatta, "god-given", which he blows at Kurukshetra, and the diadem that gives him the name Kiritin, "the crowned one".
::
