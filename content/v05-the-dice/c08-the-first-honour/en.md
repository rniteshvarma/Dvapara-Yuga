::scene{#s1 frame=narada,krishna light=narada}

On the last day of the sacrifice, when the king was to be sprinkled with the sacred water, the sages and the kings gathered in the inner enclosure. The sages argued happily among themselves, *this is so*, *this is not so*, some making the weaker case look stronger, some pouncing on others' arguments like hawks on meat. {#p1 2.36}

[Narada](narada) looked at that great crowd of Kshatriyas and grew thoughtful. He remembered what he had heard in Brahma's hall: that the gods had been told to be born on earth and kill one another and come back to heaven, and that Narayana himself had been born among the Yadavas to see it done. And he thought: the Self-Create himself will take away this great gathering of Kshatriyas. He sat through the sacrifice in awe. {#p2 2.36}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,yudhishthira,krishna,sahadeva light=bhishma,krishna}

Then [Bhishma](bhishma) told [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) that each king should be honoured with the guest-offering, the *arghya*, and the first of all should go to the foremost man present. *Whom?* asked Yudhishthira. {#p3 2.36}

> As is the sun among all luminous objects, so is the one (meaning Krishna) (who shines like the sun) among us all, in consequence of his energy, strength and prowess. {#q1 2.36}
> — Bhishma

So [Sahadeva](sahadeva) offered the first arghya to [Krishna](krishna), and Krishna accepted it. {#p4 2.36}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=shishupala,krishna,bhishma,yudhishthira light=shishupala}

[Shishupala](shishupala), king of Chedi, could not bear it. {#p5 2.37}

> O thou of the Kuru race, this one of the Vrishni race doth not deserve royal worship as if he were a king, in the midst of all these illustrious monarchs. {#q2 2.37}
> — Shishupala

The Pandavas were children, he said, who did not understand dharma, and Bhishma had misled them. If they wanted the oldest, Krishna's father was here; a well-wisher, Drupada; a teacher, Drona; a priest, Vyasa. Bhishma himself was here, and Duryodhana, and Kripa, and Karna, the favourite pupil of Parashurama. Krishna was neither priest nor teacher nor king. He had killed Jarasandha by a trick. And he had lapped up this honour, Shishupala said, *like a dog that lappeth in solitude a quantity of clarified butter*. {#p6 2.37}

Then he rose from his seat and walked out, and the kings who were with him went too. {#p7 2.37}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,shishupala,bhishma light=bhishma}

Yudhishthira ran after him and spoke gently: these kings, many older than he, all approved; he should bear it as they did. {#p8 2.38}

Bhishma was not gentle. Whoever did not approve of honouring Krishna, he said, deserved neither soft words nor persuasion. There was not one king in this assembly that Krishna had not beaten in battle. Among Brahmanas the most learned is honoured first; among Kshatriyas, the strongest; and Krishna was both. {#p9 2.38}

> Krishna is the origin of the universe and that in which the universe is to dissolve. {#q3 2.38}
> — Bhishma

Shishupala, said Bhishma, was a mere boy. {#p10 2.38}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=sahadeva,narada,shishupala,krishna light=sahadeva}

Then [Sahadeva](sahadeva) stood up. {#p11 2.39}

> If amongst ye there be any king that cannot bear to see Kesava of dark hue, the slayer of Kesi, the possessor of immeasurable energy, worshipped by me, this my foot is placed on the heads of all mighty ones (like him). {#q4 2.39}
> — Sahadeva

He showed them his foot. No king said a word. Flowers fell on Sahadeva's head, and a voice from the sky said, *Excellent, excellent.* And Narada, in his black deer-skin, said that those who would not worship the lotus-eyed Krishna were dead though they walked. {#p12 2.39}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=shishupala,krishna,yudhishthira,bhishma light=shishupala}

But Shishupala's eyes were red as copper. *When I am here to head ye all, what are ye thinking of now?* he asked the kings. They should stand against the Vrishnis and the Pandavas together, and see that the sacrifice was never completed. The kings' faces went pale with anger, and they roared like lions driven from their prey. Krishna saw that a great sea of kings was about to break. {#p13 2.39}

Yudhishthira turned to Bhishma. *What should I do, that my sacrifice may not be obstructed?* {#p14 2.40}
