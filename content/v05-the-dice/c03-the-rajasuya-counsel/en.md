::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=yudhishthira}

After Narada left, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) sighed and could not rest. He thought of Harishchandra, and of his father, and of the Rajasuya. {#p1 2.13}

His kingdom was at peace. He gave each man his due, and the only thing he ever heard said was *Blessed be Dharma!* No one in the land bore him any ill will, and so they called him Ajatashatru, *the one with no enemy*. Bhima ruled justly under him, Arjuna guarded the borders, Sahadeva gave judgment, and Nakula was humble with everyone. The rains came when they should; there was no extortion and no fear of thieves or disease. People were more attached to him, the epic says, than to their own fathers and mothers. {#p2 2.13}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,dhaumya,vyasa,krishna light=yudhishthira,krishna}

His ministers and priests told him he was worthy of the Rajasuya and the time had come. But Yudhishthira did not trust advice that agreed with him so easily. Friends, he said, do not see the difficulties; flatterers say what pleases. He sent for the one man he thought was above both. {#p3 2.13}

[Krishna](krishna) came from Dvaraka at once. When he had rested, Yudhishthira asked him plainly whether he should do it. {#p4 2.13}

> But thou, O Krishna, art above such motives. Thou hast conquered both desire and anger. It behoveth thee to tell me what is most beneficial to the world. {#q1 2.13}
> — Yudhishthira

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,jarasandha,shishupala,bhagadatta,kamsa,hamsa,dimbhaka light=krishna,jarasandha}

Krishna told him he was worthy of it. But there was a man in the way. {#p5 2.14}

[Jarasandha](jarasandha), king of Magadha, had set himself above every king on earth. [Shishupala](shishupala) of Chedi was the commander of his armies; [Bhagadatta](bhagadatta), the old friend of Pandu, bowed to him; kings had fled their own countries for fear of him. Krishna's own cousin [Kamsa](kamsa) had married two of Jarasandha's daughters, and when Krishna and Balarama killed Kamsa, Jarasandha took up arms against the Yadavas. With his two generals, [Hamsa](hamsa) and [Dimbhaka](dimbhaka), whom no weapon could kill, he had seemed unbeatable. {#p6 2.14}

So the Yadavas had fled. They left Mathura, carrying their wealth in small bundles, and went west to Kushasthali under the Raivata hills, and fortified it so strongly that their women could hold it. That was Dvaraka. {#p7 2.14}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.14}
**Why Dvaraka.** The epic gives Krishna's own account of it here: the Yadavas did not found their sea-city in triumph, but in retreat, fleeing Jarasandha.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna,jarasandha,shiva light=jarasandha}

And Jarasandha was doing something worse than conquering. {#p8 2.14}

> By him have been immured in his hillfort numerous monarchs, like a lion that hath deposited the slain bodies of mighty elephants within a cave of the king of mountains. {#q2 2.14}
> — Krishna

He meant to sacrifice a hundred kings to Shiva. He had eighty-six already; fourteen more and he would begin. While Jarasandha lived, said Krishna, there could be no Rajasuya. Free the kings and kill him, or give up the sacrifice. {#p9 2.14 2.15}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,krishna light=yudhishthira,bhima}

Yudhishthira was afraid. If Krishna himself had fled from Jarasandha, how could he think himself strong enough? Peace of mind, he said, was the highest thing; perhaps he should not do it at all. {#p10 2.15}

[Bhima](bhima) answered him. A weak king who fights a strong one perishes, he said, but even a weak king can win by watchfulness and policy. {#p11 2.15}

> In Krishna is policy, in myself strength, in Arjuna triumphs. So like the three (sacrificial) fires that accomplish a sacrifice, we shall accomplish the death of the king of Magadha. {#q3 2.15}
> — Bhima

***

::scene{#s6 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna light=arjuna}

Yudhishthira still could not bring himself to it. Bhima and Arjuna were his eyes, he said, and Krishna his mind; how could he send them to die? {#p12 2.16}

Then [Arjuna](arjuna) spoke, he who had Gandiva now, and the inexhaustible quivers and the ape-bannered chariot and the hall. Nobility of birth is praised in good company, he said, but nothing is equal to strength. {#p13 2.16}

> If, however, we do not undertake the task, the world will always think us incompetent. We have certainly the competence, O king! Why should you, therefore, regard us as incompetent? {#q4 2.16}
> — Arjuna
