::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,yudhishthira light=krishna}

[Krishna](krishna) came back to Upaplavya and told the Pandavas what had happened. That night, after their evening prayers, they called him in again. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) wanted every word: what had the grandsire said, and the teacher, and the old king and queen, and Vidura? He had heard it once, but it had slipped from his mind. {#p1 5.147}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=5.147}
Krishna's report does not match the narrator's own account of the assembly (5.124–131). Here Bhishma tells the story of his vow, Gandhari speaks of the law of succession, Dhritarashtra tells of Yadu and Devapi, and Krishna says he offered to let the Pandavas live as dependants for five villages. None of these speeches appear in the earlier telling, which has others instead. The epic keeps both versions side by side.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,duryodhana,satyavati light=bhishma}

When his own words were done, Krishna said, Duryodhana laughed aloud, and [Bhishma](bhishma) spoke in anger. He told his own story. He had been Shantanu's only son, and his father had wanted another; so Bhishma had made the vow and won [Satyavati](satyavati) for him. When [Vichitravirya](vichitravirya) died and no rain fell and the people were dying, they and his mother and the priests had begged him to take the throne, and he had refused with joined hands: *I cannot falsify my promise*. Instead they had asked Vyasa for sons. Dhritarashtra was born blind and could not reign; Pandu became king, and Pandu's sons must have his share. {#p2 5.147}

> When I am alive, what other man is competent to reign? {#q1 5.147}
> — Bhishma

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drona,vidura,gandhari light=gandhari}

[Drona](drona) said Pandu had given the throne to Dhritarashtra and gone to the forest, and Vidura had served the blind king fanning him with a palm leaf. He did not live on Duryodhana's bread, he said, but on what Bhishma gave him; where Bhishma was, Drona would be. *Victory is there where righteousness is.* {#p3 5.148}

[Vidura](vidura) turned on Bhishma himself. He had revived the dying race once, like a painter making a picture; let him not now be like the Creator, who makes creatures and destroys them. Bind Duryodhana, or take Vidura and Dhritarashtra and go to the forest. {#p4 5.148}

Then [Gandhari](gandhari) spoke in anger before all the kings. The Kuru kingdom passes in order of succession; Bhishma would not take it, so it went to Pandu, and it was his sons' and grandsons' now. {#p5 5.148}

> Keeping virtue in front, let Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, guided by king Dhritarashtra and urged by Santanu's son, rule for many long years this kingdom of the Kurus lawfully obtainable by him. {#q2 5.148}
> — Gandhari

***

::scene{#s4 frame=dhritarashtra,yadu,puru,devapi light=dhritarashtra}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) went further back. [Yadu](yadu), Yayati's eldest son, was proud and disobedient, and Yayati gave the throne to [Puru](puru), the youngest. [Devapi](devapi), Pratipa's eldest, was good and loved, but had a skin disease, and the Brahmanas forbade his crowning; he went to the forest, and Shantanu reigned. So too Dhritarashtra, though the eldest, had been passed over for his blindness. {#p6 5.149}

> When I could not obtain the kingdom, how canst thou covet it? Thou art not the son of a king, and, therefore, hast no right to this kingdom. {#q3 5.149}
> — Dhritarashtra

***

::scene{#s5 frame=krishna,duryodhana light=krishna}

Duryodhana got up and left with his eyes red, and the kings followed him. *Today the constellation Pushya is in the ascendant*, he told them; *march to Kurukshetra*. They went, with Bhishma at their head under his palm-tree banner: eleven armies. {#p7 5.150}

Krishna had tried every means, he said. First conciliation; then he had tried to divide them, recounting the Pandavas' feats, terrifying Karna, rebuking Shakuni; then conciliation again; then gifts, offering that the Pandavas would live as dependants of the old king and ask only five villages. None of it had worked. {#p8 5.150}

> I, therefore, see that chastisement, and nothing else, is now the means that should be employed against those sinful persons. {#q4 5.150}
> — Krishna
