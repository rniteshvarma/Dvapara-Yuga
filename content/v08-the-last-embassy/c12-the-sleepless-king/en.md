[Sanjaya](sanjaya) drove back to Hastinapura and went straight to the inner apartments. *If the king be awake, then only shouldst thou say so.* [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) had him brought in at once. {#p1 5.32}

Yudhishthira was well, Sanjaya said, and wanted back what had been his. With him, harmlessness was higher even than virtue, and virtue higher than wealth. Then Sanjaya spoke plainly to the old king. {#p2 5.32}

> Even like doll pulled this way and that by threads, man (in this world) moveth, swayed by a force not his own. {#q1 5.32}
> — Sanjaya

Yudhishthira had cast off his sins like a snake its old skin, and left them for Dhritarashtra to carry. The king had earned a bad name in this world and would reap misery in the next. If there was no peace, Arjuna would consume the Kurus *like a blazing fire consuming a heap of dried grass*. At the dice, he alone had thought himself successful by giving in to his son. {#p3 5.32}

> Wearied by my fast journey and very much fatigued, I solicit thy permission to go to bed now, O lion of men, for tomorrow morning will the Kurus, assembled together in the council-hall, hear the words of Ajatasatru. {#q2 5.32}
> — Sanjaya

***

::scene{#s2 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura light=vidura}

The king could not sleep. He sent for [Vidura](vidura). Sanjaya had come back and gone away after rebuking him, he said; tomorrow he would give Yudhishthira's message in the court, and Dhritarashtra did not know what it was. *Therefore, my body is burning, and that hath produced sleeplessness.* {#p4 5.33}

> Sleeplessness overtaketh thief, a lustful person, him that hath lost all his wealth, him that hath failed to achieve success, and him also that is weak and hath been attacked by a strong person. {#q3 5.33}
> — Vidura

He hoped, Vidura said, that none of these had overtaken the king; that he was not grieving because he coveted another's wealth. Dhritarashtra asked him for words of good counsel. And so, through the rest of that night, Vidura talked. {#p5 5.33}
