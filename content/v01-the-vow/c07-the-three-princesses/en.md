::scene{#s1 frame=bhishma,kashiraja,amba,ambika,ambalika light=amba,ambika,ambalika}

News reached Hastinapura that the [king of Kashi](kashiraja) had three daughters, as beautiful as Apsaras, and that all three would choose their husbands on the same day, at a *svayamvara*, a ceremony where a princess chooses from among the kings who come for her. {#p1 1.102}

At Satyavati's command, [Bhishma](bhishma) set out for Varanasi alone, in a single chariot. {#p2 1.102}

He found the city full of kings who had come from every direction, and he saw the three princesses waiting to choose. As the kings were being announced one by one, Bhishma chose the maidens himself, on his brother's behalf. He lifted them into his chariot and turned to face the assembled kings, and he spoke in a voice like the roar of the clouds. {#p3 1.102}

There were many ways to win a bride, he told them: as a gift, for a price, by the parents' consent, by the bride's own choice. Kings preferred the *svayamvara*. But the wise held one wife above all others. {#p4 1.102}

> But the sages have said that, that wife is dearly to be prized who is taken away by force, after the slaughter of opponents, from amidst the concourse of princes and kings invited to a self-choice ceremony. Therefore, ye monarchs, I bear away these maidens hence by force. Strive ye, to the best of your might, to vanquish me or to be vanquished. Ye monarchs, I stand here resolved to fight! {#q1 1.102}
> — Bhishma

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma light=bhishma}

The kings leapt up, slapping their arms and biting their lips in rage. They tore off their ornaments, put on their armour, mounted their chariots and rode after him. {#p5 1.102}

It was one man against all of them. Ten thousand arrows flew at him at once, and Bhishma stopped them in the air with his own. When the kings surrounded him and rained arrows on him like clouds on a mountain, he answered each of them with three shafts of his own; when each struck him with five, he struck each back with two. He cut down bows and banners and coats of mail, and so skilful was he, and so well did he protect himself, that even his enemies cheered him. {#p6 1.102}

He beat them all, and drove on toward Hastinapura with the three princesses. {#p7 1.102}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhishma,i2343_shalva light=i2343_shalva,bhishma}

Then one king came after him alone, calling *Stay, stay!* It was [Shalva](i2343_shalva), the lord of Saubha, determined to win the princesses back. He charged at Bhishma, the text says, like a bull elephant charging a rival for a mate. {#p8 1.102}

Bhishma stopped his chariot, as a Kshatriya must when challenged, and waited with his bow in hand. The other kings stood back to watch. Shalva covered him with thousands of arrows, and the watching kings cheered his speed. That angered Bhishma. *Lead thou my car to where Salya is,* he told his charioteer, *so that I may slay him instantly as Garuda slays a serpent.* {#p9 1.102}

With the Varuna weapon he wounded Shalva's four horses; he killed his charioteer; and with the weapon of Indra he killed the horses. He defeated Shalva, and let him go with his life. Shalva went home to his kingdom, and the other kings went home to theirs. {#p10 1.102}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=1.102}
**Shalva, not Shalya.** Ganguli's translation calls this king *Salya*, which makes it easy to confuse him with Shalya of Madra, the uncle of the Pandava twins, who fights at Kurukshetra. He is a different man: the Sanskrit names him Shalva, and a few lines later Amba calls him *the king of Saubha*, which is Shalva's kingdom. We use the name the Sanskrit gives.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,vichitravirya,satyavati,amba,ambika,ambalika light=amba,ambika,ambalika,vichitravirya}

Bhishma brought the daughters of the king of Kashi home to the Kurus as gently, the text says, as if they were his own daughters-in-law, or his younger sisters, or his daughters. He gave them to his brother, and with Satyavati he began to prepare the wedding. {#p11 1.102}

Then the eldest princess, [Amba](amba), came to him, and spoke with a soft smile. {#p12 1.102}

> At heart I had chosen the king of Saubha for my husband. He had, in his heart, accepted me for his wife. This was also approved by my father. At the self-choice ceremony also I would have chosen him as my lord. Thou art conversant with all the dictates of virtue, knowing all this, do as thou likest. {#q2 1.102}
> — Amba

::scene{#s5 frame=bhishma,amba,i2343_shalva light=amba}

She had said it in front of the Brahmins, and Bhishma took it seriously. He thought it over, consulted Brahmins who knew the Vedas, and gave Amba leave to do as she wished. {#p13 1.102}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.102}
**Remember Amba.** Here the epic lets her go in a single sentence. It will come back to her much later, in its fifth book, and tell what happened when she went to the king she had chosen. Her story is not over, and neither is her part in Bhishma's.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=vichitravirya,ambika,ambalika light=vichitravirya,ambika,ambalika}

The two younger sisters, [Ambika](ambika) and [Ambalika](ambalika), were married to [Vichitravirya](vichitravirya) with all the proper rites. They were tall, with skin the colour of molten gold and black curling hair, and they loved and honoured their husband, who was as handsome as the twin Ashvins and as brave as a god. {#p14 1.102}

For seven years he lived with his wives without a break. Then, still in the prime of his youth, he fell ill with a wasting disease. His friends and kinsmen tried every cure they could find, but nothing helped. The young king died, *setting like the evening sun*. {#p15 1.102}

::scene{#s7 frame=bhishma,satyavati,vichitravirya light=bhishma,satyavati}

Bhishma was plunged into grief and anxiety. With Satyavati he had the funeral rites performed by learned priests. {#p16 1.102}

Shantanu was gone. Both of Satyavati's sons were gone. Neither had left a child. Shantanu's line, for whose sake Bhishma had given up a throne and a wife and children of his own, had come down to one man: the one who had sworn never to continue it. {#p17 1.102 1.100}
