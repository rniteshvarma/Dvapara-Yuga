::scene{#s1 frame=i7257_maya,arjuna,krishna light=i7257_maya}

On the riverbank, with [Krishna](krishna) beside them, [Maya](i7257_maya) the Danava bowed to [Arjuna](arjuna) with folded hands. Arjuna had saved him from Krishna and from the fire. *Tell me what I have to do for thee.* {#p1 2.1}

Arjuna said he needed nothing; Maya should go where he liked, and be his friend. But Maya would not leave it there. {#p2 2.1}

> I am a great artist, a Viswakarma among the Danavas. O son of Pandu, being what I am, I desire to do something for thee. {#q1 2.1}
> — Maya

Arjuna would not take payment for saving a life, he said, but he did not want to disappoint him either. *Do thou something for Krishna.* {#p3 2.1}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,i7257_maya,yudhishthira light=krishna}

Krishna thought for a moment, and then asked for a building. {#p4 2.1}

> Indeed, build thou such a palace that persons belonging to the world of men may not be able to imitate it even after examining it with care, while seated within. {#q2 2.1}
> — Krishna

It should be an assembly hall for [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), Krishna said, a *sabha* mixing the designs of gods, Asuras and men. Maya was overjoyed. They brought him to Yudhishthira, who received him with honour; Maya told the Pandavas the history of the Danava king [Vrishaparva](vrishaparva), and then, on an auspicious day, fed thousands of Brahmanas and measured out a plot of land five thousand cubits square. {#p5 2.1}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,kunti,subhadra,draupadi,yudhishthira,arjuna light=krishna,yudhishthira}

Krishna stayed a while longer, and then wanted to go home to see his father. He bowed at [Kunti](kunti)'s feet, said goodbye to his sister [Subhadra](subhadra) with tears in his eyes, and to [Draupadi](draupadi) and Dhaumya. {#p6 2.2}

When he climbed into his golden chariot, Yudhishthira climbed up after him and sent the charioteer Daruka aside, and drove it himself; Arjuna rode behind, fanning Krishna with a white yak-tail. Two miles out, Krishna made them stop and turn back. They watched him until he was out of sight, and their minds followed him after that. {#p7 2.2}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=i7257_maya,bhima,arjuna light=i7257_maya}

Then Maya went north, past Mount Kailasa, to the lake called Bindu, where the Danavas had once held a sacrifice. He had left treasure there long ago, in the house of King Vrishaparva: crystal and gems of every kind. And in the lake lay two other things he had promised the Pandavas. {#p8 2.3}

For [Bhima](bhima), a great mace banded with gold, heavy enough to equal a hundred thousand maces. For Arjuna, a conch called Devadatta, which had come from Varuna, *at whose sound all creatures trembled in awe*. Maya brought them back, and the treasure with them. {#p9 2.3}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=i7257_maya,yudhishthira light=i7257_maya}

With it he built the hall. Its pillars were gold, its walls gold, it covered five thousand cubits, and it shone so brightly that it seemed to dim the sun, as if it were on fire. It outshone Sudharma, the hall of the Yadavas, and the mansion of Brahma himself. Eight thousand Rakshasas, huge and red-eyed, able to fly, guarded it. {#p10 2.3}

Inside, Maya made a pool. Its lotuses had leaves of dark gems and stalks of jewels; golden fish and tortoises swam in water so clear the bottom had no mud; crystal stairs led down to it, and its banks were set with pearls. {#p11 2.3}

> And beholding that tank thus adorned all around with jewels and precious stones, many kings that came there mistook it for land and fell into it with eyes open. {#q3 2.3}
> — Vaisampayana

Maya finished the hall in fourteen months, and told Yudhishthira it was ready. {#p12 2.3}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.3}
**Remember the pool.** The epic mentions, almost as a joke, that visiting kings fell into it thinking it was solid floor. One visitor's stumble in this hall will matter more than any war.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,vyasa,dhaumya light=yudhishthira}

Yudhishthira fed ten thousand Brahmanas and gave each a thousand cows, and entered the hall to music and perfume, with wrestlers, mimes and bards performing. Sages came to sit there with him, [Vyasa](vyasa) and [Dhaumya](dhaumya) and Markandeya and a great many more; kings came, and the young princes Arjuna had taught; Gandharvas and apsaras came to sing. And the Pandavas sat among them all in that hall, the epic says, like the gods around Brahma. {#p13 2.4}
