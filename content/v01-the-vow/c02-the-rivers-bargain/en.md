::scene{#s1 frame=shantanu,ganga light=shantanu,ganga}

[Ganga](ganga) answered the king, and her answer was a bargain. {#p1 1.98}

> O king, I shall become thy wife and obey thy commands. But, O monarch, thou must not interfere with me in anything I do, be it agreeable or disagreeable. Nor shall thou ever address me unkindly. As long as thou shalt behave kindly I promise to live with thee. But I shall certainly leave thee the moment thou interferest with me or speakest to me an unkind word. {#q1 1.98}
> — Ganga

[Shantanu](shantanu) said only, *Be it so.* {#p2 1.98}

For a long time it was easy. She was beautiful, generous and attentive, and she filled his days with music and dance and affection. He was so happy with her that months, seasons and years went by without his noticing. And true to his word, he never once asked her anything. {#p3 1.98}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=shantanu,ganga,prabhasa light=ganga}

Then their first child was born. As soon as he was born, Ganga carried him to the river and threw him in. *This is for thy good,* she said. The child sank, and did not rise. {#p4 1.98}

A second son was born, and the same thing happened, and then a third. Seven sons were born, one after another, and as each was born Ganga carried him to the water, smiling, and gave him to the river. {#p5 1.98}

The king was not pleased. But he said nothing, because he knew that the first unkind word would cost him his wife. {#p6 1.98}

Then the eighth child was born. Ganga lifted him as she had lifted the others and went down to the water, smiling. This time Shantanu could not bear it. {#p7 1.98}

> Kill it not! Who art thou and whose? Why dost thou kill thy own children? Murderess of thy sons, the load of thy sins is great! {#q2 1.98}
> — Shantanu

It was the angry word that Brahma had foretold, long before, in heaven. {#p8 1.96 1.98}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=ganga,shantanu light=ganga}

Ganga turned to him, and for the first time she kept nothing back. *I shall not destroy this child of thine,* she said. *But according to our agreement, the period of my stay with thee is at an end.* {#p9 1.98}

Then she told him who she was: Ganga, the daughter of Jahnu, the river worshipped by the great sages. The eight Vasus, she said, had been cursed to take human birth, and no man on earth but Shantanu was worthy to be their father, and no woman but a goddess in human form could be their mother. She had promised to free each of them from his human body the moment he was born. By drowning them, she had released them. Shantanu, as their father, had won worlds of lasting bliss. {#p10 1.98}

> Blest be thou; I leave thee, O king! But rear thou this child of rigid vows. That I should live with thee so long was the promise I gave to the Vasus. And let this child be called Gangadatta. {#q3 1.98}
> — Ganga

But Shantanu would not let her go without an answer. What had the Vasus done, he asked, that they had to be born among men? And what had this child done, that he alone must stay? {#p11 1.99}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=vasishtha,i7579_nandini light=vasishtha}

So Ganga told him the story of a cow. {#p12 1.99}

On the slopes of Mount Meru, she said, lived the sage [Vasishtha](vasishtha), whom Varuna had obtained as a son. He was also called Apava. His hermitage was a sacred place full of birds and animals, where the flowers of every season bloomed all year round. {#p13 1.99}

[Surabhi](i10365_surabhi), the daughter of Daksha, had borne a daughter to the sage [Kashyapa](kashyapa), and this daughter was a cow, [Nandini](i7579_nandini), the cow of plenty, who could grant any wish. Vasishtha kept her for his sacrificial rites, and she roamed his woods without fear. {#p14 1.99}

::scene{#s5 frame=prabhasa,i8703_prithu,vasishtha light=prabhasa}

One day the Vasus came into those woods with their wives, led by [Prithu](i8703_prithu), and wandered there at their pleasure. The wife of one of them, [Dyu](prabhasa), saw Nandini and showed her to her husband. Dyu knew the cow at once. Whoever drank her milk, he said, would stay young for ten thousand years. {#p15 1.99}

His wife had a friend on earth, Jitavati, the daughter of the royal sage Ushinara. She asked Dyu to steal the cow and her calf for that friend, so that she alone among mortals might be free of illness and old age. Dyu loved his wife, and he forgot for a moment whose cow this was. With his brothers' help he stole Nandini. {#p16 1.99}

That evening Vasishtha came home with the fruit he had gathered and found the cow and her calf gone. He searched the woods. When he could not find them, he looked with the eye of his austerities and saw that the Vasus had taken them. {#p17 1.99}

> Because the Vasus have stolen my cow of sweet milk and handsome tail, therefore, shall they certainly be born on earth! {#q4 1.99}
> — Vasishtha

***

::scene{#s6 frame=vasishtha,prabhasa,ganga light=prabhasa}

The Vasus hurried to his hermitage and begged him to relent. He would not take back his words, because he never made his angry words empty. But he softened them. The seven who had only helped would be freed within a year of being born. Dyu, whose act it was, would live on earth for a long time. {#p18 1.99}

And Vasishtha said what kind of life it would be. Dyu would be virtuous and learned in the scriptures. He would be an obedient son to his father. He would have no children, and he would abstain from the company of women. {#p19 1.99}

That was why the Vasus had come to Ganga, she told the king. That was why she had given seven sons to the river. And the eighth, the one in her arms, was Dyu himself. {#p20 1.99}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=ganga,bhishma,shantanu light=bhishma}

When she had finished, Ganga vanished, and she took the child with her. He would be known as Gangeya, *son of Ganga*, and as [Devavrata](bhishma), and in time he would surpass his father in every accomplishment. {#p21 1.99}

Shantanu went back to his capital alone, with a heavy heart. {#p22 1.99}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.99}
**Look closely at the curse.** Every condition Vasishtha laid on Dyu, a long life on earth, learning and virtue, obedience to his father, no children and no wife, will come true in the life of the boy Ganga carried away. The epic plants it here, many chapters before the vow that fulfils it.
::
