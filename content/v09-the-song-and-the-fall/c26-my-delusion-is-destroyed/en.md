Last, [Arjuna](arjuna) asked about renunciation and abandonment. Giving up works done from desire is renunciation, [Krishna](krishna) said; giving up the fruit of all work is abandonment. Sacrifice, gifts and austerity should not be given up; they purify the wise, but even they must be done without attachment to their fruit. To give up a prescribed duty from delusion is dark; from fear of bodily pain, passionate; to do it because it should be done, letting go of attachment and fruit, is good. No one with a body can give up action entirely; the one who gives up its fruit is the true renouncer. {#p1 6.42}

Knowledge, action, the doer, understanding, steadiness and happiness are each threefold, by the three qualities. Good happiness is like poison at first and nectar in the end; passionate happiness, born of the senses, is nectar at first and poison in the end; dark happiness deludes from beginning to end. {#p2 6.42}

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna light=krishna}

The duties of Brahmanas, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras are divided by the qualities born of their natures; for the Kshatriya, courage, energy, firmness, skill, not fleeing from battle, generosity and lordliness. A man reaches perfection by worshipping, through his own work, the one from whom all beings come. {#p3 6.42}

> Better is one's own duty though performed faultily than another's duty well-performed. {#q1 6.42}
> — Krishna

If from pride Arjuna thought *I will not fight*, his resolve would be vain; his own nature would make him do it. {#p4 6.42}

> The Lord, O Arjuna, dwelleth in the region of the heart of beings, turning all beings as if mounted on a machine, by his illusive power. {#q2 6.42}
> — Krishna

Take refuge in him, Krishna said; reflect on all this, and do as you choose. And then, the most secret word of all, because Arjuna was very dear to him: {#p5 6.42}

> Forsaking all (religious) duties, come to Me as thy sole refuge. I will deliver thee from all sins. Do not grieve. {#q3 6.42}
> — Krishna

Has your delusion born of ignorance been destroyed, Dhananjaya? {#p6 6.42}

> My delusion hath been destroyed, and the recollection (of what I am) hath been gained by me, O Undeteriorating one, through thy favour. I am now firm. My doubts have been dispelled. I will do thy bidding. {#q4 6.42}
> — Arjuna

So, said [Sanjaya](sanjaya) to the king, by Vyasa's grace he had heard that wonderful dialogue from Krishna himself, the lord of yoga, and remembering it, and that form of Hari, he rejoiced again and again. {#p7 6.42}

> Thither where Krishna, the Lord of Yoga (is), thither where the great bowman (Partha) is, thither, in my opinion, are prosperity, and victory, and greatness, and eternal justice. {#q5 6.42}
> — Sanjaya
