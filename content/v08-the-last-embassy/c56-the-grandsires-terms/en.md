::scene{#s1 frame=duryodhana,karna,duhshasana,shakuni light=duryodhana}

In Hastinapura, [Duryodhana](duryodhana) told [Karna](karna), [Duhshasana](duhshasana) and [Shakuni](shakuni) that Krishna had gone back angry and would stir the Pandavas up. Let the kings pitch their tents on Kurukshetra near water and firewood, with supply lines the enemy could not cut; let the road be levelled; the march would begin tomorrow. The kings rubbed their gold-braceleted arms and put on their armour, and the city looked like the ocean when the moon rises. {#p1 5.154}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,krishna,arjuna light=yudhishthira}

In the Pandava camp, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) asked Krishna once more what they should do. Krishna said Duryodhana had listened to no one, had even ordered him seized, and that Bhishma and Drona had said nothing about it. All the kings in Yudhishthira's army together did not hold as much sin as Duryodhana alone. There must be war. {#p2 5.155}

The kings looked at Yudhishthira's face, and he gave the order to form ranks. The army roared with joy. But Yudhishthira sighed. {#p3 5.155}

> How shall we fight with those reverend superiors (of ours) whom we on no account can slay? What kind of victory shall we achieve by slaying our preceptors of venerable age? {#q1 5.155}
> — Yudhishthira

[Arjuna](arjuna) reminded him of what Kunti and Vidura had said through Krishna; neither of them would counsel anything sinful, and they could not turn back now. Krishna agreed. The Pandavas made up their minds for war, and spent the night happily with their soldiers. {#p4 5.155}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana light=duryodhana}

At dawn Duryodhana arranged his eleven armies, sorting men, elephants, chariots and horses into best, middling and worst. Every chariot carried a hundred bows and spare timber for repairs; every elephant carried seven fighters. The stores held oil, treacle and sand, pots of poisonous snakes, lac to burn, and machines for hurling hot treacle and stones. Over each army he set a leader: Kripa, Drona, Shalya, Jayadratha, Sudakshina of Kamboja, Kritavarma, Ashwatthama, Karna, Bhurishravas, Shakuni and Bahlika. {#p5 5.156}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=duryodhana,bhishma,karna light=bhishma}

Then Duryodhana came to [Bhishma](bhishma) with joined hands. An army without a single commander is routed like ants. Once the Brahmanas fought the Haihaya Kshatriyas and kept losing until they chose one leader. Let Bhishma lead them, *like calves following the lead of a mighty bull*. {#p6 5.157}

Bhishma agreed, but on terms. The Pandavas were as dear to him as Duryodhana's brothers; he would fight for Duryodhana because he had promised, but he would seek their good too. Only Arjuna was his equal, and Arjuna would never fight him openly. {#p7 5.157}

> The sons of Pandu, however, O king, are incapable of being exterminated by me. I shall, therefore, slay every day ten thousand warriors. {#q2 5.157}
> — Bhishma

And there was one more condition: either Karna would fight first, or he would. The Suta's son was always comparing his own valour with Bhishma's. {#p8 5.157}

> As long as Ganga's son liveth, O king, I shall never fight. After Bhishma is slain, I shall fight with the wielder of Gandiva. {#q3 5.157}
> — Karna

So Bhishma was made commander. Drums and conchs sounded; a shower of blood fell from a cloudless sky, the earth shook, and jackals howled. Duryodhana marched to Kurukshetra with Bhishma in the van, and his camp, on a fertile, level stretch of the plain, *shone like Hastinapura itself*. {#p9 5.157}
