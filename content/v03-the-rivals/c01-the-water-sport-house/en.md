::scene{#s1 frame=vyasa,satyavati,ambika,ambalika light=satyavati}

When the funeral rites for Pandu were done, [Vyasa](vyasa) came to his mother, [Satyavati](satyavati). He saw the whole kingdom sunk in grief, and he saw further than that. {#p1 1.128}

> Mother, our days of happiness have gone by and days of calamity have succeeded. Sin beginneth to increase day by day. The world hath got old. {#q1 1.128}
> — Vyasa

The empire of the Kurus would not last, he told her. She should go into the forest and give herself to contemplation, and not stay to watch her own line destroyed in her old age. {#p2 1.128}

So Satyavati took leave of [Bhishma](bhishma) and went into the forest with her two daughters-in-law, [Ambika](ambika), and [Ambalika](ambalika), who was grieving for her son. There, in time, deep in contemplation, she left her body and went to heaven. The fisher-girl whose father had once asked a king to crown her sons did not stay to see what her grandsons would do with it. {#p3 1.128}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima,duryodhana light=bhima}

The five sons of Pandu grew up in their father's house, among a hundred cousins, and it was soon clear who was stronger. In running, in hitting a mark, in eating, in kicking up dust, [Bhima](bhima) beat every one of the sons of Dhritarashtra. {#p4 1.128}

He pulled them by the hair and set them fighting each other, laughing all the while. He held ten of them under water until they were nearly drowned. When they climbed a tree for fruit, he kicked the trunk until the fruit and the boys came down together. Knees, heads and shoulders were broken. The epic is careful to say why he did it: {#p5 1.128}

> Bhima used to make a display of his strength by thus tormenting them in childishness but not from malice. {#q2 1.128}
> — Vaisampayana

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana,bhima,yudhishthira,arjuna light=duryodhana}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana), the eldest of the hundred, watched, and began to hate him. No one could match Bhima in a fair fight, he thought; he would have to be destroyed by a trick. {#p6 1.128}

> Therefore, when he shall sleep in the garden, I shall throw him into the current of the Ganga. Afterwards, confining his eldest brother Yudhishthira and his younger brother Arjuna, I shall reign sole king without molestation. {#q3 1.128}
> — Duryodhana

At a lovely place called Pramanakoti on the bank of the Ganga he built a pleasure house hung with rich cloth, with flags on its roof, painted walls, fountains and pools of lotuses. They called it *the water-sport house*. When it was ready he invited the Pandavas to come and play in the river, and [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), who could not imagine a trick, said yes. {#p7 1.128}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=duryodhana,bhima light=bhima,duryodhana}

The princes ate together, passing morsels to one another in play. Duryodhana had mixed a strong poison into the food, and he fed Bhima a great deal of it, in the friendliest way. The epic gives him a line that has stayed with him ever since: he had *nectar in his tongue and a razor in his heart*. {#p8 1.128}

They swam all afternoon. Bhima, who had worn the others out in the water, was the most tired of all, and when he came out he lay down on the ground. The poison and the cool evening air did the rest, and he lost his senses. Duryodhana tied him up with cords of creeper and threw him into the river. {#p9 1.128}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=bhima,vasuki,i862_aryaka,kunti light=bhima,vasuki,i862_aryaka}

Bhima sank down and down until he came to the kingdom of the Nagas. Thousands of serpents bit him. But their venom fought the poison in his blood and killed it, and he woke. He burst the creepers and began crushing snakes into the ground, and the survivors fled to their king, [Vasuki](vasuki). {#p10 1.128}

Vasuki came to see. One of the serpents with him, [Aryaka](i862_aryaka), knew the young man: he was the grandfather of the father of [Kunti](kunti), and he embraced Bhima as his own. Vasuki offered gold and jewels. Aryaka asked for something better. {#p11 1.128}

> Permit him to drink of rasakunda (nectar-vessels) and thus acquire immeasurable strength. There is the strength of a thousand elephants in each one of those vessels. {#q4 1.128}
> — Aryaka

Facing east, Bhima drank a whole vessel at one breath, and then another, until he had drained eight. Then the serpents made him a bed, and he slept. {#p12 1.128}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.128}
**Kunti's serpent kin.** The epic names Aryaka as the grandfather of Kunti's father, and so a great-great-grandfather to Bhima. It says nothing more about how a Naga came to be in the line of the Yadavas; it simply lets the old serpent know his own.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=yudhishthira,kunti,vidura,duryodhana light=kunti,vidura}

The princes rode home without him, saying to one another that Bhima must have gone on ahead. Duryodhana rode home glad. {#p13 1.129}

Yudhishthira went straight to his mother. Had Bhima come? They had searched all the gardens and the woods. Kunti cried out in fear and sent him back to search, and then called for [Vidura](vidura). Duryodhana did not like Bhima, she said; he was crooked, he wanted the throne openly, and she was afraid he had killed her son. {#p14 1.129}

> Blessed dame, say not so! Protect thy other sons with care. If the wicked Duryodhana be accused, he may slay thy remaining sons. {#q5 1.129}
> — Vidura

The great sage had said all her sons would live long, Vidura told her. Bhima would come back. {#p15 1.129}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=bhima,kunti,yudhishthira,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=bhima,kunti}

On the eighth day Bhima woke, with the nectar digested and a strength in him beyond measure. The Nagas told him he now had the might of ten thousand elephants and that no one would ever beat him in a fight. They bathed him, dressed him in white, fed him rice and sugar, and carried him up to the very garden he had been taken from, and vanished. {#p16 1.129}

He ran home to his mother. He bowed to her and to his eldest brother, and smelt the heads of the younger ones, and they all held him, saying over and over: *What is our joy today, O what joy!* {#p17 1.129}

Then he told them everything. Yudhishthira's answer was the answer of a boy who had already understood where they lived. {#p18 1.129}

> Do thou observe silence on this. Do not speak of this to any one. From this day, protect ye all one another with care. {#q6 1.129}
> — Yudhishthira

***

::scene{#s8 frame=yuyutsu,bhima,duryodhana,karna,shakuni,vidura light=yuyutsu}

Duryodhana tried again. He mixed a fresh poison into Bhima's food, more deadly than the first. But [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu), Dhritarashtra's son by a Vaishya woman, was a friend to the Pandavas, and he warned them. Bhima ate it anyway, without hesitating, and digested it, and it did nothing to him at all. {#p19 1.129}

After that, Duryodhana, [Karna](karna) and [Shakuni](shakuni) tried many other schemes against the Pandavas. The Pandavas knew of every one of them, and, on Vidura's advice, kept their anger to themselves. {#p20 1.129}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.129,1.134}
**Karna, already here.** The epic names Karna beside Duryodhana and Shakuni at this point, before it has told us how he came to the court. A few sections on he appears again as a pupil of Drona, and as Arjuna's rival from the start.
::

***

::scene{#s9 frame=dhritarashtra,kripa light=kripa}

Meanwhile [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), seeing the princes idle and growing unruly, gave them a teacher. He was [Kripa](kripa), also called Gautama, a master of the Vedas who had been born, strangely, in a clump of heath, and under him the Kuru princes began to learn the use of arms. {#p21 1.129}
