::scene{#s1 frame=bhishma,yudhishthira,krishna,shishupala light=bhishma}

> Fear not, O tiger of the Kurus. Can the dog slay the lion? {#q1 2.40}
> — Bhishma

These kings were like dogs barking at a sleeping lion, said [Bhishma](bhishma), and [Krishna](krishna) was the lion, not yet awake. Shishupala was leading them all to the house of Yama; Vishnu meant to take back into himself the energy that lived in the king of Chedi, and that was why his mind had turned. {#p1 2.40}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=shishupala,bhishma,amba,vichitravirya light=shishupala}

[Shishupala](shishupala) turned on Bhishma. {#p2 2.41}

> Old and infamous wretch of thy race, art thou not ashamed of affrighting all these monarchs with these numerous false terrors! {#q2 2.41}
> — Shishupala

He mocked every deed Bhishma had praised: Krishna killed a vulture as a baby, kicked over a cart, held up a hill that was no bigger than an anthill, and then killed Kamsa, whose food he had eaten. He killed a woman, Putana. Is this the lord of the universe? And Bhishma himself: he had carried off [Amba](amba), who loved another man; his brother's widows had borne sons by someone else in his sight; his celibacy was *either from ignorance or from impotence*. {#p3 2.41}

He told the story of an old swan on the sea-shore who preached virtue to the other birds while eating their eggs, until the birds found out and killed him. *Thy behaviour, O Bhishma, is even like that of the old swan.* {#p4 2.41}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=shishupala,bhima,bhishma light=bhima}

Then he praised Jarasandha, who would not fight Krishna, *saying He is a slave*, and sneered at the way Krishna had come to him by the wrong gate in a false disguise. {#p5 2.42}

[Bhima](bhima) rose in fury. His eyes went copper-red, three lines furrowed his brow like the triple Ganga on its mountain, and he looked like Death at the end of the age. Bhishma caught hold of him, as Shiva once held back Kartikeya, and calmed him. Shishupala laughed. *Release him, O Bhishma! Let all the monarchs behold him scorched by my prowess like an insect in fire.* {#p6 2.42}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=shishupala,damaghosha,krishna,balarama light=shishupala,krishna}

So Bhishma told Bhima who Shishupala was. {#p7 2.43}

He had been born with three eyes and four arms, and brayed like an ass at birth. His parents meant to abandon him, but a voice said he would be strong and fortunate, and that the one who would kill him was already born. His mother begged to know who. *He upon whose lap this child being placed the superfluous arms of his will fall down … and at the sight of whom his third eye on the forehead will disappear.* {#p8 2.43}

Kings came from everywhere to see the child, and his father [Damaghosha](damaghosha) put him on each of their laps, a thousand kings, and nothing happened. Then Krishna and [Balarama](balarama) came to visit their father's sister, the queen of Chedi. She put her son on Krishna's lap, and the extra arms fell off and the third eye vanished. {#p9 2.43}

> O mighty-armed Krishna, I am afflicted with fear; grant me a boon. {#q3 2.43}
> — the queen of Chedi

She asked him to pardon her son's offences. {#p10 2.43}

> O aunt, even when he will deserve to be slain, I will pardon an hundred offences of his. Grieve thou not. {#q4 2.43}
> — Krishna

***

::scene{#s5 frame=bhishma,shishupala,karna light=bhishma}

That, said Bhishma, was why Shishupala dared to challenge Bhima. It was not even his own will; it was Krishna's, taking back his own energy. {#p11 2.44}

Shishupala could bear no more. Praise someone else, he told Bhishma: praise Karna, born with his armour and ear-rings, who tore Jarasandha apart in a wrestling match; praise Drona and Ashwatthama, Duryodhana, Shalya, anyone but this cowherd. Bhishma was alive only at the pleasure of these kings, like the bird that picks meat from between a lion's teeth. {#p12 2.44}

> Truly am I alive at the pleasure of these rulers of earth. But I do regard these kings as not equal to even a straw. {#q5 2.44}
> — Bhishma

The kings were enraged. Kill the old man like an animal, some cried, or burn him in a fire of straw. Bhishma answered that words could be answered with words forever. Let them do as they liked; here was Krishna. Let whoever wanted a quick death challenge him. {#p13 2.44}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.44,2.24}
**Karna and Jarasandha.** Shishupala claims that Karna once beat Jarasandha in a wrestling match. The epic does not tell that story here; it is mentioned in passing, as a boast in a quarrel.
::
