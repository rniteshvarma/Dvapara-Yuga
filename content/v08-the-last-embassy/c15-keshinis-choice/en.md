*My thirst for hearing them is not quenched*, said [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra). So [Vidura](vidura) told him a story, about truth, and land. {#p1 5.35}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vidura light=vidura}

There was once a maiden called Keshini, unrivalled in beauty, who would choose her own husband. The Daitya prince Virochana, son of Prahlada, came for her. Which were greater, she asked him, the Brahmanas or the sons of Diti? And why should Sudhanwan the Brahmana not sit on the couch? Virochana said his race were the best of all creatures. Then Sudhanwan came in the morning, and Keshini rose and gave him a seat and water for his feet. {#p2 5.35}

Virochana told him a plank or a grass mat was good enough for him. Sudhanwan answered that Virochana's own father used to sit below him. So they wagered, not gold or cattle, but their lives, and went to Prahlada himself, who would never lie even for his son. {#p3 5.35}

> Sudhanwan said, 'Having wagered our lives, we will approach thy father, for he, Prahlada, will never say an untruth even for the sake of his son.' {#q1 5.35}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=vidura light=vidura}

Prahlada did not want to answer: this was his only son. What happens, he asked, to one who answers falsely? Sudhanwan told him: a liar suffers like a wife watching her husband sleep in a co-wife's arms; a lie for a cow casts down ten of one's ancestors, for a man a thousand. {#p4 5.35}

> An untruth on account of gold ruineth the members of one's race both born and unborn, while an untruth for the sake of land ruineth everything. Therefore, never speak an untruth for the sake of land. {#q2 5.35}
> — Sudhanwan

> Angiras is superior to myself, and Sudhanwan is superior to thee, O Virochana. {#q3 5.35}
> — Prahlada

Sudhanwan, master now of Virochana's life, gave it back to Prahlada, because he had chosen truth over his son. But Virochana must wash his feet in front of Keshini. {#p5 5.35}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=vidura,dhritarashtra light=vidura}

> For these reasons, O king of kings, it behoveth thee not to say an untruth for the sake of land. {#q4 5.35}
> — Vidura

*The gods do not protect men, taking up clubs in their hands after the manner of herdsmen; unto those, however, they wish to protect, they grant intelligence.* {#p6 5.35}
