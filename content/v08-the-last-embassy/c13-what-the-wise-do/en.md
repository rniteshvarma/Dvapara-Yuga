[Vidura](vidura) began with the plain truth. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) was fit to rule the three worlds, and the king had exiled him. Dhritarashtra himself had no right to the throne, being blind. And he had handed his empire to Duryodhana, Shakuni, Karna and Duhshasana. *How canst thou hope for prosperity?* {#p1 5.33}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.33}
Vidura's counsel to the sleepless king runs through eight sections of Ganguli (5.33–40) and is one of the great collections of sayings in the epic, known as the Vidura Niti. These chapters give its shape and some of its best-known lines; a fuller telling belongs in a companion volume.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vidura light=vidura}

Then he told the king who is wise and who is a fool. The wise man is not drawn from the high ends of life by anger or joy, pride or false modesty; his plans stay hidden until they are done; heat and cold, fear and fortune do not stop him. {#p2 5.33}

> He who exulteth not at honours, and grieveth not at slights, and remaineth cool and unagitated like a lake in the course of Ganga, is reckoned as wise. {#q1 5.33}
> — Vidura

The fool is ignorant yet vain, poor yet proud; he takes his foe for a friend and hates his friend; he enters uninvited and talks unasked; being guilty, he blames others. {#p3 5.33}

> Poison slayeth but one person, and a weapon also but one; wicked counsels, however, destroy an entire kingdom with king and subject. {#q2 5.33}
> — Vidura

***

::scene{#s3 frame=vidura,dhritarashtra light=vidura}

He spoke of forgiveness. Its one defect is that people take the forgiving to be weak. {#p4 5.33}

> Forgiveness is a virtue of the weak, and an ornament of the strong. {#q3 5.33}
> — Vidura

And then, in numbered sayings, by twos and threes and on up to tens: two thorns in the flesh, a poor man's desires and a powerless man's anger; three gates of hell, lust, anger and greed; six faults for one who would prosper, sleep, drowsiness, fear, anger, idleness and delay; seven vices that ruin kings, women, dice, hunting, drink, harsh speech, cruel punishment and waste. {#p5 5.33}

He ended where he had begun. Pandu, under a curse, had five sons in the forest, *like five Indras*. Dhritarashtra had raised them and taught them; they obeyed him. Let him give them back their share and be happy with his sons. {#p6 5.33}
