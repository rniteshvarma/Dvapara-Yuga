::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,yudhishthira,dhrishtaketu,arjuna light=krishna}

When they heard of the exile, the Bhojas, the Vrishnis and the Andhakas came to the forest, with the Panchalas, [Dhrishtaketu](dhrishtaketu) of Chedi, and the Kekaya brothers, all of them angry. With [Krishna](krishna) at their head, they sat down around Yudhishthira. {#p1 3.12}

> The earth shall drink the blood of Duryodhana and Karna, of Dussasana and the wicked Sakuni! {#q1 3.12}
> — Krishna

He seemed about to burn up the world. [Arjuna](arjuna) calmed him by reciting his ancient deeds: his ten thousand years of austerity on the Gandhamadana, the three strides with which he crossed heaven and earth, the demons he had killed in a thousand births. {#p2 3.12}

> Thou art mine and I am thine, while all that is mine is thine also! {#q2 3.12}
> — Krishna

*Thou art Nara and I am Narayana*, Krishna told him. *No one can understand the difference that is between us.* {#p3 3.12}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=draupadi,krishna,dhrishtadyumna,prativindhya,sutasoma,shatanika,shrutakarma light=draupadi}

Then [Draupadi](draupadi) came to him with her brother [Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna) beside her. How could she, wife of the Pandavas, sister of Dhrishtadyumna, friend of Krishna, be dragged into that hall, bleeding, in one cloth, trembling and weeping, and laughed at? Fie on Bhima's strength and Arjuna's Gandiva, she said, if Duryodhana still drew breath. Even a weak husband protects his wife. She had five sons, [Prativindhya](prativindhya), [Sutasoma](sutasoma), Shrutakirti, [Shatanika](shatanika) and [Shrutakarma](shrutakarma); for their sake, if nothing else, she should have been protected. {#p4 3.12}

She went through every wrong Duryodhana had done since they were children, the poison, the river, the snakes, the burning house. Then she covered her face with her hands and wept. {#p5 3.12}

> Husbands, or sons, or friends, or brothers, or father, have I none! Nor have I thee, O thou slayer of Madhu. {#q3 3.12}
> — Draupadi

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,draupadi,arjuna,dhrishtadyumna light=krishna,draupadi}

The wives of the men she was angry with, said Krishna, would weep as she wept, seeing their husbands dead on the ground, covered in Arjuna's arrows. She would be queen of kings again. {#p6 3.12}

> The heavens might fall, or the Himavat might split, the earth might be rent, or the waters of the ocean might dry up, but my words shall never be futile! {#q4 3.12}
> — Krishna

Draupadi looked sideways at Arjuna, and Arjuna said, *It shall be even as the slayer of Madhu hath said.* And Dhrishtadyumna made his own list: {#p7 3.12}

> I will slay Drona, Sikhandin will slay the grandfather. {#q5 3.12}
> — Dhrishtadyumna

Bhima would kill Duryodhana, he said, and Arjuna Karna. {#p8 3.12}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna,yudhishthira light=krishna}

Then Krishna told Yudhishthira why he had not been there. Had he been at Dvaraka, he would have come to the game uninvited and stopped it, by argument or by force. But he had been away. {#p9 3.13}

While he was at Indraprastha for the Rajasuya, Shalva, a friend of Shishupala, had attacked Dvaraka in Saubha, his flying city, to avenge Shishupala's death. Krishna had gone after him, and fought him in the sky over the sea, and at last brought down Saubha and cut Shalva in two with his discus. Only then had he come home and heard what had happened. {#p10 3.14 3.22}

> It is difficult to confine the waters after the dam is broken! {#q6 3.22}
> — Krishna

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=3.13,3.22}
**Where was Krishna?** In the popular telling, Krishna saves Draupadi's robe from far away. Here, in the forest, he says he was absent from the whole affair, fighting Shalva, and learned of it only when he got home. The two do not easily fit together, and the Critical Edition's version of the robe scene names no rescuer at all.
::

::scene{#s5 frame=krishna,subhadra,abhimanyu,dhrishtadyumna,yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

Then Krishna took his leave. He set [Subhadra](subhadra) and [Abhimanyu](abhimanyu) in his golden chariot and drove back to Dvaraka. Dhrishtadyumna went home to Panchala and took Draupadi's sons with him. {#p11 3.22}

The Pandavas went deeper into the forest, to the Dvaita woods, giving away gold and cloth and cattle as they went. The people of the Kuru country gathered round Yudhishthira once more, weeping, *Alas, O lord! O Dharma!*, and Arjuna told them the king was going to the forest to take away his enemies' good name. Then they went home. {#p12 3.23}
