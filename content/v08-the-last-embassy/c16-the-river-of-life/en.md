::scene{#s1 frame=vidura light=vidura}

[Vidura](vidura) went on through the night. He told how the gods called Sadhyas questioned a wandering sage, the son of Atri, who taught them to bear slander silently. {#p1 5.36}

> Harsh words burn and scorch the very vitals, bones, heart, and the very sources of the life of men. {#q1 5.36}
> — Vidura

He named the seventeen kinds of fool who strike the empty air with their fists or try to bend the rainbow. He said why men do not live their hundred years: too much pride, too much talk, too much eating, anger, craving, and quarrels within the family. He spoke of guests, of gifts, of friends. {#p2 5.37 5.38}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vidura,dhritarashtra,duryodhana light=dhritarashtra}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) said that man is not the master of his fortunes; he is *like a wooden doll moved by strings*. Vidura reminded him of something he had said long ago. {#p3 5.39}

> O king, as soon as Duryodhana was born I told thee,—thou shouldst abandon this one son, for by abandoning him thou wouldst secure the prosperity of thy hundred sons. {#q2 5.39}
> — Vidura

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.39}
The omens at [Duryodhana](duryodhana)'s birth, and Vidura's advice then to give up the child, are told in Volume II, chapter 5.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=vidura light=vidura}

Virtue, he said, must never be given up for desire or fear or greed, or even for life itself; virtue is everlasting, pleasure and pain pass. Kings who ruled rich lands are gone. The son raised with such care is carried to the pyre, and others enjoy his wealth; he goes on with only his merits and his sins. {#p4 5.40}

> O king, life is a river whose waters are the five senses, and whose crocodiles and sharks are desire and anger. Making self-control thy raft, cross thou its eddies which are represented by repeated births! {#q3 5.40}
> — Vidura

***

::scene{#s4 frame=dhritarashtra,duryodhana light=dhritarashtra}

The king agreed with all of it. {#p5 5.40}

> Although, however, I incline in my mind towards the Pandavas even as thou teachest me to do, yet as soon as I come in contact with Duryodhana it turneth off in a different way. {#q4 5.40}
> — Dhritarashtra

*No creature is able to avert fate.* {#p6 5.40}
