::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

The Pandavas left Hastinapura by the Vardhamana gate, armed, with Draupadi, heading north. Fourteen servants followed on chariots with their wives. {#p1 3.1}

The citizens blamed Bhishma, Vidura, Drona and Kripa, and said to one another that the whole earth was doomed with Duryodhana as its ruler. And they went after the Pandavas, caught them up, and begged with joined hands to be allowed to come too. {#p2 3.1}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) thanked them and sent them home. His grandfather, the king, Vidura and his mother were all in the city, and all in grief. {#p3 3.1}

> Therefore, if ye are minded to seek our welfare, cherish ye them with care, uniting together as they are overwhelmed with sorrow and afflictions. {#q1 3.1}
> — Yudhishthira

The people wailed, *Alas, O king!*, and turned back unwillingly. That night the Pandavas reached the great banyan called Pramana on the bank of the Ganga, and took nothing but water. {#p4 3.1}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,dhaumya light=yudhishthira}

But a crowd of Brahmanas had followed them, and in the morning they would not go back either. *Our path is even that on which ye are for setting out!* Yudhishthira was in despair. They would be living on roots and fruits and game in a forest full of beasts; his brothers were stunned with grief. How could he watch Brahmanas go hungry for his sake? {#p5 3.2}

A wise Brahmana named Saunaka spoke to him about desire and sorrow and the fruits of action. But it was the family priest, [Dhaumya](dhaumya), who told him what to do: every creature on earth is fed by the sun. Worship the sun. And he taught him the hundred and eight names of Surya. {#p6 3.2 3.3}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,surya,draupadi light=surya,yudhishthira}

Yudhishthira stood in the river, purified himself, and prayed. And [Surya](surya), blazing like fire, showed himself. {#p7 3.3}

> And, O king, accept this copper-vessel which I give unto thee. And, O thou of excellent vows, as long as Panchali will hold this vessel, without partaking of its contents fruits and roots and meat and vegetables cooked in thy kitchen, these four kinds of food shall from this day be inexhaustible. {#q2 3.3}
> — Surya

*And on the fourteenth year from this, thou shall regain thy kingdom.* Then the god vanished. {#p8 3.3}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,draupadi,dhaumya light=draupadi}

Yudhishthira came out of the water and touched Dhaumya's feet. Then he went to the kitchen with [Draupadi](draupadi) and cooked the day's food himself. It was very little, but it grew and would not run out. He fed the Brahmanas, then his brothers, then ate what was left. Last of all Draupadi ate, and when she had eaten, the day's food was gone. {#p9 3.3}

Then, with Dhaumya and the Brahmanas, they set out for the Kamyaka forest. {#p10 3.3}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.3}
**The inexhaustible pot.** Retellings call it the *akshaya patra*, the vessel that never empties. Ganguli's text simply calls it a copper vessel. Its one rule matters: the food lasts each day until Draupadi has eaten, and not after. That rule will be tested later in the forest.
::
