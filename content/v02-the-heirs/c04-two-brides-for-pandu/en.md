::scene{#s1 frame=kunti,kuntibhoja light=kunti}

[Kunti](kunti) grew up beautiful, accomplished and good, faithful to her vows. And yet, the text says, no king came to ask for her hand. So her father [Kuntibhoja](kuntibhoja) invited the kings and princes of other lands to a *svayamvara*, and let his daughter choose her own husband from among them. {#p1 1.112}

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti,pandu light=pandu,kunti}

Kunti walked into the hall and saw [Pandu](pandu), the first of the Bharatas, among all the crowned heads: proud as a lion, broad-chested, outshining every other king, like Indra among them. She was deeply stirred. Trembling, and with great modesty, she walked up to him and placed the bridal garland around his neck. {#p2 1.112}

The other kings went home as they had come, on their elephants and horses and chariots. Kuntibhoja performed the wedding rites and sent his son-in-law home loaded with wealth, and Pandu brought his queen to Hastinapura with banners flying and sages blessing him on the road. {#p3 1.112}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhishma,shalya,madri light=shalya,madri}

Some time later, [Bhishma](bhishma) decided that Pandu should have a second wife. With an army, old counsellors, Brahmins and sages, he went to the capital of Madra. The king of Madra, [Shalya](shalya), came out to meet him and welcomed him with every honour: a white carpet to sit on, water for his feet, the offerings due to an honoured guest. {#p4 1.113}

Bhishma came to the point. He had heard that Shalya had a sister, [Madri](madri), famous for her beauty and her virtue, and he wanted her for Pandu. {#p5 1.113}

Shalya answered that there was no family he would rather join. But his house had a custom, handed down from his ancestors, good or bad, which he could not break, and Bhishma surely knew of it. So it was not proper for Bhishma simply to say, *give me your sister*. {#p6 1.113}

Bhishma understood. The custom was a lawful one, he said, approved by the wise and the good. And he gave Shalya gold, coined and uncoined, precious stones by the thousand, elephants and horses and chariots, cloth and ornaments, pearls and coral. Shalya accepted the gifts gladly and gave his sister, adorned with jewels, to the Kurus. {#p7 1.113}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.113,1.102}
**Two kings called "Salya".** In Ganguli's translation this king of Madra is *Salya*, and here that is right: he is Shalya, Madri's brother, who will be uncle to her sons and will fight at Kurukshetra. In Volume I the same spelling was used for a different king, Shalva of Saubha, the man Amba had chosen.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=pandu,kunti,madri light=pandu,madri}

On a day the wise declared auspicious, Pandu married Madri, and gave her beautiful rooms of her own. For thirty days he lived at ease with his two wives. Then he set out to conquer the world. {#p8 1.113}

::scene{#s5 frame=pandu,bhishma light=pandu}

He bowed to Bhishma and the elders, took leave of Dhritarashtra, and marched out with a great army. He crushed the robber tribes of Dasharna, then Dirgha, the proud king of Magadha, whom he killed in his own capital. He subdued Mithila and the Videhas, and Kashi, Sumbha and Pundra, and his arrows went through the kings of the earth like a forest fire. Kings came to him with joined palms, bringing gold and silver, gems and pearls, horses, elephants, cattle and camels. {#p9 1.113}

When he came home, the line of his wagons was so long that the people who came out to meet him could not see its end. The citizens said that the fame of Shantanu and of the great Bharata, which had been dying, had come back to life. Pandu bowed at the feet of Bhishma, *who was a father to him*, and Bhishma embraced him and wept with joy. {#p10 1.113}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=pandu,dhritarashtra,satyavati,ambalika,vidura light=pandu,dhritarashtra}

At Dhritarashtra's word, Pandu gave the wealth he had won to Bhishma, to their grandmother [Satyavati](satyavati) and to their mothers, and sent a share to [Vidura](vidura). [Ambalika](ambalika) embraced her victorious son, the text says, as the queen of heaven embraces her son Jayanta. With that wealth [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) performed five great sacrifices. {#p11 1.114}

::scene{#s7 frame=pandu,kunti,madri light=pandu,kunti,madri}

Then Pandu did something unexpected. Not long after his triumph he left his palace and its soft beds and went to live in the forest with his two wives, Kunti and Madri, hunting deer. They made their home on the southern slopes of the Himalayas, among great *sala* trees, and the forest people who saw him there, armed and armoured with his two queens beside him, took him for a god walking among them. At Dhritarashtra's command, everything they needed was sent to them from Hastinapura. {#p12 1.114}

::scene{#s8 frame=vidura,bhishma light=vidura}

Meanwhile Bhishma heard that a king named Devaka had a daughter, young and beautiful, born to him by a Shudra wife. He brought her home and married her to Vidura, and Vidura had many children by her, as gifted as himself. {#p13 1.114}

So all three brothers were married. Pandu had gone to the forest, and Dhritarashtra stayed in Hastinapura with Gandhari, who was waiting for her hundred sons. {#p14 1.114 1.115}
