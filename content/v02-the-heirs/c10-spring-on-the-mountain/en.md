::scene{#s1 frame=pandu,madri light=pandu,madri}

Watching his five sons grow up on the mountainside, [Pandu](pandu) felt the old strength come back into his arms. {#p1 1.125}

It was spring, the season that stirs every creature. Pandu wandered through the woods with [Madri](madri), where every tree was in new flower: palasha and tilaka, mango and champaka, ashoka and kesara, with bees humming among them and the koel singing from every branch, and pools full of lotuses. They were alone, and Madri was dressed in thin, light clothes. {#p2 1.125}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=pandu,madri,i5755_kindama light=pandu,i5755_kindama}

Desire rose in Pandu like a forest fire, and he could not master it. He forgot the curse. He took hold of Madri, and though she trembled with fear and resisted him with all her strength, he would not be held back. The epic says that Time itself, the great Destroyer, had taken his reason, and that he reached for her *as if he wished to put an end to his own life.* {#p3 1.125}

And in that moment, as the sage [Kindama](i5755_kindama) had foretold, Pandu died in his wife's arms. {#p4 1.125 1.118}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=madri,kunti,pandu light=madri,kunti}

Madri held her husband's body and cried out. [Kunti](kunti) heard her and came running with the children. *Come hither alone, O Kunti,* Madri called, *and let the children stay there.* {#p5 1.125}

Kunti saw them both lying there. She had watched over him so carefully, she said; how had he forgotten the curse? Why had Madri gone with him into the woods alone? And then, in her grief, something else: *thou art really to be envied, for thou hast seen the face of our lord suffused with gladness and joy.* {#p6 1.125}

> Revered sister, with tears in my eyes, I resisted the king, but he could not control himself, bent on, as it were making the Rishi's curse true. {#q1 1.125}
> — Madri

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kunti,madri,nakula,sahadeva light=kunti,madri}

Kunti said that as the elder wife she should follow him into death, and that Madri should raise the children. But Madri would not let him go. He had died seeking her, she said, and she would follow him. And she said something more. {#p7 1.125}

> O revered one, if I survive thee, it is certain I shall not be able to rear thy children as if they were mine. Will not sin touch me on that account? But, thou, O Kunti, shall be able to bring my sons up as if they were thine. {#q2 1.125}
> — Madri

She asked Kunti to take care of the children. She had no other wish. Then the daughter of the king of Madra climbed onto her husband's funeral pyre. {#p8 1.125}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.125,1.126}
**What happened next.** The epic says plainly that Madri went onto the pyre. Yet in the next section the sages carry *the unburnt portions* of both bodies to Hastinapura, and there they are cremated again with full rites. The text keeps both details side by side, and so do we.
::

So the curse came true, every word of it: Pandu died in the moment of love, and the wife who was with him followed him to the realm of the dead. Kunti, who had once been jealous of Madri, was left alone on the mountain with five boys, two of them not her own, and she would raise them all as her sons. {#p9 1.125 1.118}
