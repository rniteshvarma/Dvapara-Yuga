::scene{#s1 frame=drona,kripa,ashwatthama light=drona}

[Drona](drona) came to Hastinapura and told no one who he was. He lived quietly in the house of his wife's brother, [Kripa](kripa), and between Kripa's lessons his son [Ashwatthama](ashwatthama) taught the sons of Kunti a little of the use of arms. No one yet knew how good Ashwatthama was. {#p1 1.133}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,duryodhana,drona light=yudhishthira,duryodhana}

One day the princes went out of the city together to play with a ball, and the ball fell into a dry well. They tried everything to get it out, and nothing worked. They stood looking at one another, ashamed, not knowing what to do. {#p2 1.133}

Nearby stood a Brahmana, dark, old and lean, who had just finished his daily worship at the sacred fire. The princes gathered round him. He looked at them, knew his own skill, and smiled a little. {#p3 1.133}

> Shame on your Kshatriya might, and shame also on your skill in arms! You have been born in the race of Bharata! How is it that ye cannot recover the ball? {#q1 1.133}
> — the Brahmana

If they promised him a dinner, he said, he would bring up the ball with blades of grass, and this ring too. And he took off his ring and dropped it into the well. {#p4 1.133}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) told him he asked for a trifle: with Kripa's leave, they would give him enough to live on for life. {#p5 1.133}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drona,arjuna,yudhishthira light=drona}

The Brahmana charged a handful of long grass with mantras, so that each blade had the power of a weapon. He threw one blade and it pierced the ball. He threw another, and it pierced the first; then a third into the second, and so on, until he had a chain of grass, and drew the ball up by it. {#p6 1.133}

The princes stared, wide-eyed, and asked for the ring. He took a bow, shot an arrow into the well, and the arrow came up with the ring on it. He handed it to them, quite calmly. {#p7 1.133}

> We bow to thee, O Brahmana! None else owneth such skill. We long to know who thou art and whose son. What also can we do for thee? {#q2 1.133}
> — the princes

*Go to Bhishma,* he said, *and describe to him my likeness and skill. The mighty one will recognize me.* {#p8 1.133}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,drona light=bhishma,drona}

They ran to [Bhishma](bhishma) and told him everything, and Bhishma knew at once who it was. Here, he thought, was the teacher his grandsons needed. He went in person to welcome Drona, brought him to the palace, and asked him, carefully, why he had come. {#p9 1.133}

Drona told him. In his youth, he said, he had lived as a student at the hermitage of the sage Agnivesha, and the prince of the Panchalas, [Drupada](drupada), had lived there with him. They had studied together from boyhood. Drupada used to tell him that when his father made him king, the kingdom would be Drona's. {#p10 1.133}

> O friend, this, indeed, is my solemn promise. My dominion, wealth and happiness, shall all be dependent on thee. {#q3 1.133}
> — Drupada, as Drona remembered him

***

::scene{#s5 frame=drona,kripi,ashwatthama light=ashwatthama,drona}

Then Drona told Bhishma why he had gone to Drupada at last. {#p11 1.133}

One day his little son had seen rich men's sons drinking milk, and had cried for some. Drona wandered from country to country looking for a cow, and found none; he would not take one from a man who had only a few. When he came back empty-handed, Ashwatthama's playmates had given the boy water mixed with rice flour, and he was dancing for joy, crying: *I have taken milk, I have taken milk!* {#p12 1.133}

The other children smiled at him. People said: *Fie upon the indigent Drona, who strives not to earn wealth.* Drona was beside himself. He resolved that he would never be anyone's servant for money. Instead he would go to his old friend, who was now a king. {#p13 1.133}

He told Bhishma what Drupada had said to him, every word of it, and how it ended. {#p14 1.133}

> I do not remember ever having promised thee my kingdom. But, O Brahmana, I can now give thee food and shelter for one night. {#q5 1.133}
> — Drupada

He had left, Drona said, with his wife, *vowing to do that which I will certainly do soon enough.* And he had come to the Kurus looking for pupils. {#p15 1.133}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=bhishma,drona light=bhishma}

Bhishma's answer held nothing back. {#p16 1.133}

> String thy bow, O Brahmana, and make the Kuru princes accomplished in arms. {#q6 1.133}
> — Bhishma

The wealth of the Kurus was his, Bhishma said, and their kingdom; whatever he wished for he should think of as already done. The Kurus were lucky to have him. Bhishma gave him a house full of grain and every kind of wealth, and handed over his grandsons to him as pupils. {#p17 1.133 1.134}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=drona,arjuna,yudhishthira,bhima,duryodhana light=drona,arjuna}

One day Drona called the princes apart, had them touch his feet, and spoke with a full heart. {#p18 1.134}

> I have in my heart a particular purpose. Promise me truly, ye sinless ones, that when ye have become skilled in arms, ye will accomplish it. {#q7 1.134}
> — Drona

The princes said nothing. Only [Arjuna](arjuna) vowed he would do it, whatever it was. Drona held him close, smelt his head again and again, and wept for joy. {#p19 1.134}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.134,1.133}
**What the purpose was.** Drona does not name it here, and Arjuna does not ask. The reader has just heard Drona tell Bhishma about Drupada and his vow.
::

***

::scene{#s8 frame=drona,karna,arjuna,duryodhana,radha light=karna,arjuna}

Princes came from many lands to learn from Drona, the Vrishnis and the Andhakas among them. And so did [Karna](karna), the son of [Radha](radha), of the Suta caste. From the first he was jealous of Arjuna and defied him again and again, and with [Duryodhana](duryodhana) behind him, he made light of the Pandavas. {#p20 1.134}

Arjuna stayed by his teacher's side. The lessons were the same for everyone, but in lightness and skill he outstripped them all, and Drona came to believe that no pupil of his would ever equal him. {#p21 1.134}

***

::scene{#s9 frame=drona,arjuna,ashwatthama light=arjuna,ashwatthama}

Drona was a father too. He gave each pupil a narrow-mouthed jar to fetch water in, so that it would take a long while to fill. To his own son he gave a jar with a wide mouth, and in the time it saved he taught Ashwatthama things the others did not learn. {#p22 1.134}

Arjuna noticed. He began filling his narrow jar with the weapon of Varuna, the god of the waters, and came back as quickly as the teacher's son. So he lost nothing. {#p23 1.134}

Drona saw how hungry he was for the art. He told the cook in secret: *Never give Arjuna his food in the dark, nor tell him that I have told thee this.* {#p24 1.134}

But one night, while Arjuna was eating, the wind blew out the lamp, and he went on eating, his hand finding his mouth by habit. If the hand could find its way in the dark, he thought, so could an arrow. From then on he practised with his bow at night. Drona heard the twang of the bowstring in the dark, came out, and held him. {#p25 1.134}

> Truly do I tell thee that I shall do that unto thee by which there shall not be an archer equal to thee in this world. {#q8 1.134}
> — Drona
