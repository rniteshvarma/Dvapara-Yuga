::scene{#s1 frame=i7040_mandapala light=i7040_mandapala}

Why did the fire spare four small birds? The epic tells their story. {#p1 1.231}

There was a sage named [Mandapala](i7040_mandapala), who lived in celibacy and died full of merit. But in the world of the ancestors the regions he had earned were shut to him. Every man is born with debts, the gods told him, and the debt to the ancestors is paid only with children. He had none. {#p2 1.231}

So he thought about which creatures have the most young the soonest, and became a bird, a Sarngaka, and took a mate named [Jarita](i4820_jarita). She laid four eggs, and the sons inside them were sages who could recite the Vedas. Then, while they were still in the eggs, he left them in the Khandava forest and went off with another mate, [Lapita](i6453_lapita). Jarita stayed and raised them. {#p3 1.231}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=i7040_mandapala,i239_agni light=i7040_mandapala,i239_agni}

When Mandapala saw Agni coming to burn Khandava, he remembered his children and praised the god: *Thou art, O Agni, the mouth of all the worlds!* Agni, pleased, asked what he wanted. *Spare my children,* said Mandapala. *So be it,* said Agni. {#p4 1.231}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=i4820_jarita,i4821_jaritari,i9550_sarisrikka,i10028_stambamitra light=i4820_jarita}

The fire came, and the chicks, still without feathers, could not fly. Jarita wept. She could not carry them all, and she would not leave them. *I shall even cover you with my wings and die with you.* {#p5 1.232}

The chicks told her to go. If they died, she could have other children; if she died, their line ended. She found a mouse-hole for them to hide in, and they refused: the mouse would eat them, and a death by fire was better than being eaten. A hawk had carried off the mouse, she said; she had seen it. They did not believe her. {#p6 1.232 1.233}

> Thou art young and handsome, and capable of seeking out thy husband. Go unto thy husband. Thou shalt obtain good children again. {#q1 1.233}
> — the young birds

So she flew to safety. And the fire came on. {#p7 1.233}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=i4821_jaritari,i9550_sarisrikka,i10028_stambamitra,i239_agni light=i239_agni}

Then the four brothers, [Jaritari](i4821_jaritari), [Sarisrikka](i9550_sarisrikka), [Stambamitra](i10028_stambamitra) and the youngest, Drona, each sang a hymn to Agni. *Our mother is not to be seen, and we know not our father,* one said. *We have none to protect us save thee.* {#p8 1.234}

Agni was pleased, especially with Drona's hymn, and remembered his promise to their father. {#p9 1.234}

> Thou art a Rishi, O Drona! For what thou hast said is Brahma (Vedic truth). I shall do your pleasure. Fear not! {#q2 1.234}
> — Agni

Drona's one request was that Agni burn the cats that troubled them every day. And Agni passed them by. {#p10 1.234}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.234}
**Another Drona.** The youngest chick shares his name with the great teacher, but they are not the same: this Drona is a bird, a sage born as a Sarngaka in the Khandava forest.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=i7040_mandapala,i6453_lapita,i4820_jarita light=i4820_jarita}

Far away, Mandapala worried. Lapita, jealous, told him to go back to Jarita if he cared so much. {#p11 1.235}

When the fire had passed, Jarita hurried back and found all four alive, and they wept, and she wept, and held them one by one. Then Mandapala arrived, and none of them would speak to him. *Go now unto that Lapita of sweet smiles,* said Jarita. {#p12 1.235}

He told them he had asked Agni to spare them, and had trusted the god's word and their mother's goodness. In the end his sons came forward and honoured him, and he took Jarita and the children and went away to another country. {#p13 1.235 1.236}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=indra,krishna,arjuna,i239_agni,i7257_maya light=krishna,arjuna}

So Agni, with the help of Krishna and Arjuna, burned the forest of Khandava, and drank rivers of fat, and was well again. {#p14 1.236}

Then [Indra](indra) came down from the sky with the Maruts. *Ye have achieved a feat that a celestial even could not,* he said. *Ask ye each a boon.* {#p15 1.236}

Arjuna asked for all of Indra's weapons. Indra said he would give them when the time came: when Madhava was pleased with Arjuna, and for Arjuna's hard penance. Krishna asked that his friendship with Arjuna should last for ever, and Indra granted it. {#p16 1.236}

::aside{#a3 kind=versions src=1.236}
**Madhava or Mahadeva?** Ganguli's text has Indra name *Madhava*, Krishna, here. Other readings have *Mahadeva*, Shiva; and it is in the forest years, after Arjuna has won Shiva's favour by penance, that Indra at last gives him his weapons.
::

Agni, satisfied at last after fifteen days, thanked them and told them they could go wherever they wished. And Arjuna, Krishna and Maya the Asura, the three of them, walked a little way and sat down together on the pleasant bank of a river. {#p17 1.236}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.236}
**The end of the beginning.** Here the first book of the Mahabharata, the Adi Parva, ends: with two friends and a rescued Asura sitting by a river. The next book, the Sabha Parva, opens on that same riverbank, with Maya asking how he can repay his debt.
::
