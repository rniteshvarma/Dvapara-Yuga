[Karna](karna) looked round at the others and saw them anxious, unwilling to fight. Whoever was coming, Virata or Arjuna, he would hold him back *as the banks resist the swelling sea*. His arrows would cover Partha like locusts on a tree. Arjuna had spent thirteen years as an ascetic, he said; he would strike mildly now, and be fit only to receive Karna's shafts. He had his weapons from Jamadagni's son. He would bring down the ape from the banner-top with his javelin. {#p1 4.48}

> Slaying Arjuna in battle, I will discharge today that debt, difficult of repayments, but promised of old by me unto Dhritarashtra's son. {#q1 4.48}
> — Karna

Let the Kauravas take the cattle and go, he said, or stay on their chariots and watch him fight. {#p2 4.48}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kripa,karna light=kripa}

> O Radheya, thy crooked heart always inclineth to war. {#q2 4.49}
> — Kripa

Of all the ways the scriptures know, said [Kripa](kripa), war was the most sinful, and it succeeded only at the right time and place. This was not it. Then he listed what Arjuna had done alone: saved the Kurus from the Gandharvas, satisfied Agni, carried off Subhadra and challenged Krishna, fought Rudra in the guise of a forester, rescued Draupadi from Jayadratha in this very forest, studied weapons five years under Indra, overthrown the Nivatakavachas. {#p3 4.49}

> What, however, O Karna, hath been achieved by thee single-handed like any of the sons of Pandu, each of whom had alone subjugated many lords of earth? {#q3 4.49}
> — Kripa

To fight him alone was to pull the fangs of an angry snake with one finger, or to swim the ocean with a stone tied to one's neck. They had come, without knowing it, to where Partha lay *like fire hidden in a well*. Fight they must, but all six great warriors together, not Karna alone. {#p4 4.49}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=ashwatthama,karna,duryodhana,shakuni light=ashwatthama}

[Ashwatthama](ashwatthama) was harsher. The cattle had not yet crossed the border, let alone reached Hastinapura. Why boast? {#p5 4.50}

> Fire burneth mutely and mutely doth the sun shine. {#q4 4.50}
> — Ashwatthama

The Pandavas had won the earth by the scriptures' rules; [Duryodhana](duryodhana) had won his kingdom at dice, like a meat-seller cheating. In what single combat had Karna beaten Arjuna, or Bhima, or the twins? What he had done was drag a princess into court in a single garment. {#p6 4.50}

> The Gandiva, however, doth not cast dice such as the Krita or the Dwapara, but it shooteth upon foes blazing and keen-edged shafts by myriads. {#q5 4.50}
> — Ashwatthama

Let [Shakuni](shakuni), that clever gambler, fight now, he said. As for himself, he would not fight Dhananjaya. He would fight the king of the Matsyas, if he came after his cattle. {#p7 4.50}
