The king of the Kalingas, Shrutayush, came at [Bhima](bhima) with thousands of chariots, and Ketumat of the Nishadas with ten thousand elephants. Bhima rushed in with the Chedis, the Matsyas and the Karushas, and the field became like a cremation ground; men could not tell friend from foe and cut down their own. Then the Chedis turned back, and Bhima, relying on his own arms, did not. {#p1 6.54}

The Kalinga prince Shakradeva killed his horses; from his stranded chariot Bhima hurled an iron mace and killed Shakradeva with his charioteer and banner. When the king surrounded him with thousands of chariots, Bhima took up a sword and a shield bright with golden crescents, and cut down in the air the king's poisonous arrow and his fourteen darts. {#p2 6.54}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima light=bhima}

Then he saw the Kalinga prince Bhanumat on his elephant, and roared so that the Kalinga army shook. {#p3 6.54}

> In that battle they no longer regarded Bhima, O bull among men, as a human being. {#q1 6.54}
> — Sanjaya

He leapt up by the tusks onto the elephant's back, cut Bhanumat in two at the waist, and with the same sword cut off the elephant's head; it fell like a mountain whose base the sea has eaten. Then on foot he went through the elephants and horses and chariots like a wheel of fire, leaping, whirling, running, cutting off heads and trunks, dragging men down from their chariots, killing some with his feet and some with his thighs as he ran. {#p4 6.54}

His charioteer Ashoka brought him a fresh chariot, and Shrutayush pierced his chest with nine arrows; Bhima, *like a snake struck with a rod*, killed him with seven iron shafts, and his wheel-guards, and Satyadeva and Satya, and Ketumat. Then he took his mace again and sent seven hundred, and two thousand more, to Yama's abode. {#p5 6.54}

> This is Death himself that is fighting in Bhima's shape with the Kalingas. {#q2 6.54}
> — the troops

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,dhrishtadyumna,satyaki,bhishma light=dhrishtadyumna}

[Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna), to whom no one was dearer than Bhima and Satyaki, came to guard his wing, and [Satyaki](satyaki) the other. Hearing the cries, [Bhishma](bhishma) came; the three of them struck him, and he killed Bhima's horses. Bhima hurled a dart, which Bhishma cut in two, and leapt down with a mace; Dhrishtadyumna took him up on his own chariot. Satyaki shot down Bhishma's charioteer, and Bhishma's horses carried him off the field like the wind. {#p6 6.54}

Then no one on the Kuru side would face Bhima. He embraced Dhrishtadyumna, and Satyaki embraced him: by his arms alone the whole Kalinga division had been crushed, its king and its princes killed. {#p7 6.54}
