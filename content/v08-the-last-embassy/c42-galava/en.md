[Narada](narada) spoke last. *Persons that listen to the counsels of friends are rare.* Obstinacy, he said, leads to ruin, and he told the story of Galava. {#p1 5.106}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vishvamitra light=vishvamitra}

Dharma once came to [Vishvamitra](vishvamitra) disguised as Vasishtha, hungry. By the time Vishvamitra had cooked the rice, his guest had eaten elsewhere and left, telling him to wait. So Vishvamitra stood a hundred years with the bowl on his head, living on air, while his pupil Galava served him. When Dharma came back and ate, Vishvamitra became a Brahmana, and set Galava free. {#p2 5.106}

But Galava insisted on paying a teacher's fee. Again and again Vishvamitra said *Go*; again and again Galava asked what he should give. At last, a little angry, Vishvamitra named it. {#p3 5.106}

> Give me eight hundred steeds, every one of which should be as white as the rays of the moon, and every one of which should have one ear black. {#q1 5.106}
> — Vishvamitra

***

::scene{#s3 frame=garuda,yayati light=yayati}

Galava despaired. His friend [Garuda](garuda) carried him across the world, and at last to King [Yayati](yayati), who had no such horses but gave him his daughter Madhavi: kings would pay that price as her bride-price. Three kings, Haryashva of Ayodhya, Divodasa of Kashi and Ushinara of the Bhojas, each had only two hundred such horses; each had a son by Madhavi and gave her back, for she had a boon to become a maiden again after each birth. {#p4 5.113 5.116 5.117 5.118}

There were no more such horses in the world, Garuda told him: the rest had been lost in the river Vitasta. So Galava brought Vishvamitra six hundred horses and Madhavi for the last two hundred, and Vishvamitra had a son by her, Ashtaka. Then Madhavi, at her own svayamvara, chose the forest, and lived there like a deer. {#p5 5.119 5.120}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yayati,narada,duryodhana light=yayati}

Yayati, in heaven, fell through vanity, and his daughter's four sons and Madhavi saved him with their merit. {#p6 5.121 5.122}

> There is not a greater sinner than he who is consumed by the fire of vanity. {#q2 5.123}
> — Brahma, to Yayati

> O monarch, even such was the distress into which Yayati fell in consequence of vanity, and such was the distress into which Galava fell owing to his obstinacy. {#q3 5.123}
> — Narada

Obstinacy is always the root of ruin, said Narada. Forsake vanity and wrath, son of Gandhari; make peace with the sons of Pandu. {#p7 5.123}
