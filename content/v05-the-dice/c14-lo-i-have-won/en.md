::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,bhishma,drona,kripa,vidura,shakuni,yudhishthira light=shakuni,yudhishthira}

The kings took their seats, with [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) at their head. [Bhishma](bhishma), [Drona](drona), [Kripa](kripa) and [Vidura](vidura) sat behind them, with cheerless hearts. {#p1 2.59}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) staked a string of pearls, churned from the ocean long ago and set in pure gold. Shakuni took up the dice and threw. {#p2 2.59}

> Lo, I have won! {#q1 2.59}
> — Shakuni

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,shakuni light=yudhishthira}

*You have won it by unfair means*, said Yudhishthira, *but don't be proud.* And he staked again: jars each holding a thousand gold coins. His royal chariot, with its eight moon-white horses. A hundred thousand serving-girls skilled in the sixty-four arts, and thousands of serving-men. A thousand war-elephants. As many chariots, with their warriors. The Gandharva horses Chitraratha had given Arjuna. Ten thousand carts, and sixty thousand broad-chested soldiers. Four hundred chests of gold. {#p3 2.60}

Each time, Shakuni, *adopting unfair means*, threw the dice and said the same words. {#p4 2.60}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=2.60}
**Shakuni's dice.** Ganguli says only that he played by unfair means, over and over. In popular retellings the dice are made from his father's bones and obey him because of his family's grudge against the Kurus; that story does not appear in the epic.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=vidura,dhritarashtra,duryodhana light=vidura}

Vidura could not keep silent. He spoke to Dhritarashtra. {#p5 2.61}

> A jackal is living in thy house, O king, in the form of Duryodhana. {#q2 2.61}
> — Vidura

When Duryodhana was born he had howled like a jackal, Vidura said, and everyone had known what it meant. Like the man gathering honey on a cliff, he sees the honey and not the drop. The Yadavas had cast out Kamsa for the good of their people; let Arjuna kill this one. {#p6 2.61}

> In exchange of a crow, O great king, buy these peacocks—the Pandavas; and in exchange of a jackal, buy these tigers. {#q3 2.61}
> — Vidura

Do not be the king who killed the birds that spat gold, he said. Pluck flowers from the Pandavas day after day, as a gardener does; do not burn them to the root. Send Shakuni home. {#p7 2.61 2.62}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=duryodhana,vidura light=duryodhana}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) turned on him. {#p8 2.63}

> Thou hast been cherished by us like a serpent on our lap. {#q4 2.63}
> — Duryodhana

Vidura always praised their enemies, he said; everyone knew whom he really loved. *Go whither-so-ever thou pleasest.* Vidura answered that a man who wants to hear only pleasant words should ask women and idiots. Then he bowed to the king and fell silent. {#p9 2.63}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira,shakuni,nakula,sahadeva,arjuna,bhima light=yudhishthira}

*What have you left?* asked Shakuni. {#p10 2.64}

Yudhishthira staked his countless wealth, and lost. His cattle and herds from the Parnasa to the Sindhu, and lost. His city, his country, and all his people except the Brahmanas, and lost. The princes' ornaments, and lost. {#p11 2.64}

Then he staked [Nakula](nakula), and lost him. Then [Sahadeva](sahadeva), and lost him. Shakuni needled him: the sons of Madri were gone, but perhaps Bhima and Arjuna were dearer to him? *Wretch*, said Yudhishthira, *you are trying to divide us.* And he staked [Arjuna](arjuna), and lost him; and [Bhima](bhima), and lost him. {#p12 2.64}

> I alone, the eldest of all my brothers and dear unto them, am still unwon. Won by thee, I will do what he that is won will have to do. {#q5 2.64}
> — Yudhishthira

Shakuni threw, and won. *Thou hast permitted thyself to be won. This is very sinful*, he said. Then: {#p13 2.64}

> Stake thou Krishna, the princess of Panchala. By her, win thyself back. {#q6 2.64}
> — Shakuni

***

::scene{#s6 frame=yudhishthira,draupadi,vidura,dhritarashtra,karna,duhshasana light=draupadi}

And Yudhishthira staked [Draupadi](draupadi). He praised her as he did it: neither short nor tall, her eyes like autumn lotuses, the last to sleep and the first to wake, who looked after everyone down to the cowherds. {#p14 2.64}

*Fie! Fie!* cried the old men in the hall. Bhishma, Drona and Kripa broke out in sweat. Vidura sat with his head in his hands, sighing like a snake. But Dhritarashtra kept asking, delighted, *Hath the stake been won? Hath the stake been won?* [Karna](karna) and [Duhshasana](duhshasana) laughed aloud; everyone else wept. {#p15 2.64}

And Shakuni said, *Lo, I have won*, and took up the dice. {#p16 2.64}
