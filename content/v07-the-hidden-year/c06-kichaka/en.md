Ten months passed. [Draupadi](draupadi), who deserved to be served, served [Sudeshna](sudeshna) and kept the women of the palace pleased. Then, as the year was nearing its end, [Kichaka](kichaka) saw her: the commander of Virata's army, and the queen's brother. He went straight to his sister. {#p1 4.14}

> This damsel maddens me with her beauty, even as a new wine maddens one with its fragrance. {#q1 4.14}
> — Kichaka

Then he went to Draupadi himself, *like a jackal in the forest accosting a lioness*. He praised her face, her eyes, her voice, her body, part by part. He would give up all his old wives and make them her slaves, and be her slave himself. {#p2 4.14}

She answered that she was a servant of low birth, and the wife of others, and that good men take delight only in their own wives. Kichaka would not hear it. He was the real lord of the kingdom, he said. {#p3 4.14}

> It is me depending upon whom the people of this realm live. {#q2 4.14}
> — Kichaka

> Why dost thou desire me, even like an infant lying on its mother's lap wishing to catch the moon? {#q3 4.14}
> — Draupadi

Five Gandharvas were her husbands, she told him. Even if he went into the earth or up into the sky, he would not escape them. {#p4 4.14}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=sudeshna,kichaka,draupadi light=sudeshna}

Refused, Kichaka went back to Sudeshna, crying that he was dying of desire. The queen was moved to pity. She thought it over, and gave him a plan: on some festival day he should lay in wine and food, and she would send her Sairindhri to fetch wine; alone with her, he could win her over. {#p5 4.15}

He had the best wines filtered and his cooks prepare meats and dishes, and when all was ready Sudeshna called her maid. {#p6 4.15}

> Get up, O Sairindhri and repair to Kichaka's abode to bring wine, for, O beauteous lady, I am afflicted with thirst. {#q4 4.15}
> — Sudeshna

Draupadi begged her to send someone else. The queen knew how shameless he was; she remembered the conditions on which Draupadi had come. Sudeshna said only that he would not harm someone sent from her house, and handed her a golden vessel with a lid. {#p7 4.15}

Weeping, Draupadi went. She prayed that, as she had never known any man but her husbands, Kichaka should not overpower her. She worshipped the Sun for a moment, and [Surya](surya) ordered a Rakshasa to guard her, unseen, from then on. {#p8 4.15}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=kichaka,draupadi light=draupadi}

Kichaka rose to meet her *like a person wishing to cross to the other shore, when he obtains a boat*. He offered her gold and gems and silks, and his bed, and wine. She said she had come for the queen's wine. Others would carry that, he said, and caught her by the right arm. {#p9 4.16}

> As I have never, from intoxication of the senses, been unfaithful to my husbands even at heart, by that Truth, O wretch, I shall behold thee dragged and lying powerless on the ground. {#q5 4.16}
> — Draupadi

She turned to run, and he caught the end of her upper garment. Trembling with rage, she threw him to the ground, and he fell *like a tree whose roots had been cut*. She ran for the court, where Yudhishthira was. Kichaka ran after her, caught her by the hair, threw her down, and kicked her in front of the king. Then the Rakshasa struck him with the force of the wind, and he fell senseless. {#p10 4.16}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhima,yudhishthira,virata,draupadi light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) and [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) were sitting there. Bhima ground his teeth; his forehead ran with sweat; he pressed it with his hands and was about to rise. Yudhishthira, afraid they would be discovered, pressed his thumbs to stop him, and spoke to him as to a cook: {#p11 4.16}

> Lookest thou, O cook, for trees for fuel. If thou art in need of faggots, then go out and fell trees. {#q6 4.16}
> — Yudhishthira

Draupadi stood weeping at the door of the court, and her husbands could not move. She spoke to [Virata](virata). {#p12 4.16}

> Alas, the son of a Suta hath kicked today the proud and beloved wife of those whose foe can never sleep in peace even if four kingdoms intervene between him and them. {#q7 4.16}
> — Draupadi

Where were they, those warriors? Why did they sit, like eunuchs, and watch? And the king was acting like a robber, not a king, and the courtiers who served him were no better. Virata said he could not judge a quarrel he had not seen. But the courtiers, once they learned what had happened, cried *Well done!* and condemned Kichaka. {#p13 4.16}

Yudhishthira's forehead was wet with anger, but he told her to go back to Sudeshna's rooms. Her Gandharvas would act when the time was right. {#p14 4.16}

> O Sairindhri, thou art ignorant of the timeliness of things, and it is for this that thou weepest as an actress, besides interrupting the play of dice in Matsya's court. {#q8 4.16}
> — Yudhishthira

> They of whom I am the wedded wife are, I ween, extremely kind. And as the eldest of them all is addicted to dice, they are liable to be oppressed by all. {#q9 4.16}
> — Draupadi

***

::scene{#s5 frame=sudeshna,draupadi light=draupadi}

She ran to Sudeshna with her hair loose and her eyes red. Kichaka had struck her in the court before the king, she said, *as if in the midst of a solitary wood*. The queen offered to have him killed, if Draupadi wished. {#p15 4.16}

> Even others will slay him,—even they whom he hath wronged, I think it is clear that he will have to go to the abode of Yama this very day! {#q10 4.16}
> — Draupadi
