::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=yudhishthira}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) wept over them: Bhima, who had sworn to break Duryodhana's thigh; Arjuna, of whom the gods had said at his birth that none would ever beat him. There were no wounds on them, no footprints, and their faces had not lost their colour; it was not poison. Who but Yama could have done this? {#p1 3.311}

He went down to the water, and the voice spoke again. {#p2 3.311}

> I am a crane, living on tiny fish. It is by me that thy younger brothers have been brought under the sway of the lord of departed spirits. {#q1 3.311}
> — the Yaksha

*If thou … answer not the questions put by me, even thou shalt number the fifth corpse.* No bird could have done this, said Yudhishthira; who are you? *I am … a Yaksha*, said the voice, and Yudhishthira saw him perched on a tree, tall as a palm and bright as fire, roaring like a cloud. {#p3 3.311}

> I do not, O Yaksha, covet, what is already in thy possession! {#q2 3.311}
> — Yudhishthira

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

So the Yaksha asked, and Yudhishthira answered. What makes the sun rise, and in whom is he established? *Brahma maketh the Sun rise … and he is established in truth.* What is the divinity of Kshatriyas, and what their impiety? *Arrows and weapons are their divinity … and refusal of protection is that act of theirs which is like that of the impious.* {#p4 3.311}

> What is weightier than the earth itself? What is higher than the heavens? What is fleeter than the wind? And what is more numerous than grass? {#q3 3.311}
> — the Yaksha

> The mother is weightier than the earth; the father is higher than the heaven; the mind is fleeter than the wind; and our thoughts are more numerous than grass. {#q4 3.311}
> — Yudhishthira

On and on: what, renounced, makes one agreeable? Pride. What, renounced, leaves no regret? Wrath. What makes one wealthy? Giving up desire. Happy? Giving up avarice. {#p5 3.311}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

And then the last four. {#p6 3.311}

> Who is truly happy? What is most wonderful? What is the path? And what is the news? {#q5 3.311}
> — the Yaksha

The happy man, said Yudhishthira, is he who cooks a few vegetables in his own house at the end of the day, owes nothing, and need not leave home. {#p7 3.311}

> Day after day countless creatures are going to the abode of Yama, yet those that remain behind believe themselves to be immortal. What can be more wonderful than this? {#q6 3.311}
> — Yudhishthira

Argument settles nothing, the scriptures differ, there is no sage whose word all accept, and the truth about dharma is hidden in a cave: *that alone is the path along which the great have trod.* And the news? This world is a pan, the sun is its fire, days and nights its fuel, months and seasons its ladle, *Time is the cook that is cooking all creatures in that pan … this is the news.* {#p8 3.311}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,nakula,bhima,arjuna,kunti light=yudhishthira}

The Yaksha was satisfied. One of your brothers may live, he said. Choose. {#p9 3.311}

Yudhishthira chose Nakula, *darkish of hue … tall like a large Sala tree*. {#p10 3.311}

Why Nakula? asked the Yaksha. Bhima is dear to you, and all of you depend on Arjuna. Why a stepbrother? {#p11 3.311}

> My father had two wives, Kunti and Madri. Let both of them have children. This is what I wish. As Kunti is to me, so also is Madri. {#q7 3.311}
> — Yudhishthira

Abstention from harm, he said, was higher than any gain. {#p12 3.311}

> Since abstention from injury is regarded by thee as higher than both profit and pleasure, therefore, let all thy brothers live, O bull of Bharata race! {#q8 3.311}
> — the Yaksha

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.311}
**The Yakshaprashna.** The Yaksha asks Yudhishthira well over a hundred questions in this one section, on the gods, the castes, virtue, the body and death. This chapter keeps only a few. Some answers are riddles whose point has been argued over for centuries, and the dialogue is still recited on its own.
::
