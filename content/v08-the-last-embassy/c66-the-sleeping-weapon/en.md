One night [Bhishma](bhishma) lay alone, covered in arrow wounds, and thought: he could not beat this Brahmana. If he could, let the gods show themselves to him tonight. Towards morning the eight Brahmanas who had held him up came to him in a dream. {#p1 5.186}

> Rise, O Ganga's son, thou needst have no fear! We will protect thee, for thou art our own body! {#q1 5.186}
> — the eight Brahmanas

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.186}
The text does not name the eight. *Thou art our own body* and their number point to the eight Vasus, Bhishma's own brothers in his former life, whose story opens Volume I.
::

A weapon called Prasvapa, the *sleeper*, made by the divine artificer, would come to him, they said; he had known it in a former life, and no one on earth, not Parashurama, knew it. It would not kill Parashurama, only put him to sleep, so Bhishma would incur no sin; then he could wake him with the weapon Samvodhana. {#p2 5.186}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,parashurama light=parashurama}

The next day was worse than any before. Parashurama's dart struck Bhishma's shoulder and the blood ran like red earth down a mountain after rain; Bhishma's arrow struck Parashurama's forehead; Parashurama's arrow struck Bhishma's chest and he fell; Bhishma's dart struck Parashurama's breast and he trembled until Akritavrana held him. Then Parashurama called up the Brahma weapon, and Bhishma met it with the same. The two weapons blazed in mid-air like the end of an age; the sky caught fire, the earth shook, and gods, Asuras and Rakshasas cried out. {#p3 5.187}

This was the moment, Bhishma thought, and the mantras for Prasvapa came into his mind. {#p4 5.187}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=narada,bhishma,parashurama light=narada}

A great noise rose in the sky: *Do not let it go!* Bhishma aimed it anyway. Then [Narada](narada) spoke: the gods themselves forbade it; Parashurama was an ascetic and his teacher; do not shame him. The eight Brahmanas, smiling in the sky, said the same. Bhishma withdrew Prasvapa. {#p5 5.188}

> Wretch that I am, I am vanquished, O Bhishma! {#q2 5.188}
> — Parashurama

Then Parashurama's father and his forefathers stood around him. Never fight a Kshatriya again, they said; study and vows are a Brahmana's wealth; let this be the last time he took up the bow. {#p6 5.188}

> Bhishma is one of the foremost of Vasus! {#q3 5.188}
> — Parashurama's ancestors

> That foremost of the Pandavas, Arjuna, the mighty son of Indra, hath been ordained by the Self-create to be the slayer of Bhishma! {#q4 5.188}
> — Parashurama's ancestors

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,parashurama,ganga light=bhishma}

Parashurama would not stop: he had never left a field. Let them stop Bhishma. So Narada and the sages came to Bhishma, and he would not stop either: he had vowed never to turn his back in battle. At last the sages and Ganga stood between them, and told Parashurama *the hearts of Brahmanas are made of butter*; neither could kill the other. The ancestors made him lay down his weapons. Then Bhishma, mangled as he was, went and worshipped him, and Parashurama smiled with love. {#p7 5.188}

> There is no Kshatriya equal to thee on the earth! {#q5 5.188}
> — Parashurama
