::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=draupadi}

The Pandavas lived by their rule, and brought many kings under their sway. [Draupadi](draupadi) was devoted to all five of them, and they to her; the epic compares her to the river Sarasvati, delighting in the elephants that delight in her. The whole Kuru race prospered. {#p1 1.215}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

Then one day robbers drove off a Brahmana's cattle, and the Brahmana came to Indraprastha beside himself with anger, crying out at the palace gate. {#p2 1.215}

> A king that taketh the sixth part of the produce of the land without protecting the subject, hath been called by the wise to be the most sinful person in the whole world. {#q1 1.215}
> — the Brahmana

[Arjuna](arjuna) heard him and called out *No fear!* But the weapons were kept in a room where, at that moment, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) was sitting with Draupadi. {#p3 1.215}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,yudhishthira light=arjuna}

Arjuna stood and thought it through. If he did not help, the king would carry the sin of an unprotected subject, and their name would be shamed through the kingdom. If he went in, he broke the rule, and must go into exile. {#p4 1.215}

> Virtue is superior to the body and lasteth after the body hath perished! {#q2 1.215}
> — Arjuna

He went in, spoke to Yudhishthira, came out with his bow, and went after the robbers in his chariot with the Brahmana. He shot them down, made them give up the cattle, gave the Brahmana back his cows, and came home to everyone's praise. {#p5 1.215}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna,yudhishthira light=arjuna,yudhishthira}

Then he went to Yudhishthira and asked his leave to keep the vow. He had seen the king sitting with Draupadi; he must go to the forest. {#p6 1.215}

Yudhishthira was stricken. *Why!* he said. He knew why Arjuna had come in, he said, and he was not displeased. And by custom it was no fault for a younger brother to enter where an elder sat with his wife; the fault would only be the other way round. Arjuna's virtue was untouched. He should stay. {#p7 1.215}

> I have heard, even from thee, that quibbling is not permitted in the discharge of duty. I cannot waver from truth. Truth is my weapon. {#q3 1.215}
> — Arjuna

So, with the king's leave, Arjuna went to live in the forest for twelve years. {#p8 1.215}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.215,1.214}
**Arjuna holds the line.** The rule the brothers made for Narada was meant to keep the peace. Yudhishthira offers him a way out on a technicality, and Arjuna turns it down, using his brother's own teaching against him.
::
