Amaravati was full of flowers of every season and the Nandana gardens where the Apsaras played. No one could see it who had not done austerities or offered sacrifice; it was not for those who had turned their backs in battle, or drunk liquor, or eaten unclean meat. Thousands of chariots moved through its streets, and the gods and Gandharvas came out to bless [Arjuna](arjuna) to the sound of conches and drums. {#p1 3.43}

On the starry road called Suravithi he met the gods one by one, the Adityas, Vasus, Rudras and Maruts, the twin Ashvins, the old royal sages led by Dilipa, Narada and Tumburu. Last of all he saw [Indra](indra) under a white umbrella, and bowed his head to the ground. {#p2 3.43}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=indra,arjuna light=indra,arjuna}

Indra embraced him, sat him on half of his own throne, smelt his head, and took him on his lap. He stroked Arjuna's arms, hard from the bowstring, and could not stop looking at him; *the more he gazed, the more he liked to gaze on*. Father and son on one seat lit up the hall like the sun and moon together in the sky. {#p3 3.43}

Tumburu and the Gandharvas sang, and the Apsaras danced, Ghritachi, Menaka, Rambha, Urvashi and many more, the ones whose work it was to break the vows of ascetics. {#p4 3.43}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,indra light=arjuna}

So Arjuna lived in his father's palace and learned the weapons of heaven, and how to call them back. Indra gave him his own thunderbolt, and the lightnings. Then Arjuna remembered his brothers. But at Indra's command he stayed five full years in heaven. {#p5 3.44}

When he had all the weapons, Indra told him to learn music and dance from the Gandharva Chitrasena, the arts of heaven that are not found among men. *It will be to thy benefit.* So Chitrasena became his friend and taught him song, instruments and dance. {#p6 3.44}

> But the active Arjuna obtained no peace of mind, remembering the unfair play at dice of Sakuni, the son of Suvala, and thinking with rage of Dussasana and his death. {#q1 3.44}
> — the narrator

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.44}
**Why a warrior learns to dance.** Indra does not say why Arjuna needs the dance of the Gandharvas. The reader will find out in the thirteenth year of the exile.
::
