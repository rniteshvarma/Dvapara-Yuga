[Uttara](uttar) put the silver horses through every pace, wheeling and circling and turning left until the Kurus were bewildered, and brought the chariot face to face with [Kripa](kripa). [Arjuna](arjuna) called out his name and blew Devadatta, and the Kurus marvelled that the conch did not break into a hundred pieces. Kripa, unable to bear it, blew his own sea-born conch and twanged his bow. {#p1 4.57}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kripa,arjuna light=kripa}

Kripa pierced Arjuna with ten arrows. Arjuna shot back iron arrows, and Kripa cut them to pieces in the air. Arjuna covered the sky; Kripa answered with ten thousand shafts and a roar. Then Arjuna struck Kripa's four horses, and they reared, and Kripa was thrown from his place. {#p2 4.57}

> And seeing Gautama thrown off his place, the slayer of hostile heroes, the descendant of the Kuru race, out of regard for his opponent's dignity, ceased to discharge his shafts at him. {#q1 4.57}

Kripa regained his place and struck again. Arjuna cut his bow and his finger-guards, and his coat of mail, without wounding him, so that he stood *like a serpent which hath in season cast off its slough*. Every bow Kripa took up, Arjuna cut. Kripa hurled a javelin like a thunderbolt, and Arjuna cut it with ten arrows. {#p3 4.57}

Then thirteen arrows: one for the yoke, four for the horses, one for the driver's head, three for the chariot's triple pole, two for the wheels, one for the flagstaff, and the thirteenth, *as if smiling in derision*, into Kripa's chest. Kripa leapt down and hurled a mace, and Arjuna's arrows sent it back along its path. {#p4 4.57}

Kripa's warriors rushed in from every side to save him. Uttara wheeled the chariot left in the circling movement called Yamaka and held them off, and they carried Kripa away. {#p5 4.57}
