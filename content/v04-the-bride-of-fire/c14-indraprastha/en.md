::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura light=dhritarashtra}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) gave in. Bhishma, Drona and Vidura were right, he said; the sons of Pandu were his sons too, by law, and as much entitled to the kingdom as his own. {#p1 1.208}

> From sheer good fortune the sons of Pritha are alive; and from good fortune alone those mighty car-warriors have obtained the daughter of Drupada. {#q1 1.208}
> — Dhritarashtra

He sent [Vidura](vidura) to bring them home, with Kunti and Draupadi. {#p2 1.208}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vidura,drupada,krishna,yudhishthira light=vidura,drupada}

Vidura came to Drupada's court with jewels and wealth for everyone, embraced the Pandavas, and greeted [Krishna](krishna), who was there with them. He told [Drupada](drupada) how glad Dhritarashtra and Bhishma and Drona were of the alliance, and asked him to let the Pandavas come home; the whole city was longing to see them, and to see the princess of Panchala. {#p3 1.208}

Drupada said it was not for him to say. If [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) and his brothers wished to go, and if Krishna and Balarama agreed, let them go. Yudhishthira said they would do whatever Drupada wished. Krishna said he thought they should go, but that Drupada should decide. And Drupada said that Krishna cared for the Pandavas more than Yudhishthira himself did, and agreed. {#p4 1.209}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,kunti,draupadi,vikarna,drona,kripa light=yudhishthira,draupadi}

So they set out for the city named after the elephant, taking their time, with Krishna and Vidura, Kunti and Draupadi. Dhritarashtra sent [Vikarna](vikarna), Chitrasena, [Drona](drona) and [Kripa](kripa) out to meet them. {#p5 1.209}

The whole city came out to look. *Here returns that tiger among men who always protects us as if we were his nearest relatives,* people said; and *it seems that king Pandu returneth today from the forest.* {#p6 1.209}

They bowed at the feet of Dhritarashtra and Bhishma and the elders, and rested in the rooms given to them. {#p7 1.209}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=dhritarashtra,bhishma,yudhishthira light=dhritarashtra,yudhishthira}

Then Dhritarashtra and Bhishma called them to court. {#p8 1.209}

> Repair ye to Khandavaprastha so that no difference may arise again (between you and your cousins). {#q2 1.209}
> — Dhritarashtra

They should take half the kingdom and live there, protected by Arjuna, where no one could harm them. The Pandavas agreed, bowed to the king, and went, content with half a kingdom that was, the epic says, *unreclaimed desert*. {#p9 1.209}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.209}
**Half of what?** Bhishma had urged the king to give them half the land. What they were given was Khandavaprastha, wild country beside a forest, while Hastinapura stayed with Duryodhana. The Pandavas did not argue.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,krishna,vyasa light=yudhishthira,krishna}

With Krishna at their head, they made the wild place beautiful. With [Vyasa](vyasa)'s help they chose an auspicious site, performed the rites and measured out a city. {#p10 1.209}

They surrounded it with a moat as wide as the sea and walls as white as clouds or moonlight, reaching to the sky. The gates had doors like Garuda's outspread wings; the battlements bristled with weapons and war-machines and armed men. The streets were wide and safe. It looked like Amaravati, the city of Indra, and so it was called Indraprastha. {#p11 1.209}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.209}
**Who built it.** The epic says the Pandavas, with Krishna and Vyasa, laid out and built Indraprastha. The divine architect Vishvakarma, whom later retellings often credit with the whole city, does not appear here. The famous assembly hall comes later, and is built by someone else entirely.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=yudhishthira,krishna,balarama light=yudhishthira}

Brahmanas who knew every Veda and every language came to live there, and merchants from everywhere, and craftsmen of every art. Gardens grew around it, of mango and ashoka, champak and jackfruit, sal and palm, full of peacocks and cuckoos, with pleasure-houses and lotus pools. {#p12 1.209}

The Pandavas grew happier every day. And when they were settled, Krishna took his leave and went home with Balarama to Dvaraka. {#p13 1.209}
