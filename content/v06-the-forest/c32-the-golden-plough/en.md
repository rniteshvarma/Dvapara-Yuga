::scene{#s1 frame=bhishma,duryodhana,karna light=bhishma}

Back in Hastinapura, [Bhishma](bhishma) did not spare Duryodhana. I told you not to go, he said, and you went, and were taken captive, and freed by the Pandavas, and you are not even ashamed. And Karna, whom you praise so much, ran away in front of you. Make peace with your cousins. {#p1 3.251}

Duryodhana laughed, and walked out with [Karna](karna) and Shakuni. Bhishma, ashamed, went home. {#p2 3.251}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=karna,duryodhana light=karna}

Then Karna offered to conquer the earth for Duryodhana, as the Pandavas had done for Yudhishthira. He set out with a great army and besieged Drupada's city and took tribute from him. He went north and east, subdued the Angas and Vangas and Kalingas, went south and fought [Rukmi](rukmi), who gave him gold and said he had only kept the vow of a Kshatriya, and took tribute from the Pandyas, from Shishupala's son, from the west. {#p3 3.252}

When he came home, Duryodhana met him in the city, and Karna touched the feet of Dhritarashtra and Gandhari like a son. From that day, Duryodhana and Shakuni thought the Pandavas as good as beaten. {#p4 3.252}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana,dhritarashtra light=duryodhana}

Now Duryodhana wanted a Rajasuya of his own. His priests told him he could not have one. {#p5 3.253}

> O foremost of the Kauravas, while Yudhishthira is living, that best of sacrifices cannot be performed in thy family, O Prince of kings! {#q1 3.253}
> — the priest

And his father was still alive. But there was another sacrifice, they said, as great as the Rajasuya, which no one but Vishnu had ever performed: the Vaishnava. He would make a plough of the tribute gold and plough the sacrificial ground with it. {#p6 3.253}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,duryodhana light=yudhishthira}

The golden plough was made and the sacrifice began. Duhshasana sent a messenger to the Dvaita woods to invite the Pandavas. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) said it was good fortune that Duryodhana was holding such a sacrifice, but they could not come until the thirteenth year was done. {#p7 3.254}

[Bhima](bhima) had a message of his own. {#p8 3.254}

> When after the expiration of the thirteenth year, that lord of men, the Pandava, will, in the sacrifice of battle, pour upon the Dhritarashtras, the clarified butter of his ire, then will I come! {#q2 3.254}
> — Bhima

***

::scene{#s5 frame=karna,duryodhana light=karna}

When the sacrifice was over, Karna told Duryodhana he would honour him again the day he performed the true Rajasuya, after the Pandavas were dead. And then he made a vow before the Kurus. {#p9 3.255}

> So long as I do not slay Arjuna, I shall not allow any one to wash my feet, nor shall I taste meat. And I shall observe the Asura vow and whoever may solicit me (for any thing), I never shall say, 'I have it not.' {#q3 3.255}
> — Karna

The sons of Dhritarashtra cheered as if the Pandavas were already beaten. {#p10 3.255}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.255}
**"I have it not."** Karna's vow has two halves. The first is about killing Arjuna. The second, never to refuse anyone who asks him for anything, is the one that will matter first, and Indra has already been named as the one who will come to ask.
::
