::scene{#s1 frame=bhima light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) climbed the Gandhamadana like a lion in anger, without tiring and without fear, *having for his provisions on the journey the words of Draupadi*. He tore through a plantain forest, bathed in a lake like an elephant, and blew his conch until the whole mountain echoed. {#p1 3.145 3.151}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=hanuman,bhima light=hanuman}

High on the mountain, [Hanuman](hanuman) heard him, and knew him for his brother, for both were sons of the wind. The path beyond led to the world of the gods, and no mortal could go that way and live. So Hanuman lay down across the narrow path among the plantain trees, as if drowsy, and lashed his long tail until the mountain shook. {#p2 3.145}

Bhima found an old monkey lying in his way, copper-faced, his tail raised like a banner. He shouted. The monkey opened his eyes a little and looked at him with disregard. {#p3 3.145}

> Ill as I am, I was sleeping sweetly. Why hast thou awakened me? {#q1 3.145}
> — Hanuman

Beyond here no man could go, the monkey said. Stay, eat some fruit, and go back. {#p4 3.145}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,hanuman light=bhima}

*Who art thou?* said Bhima, and gave his name and lineage. *Arise!* {#p5 3.146}

I am too ill to rise, said the monkey. Jump over me. Bhima said he would not leap over any creature in which the Supreme Soul lives; otherwise he would leap over it, and the mountain too, *even as Hanuman had bounded over the ocean*. And who is Hanuman? asked the monkey. My brother, said Bhima, and I am his equal. {#p6 3.146}

> In consequence of age, I have no strength to get up. From pity for me, do thou go, moving aside my tail. {#q2 3.146}
> — Hanuman

Bhima smiled and took the tail in his left hand, meaning to fling the old monkey off the path. It did not move. He took it in both hands and pulled until his eyes rolled and his body ran with sweat, and still he could not lift it. {#p7 3.146}

He stood beside the monkey, ashamed, and bowed with joined hands. *Art thou a Siddha, or a god, or a Gandharva?* {#p8 3.146}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=hanuman,bhima light=hanuman}

*I am a monkey, by name Hanuman*, son of the wind, friend of Sugriva, servant of Rama. And he told Bhima Rama's story: how Ravana stole Sita, how the monkeys found her, how Rama killed Ravana and came home. And he had asked Rama for one boon: to live as long as the story of Rama's deeds was told on earth. {#p9 3.146 3.147}

Bhima bowed to his elder brother with joy. *None is more fortunate than I am.* He asked to see the form in which Hanuman had leapt the ocean. {#p10 3.148}

> That form of mine neither thou, not any one else can behold. At that age, the state of things was different, and doth not exist at present. {#q3 3.148}
> — Hanuman

Each age is smaller than the one before, Hanuman said, and he told Bhima of the four ages, Krita, Treta, Dvapara and Kali, and how dharma shrinks in each. But Bhima would not go without seeing it. {#p11 3.148 3.149}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=hanuman,bhima light=hanuman}

So Hanuman smiled, and grew, until he covered the plantain grove and stood as high as the Vindhya mountains, coppery-eyed, his tail lashing, bright as the sun. Bhima's hair stood on end and he shut his eyes. {#p12 3.149}

> O sinless one, thou art capable of beholding my size up to this extent. I can, however, go on swelling my size as long as I wish. {#q4 3.149}
> — Hanuman

Then he shrank again and embraced Bhima, and all Bhima's weariness left him. *Do not tell any one that I abide here.* Touching a man again had reminded him of Rama, he said, with tears in his eyes. Ask me a boon. Shall I crush Hastinapura with rocks, or bind Duryodhana and bring him to you? {#p13 3.150}

Bhima said he took all that as already done. Only be pleased with us. {#p14 3.150}

> Remaining on the flagstaff of Arjuna's car will I emit fierce shouts that will damp the energy of thy foes. {#q5 3.150}
> — Hanuman

Then he showed Bhima the way and vanished. And in the afternoon Bhima came to a river full of golden lotuses, and found the Saugandhika flowers there, bright as the rising sun. {#p15 3.150 3.151}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.150}
**The ape on the banner.** This is why Arjuna's chariot flies the banner of the monkey, *kapidhvaja*. In the war to come, Hanuman will ride on it.
::
