::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,bhima,arjuna,yudhishthira light=krishna}

Hamsa and Dimbhaka were dead, said [Krishna](krishna), and Kamsa too. The time had come. Jarasandha could not be beaten in battle by gods or Asuras together, but he could be beaten man to man, with bare arms. {#p1 2.20}

> In me is policy, in Bhima is strength and in Arjuna is triumph; and therefore, as prelude to performing the Rajasuya, we will certainly achieve the destruction of the ruler of Magadha. {#q1 2.20}
> — Krishna

If the three of them came to him in secret, Jarasandha's pride would make him fight one of them, and he would choose Bhima. *Make over to me, as a pledge, Bhima and Arjuna.* {#p2 2.20}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) gave in. *Partha cannot live without Sauri, nor can Sauri live without Partha.* Let Arjuna follow Krishna, and Bhima follow Arjuna. {#p3 2.20}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,bhima,arjuna light=krishna,bhima,arjuna}

So the three set out disguised as Snataka Brahmanas, students just finished with their studies, and crossed the Gandaki and the Sarayu, Kosala and Mithila, the Ganga and the Sona, until they came to Girivraja, the hill-city of Magadha, ringed by five hills. {#p4 2.20 2.21}

They did not go in by the gate. They broke down the sacred Chaityaka peak, where hung three drums made from the hide of a monster that Brihadratha had killed, drums that sounded for a month once struck, and went in that way, as if setting their feet on Jarasandha's head. They took garlands from the flower-sellers' stalls by force and walked through the city in coloured robes and sandal paste, like Himalayan lions looking at a cattle-fold. {#p5 2.21}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=jarasandha,krishna,bhima,arjuna light=jarasandha}

[Jarasandha](jarasandha) rose to receive his guests with water for their feet and the guest-offering, as he always did for Snatakas, even at midnight. [Bhima](bhima) and [Arjuna](arjuna) said nothing. They were under a vow of silence until midnight, said Krishna. {#p6 2.21}

At midnight the king came to them, and looked at them closely. {#p7 2.21}

> Who are ye, therefore, thus decked with flowers, and with hands bearing the marks of the bow-string? {#q2 2.21}
> — Jarasandha

They looked like Brahmanas but had a Kshatriya's strength, he said. They had broken his sacred peak and come in by the wrong gate, and they would not accept his worship. Why? {#p8 2.21}

*An enemy's house is entered by the wrong gate,* said Krishna, *and a friend's by the right one.* {#p9 2.21}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=jarasandha,krishna light=jarasandha,krishna}

Jarasandha could not remember ever having wronged them. Krishna told him: he had imprisoned the kings of the earth to offer them to Shiva like animals. {#p10 2.22}

> The slaughter of human being as sacrifice unto the gods is never seen. Why dost thou, therefore, seek to perform a sacrifice unto god Sankara by slaughtering human beings? {#q3 2.22}
> — Krishna

Then Krishna told him who they were. *I am Hrishikesha, otherwise called Sauri, and these two heroes among men are the sons of Pandu.* Free the kings, or fight. {#p11 2.22}

Jarasandha answered that he had never taken a king without first defeating him, and that a Kshatriya does as he likes with those he defeats. He would not free them out of fear. He would fight them with armies, or one, two or three of them at once. {#p12 2.22}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=jarasandha,sahadeva_m,bhima light=jarasandha,bhima}

He had his son [Sahadeva](sahadeva_m) crowned in his place. Krishna asked him which of the three he would fight. Jarasandha chose Bhima. {#p13 2.22 2.23}

> I will fight with thee. It is better to be vanquished by a superior person. {#q4 2.23}
> — Jarasandha

He took off his crown and bound up his hair, and they went at each other with bare hands. They slapped their armpits till the enclosure shook, struck neck against neck and forehead against forehead until sparks flew, twisted each other's limbs like fibres for rope, and threw each other face down. The whole city came to watch, packed so close there was no space between body and body. {#p14 2.23}

They began on the first day of the month of Kartika, and fought day and night without food until the thirteenth. On the night of the fourteenth, Jarasandha began to tire. {#p15 2.23}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=krishna,bhima,jarasandha,vayu light=bhima}

Krishna spoke to Bhima as if to spur him on. A tired enemy should not be pressed too hard, he said, or he might die; fight him only with as much strength as he has left. Bhima understood, and made up his mind to kill him. {#p16 2.23}

> O Bhima, exhibit today upon Jarasandha the strength thou hast luckily derived, the might thou hast obtained from (thy father), the god Maruta. {#q5 2.24}
> — Krishna

Bhima lifted Jarasandha high, whirled him round a hundred times, pressed his knee into his spine, and broke him in two. His roar and Jarasandha's death-cry together were so loud that the people of Magadha thought the Himalayas were falling. {#p17 2.24}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=2.24,2.17}
**How it ends.** In Ganguli's text, Bhima simply breaks Jarasandha across his knee. The famous version, in which Krishna splits a blade of grass to show Bhima how to tear the body apart along the seam where Jara joined it and throw the halves in opposite directions, belongs to later retellings. The two halves of his birth are the epic's; the blade of grass is not.
::

***

::scene{#s7 frame=krishna,bhima,arjuna,sahadeva_m,yudhishthira light=krishna}

They left the body at the palace gate, as if asleep. Krishna had Jarasandha's own chariot made ready, a chariot Indra had once fought from, and went in and freed the imprisoned kings, who brought him jewels. Garuda came at Krishna's thought and perched on its flagstaff. {#p18 2.24}

The freed kings asked what they could do for him. *Yudhishthira is desirous of performing the sacrifice of Rajasuya,* Krishna said; help him. And when Jarasandha's son Sahadeva came out, frightened, with gifts, Krishna reassured him and crowned him king of Magadha. {#p19 2.24}

Then the three of them drove home to Indraprastha, unwounded. *From good fortune, the mighty Jarasandha hath been slain by Bhima,* Krishna told Yudhishthira, *and the kings confined have been all set free.* {#p20 2.24}
