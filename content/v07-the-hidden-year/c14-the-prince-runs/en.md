The horses seemed to fly. Near the cemetery they sighted the Kuru army, spread out *like the vast sea or a forest of innumerable trees moving through the sky*, its dust reaching heaven, with Karna and Duryodhana, Kripa, Bhishma, Drona and Ashwatthama at its head. [Uttara](uttar)'s hair stood on end. {#p1 4.38}

> I dare not fight with the Kurus. See, the bristles on my body have stood on their ends. {#q1 4.38}
> — Uttara

His father had taken the whole army, he said. He was only a boy, alone. Brihannala should stop. {#p2 4.38}

[Arjuna](arjuna) would not. Uttara had boasted in front of men and women; they would laugh at him if he came home without the cattle. And Brihannala could not go back either, after all the Sairindhri's praise. {#p3 4.38}

> Let the Kurus rob the Matsyas off all their wealth. Let men and women, O Vrihannala, laugh at me. Let my kine perish, let the city be a desert. Let me stand exposed before my father. Still there is no need of battle. {#q2 4.38}
> — Uttara

***

::scene{#s2 frame=uttar,arjuna light=arjuna}

He jumped down, threw away his bow and arrows, and ran. {#p4 4.38}

> This is not the practice of the brave, this flight of a Kshatriya from the field of battle. Even death in battle is better than flight from fear. {#q3 4.38}
> — Arjuna

Arjuna jumped down after him, his long braid and red garments flying, and some soldiers laughed to see it. But the Kurus watching began to wonder. *He is partly a man and partly a woman.* Yet the head, the neck, the arms like maces, the walk, were Arjuna's. Who but Arjuna would come against them alone? It must be Virata's boy, out of childishness, with Arjuna in disguise as his driver. {#p5 4.38}

Within a hundred steps Arjuna caught Uttara by the hair. The prince begged: a hundred gold coins, eight lapis stones set in gold, a chariot with a golden flagstaff, ten elephants, only let him go. Arjuna laughed and dragged him back to the chariot. If he would not fight, let him drive, and Arjuna would fight. {#p6 4.38}

> Fear not, O chastiser of foes, thou art a Kshatriya and the foremost of royal princes. {#q4 4.38}
> — Arjuna

He lifted the fainting prince back onto the chariot and turned towards the Shami tree. {#p7 4.38}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drona,bhishma,karna,duryodhana light=drona}

The Kuru warriors watched him turn, and were afraid. [Drona](drona) read the signs: hot winds raining gravel, dry clouds, weapons sliding from their cases, jackals howling, horses weeping, banners trembling with no wind. {#p8 4.39}

> This mighty bowman, this foremost of all wielders of weapons, this hero that hath come in the habit of a person of the third sex, is the son of Pritha. There is no doubt of this. {#q5 4.39}
> — Drona

Taught by Indra himself, he said, and the man who had satisfied Mahadeva disguised as a hunter. [Karna](karna) had heard enough of Arjuna's virtues. {#p9 4.39}

> Arjuna, however, is not equal to even a full sixteenth part of myself or Duryodhana! {#q6 4.39}
> — Karna

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) was glad. If this was Arjuna, the Pandavas had been found, and would have to go to the forest for twelve more years. And if it was someone else, he would bring him down with arrows. Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and Ashwatthama all praised his spirit. {#p10 4.39}
