::scene{#s1 frame=karna,shakuni,duryodhana light=karna}

In Hastinapura, [Karna](karna) and [Shakuni](shakuni) talked to [Duryodhana](duryodhana). The whole earth was his now, they said, and the prosperity that had once been Yudhishthira's. Why not go and let the Pandavas see it? {#p1 3.235}

> The happiness that one derives from beholding his foes in grief, is greater than what one may derive from the acquisition of offering or wealth or kingdom! {#q1 3.235}
> — Karna and Shakuni

Let Duryodhana's wives, in fine clothes, look at Draupadi in her deer-skin, and let her grief be greater than it was in the assembly. {#p2 3.235}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,karna,dhritarashtra light=duryodhana}

Duryodhana loved the idea. *What joy can be greater … than upon beholding the daughter of Drupada dressed in red rags in the woods?* But his father would never let him go. {#p3 3.236}

The next morning Karna had a plan. The royal herds were at the cattle stations in the Dvaita forest, and it was time to count them and brand the calves. A king ought to inspect his cattle. Shakuni said he had thought of exactly the same thing, and the three laughed and clasped hands. {#p4 3.236}

A herdsman named Samanga, coached in advance, told [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) the cattle needed counting. The king hesitated: the Pandavas were near there, Bhima was hot-tempered, and Draupadi was *energy's self*. Shakuni promised they would not go near the Pandavas. The king gave his permission, though not willingly. {#p5 3.237}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana,karna,duhshasana,shakuni light=duryodhana}

So Duryodhana set out with Karna, Duhshasana, Shakuni, his brothers and thousands of women, with eight thousand chariots, thirty thousand elephants, traders and bards and huntsmen, and the citizens followed with their wives. They camped four miles from the lake. Duryodhana counted his cattle and marked the calves, and then went to the lake, where Yudhishthira and Draupadi were performing a sacrifice nearby, and ordered pleasure-houses built on its banks. {#p6 3.237 3.238}

But the Gandharvas were already there. Their king, Chitrasena, had come from Kubera's city with his Apsaras and closed the lake to everyone. *Your wicked king Duryodhana must be destitute of sense*, they told his men. {#p7 3.238}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=duryodhana,karna light=karna}

Duryodhana sent his warriors to drive them off. Chitrasena ordered the Gandharvas to punish them. The Kuru soldiers ran at the first sight of them, all but Karna, who stood and cut down hundreds with his arrows. Duryodhana, Shakuni, Duhshasana and the others came back to support him, and for a while they held. {#p8 3.238}

Then the Gandharvas used illusion, and it seemed every Kuru was surrounded by ten of them. The army fled. Karna stood like a hill, until they cut his chariot to pieces, and then he jumped onto Vikarna's chariot and fled too. {#p9 3.239}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=duryodhana,duhshasana,k_vivimshati,k_vinda,k_anuvinda light=duryodhana}

Duryodhana alone would not run. The Gandharvas shot his chariot to pieces and killed his charioteer and horses, and when he fell to the ground Chitrasena seized him. They took Duhshasana too, and [Vivimshati](k_vivimshati), [Vinda](k_vinda) and [Anuvinda](k_anuvinda), and all the royal women, and led them away in chains. {#p10 3.240}

And the soldiers who had fled went where they could find help: to the Pandavas. {#p11 3.240}
