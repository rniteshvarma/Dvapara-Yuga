::scene{#s1 frame=shakuni,dhritarashtra,duryodhana light=dhritarashtra}

So Shakuni went to [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), sitting blind on his throne, and told him his eldest son had grown pale and thin with some grief he would not speak of. {#p1 2.48}

The king asked his son what could be wrong. All the wealth of the kingdom was his to command, the best food, the best horses, beautiful women and fine houses. Why did he grieve *as if he were destitute*? {#p2 2.48}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,dhritarashtra,yudhishthira light=duryodhana}

Duryodhana told him. He had been the one at the Rajasuya who received the tribute; his hands had grown tired taking it in, and the kings who brought it had to wait while he rested. {#p3 2.49}

Then he described it all, item by item: thousands of slave-girls and horses and elephants, cloth and skins and blankets, gold and gems, the tribes of the east and the hills, the Brahmanas who waited at the gate with their tribute and were not even let in. He told how the kings themselves had served the food, *like Vaisyas*, how they had each brought a piece of the chariot for Yudhishthira's bath, and how Krishna had bathed him with Varuna's own conch. When all the conches blew at once, he said, the weaker kings had fainted, and Krishna and the Pandavas had laughed at them, and at him. {#p4 2.50 2.51 2.52}

> O ruler of men, a yoke that is tied (to the bullock's shoulders) by a blind man becomes loosened. Even such is the case with us. {#q1 2.52}
> — Duryodhana

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,duryodhana light=dhritarashtra}

Dhritarashtra answered him gently. Yudhishthira did not know deceit and did not envy him. If Duryodhana wanted the glory of a great sacrifice, let him perform one; the kings would bring him wealth too. {#p5 2.53}

> The sons of Pandu are as thy arms. Do not lop off those arms of thine. {#q2 2.53}
> — Dhritarashtra

***

::scene{#s4 frame=duryodhana,dhritarashtra light=duryodhana}

Duryodhana was not moved. A man who has only heard of many things knows them as a spoon knows the taste of soup. The ways of kings are not the ways of other men; for a Kshatriya, success is the only measure, by fair means or foul. Indra himself had cut off Namuchi's head after promising him peace. A foe left to grow eats a man from the roots, like white ants in a tree. {#p6 2.54}

> Discontent is the root of prosperity. Therefore, O king, I desire to be discontented. {#q3 2.54}
> — Duryodhana

***

::scene{#s5 frame=shakuni,duryodhana,dhritarashtra,vidura light=shakuni}

Then Shakuni spoke. {#p7 2.55}

> Know, O Bharata, that betting is my bow, the dice are my arrows, the marks on them my bow-string, and the dice-board my car. {#q4 2.55}
> — Shakuni

Dhritarashtra said he would ask [Vidura](vidura) first. Vidura, said Duryodhana, always worked for the sons of Pandu; he would turn the king's heart. If his father consulted Vidura and refused, Duryodhana would kill himself, and then the king could be happy with Vidura and enjoy the whole earth. Dice were an old invention, he said. There was no killing in them, no weapons. {#p8 2.48 2.55}

The old king gave way. {#p9 2.55}

> Do what may be agreeable to thee, O ruler of men. But thou shall have to repent for acting according to these words. {#q5 2.55}
> — Dhritarashtra

And regarding Fate as supreme, he ordered a hall to be built: the crystal-arched palace, with a thousand columns and a hundred gates, decked with gold and lapis lazuli. Thousands of craftsmen built it quickly. Then he sent for Vidura. {#p10 2.55}
