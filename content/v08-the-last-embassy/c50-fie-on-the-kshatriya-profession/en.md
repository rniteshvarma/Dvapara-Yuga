::scene{#s1 frame=kunti,arjuna,bhima light=kunti}

Then [Kunti](kunti) gave Krishna her words for the others. For [Arjuna](arjuna): when he was born in the hermitage, a voice from the sky had said he would rival Indra, conquer the Kurus with Bhima's help and Vasudeva's, win back his father's share and perform three great sacrifices. Let it be as that voice said. For [Bhima](bhima): {#p1 5.137}

> The time hath come for that in view of which Kshatriya lady bringeth forth a son! {#q1 5.137}
> — Kunti

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti,draupadi,nakula,sahadeva light=draupadi}

For [Draupadi](draupadi), only praise: her conduct toward Kunti's sons had always been worthy of her. For the twins, a harder word. Before their eyes the princess of Panchala had been called cruel names; who could forgive that? {#p2 5.137}

> The deprivation of their kingdom grieved me not. Their defeat at dice grieved me not. But that noble and fair Draupadi, however, while weeping in the midst of the assembly, had to hear those cruel and insulting words is what grieveth me most. {#q2 5.137}
> — Kunti

Let Arjuna always follow the path Draupadi pointed out. Let Krishna remind them of what Duhshasana said to Bhima. And let him tell them she was well. {#p3 5.137}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,karna light=krishna}

Krishna walked around her in reverence and left. He sent back the Kuru chiefs who had followed him, took [Karna](karna) up into his own chariot, and drove out of the city with Satyaki. Behind him the Kurus talked of the wonder they had seen, and said that the whole earth was caught in the net of death through Duryodhana's folly. Outside the city he talked with Karna for a long time; then he set Karna down and drove on to Upaplavya, the horses running *as if drinking the skies*. {#p4 5.137}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,drona,duryodhana light=bhishma}

[Bhishma](bhishma) and [Drona](drona) turned to [Duryodhana](duryodhana) again. Had he heard what Kunti said? Her sons would act on it, and Vasudeva approved it. They had borne everything before because they were bound by their word; they were not bound now. He had seen Arjuna beat them all at Virata's city, and seen him free Duryodhana himself from the Gandharvas. {#p5 5.138}

Let him put down his bow, smooth the frown from his brow, and embrace Yudhishthira. The stars were hostile; jackals howled in every quarter; vultures circled the army; the city and the palace did not look as they had. War and peace were in his hands. {#p6 5.138}

Duryodhana frowned, looked down, glanced at them sideways, and said nothing. {#p7 5.139}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=drona,arjuna,ashwatthama light=drona}

The two old men looked at each other. Bhishma said there could be no greater grief than having to fight Yudhishthira. Drona said more. {#p8 5.139}

> My affection for Dhananjaya is greater than that which I bear for my son Aswatthaman. {#q3 5.139}
> — Drona

> Alas, in observance of the Kshatriya duties, I shall have to fight even against that Dhananjaya who is dearer to me than my son. Fie on the Kshatriya profession. {#q4 5.139}
> — Drona

Their own work was done, Drona said: they had given gifts and offered sacrifices and their years had run out. It was Duryodhana who would lose everything. How could he beat a man whom Draupadi, truthful and austere, prayed for every day? There was no need for war. {#p9 5.139}
