::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna,bhima,krishna light=yudhishthira}

When Arjuna took up Gandiva again, the Pandava army roared and blew its conchs, and the gods, Gandharvas and sages gathered with Indra to watch. Then [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) took off his armour, laid down his weapons, got down from his chariot, and with joined hands walked silently on foot toward the enemy army, toward Bhishma. His brothers ran after him: where was he going, unarmed, toward an army in mail? He said nothing. {#p1 6.43}

[Krishna](krishna) smiled. He knew. Yudhishthira was going to pay his respects to his elders, Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and Shalya; it was said of old that a man who honours his elders before fighting them wins. {#p2 6.43}

The Kuru army jeered. *He is a disgrace to his race; he comes in fear to Bhishma for shelter.* Then both armies fell silent, waiting to see what he would say. {#p3 6.43}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,bhishma light=bhishma}

He walked through the bristling ranks, took [Bhishma](bhishma)'s feet in his hands, and asked his leave to fight, and his blessing. Had he not come, Bhishma said, he would have cursed him; he was pleased; let him fight and win. {#p4 6.43}

> A man is the slave of wealth, but wealth is no one's slave. This is very true, O king. I have been bound by the Kauravas with (their) wealth. {#q1 6.43}
> — Bhishma

So he spoke *like a eunuch*, he said: except for fighting, what did Yudhishthira want? Give me counsel for my good every day, Yudhishthira said, though you fight for them. And tell me: how can you be conquered, how can your death be brought about? {#p5 6.43}

> I do not see the person, O sire, who can vanquish me in battle. The time also of my death is not yet come to me once again. {#q2 6.43}
> — Bhishma

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,drona light=drona}

He went to [Drona](drona), and Drona said the same: he would have cursed him had he not come; he was bound by wealth; he would fight for the Kurus but pray for Yudhishthira's victory. Victory was certain where Krishna was. As long as Drona fought, the Pandavas could not win; let them seek his speedy death. How, then? No one could kill him while he fought, Drona said; {#p6 6.43}

> I also tell thee truly that I will cast off my arms in battle, having heard something very disagreeable from some one of credible speech. {#q3 6.43}
> — Drona

To Kripa Yudhishthira could not even finish his question; he stood silent, overcome. Kripa understood: he could not be killed, he said, but he would pray every morning for Yudhishthira's victory. And [Shalya](shalya), his uncle, said the same words about wealth, and promised again what he had promised before the war: to weaken Karna's spirit in battle. {#p7 6.43}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=6.43}
Shalya first made this promise at Upaplavya, after Duryodhana had won him over with hospitality on his road; Volume VIII, chapter 2 tells it.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna,karna,yuyutsu,yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

Krishna went to [Karna](karna): since he would not fight while Bhishma lived, let him join the Pandavas until then. Karna refused; he had given his life to Duryodhana. Then Yudhishthira called out between the armies: {#p8 6.43}

> He who will choose us, him we shall choose for our ally! {#q4 6.43}
> — Yudhishthira

[Yuyutsu](yuyutsu), Dhritarashtra's son by a Vaishya woman, answered gladly and crossed over to the Pandavas with drums beating. On him, said Yudhishthira, now rested the thread of Dhritarashtra's line and his funeral offerings. Then the Pandavas put their armour back on, and the kings of both sides praised their courtesy to their elders, and many wept. {#p9 6.43}
