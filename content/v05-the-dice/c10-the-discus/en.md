::scene{#s1 frame=shishupala,krishna light=shishupala}

[Shishupala](shishupala) turned to [Krishna](krishna). {#p1 2.45}

> O Janarddana, I challenge thee. Come, fight with me until I slay thee today with all the Pandavas. {#q1 2.45}
> — Shishupala

The Pandavas deserved to die with him, he said, for honouring a slave and a wretch who was no king. {#p2 2.45}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,shishupala,rukmini light=krishna}

Krishna spoke to the kings softly. This son of his father's sister had always been an enemy of the Yadavas, he said, though they had never wished him harm. When they were away at Pragjyotisha, he had come and burnt Dvaraka. While King Bhoja was at play on the Raivataka hill, he had fallen on his attendants, killed many and led others away in chains. He had stolen the horse of Krishna's father's horse-sacrifice. He had carried off the wife of Babhru on the road to the Sauviras, and, disguised as the king of Karusha, Bhadra, the princess of Vishala, who was promised to that king. And he had wanted [Rukmini](rukmini), and not got her. {#p3 2.45}

All this Krishna had borne for his aunt's sake. Now they had all seen it with their own eyes. {#p4 2.45}

Shishupala laughed. Was Krishna not ashamed to say before all these kings that his own wife had first been meant for someone else? {#p5 2.45}

> O Krishna, pardon me if thou pleasest, or pardon me not. But angry or friendly, what canst thou do unto me? {#q2 2.45}
> — Shishupala

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,shishupala light=krishna,shishupala}

Krishna thought of his discus, and it came into his hand. {#p6 2.45}

> As asked by his mother, a hundred offences (of his) were to be pardoned by me. Even this was the boon she had asked, and even this I granted her. That number, ye kings, hath become full. {#q3 2.45}
> — Krishna

And he cut off Shishupala's head with the discus, and the king of Chedi fell like a cliff struck by lightning. {#p7 2.45}

A light like the sun rose out of his body, bowed to Krishna, and entered him. The sky, though cloudless, rained; thunder rolled; the earth shook. Some kings sat silent and stared. Some rubbed their palms in rage, some bit their lips; some praised Krishna in private. {#p8 2.45}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.45,2.43}
**A hundred, counted.** The epic does not number Shishupala's insults one by one, and later retellings that tally them exactly are adding to it. What it gives is the promise to his mother and Krishna's word that the number was full.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,dhrishtaketu,krishna light=yudhishthira}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) had Shishupala's funeral rites performed with honour, and crowned his son [Dhrishtaketu](dhrishtaketu) king of Chedi. Then, with Krishna guarding it with his bow, discus and mace, the great sacrifice was completed. {#p9 2.45}

The kings came to Yudhishthira after his final bath. *By good fortune thou hast come out successful. Thou hast obtained the imperial dignity.* He honoured each one, and his brothers rode with them to the borders. Krishna said goodbye to Kunti, Draupadi and Subhadra, and to Yudhishthira he gave a last piece of advice: be to your people as a cloud is to all creatures, as a great tree to the birds. Then he drove home to Dvaraka. {#p10 2.45}

And when everyone had gone, two guests stayed behind in that hall: [Duryodhana](duryodhana), and [Shakuni](shakuni). {#p11 2.45}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=vyasa,yudhishthira light=vyasa}

[Vyasa](vyasa) came too, with his disciples, to bless his grandson. Yudhishthira asked him something that had troubled him. Narada had said the Rajasuya brings portents in the sky, the air and the earth. Had they ended with the death of the king of Chedi? {#p12 2.46}

> For thirteen years, O king, those portents will bear mighty consequences ending in destruction, O king of kings, of all the Kshatriyas. {#q4 2.46}
> — Vyasa

Yudhishthira would be the cause, Vyasa said, for the sins of Duryodhana and by the strength of Bhima and Arjuna. That night he would dream of Shiva, blue-throated, on his bull, gazing south, towards the land of the dead. *None can rise superior to the influence of Time.* And Vyasa went away to Kailasa. {#p13 2.46}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna,bhima,nakula,sahadeva light=yudhishthira}

Yudhishthira was stricken. If he was to be the cause of the death of every Kshatriya, he told his brothers, why should he live? Arjuna begged him not to give way. So Yudhishthira made a vow instead. {#p14 2.46}

> For thirteen years, what ever purpose have I to live for, I shall not speak a hard word to my brothers or to any of the kings of the earth. {#q5 2.46}
> — Yudhishthira

It is disagreement, he said, that causes war. He would do what others wished and keep war far away. His brothers approved. {#p15 2.46}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=2.46}
**A vow to never say no.** Yudhishthira's answer to the prophecy is to refuse no one and quarrel with no one for thirteen years. It is the most generous vow in the epic, and the one that will make him impossible to refuse a game of dice.
::
