::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,narada,krishna light=dhritarashtra}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) answered [Narada](narada) first. It was all true, and he wished for the same thing. {#p1 5.124}

> O holy one, it is even so as thou, O Narada, sayest. My wish also is precisely such, but, O holy one, I have no power (to carry them out)! {#q1 5.124}
> — Dhritarashtra

Then he turned to [Krishna](krishna). He was not his own master, he said; Duryodhana never did what he wished, and would not listen to Gandhari, or Vidura, or Bhishma. Let Krishna himself try to persuade that wicked son of his. That would be the act of a true friend. {#p2 5.124}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,duryodhana light=krishna}

So Krishna went nearer to the ever-angry [Duryodhana](duryodhana) and spoke to him gently. He was born of a wise line, learned and well-mannered; only the low-born and the shameless acted as he was acting now. The way he had chosen was sinful and frightful, it led to death, and he could not hold to it for long. {#p3 5.124}

> That mortal who having listened to the counsels of friends doth not act according to them, is consumed at the end by the consequences of his disregard, like him who swalloweth the fruit called Kimpaka. {#q2 5.124}
> — Krishna

The Pandavas had been wronged by him from their birth, and they had never been angry; let him be as generous to them. The wise seek virtue, profit and pleasure together, and virtue first, for neither of the others ever comes apart from it. A man who deals falsely with the righteous cuts himself down, *like a forest with an axe*. {#p4 5.124}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,duryodhana,arjuna light=arjuna}

He was leaning on Duhshasana, on Karna and Shakuni, men far below the Pandavas in wisdom and in valour, while all his kings together could not look Bhima in the face when Bhima was angry. And Arjuna, who had beaten the gods at Khandava and held off a whole army alone at Virata's city, and had pleased Shiva himself in battle: who would face him? {#p5 5.124}

> With myself again as his second when that son of Pritha will rush to the field of battle against an enemy, who is there that is competent to challenge him then? Can Purandara himself do so? {#q3 5.124}
> — Krishna

Let him not be called the destroyer of his own race. If he made peace, the Pandavas would make him heir and his father sovereign of the whole empire. Let him give them half the kingdom, and keep the rest in happiness, with his friends. {#p6 5.124}
