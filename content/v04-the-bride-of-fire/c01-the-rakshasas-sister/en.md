::scene{#s1 frame=bhima,kunti,yudhishthira,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) went on through the forest with his family on his body, and the trees shook as his chest struck them, and the wind of his passing was like the hot winds of early summer. They swam rivers and went in disguise, for fear of the sons of Dhritarashtra. By evening they had come to a terrible forest, with little fruit or water, full of the cries of birds and beasts, and a wild wind blowing in the dark. {#p1 1.153}

They could go no further. They sat down, without food or water. [Kunti](kunti), burning with thirst, said it again and again: {#p2 1.153}

> I am the mother of the five Pandavas and am now in their midst. Yet I am burning with thirst! {#q1 1.153}
> — Kunti

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima,kunti light=bhima,kunti}

Bhima set them down under a great banyan tree and went towards the sound of water birds. He found a lake, drank and bathed, and carried water back for them by soaking his upper cloth in it, four miles there and back. {#p3 1.153}

When he came back they were all asleep on the bare ground, and he wept. His mother, the sister of Vasudeva, the daughter-in-law of Vichitravirya, the wife of Pandu, who had always slept in palaces, was sleeping in the dirt. So was Yudhishthira, who deserved to rule the three worlds, and Arjuna, and the twins. {#p4 1.153}

> He who hath no jealous evil-minded relatives, liveth in happiness in this world like a single tree in a village. {#q2 1.153}
> — Bhima

The sons of Dhritarashtra were alive, he said, only because Yudhishthira had not told him to kill them. Then he sat down to watch over his sleeping family. {#p5 1.153}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=i4487_hidimba,hidimbi light=i4487_hidimba}

Not far away, in a sal tree, lived a Rakshasa named [Hidimba](i4487_hidimba), a man-eater with long sharp teeth, red hair and beard, and a body like a dark cloud. He smelt the sleeping humans and sent his sister to fetch them. {#p6 1.154}

> My mouth waters at the anticipated relish of such food. {#q3 1.154}
> — Hidimba

Kill them all and bring them, he told her; they would eat together and then dance. {#p7 1.154}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.154}
**Two Hidimbas.** Ganguli calls both the Rakshasa and his sister *Hidimva*. Later tradition names the sister Hidimbi, and that is the name we use for her here, to keep the two apart.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=hidimbi,bhima light=hidimbi,bhima}

[Hidimbi](hidimbi) went, and found them all asleep except Bhima, sitting awake on guard. She looked at him, golden-skinned and broad-shouldered like a lion, with eyes like lotus petals, and fell in love on the spot. {#p8 1.154}

> A woman's love for her husband is stronger than her affection for her brother. {#q4 1.154}
> — Hidimbi

She would not obey her brother. She took a beautiful human form, decked herself with ornaments, and came to Bhima smiling. She told him the truth: her brother had sent her to kill them for his food. But she wanted no husband but him. She could fly; she would carry him away to the mountains, where they would be happy. {#p9 1.154}

> O Rakshasa woman, who can, like a Muni having all his passions under control, abandon his sleeping mother and elder and younger brothers? {#q5 1.154}
> — Bhima

Then wake them, she said, and she would carry them all to safety. Bhima would not wake them: no Rakshasa could stand against his arms. *Thou mayst stay or go as thou likest,* he said, *or mayst even send thy cannibal brother. I care not.* {#p10 1.154}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=i4487_hidimba,hidimbi,bhima light=i4487_hidimba,bhima}

Hidimba, tired of waiting, climbed down from his tree and came, red-eyed, his hair standing on end. Hidimbi was frightened and begged Bhima to let her fly them all away. *Fear not,* he told her. *Hate me not, thinking that I am a man.* {#p11 1.155}

Hidimba saw his sister in her lovely human form and understood. He called her unchaste and a traitor to her race, and ran at her to kill her. {#p12 1.155}

> Smite me first,—it behoveth thee not to kill a woman, especially when she hath been sinned against instead of sinning. {#q6 1.155}
> — Bhima

It was not her fault, Bhima said: it was the god of desire. The Rakshasa should fight him instead. *Accomplish all this first, and then mayst thou vaunt,* said Hidimba, and rushed at him. {#p13 1.155}

Bhima caught his outstretched arms, as if in play, and dragged him away thirty-two cubits, like a lion dragging a small animal, and then further still, so his yells would not wake his brothers. They grappled like two mad elephants, breaking trees and tearing creepers. And the noise woke the Pandavas and their mother, who sat up to find a beautiful stranger sitting before them. {#p14 1.155}
