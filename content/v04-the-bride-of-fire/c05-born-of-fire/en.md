::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,kunti light=kunti}

A few days later another Brahmana, a traveller of strict vows, came to lodge in their host's house, and the Pandavas asked him for his news. He told them of countries and holy rivers, of kings and cities; and then of something stranger. In the land of the Panchalas, King Drupada's daughter was to choose a husband, and she and her brother had been born not of a woman but out of a sacrificial fire. {#p1 1.167}

The Pandavas wanted to hear everything. So the traveller told them. {#p2 1.167}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=drona,drupada light=drona,drupada}

He began with what the Pandavas knew better than he did: how [Drona](drona) and [Drupada](drupada) had been boys together, how Drupada had spurned his old friend, and how Drona's pupils had taken Drupada prisoner, and Drona had kept half his kingdom. {#p3 1.168}

They swore eternal friendship and parted. But the humiliation never left the king's mind for a moment, and he began to waste away. {#p4 1.168}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drupada,i11185_upayaja,i12489_yaja light=drupada,i11185_upayaja}

Drupada wandered from hermitage to hermitage, looking for Brahmanas who knew the rites, sighing for revenge. He had sons, but none who could beat Drona. On the banks of the Ganga and the Yamuna he found two brothers of the line of Kashyapa, [Yaja](i12489_yaja) and [Upayaja](i11185_upayaja). {#p5 1.169}

He courted the younger, Upayaja, for a whole year, serving him and offering him ten thousand cows for a rite that would give him a son to kill Drona. Upayaja refused. But at last he told the king to try his elder brother, who, he said, was less particular. He had once seen Yaja pick up a fallen fruit without asking whether the ground was clean. {#p6 1.169}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=drupada,i12489_yaja,drona light=drupada,i12489_yaja}

So Drupada went to Yaja and offered him eighty thousand cows. {#p7 1.169}

> Enmity with Drona burneth my heart; it behoveth thee therefore to cool that heart of mine. {#q1 1.169}
> — Drupada

Drona joined a Brahmana's power to a Kshatriya's, the king said, like a fire fed with butter, and no Kshatriya on earth could stand against him. Only a Brahmana's greater power could. *So be it,* said Yaja, and asked his brother to help him, and Upayaja told the king what the sacrifice would need. {#p8 1.169}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=dhrishtadyumna,i12489_yaja,drupada light=dhrishtadyumna}

When all was ready, Yaja poured the butter on the fire and called the queen. *A son and a daughter have arrived for thee!* But the queen asked him to wait; her mouth was still perfumed, and she was not ready. {#p9 1.169}

> O lady, whether thou comest or waitest, why should not the object of this sacrifice be accomplished when the oblation hath already been prepared by me and sanctified by Upayaja's invocations? {#q2 1.169}
> — Yaja

He poured the offering into the fire, and out of the flames rose a youth like a god, blazing like fire, terrible to see, with a crown on his head, armour on his body, sword, bow and arrows in his hands, roaring. He leapt into a chariot and drove it round. The Panchalas shouted for joy, and a voice spoke from the sky. {#p10 1.169}

> This prince hath been born for the destruction of Drona. {#q3 1.169}
> — a voice from the sky

***

::scene{#s6 frame=draupadi light=draupadi}

Then from the middle of the altar rose a daughter. Her eyes were large and black as lotus petals, her skin was dark, her hair blue-black and curling. The fragrance of a blue lotus came from her body and could be smelt two miles away. There was no one on earth as beautiful. {#p11 1.169}

> This dark-complexioned girl will be the first of all women, and she will be the cause of the destruction of many Kshatriyas. {#q4 1.169}
> — a voice from the sky

She would, in time, do the work of the gods, the voice said, and with her many dangers would come to the Kauravas. {#p12 1.169}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=dhrishtadyumna,draupadi,drupada light=dhrishtadyumna,draupadi}

The queen asked Yaja that the two should know no mother but her, and he agreed. The Brahmanas named the boy [Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna), for his boldness and because he was born with armour and weapons. The girl, because she was so dark, they named Krishna. She is [Draupadi](draupadi), Drupada's daughter. {#p13 1.169}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.169,1.167}
**Krishna and Krishna.** Draupadi's own name in the epic is Krishna, *the dark one*, the same name as her future friend, Krishna of the Vrishnis. She is also called Panchali, after her people, and Yajnaseni, after her father's other name.
::

***

::scene{#s8 frame=drona,dhrishtadyumna light=drona,dhrishtadyumna}

And then the strangest thing of all. Drona took the Panchala prince into his own house, and taught him every weapon, the boy born to kill him. {#p14 1.169}

> The high-souled son of Bharadwaja, regarding destiny to be inevitable, did what would perpetuate his own great deeds. {#q5 1.169}
> — the traveller

***

::scene{#s9 frame=kunti,yudhishthira,arjuna light=kunti}

When the Pandavas heard all this, the epic says, they felt as if they had been pierced by darts, and could not settle. [Kunti](kunti) saw it. {#p15 1.170}

They had lived here many nights, she said, and seen every wood and garden; alms were harder to come by now. They had never seen the Panchala country, and its king was said to be good to Brahmanas. *It is not good to live long in one place.* Should they go? {#p16 1.170}

Yudhishthira said they would do as she wished, if his brothers agreed. {#p17 1.170}
