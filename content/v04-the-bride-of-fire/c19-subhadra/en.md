::scene{#s1 frame=balarama,revati,ugrasena,pradyumna,samba,satyaki,krishna,arjuna light=krishna,arjuna}

A few days later the Vrishnis and Andhakas held a great festival on the Raivataka hill, with gifts to the Brahmanas, music and dancing, mansions hung with gems and trees of coloured cloth. [Balarama](balarama) roamed about tipsy with his wife [Revati](revati); old King [Ugrasena](ugrasena) came with his thousand wives; [Pradyumna](pradyumna) and [Samba](samba), flushed with wine and garlanded, went about like gods. {#p1 1.221}

[Krishna](krishna) and [Arjuna](arjuna) walked through it all together. And they saw Krishna's sister, the daughter of [Vasudeva](vasudeva), among her maids: [Subhadra](subhadra), called Bhadra. Arjuna could not take his eyes off her. {#p2 1.221}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,arjuna,subhadra light=krishna,arjuna}

Krishna smiled. {#p3 1.221}

> How is this? Can the heart of one that rangeth the woods be agitated by the god of desire? {#q1 1.221}
> — Krishna

She was his sister, he said, his father's favourite daughter. If Arjuna's heart was set on her, he would speak to their father himself. Arjuna said he would do anything a man could do to win her. {#p4 1.221}

> In the case of Kshatriyas that are brave, a forcible abduction for purposes of marriage is applauded, as the learned have said. {#q2 1.221}
> — Krishna

A svayamvara was uncertain, Krishna said; who knew what a girl would do? Better to carry her off. So they sent swift messengers to [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) at Indraprastha, and Yudhishthira agreed. {#p5 1.221}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.221,1.223}
**Her side of it.** The epic never tells us what Subhadra thought. The plan is made by her brother and Arjuna and approved by Yudhishthira; only in Krishna's later speech to the Vrishnis is the custom defended at length.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,subhadra light=arjuna,subhadra}

Subhadra went to worship the Raivataka hill and was coming back towards Dvaraka. Arjuna, in armour, sword at his side, in his golden chariot drawn by Krishna's horses Shaibya and Sugriva, came as if out hunting, seized her, put her in his chariot, and drove off towards Indraprastha. {#p6 1.222}

Her guards ran crying to the Vrishni assembly hall, Sudharma, and the officer of the court blew the golden trumpet that called all to arms. The Vrishnis left their food and their drink and came running, and when they heard, they leapt up, red-eyed with wine, calling for their chariots and their bows. {#p7 1.222}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=balarama,krishna light=balarama}

Then Balarama, tall and white as Kailasa, in blue robes and a garland of wild flowers, drunk and proud, stopped them. Why were they roaring, he asked, when Krishna sat silent? Let him say what he intended. {#p8 1.222}

Then he turned on his brother. {#p9 1.222}

> What man is there born of a respectable family that would break the plate after having dined from it! {#q3 1.222}
> — Balarama

They had honoured Arjuna for Krishna's sake, he said, and Arjuna had put his foot on their heads. Alone, he would rid the earth of Kauravas. And all the Vrishnis roared approval. {#p10 1.222}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=krishna,balarama,arjuna light=krishna}

Krishna answered them calmly. Arjuna had not insulted their family; he had honoured it. He knew they would never sell a daughter; he knew a svayamvara was uncertain; and who would accept a bride given away like an animal? So he had taken her by force, as the law allows. {#p11 1.223}

> I do not see, in all the worlds with Indra and the Rudras, the person that can by force vanquish Partha in battle, except the three-eyed god Mahadeva. {#q4 1.223}
> — Krishna

If Arjuna beat them and reached his city, Krishna said, their fame would be gone. There was no disgrace in conciliation. Go after him, make peace, and bring him back. {#p12 1.223}

They did. Arjuna came back to Dvaraka and married Subhadra there, and stayed a year among the Vrishnis. The last year of his exile he spent at the holy lake of Pushkara. Then the twelve years were over, and he went home to Indraprastha. {#p13 1.223}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=arjuna,draupadi,subhadra,kunti light=draupadi,subhadra}

He bowed to the king and to the Brahmanas, and then went to [Draupadi](draupadi). She was jealous, and said so. {#p14 1.223}

> Why tarriest thou here, O son of Kunti? Go where the daughter of the Satwata race is! A second tie always relaxeth the first one upon a faggot! {#q5 1.223}
> — Draupadi

Arjuna begged her pardon, again and again. Then he went back to Subhadra and had her go into the women's quarters not in red silk, as a queen, but dressed as a simple cowherd girl; and she looked lovelier in it. She bowed first to [Kunti](kunti), who smelt her head and blessed her. Then she went quickly to Draupadi and bowed to her. {#p15 1.223}

> I am thy maid! {#q6 1.223}
> — Subhadra

Draupadi rose and embraced her. *Let thy husband be without a foe!* And Subhadra, glad at heart, said: *So be it!* {#p16 1.223}
