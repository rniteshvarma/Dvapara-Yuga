::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura,gandhari light=gandhari}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) did not answer Krishna. He sent [Vidura](vidura) at once for [Gandhari](gandhari): perhaps she could turn their son. Vidura brought her, and the king told her that her son had walked out of the court, disregarding his elders, about to throw away kingdom and life for his lust for a kingdom. {#p1 5.129}

Gandhari did not spare her husband. {#p2 5.129}

> Indeed, O Dhritarashtra, thou so fond of thy son, art very much to be blamed for this, for knowing well his sinfulness, thou followest yet his counsel. {#q1 5.129}
> — Gandhari

He was reaping now what he had sown, she said, in giving the kingdom to a fool with wicked friends. Bring the boy back. {#p3 5.129}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=gandhari,duryodhana light=gandhari}

So Vidura brought [Duryodhana](duryodhana) in again. He came back expecting his mother's words, his eyes red as copper, breathing like a snake. {#p4 5.129}

No one wins or keeps a kingdom by wanting it, Gandhari told him. A king must conquer himself before his counsellors, and his counsellors before his enemies. {#p5 5.129}

> If not controlled, these can even slay their possessor, like unbroken and furious horses, capable of killing an unskilful driver. {#q2 5.129}
> — Gandhari

Lust and anger, held by wisdom, are like two fish in a fine net. Let him seek Krishna's protection, for if Keshava was gracious both sides would be happy. There was no good in battle, no virtue, no profit, and even victory was never sure. {#p6 5.129}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=gandhari,duryodhana,bhishma,drona light=gandhari}

His father and Bhishma and Bahlika had once given the Pandavas their share, and look: he ruled a whole earth that they had cleared of thorns. Half the earth was enough for him and his friends. {#p7 5.129}

> O son, persecution of the sons of Pandu for full thirteen years hath been enough. {#q3 5.129}
> — Gandhari

And let him not believe that Bhishma, Drona and Kripa would fight for him with all their strength. They loved both sides alike; they might give their lives for the king's bread, but they would never look on Yudhishthira with anger. {#p8 5.129}

> It is never seen in this world that men acquire wealth by avarice. Give up thy avarice then, O son, and desist, O bull of Bharat's race. {#q4 5.129}
> — Gandhari
