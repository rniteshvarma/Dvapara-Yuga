::scene{#s1 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

Arjuna did not go alone. Brahmanas learned in the Vedas, reciters of old stories, musicians and ascetics followed him, and he went among them like Indra with the Maruts, through forests and past lakes and rivers and seas, until they came to the place where the Ganga leaves the mountains. They settled there, and the Brahmanas lit their sacred fires along the bank, until the place itself became beautiful. {#p1 1.216}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,ulupi,kauravya light=ulupi,arjuna}

One day Arjuna went down into the river to bathe and to offer water to his ancestors. As he was climbing out, he was pulled down to the bottom by [Ulupi](ulupi), daughter of the Naga king. {#p2 1.216}

He found himself in the palace of [Kauravya](kauravya), king of the Nagas, and there a sacrificial fire was burning, ready for him. So he made his offerings into it, fearlessly, and Agni was pleased with him. Then he turned to the girl and smiled. *O handsome girl, what an act of rashness hast thou done!* Whose was this place, and who was she? {#p3 1.216}

She was Ulupi, she said, daughter of Kauravya of the line of Airavata. She had seen him come down to bathe and had lost her heart to him. She was unmarried; would he give himself to her? {#p4 1.216}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,ulupi light=arjuna,ulupi}

> Commanded by king Yudhishthira, O amiable one, I am undergoing the vow of Brahmacharin for twelve years. I am not free to act in any way I like. {#q1 1.216}
> — Arjuna

But he did not want to refuse her, he said, if he could do it without untruth or breaking his duty. {#p5 1.216}

Ulupi knew all about the vow. It was made for Draupadi's sake, she said, and concerned only her; it was no breach to accept another. And it was a duty to relieve the distressed; if she was refused, she would die. So, the epic says, Arjuna did all she wished, *making virtue his motive*, and stayed the night in the Naga king's palace. {#p6 1.216}

In the morning she brought him back to the bank of the Ganga, and before she left she gave him a boon: that every creature of the water would be at his mercy. {#p7 1.216}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.216}
**A son not mentioned.** Ulupi will bear Arjuna a son, Iravat, who will die at Kurukshetra. The epic does not say so here; it tells of him only much later, in the books of the war.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

Arjuna told the Brahmanas everything and went on into the Himalayas, to the peaks of Agastya, Vasishtha and Bhrigu, giving away cattle and houses as he went. Then he turned east, to the sacred waters of the Naimisha forest, the Kaushiki, Gaya and the Ganga, and the holy places of Vanga and Kalinga. At the gate of Kalinga the Brahmanas took their leave, and he went on towards the sea with only a few attendants. {#p8 1.217}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=arjuna,chitravahana,chitrangada_m light=chitrangada_m}

Going slowly along the sea-shore past the Mahendra mountains, he came to Manipura, to its king, [Chitravahana](chitravahana). There he saw the king's daughter, [Chitrangada](chitrangada_m), roaming freely in her father's palace, and wanted her. He went to her father and asked for her. {#p9 1.217}

Chitravahana told him a story. An ancestor of his, King Prabhanjana, had been childless, and had won from Shiva a boon: every king of his line would have one child. Every one of them had had a son. Chitravahana had only a daughter, and he had made her his *putrika*, his daughter to stand as a son. {#p10 1.217}

> Therefore, one amongst the sons that may be begotten upon her by thee, O Bharata, shall be the perpetuator of my race. That son is the dower for which I may give away my daughter. {#q2 1.217}
> — Chitravahana

*So be it,* said Arjuna. He married her and stayed in that city three years. When she had borne a son, he embraced her lovingly, took leave of her father, and set out again. {#p11 1.217}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.217}
**Where was Manipura?** The epic puts Chitravahana's city on the eastern sea-coast, past Kalinga and the Mahendra hills. Whether it is the Manipur of today in the north-east has long been argued over; the text itself places it by the sea.
::
