::scene{#s1 frame=duhshasana,duryodhana,karna,shakuni light=duhshasana}

[Duhshasana](duhshasana) ran to his brother. {#p1 2.73}

> That which we had won after so much trouble, the old man (our father) hath thrown away. {#q1 2.73}
> — Duhshasana

[Duryodhana](duryodhana), [Karna](karna) and [Shakuni](shakuni) went to the king in private. {#p2 2.73}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,dhritarashtra light=duryodhana}

Brihaspati taught, said Duryodhana, that enemies who do wrong by trick or force must be killed by any means. The Pandavas were angry snakes now, and a snake once roused does not forgive. Even now Arjuna was riding off, taking up his Gandiva and breathing hard; Bhima was whirling his mace; Nakula had his sword out. Which of them would forgive what was done to Draupadi? {#p3 2.73}

So let them play once more, for exile. The losers would go to the forest dressed in skins for twelve years, and spend the thirteenth hidden in some town; if they were found, twelve more years. In thirteen years the Kauravas could make allies, raise an army, and be ready. {#p4 2.73}

Dhritarashtra agreed at once. {#p5 2.73}

> Bring back the Pandavas then, indeed, even if they have gone a great way. Let them come at once again to cast dice. {#q2 2.73}
> — Dhritarashtra

Drona, Somadatta, Bahlika, Kripa, Vidura, Ashwatthama, [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu), Bhurishravas, Bhishma and [Vikarna](vikarna) all said, *Let not the play commence.* He did not listen. {#p6 2.73}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=gandhari,dhritarashtra,duryodhana light=gandhari}

Then [Gandhari](gandhari) spoke to her husband. When Duryodhana was born, she reminded him, he had howled like a jackal, and Vidura had said he should be sent from the world. Who rebuilds a broken dam, or relights a fire that has gone out? {#p7 2.74}

> Therefore, at my word, O king, abandon this wretch of our race. {#q3 2.74}
> — Gandhari

Wealth won by wickedness is soon lost, she said; what is won gently takes root and passes from father to son. {#p8 2.74}

> If the destruction of our race is come, let it take place freely. I am ill able to prevent it. {#q4 2.74}
> — Dhritarashtra

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

The messenger caught up with [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) far along the road. *The assembly is ready. Come and cast the dice.* {#p9 2.75}

> Although I know that it will prove destructive to me, yet I cannot refuse. {#q5 2.75}
> — Yudhishthira

> Although (a living) animal made of gold was an impossibility, yet Rama suffered himself to be tempted by a (golden) deer. {#q6 2.75}
> — the narrator

So he turned back with his brothers, knowing all about Shakuni's tricks, and sat down to play again. {#p10 2.75}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=shakuni,yudhishthira light=shakuni}

[Shakuni](shakuni) named the single stake: twelve years in the forest in deer-skins, the thirteenth hidden among people, twelve more if found; then each side gives the other back its kingdom. {#p11 2.75}

The hall threw up its arms. *Fie on the friends of Duryodhana*, they cried, *that they do not tell him his danger!* {#p12 2.75}

Yudhishthira heard them, and sat down anyway, out of shame and a sense of duty. *How can a king like me refuse, when summoned to dice?* Shakuni took up the dice. {#p13 2.75}

> Lo, I have won. {#q7 2.75}
> — Shakuni
