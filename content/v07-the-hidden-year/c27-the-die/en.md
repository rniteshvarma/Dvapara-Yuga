::scene{#s1 frame=arjuna,uttar light=arjuna}

Kuru soldiers came out of the forest, hungry and thirsty and terrified, their hair loose and their palms joined. *We are thy slaves.* [Arjuna](arjuna) told them to go in peace: he would not take the lives of those in distress. {#p1 4.66}

Then he spoke to [Uttara](uttar). Only he knew that the sons of Pritha lived with his father. He must not praise them in the city, or the king might hide in fear. He should say the deed was his own. {#p2 4.66}

> The feat thou hast achieved is beyond my power. I do not possess the ability to achieve it. I shall not, however, O Savyasachin, discover thee to my father, as long as thou wilt not tell me to do it. {#q1 4.66}
> — Uttara

At the Shami tree the blazing ape rose into the sky with the other creatures of the banner, the illusion melted, and Uttara's lion banner stood on the chariot again. They put back the weapons and took up their ornaments and robes. Arjuna braided his hair as before, took the reins, and became Brihannala again, Uttara's driver. Cowherds ran ahead with the news. {#p3 4.66}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=virata,yudhishthira light=virata}

[Virata](virata) came home in triumph from the Trigartas with the four Pandavas, and asked where Uttara was. The women told him: the prince had gone out alone, with only Brihannala, against Bhishma, Kripa, Karna, Duryodhana, Drona and Drona's son. The king was stricken and sent troops after him. *I myself think that he who hath got a person of the neuter sex for his car-driver is not alive.* {#p4 4.67}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) smiled. With Brihannala driving, he said, the prince could beat gods and Asuras together. Then the messengers arrived: the Kurus beaten, the cattle back, the prince well. Virata's hair stood on end with joy. He ordered the roads decked with flags, music, the princess and her maidens to go out and meet her brother. {#p5 4.67}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=virata,yudhishthira,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

> O Sairindhri, fetch the dice. And, O Kanka, let the play commence. {#q2 4.67}
> — Virata

A man filled with joy should not play with a cunning gambler, said Kanka; he had seen what dice did to Yudhishthira. But if the king wished, they would play. As they played, Virata boasted that his son had beaten the Kauravas. {#p6 4.67}

> Why should not he conquer that hath Vrihannala for his charioteer? {#q3 4.67}
> — Yudhishthira

Virata grew angry: how dare he compare a eunuch with his son? Yudhishthira said it again: where Bhishma and Drona and Karna and Duryodhana stood together, who but Brihannala could face them all? {#p7 4.67}

> Saying this, the king inflamed with anger forcibly struck Yudhishthira in the face with a dice, and reproached him angrily, saying, 'Let it not occur again!' {#q4 4.67}

Blood ran from his nose, and Yudhishthira caught it in his hands before it touched the ground. He glanced at [Draupadi](draupadi). She understood, brought a golden vessel of water, and caught the blood in it. {#p8 4.67}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,uttar,arjuna light=yudhishthira}

At that moment the porter announced that Uttara was at the gate with Brihannala. Bring them both in, said the king. But Yudhishthira whispered to the porter. {#p9 4.67}

> Let Uttara enter alone; Vrihannala must not come in. Such is the vow of that hero of mighty arms that whoever causeth a wound on my person or sheddeth my blood except in battle, shall not live. {#q5 4.67}
> — Yudhishthira
