::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

At every sacred place, Lomasa told the story of the place. This is a small sample of what the Pandavas heard on their long road. {#p1 3.96}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

At Agastya's hermitage, Yudhishthira asked about the demon Vatapi. There had been a demon named Ilvala, Lomasa said, who hated Brahmanas. He would turn his brother Vatapi into a ram, cook him, and feed him to a Brahmana guest; then he would call his brother's name, and Vatapi would come back to life and burst out of the guest's side, laughing. {#p2 3.96}

The great sage Agastya, needing wealth for his wife Lopamudra, came to Ilvala's house with three kings. The kings turned pale when they saw the ram. *Yield ye not to grief, I will eat up the great Asura*, said Agastya, and ate the whole of it. Ilvala called and called; nothing came out of Agastya but wind. Vatapi had been digested. {#p3 3.99}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

On the river Kaushiki, Lomasa told of Rishyashringa, a sage's son who had grown up in the forest and had never seen a woman. When the kingdom of Anga had no rain, its king sent a courtesan to bring the young man to his city, for rain followed him wherever he went. {#p4 3.110}

When she had gone, Rishyashringa told his father that a religious student had come to the hermitage, with fragrant hair tied with strings of gold, a voice like a blackbird's, jingling ornaments on his wrists and feet, two hairless balls of flesh under his throat, and a ball in his hand that he bounced to the sky. His father told him it was a Rakshasa. {#p5 3.112 3.113}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,parashurama,jamadagni light=yudhishthira}

On the Mahendra mountain they met a disciple of [Parashurama](parashurama), who told how, after Parashurama killed the thousand-armed Kartavirya, Kartavirya's kinsmen came to the hermitage while he was away and shot his father [Jamadagni](jamadagni), who would not fight because he was at his penances; and how Parashurama came home carrying firewood, found his father dead, and swept the Kshatriyas from the earth seven times over. Then Parashurama himself came, and Yudhishthira honoured him. {#p6 3.116 3.117}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

They heard many more. How the Ganga came down from heaven for King Bhagiratha, and Shiva stood beneath to bear her fall. How the old sage Chyavana, buried in an anthill, was blinded by the princess Sukanya, married her, and was made young again by the twin Ashvins. How King Ushinara gave a hawk his own flesh to save a dove that had come to him for refuge. How the boy Ashtavakra, bent in eight places by his father's curse, out-argued the greatest scholar at King Janaka's court. {#p7 3.107 3.122 3.131 3.134}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=3.117}
**Seven times or twenty-one.** The disciple here says Parashurama destroyed the Kshatriyas seven times; the familiar number, twenty-one, comes from other passages and later tellings.
::

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=3.96,3.134}
**The tirtha tales.** Around fifty sections of the Vana Parva are tales like these, attached to the places the Pandavas visit. Many are famous in their own right. This volume keeps only their outlines so as not to lose the main thread; a companion volume of the epic's inner stories will tell them in full.
