What is Brahman, [Arjuna](arjuna) asked, and the self within, and action, and the rest; and how is Krishna to be known at the hour of death by those who have mastered themselves? {#p1 6.32}

Brahman is the supreme, the imperishable, [Krishna](krishna) answered. Whatever a man remembers as he leaves the body, to that he goes, having always dwelt on it. {#p2 6.32}

> Remembering me alone in (his) last moments, he that, casting off his body, departeth (hence), cometh into my essence. {#q1 6.32}
> — Krishna

> Therefore, think of me at all times, and engage in battle. {#q2 6.32}
> — Krishna

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna light=krishna}

The one who goes forth closing all the doors of the body, holding the mind in the heart and the breath between the brows, saying the single syllable *Om*, and thinking of him, reaches the highest goal. All the worlds up to Brahma's come round again; but whoever reaches Krishna is not born again. {#p3 6.32}

A day of Brahma lasts a thousand ages and his night a thousand more; at dawn all things come forth from the unmanifest, and at nightfall they dissolve into it, again and again. But beyond that unmanifest is another, eternal, which does not perish when all things perish; that is his supreme abode, from which no one returns. {#p4 6.32}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,arjuna light=krishna}

There are two paths for those who leave this life. Fire, light, day, the bright fortnight, the six months of the sun's northern course: by that path the knowers of Brahman go to Brahman. Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern course: by that path the yogi reaches the light of the moon, and returns. {#p5 6.32}

Knowing these two paths, no yogi is deluded. So at all times, Arjuna, be a yogi; whatever merit lies in the Vedas, in sacrifice, austerity and gifts, he passes beyond it all to the supreme and first abode. {#p6 6.32}
