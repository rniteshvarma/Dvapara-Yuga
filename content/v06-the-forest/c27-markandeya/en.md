::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,satyabhama,arjuna,draupadi,yudhishthira light=krishna}

In Kamyaka a Brahmana told them that Krishna would come soon, and the sage Markandeya too. As he spoke, [Krishna](krishna) arrived in his chariot with [Satyabhama](satyabhama). He bowed to Yudhishthira and Bhima, embraced [Arjuna](arjuna) again and again after so long, and comforted Draupadi, and Satyabhama embraced her. {#p1 3.182}

He told [Draupadi](draupadi) about her sons. Drupada and her brothers offered them a kingdom, but the boys liked best to be at Dvaraka, learning arms with Abhimanyu; Subhadra looked after them as Draupadi herself would, and Pradyumna was their teacher. And Krishna told Yudhishthira that the Yadavas were ready to fight the Kurus whenever his promise was fulfilled. {#p2 3.182}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,yudhishthira,narada light=krishna}

Then the sage Markandeya arrived. He had lived many thousands of years and devoted all of them to austerity. {#p3 3.182}

> Signs of old age he had none; and deathless he was, and endued with beauty and generous and many good qualities. And he looked like one only twenty-five years old. {#q1 3.182}
> — the narrator

Krishna asked him to tell them the old stories, and the laws by which kings and women and sages live. [Narada](narada) came too. *Wait a moment*, said Markandeya. *A great deal will be narrated.* {#p4 3.182}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna light=krishna}

Over many days he told them a great deal: of Manu and the fish that grew too big for every pond and saved him from the flood; of the four ages, and how in the Kali age men would grow short-lived and greedy, and how at its end a Brahmana named Kalki would be born in the town of Shambhala to set the world right. {#p5 3.186 3.187 3.190}

And he told them what he had seen himself, at the end of an age. {#p6 3.187}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna light=krishna}

When the world was destroyed, he said, and everything was one flood, he wandered alone over the water until he was exhausted. Then he saw a great banyan tree, and on one of its branches, on a bed, sat a child with a face like the moon and eyes like lotus petals, the Shrivatsa mark on his chest. *O Markandeya … rest thou here as long as thou wishest. … entering within my body, rest thou there.* {#p7 3.187}

The child opened his mouth, and Markandeya found himself inside, and saw there the whole earth: its cities and kingdoms, the Ganga and the Yamuna and every river, the seas, the mountains, the gods and the demons. He wandered in the child's body for many centuries, living on fruits, and never found its end. Then he was blown out of the child's mouth by a gust of wind, and saw the child on the banyan bough again, having swallowed the universe. {#p8 3.187}

The child told him he was Narayana. {#p9 3.188}

> And the Deity, of eyes large as lotus leaves, seen by me, in days of yore is this tiger among men, this Janardana who hath become thy relative! {#q2 3.188}
> — Markandeya

The Pandavas and Draupadi bowed down to Krishna, and he comforted them all with sweet words. {#p10 3.188}

**Markandeya's discourses.** The *Markandeya-samasya* runs to more than forty sections: the story of the flood, the ages of the world, the greatness of Brahmanas and of faithful wives, the birth of the war-god Skanda, and much else, ending around section 230. This chapter keeps only its frame and its most famous vision; the rest belongs to a companion volume.
