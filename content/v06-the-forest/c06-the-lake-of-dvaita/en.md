Twelve years, said [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) to his brothers. Find us a place where we can live them well: plenty of birds and deer, fruit and flowers, and good people nearby. {#p1 3.24}

You know the sages better than any of us, [Arjuna](arjuna) answered, so we will live wherever you like. But there is a lake here called Dvaitavana, full of flowers and birds and holy water. Shall we stay there? {#p2 3.24}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

So they went to the Dvaita woods with their crowd of Brahmanas. It was the end of summer, and the sal and palm and mango and kadamba trees were in flower. Peacocks and cuckoos sang from the treetops, herds of elephants wandered through, and ascetics in bark and matted hair came out to meet the king. {#p3 3.24}

Yudhishthira sat down at the foot of a great flowering tree, as his father Pandu once had. The brothers and Draupadi, tired, sat around him. *That mighty tree … with those five illustrious bowmen … looked like a mountain with (five) huge elephants resting on its side.* {#p4 3.24}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,dhaumya,draupadi light=yudhishthira}

They settled there, and [Dhaumya](dhaumya) performed the rites for them as a father would. One day the ancient sage Markandeya came as a guest. He looked at Draupadi and the brothers among the ascetics, and smiled. {#p5 3.25}

All the other sages were grieved to see him here, said Yudhishthira, hurt. Why was Markandeya smiling? {#p6 3.25}

> Beholding to-day the calamity, I recollect Rama, the son of Dasaratha, devoted to truth! {#q1 3.25}
> — Markandeya

He had seen Rama himself, bow in hand, on the Rishyamuka hills, a man equal to Indra living in the forest at his father's command. And he said it again and again, about Rama and the old kings, the seven sages in the sky, even the elephants who keep the laws of their kind: {#p7 3.25}

> Therefore should no one act unrighteously, saying,—I am mighty! {#q2 3.25}
> — Markandeya

Yudhishthira, he said, would get his kingdom back through his own strength when his exile was done. And he went on north. {#p8 3.25}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.25}
**Rama's shadow.** This is the first time the Vana Parva sets the Pandavas beside Rama, the other prince who went into the forest for the sake of a promise. It will not be the last. Later in the forest, Markandeya tells Yudhishthira the whole Ramayana.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna light=yudhishthira}

The Dvaita woods filled with Brahmanas, and the lake rang with recitation of the Vedas mixed with the twang of the Pandavas' bows. One evening, at the hour when the sacred fires were lit, the sage Baka of the Dalbhya family told Yudhishthira what that sound meant. {#p9 3.26}

Brahmana and Kshatriya together, he said, are like fire and wind: each makes the other stronger, and together they burn down every enemy. A Kshatriya without a Brahmana grows weak like an elephant without its driver. Keep wise Brahmanas near you. And the sages of the forest all honoured Yudhishthira, as the Rishis honour Indra. {#p10 3.26}
