::scene{#s1 frame=duryodhana,dhritarashtra light=duryodhana}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) was furious. The gods, he said, do not act from desire or anger; if they took sides, the Pandavas would never have suffered thirteen years. And his own power was greater than the gods'. {#p1 5.61}

> Charmed by me, Agni will be instantly extinguished, even if he blazes up all around for consuming all creatures. {#q1 5.61}
> — Duryodhana

He could join split mountains, stop storms, harden water for chariots to cross. Whatever he said would be, had been. *Never had I, O king, praised myself before, for to praise one's own self is mean.* {#p2 5.61}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=karna,bhishma,parashurama light=karna}

[Karna](karna) spoke next, to cheer the Kurus. Rama had found out how Karna got the Brahma weapon from him by false pretences, and cursed him: when his hour came, he would forget it. But he still had the weapon, and his hour had not come. He would kill the Panchalas, the Matsyas and the sons of Pritha in the blink of an eye. {#p3 5.62}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.62}
Karna's training under [Parashurama](parashurama), whom he deceived by passing himself off as a Brahmana, and the curse that followed, are told more fully in the Shanti Parva.
::

> What sayest thou, O Karna? Thy intellect is clouded at the approach of thy hour. {#q2 5.62}
> — Bhishma

Indra's dart, said [Bhishma](bhishma), would be shattered by Krishna's discus; the serpent-mouthed arrow Karna worshipped with garlands would perish with him under Arjuna's shafts. {#p4 5.62}

> I lay down my weapons. The Grandsire will henceforth behold me in court only and not in battle. After thou hast become quiet, the rulers of the earth will behold my prowess in this world. {#q3 5.62}
> — Karna

And he left the court and went home. Bhishma laughed. So much for the man who had promised to kill thousands. *That moment, when, representing himself as a Brahmana unto the holy and blameless Rama, Vikartana's son obtained that weapon, that vile wretch lost both his virtue and asceticism.* {#p5 5.62}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana,vidura light=vidura}

The Pandavas were men like any other, said Duryodhana; he, Karna and Duhshasana would kill them, and drag them *like hunters dragging a herd of deer by a net*. [Vidura](vidura) answered by praising self-restraint: its marks are forgiveness, steadiness, harmlessness, truthfulness, gentleness and contentment; a man of self-restraint is *as grave as the ocean*. {#p6 5.63}
