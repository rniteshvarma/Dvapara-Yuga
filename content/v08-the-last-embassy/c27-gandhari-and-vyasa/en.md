Duryodhana paid no heed, and the kings rose and left. Alone, [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) asked [Sanjaya](sanjaya) in secret which side would perish. {#p1 5.67}

> I will not say anything to thee in secret, O king, for then thou mayst entertain ill-feelings towards me. {#q1 5.67}
> — Sanjaya

Let the king bring [Vyasa](vyasa), his father, and Queen [Gandhari](gandhari). Vidura brought them in, and Vyasa told Sanjaya to tell the king everything. {#p2 5.67}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=sanjaya,krishna light=krishna}

So Sanjaya spoke of [Krishna](krishna). His discus, invisible to the Kurus, five cubits across, took any size at his will. He had overcome Naraka, Shambara, Kamsa and Shishupala *in seeming playfulness*. {#p3 5.68}

> If the whole universe be placed on one scale and Janardana on the other, even then Janardana will outweigh the entire universe. {#q2 5.68}
> — Sanjaya

> Wherever there is truthfulness, wherever virtue, wherever modesty, wherever simplicity, even there is Govinda. And thither where Krishna is, success must be. {#q3 5.68}
> — Sanjaya

How did Sanjaya know this, and the king not? He had knowledge, Sanjaya said, and the king had none; and he had come to it through faith. {#p4 5.69}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,duryodhana,gandhari light=gandhari}

Seek Krishna's protection, Dhritarashtra told his son. {#p5 5.69}

> If the divine son of Devaki united in friendship with Arjuna, were to slay all mankind, I cannot, even then, resign myself to Kesava. {#q4 5.69}
> — Duryodhana

> Thou covetous wretch that disregardest the commands of the aged, abandoning thy father and myself and giving up prosperity and life, enhancing the joy of thy foes, and afflicting me with deep distress, thou wilt, O fool, remember thy father's words, when struck by Bhimasena, thou wilt bite the dust. {#q5 5.69}
> — Gandhari

***

::scene{#s4 frame=vyasa,dhritarashtra,sanjaya light=vyasa}

Vyasa told the king to listen to Sanjaya, who knew Krishna; men who are not content with what they have die again and again, *like blind men (falling into pits) when led by the blind*. And Sanjaya told the king the meaning of Krishna's names, Vasudeva, Vishnu, Madhusudana, Pundarikaksha, Janardana, Hrishikesha, Govinda and the rest, and said that Krishna himself would come to Hastinapura to prevent the slaughter of the Kurus. {#p6 5.70}

> O Sanjaya, I envy those gifted with sight, who will behold before them that Vasudeva whose body endued with great beauty shineth with effulgence, illuminating the cardinal and subsidiary points of the compass. {#q6 5.71}
> — Dhritarashtra
