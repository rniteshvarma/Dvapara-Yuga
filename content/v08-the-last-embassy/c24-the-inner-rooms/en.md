::scene{#s1 frame=sanjaya,krishna,arjuna,draupadi,satyabhama light=sanjaya}

Then tell me what Krishna and Arjuna said, said the king. [Sanjaya](sanjaya) described how he had found them. {#p1 5.59}

He had gone into the inner apartments with his eyes lowered, where not even Abhimanyu or the twins could go. There sat [Krishna](krishna) and [Arjuna](arjuna) on a golden dais, garlanded, in fine robes, merry with wine, with [Draupadi](draupadi) and [Satyabhama](satyabhama). {#p2 5.59}

> And I beheld Kesava's feet resting upon Arjuna's lap while those of the high-souled Arjuna rested upon the laps of Krishna and Satyabhama. {#q1 5.59}
> — Sanjaya

Arjuna pointed him to a golden footstool; Sanjaya only touched it and sat on the floor. Seeing the two of them together, *Indra and Vishnu seated together*, a great fear came over him, and he knew Yudhishthira's wishes would be fulfilled. {#p3 5.59}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,sanjaya light=krishna}

Arjuna lifted Krishna's foot from his lap with a hand scarred by the bowstring, and asked him to speak. Krishna sat up straight *like Indra's banner*. {#p4 5.59}

> Do ye celebrate diverse sacrifices, making presents unto the Brahmanas, and rejoice with your sons and wives, for a great danger threatens ye? {#q2 5.59}
> — Krishna

> That debt, accumulating with time, hath not yet been paid off by me. {#q3 5.59}
> — Krishna

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=5.59}
Ganguli's text is terse here: Krishna speaks of a debt, with tears, "while I was at a distance". In fuller readings of this line, the debt is Draupadi's cry to him in the dicing hall when he was far away, which he has not yet repaid.
::

Who would challenge Arjuna with Krishna beside him, unless his life were already over? Virata's city was proof enough. {#p5 5.59}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,duryodhana light=dhritarashtra}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) weighed both sides, and saw that the Pandavas had strength both human and divine. Agni would remember Khandava; Dharma and the other gods would come to their sons. And Arjuna, who takes five hundred arrows at once and shoots them unseen, he could see already at his work. {#p6 5.60}

> I am for peace with the Parthas and not for war. O child, I always deem the Pandavas mightier than the Kurus. {#q4 5.60}
> — Dhritarashtra
