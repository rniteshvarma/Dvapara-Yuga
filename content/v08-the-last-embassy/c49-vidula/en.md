::scene{#s1 frame=kunti,krishna light=kunti}

To show what she meant, [Kunti](kunti) told [Krishna](krishna) an old story for Yudhishthira: the conversation of Vidula and her son. {#p1 5.133}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.133}
Vidula's son is called Sanjaya, but he is not Dhritarashtra's charioteer. The story runs through four sections (5.133–136) and is told here in outline; the full text belongs in the companion volume of stories.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti light=kunti}

Vidula was a high-born lady, learned, proud, a little quick to anger, and devoted to the duties of the Kshatriya. Her son had been beaten by the king of the Sindhus and lay at home in despair. She stood over him. {#p2 5.133}

> Like a brand of Tinduka wood, blaze up even for a moment, but never smoulder from desire, like a flameless fire of paddy chaff. It is better to blaze up for a moment than smoke for ever and ever. {#q1 5.133}
> — Vidula

Rise, coward, she said. Do not lie there like a corpse, delighting your enemies. Little streams fill with a little water, and a mouse's paws with a little food; only a coward is satisfied with a little. Better die pulling a snake's fangs than die miserable like a dog. {#p3 5.133}

The son answered: if she lost him, what use would the whole earth be to her, or her jewels, or her life? {#p4 5.133}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=kunti light=kunti}

She did not soften. His name was Sanjaya, *the victorious*; let him be true to it. A Brahmana had once said of him as a child that he would fall into great distress and rise again, and she held to that. The Sindhu king had followers, but they were weak; his open enemies would come over the moment Sanjaya showed his strength. {#p5 5.134}

> If I have to say—Nay—to a Brahmana, my heart will burst, for neither I nor my husband ever said—Nay—to a Brahmana before. {#q2 5.134}
> — Vidula

> In the absence of boats, be thou our boat. Make for us a place where place there is none. Revive us that are dead. {#q3 5.134}
> — Vidula

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kunti light=kunti}

*Your heart is made of steel*, the son said; she spoke to her only son as if she were not his mother. A mother's love that kept silent while her son sank into disgrace, she answered, would be no better than a she-ass's for its foal. {#p6 5.135}

Without wealth or allies, he asked, how could he win? Success is never certain, she said; but the man who does nothing has only one result, failure, while the man who acts has two. Gather the angry, the greedy, the humbled and the proud to your side; pay them before it is due, and speak sweetly. {#p7 5.135}

Then she told him what she had kept back: there were treasure-houses that only she knew of, and friends who would never turn from battle. All this she had said to test him and to rouse him. {#p8 5.136}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=kunti light=kunti}

The son's despair left him at once. He had been drawing her out, he said, so that he might hear more. {#p9 5.136}

> Pierced by the wordy arrows of his mother, the son roused himself like a steed of proud mettle and achieved all that his mother had pointed out. {#q4 5.136}

This story is called *Jaya*, Kunti said, and should be told to a king overcome by his enemies; a woman with child who hears it often will bear a hero. {#p10 5.136}
