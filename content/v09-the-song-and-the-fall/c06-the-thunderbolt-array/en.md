Seeing the eleven Kuru armies arrayed, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) turned to [Arjuna](arjuna). Brihaspati taught that the few should be packed close and the many spread wide; against so many, they should form the needle-mouthed array. Arjuna answered that he would make the *Vajra*, the thunderbolt array, which Indra himself designed, and [Bhima](bhima) would fight at its point. {#p1 6.19}

> The man breathes not in the world who would bear to cast his eyes upon that bull among men, Vrikodara of fierce deeds, when he is angry. {#q1 6.19}
> — Arjuna

So Bhima led, with Dhrishtadyumna, the twins and Dhrishtaketu; the twins guarded his wheels, the sons of Draupadi and Abhimanyu his back. Shikhandi came behind, guarded by Arjuna, *for the destruction of Bhishma*. Yudhishthira stood in the centre among his elephants, and above every standard on both sides rose the one gigantic ape on Arjuna's chariot. {#p2 6.19}

At dawn a wind blew with drops of rain from a cloudless sky; dry winds drove pebbles along the ground; meteors fell eastward and broke against the rising sun, which came up without splendour; and the earth cracked open with a roar. {#p3 6.19}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,bhishma,drona light=bhishma}

Both armies were equally glad, Sanjaya said. The Kurus faced west, the Pandavas east; the Kurus looked like the army of the Danavas, the Pandavas like the gods. The wind blew from behind the Pandavas into the Kuru faces, the beasts of prey howled at the Kurus, and the Kuru elephants could not bear the smell of the Pandava elephants. {#p4 6.20}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) rode a lotus-coloured elephant in the centre, Shakuni behind him with his mountaineers. [Bhishma](bhishma), all in white, looked like a white mountain at the head of all; [Drona](drona) on a golden car with red horses guarded the rear like Indra; Kripa held the north with the Shakas and Yavanas, Kritavarma the south. Ten thousand chariots of the Samshaptakas, sworn to kill Arjuna or die, went out with the Trigartas. {#p5 6.20}

> Illimitable as thy army was, O ruler of men, it looked terrible; but the army of the Pandavas, although it was not such (in number), yet seemed to me to be very large and invincible since Kesava and Arjuna were its leader. {#q2 6.20}
> — Sanjaya
