::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi,narada light=yudhishthira}

At Indraprastha [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) ruled justly, with his brothers to help him. One day, as they sat in state, the sage [Narada](narada) came to them. Yudhishthira gave him his own seat and the guest-offering with his own hands, and sent for [Draupadi](draupadi); she came, bowed at the sage's feet and stood veiled with folded hands, and Narada blessed her and sent her back inside. {#p1 1.210}

Then he spoke to the brothers alone. {#p2 1.210}

> The renowned princess of Panchala is the wedded wife of you all. Establish a rule amongst yourselves so that disunion may not arise amongst you. {#q1 1.210}
> — Narada

There were once two brothers, he said, who shared everything: one kingdom, one house, one bed, one seat, one dish. And they killed each other for the sake of a woman. {#p3 1.210}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=i10312_sunda,i11181_upasunda,brahma light=i10312_sunda,i11181_upasunda}

They were [Sunda](i10312_sunda) and [Upasunda](i11181_upasunda), Asura sons of Nikumbha, of the line of Hiranyakashipu. They were so alike, the sage said, that they seemed one person divided in two; they never went anywhere apart. {#p4 1.211}

They wanted to rule the three worlds, and went to the Vindhya mountains to do penance: matted hair, bark clothes, living on air, standing on their toes, throwing pieces of their own flesh into the fire, until the mountains steamed with the heat of it. The gods sent riches and beautiful women to tempt them, and then a vision of their own mothers and wives fleeing from a Rakshasa, crying for help. They did not move. {#p5 1.211}

At last [Brahma](brahma) came and offered them a boon. They asked for every weapon, every illusion, great strength, any form at will, and immortality. Immortality he would not give. So they asked for this instead: {#p6 1.211}

> O Grandsire, let us have no fear then from any created thing, mobile or immobile, in the three worlds, except only from each other! {#q2 1.211}
> — Sunda and Upasunda

***

::scene{#s3 frame=i10312_sunda,i11181_upasunda light=i10312_sunda,i11181_upasunda}

With that boon they took the heaven of Indra, and the worlds of the Yakshas, the Rakshasas and the Nagas, and then turned on the earth. Brahmanas and kings feed the gods with their sacrifices, they said; kill them all. {#p7 1.212}

They threw the sacred fires into the water, and the sages' curses bounced off them like arrows off stone. They took the shapes of mad elephants, lions and tigers to hunt down the sages hiding in caves. Sacrifice stopped, and study, and trade, and marriage, and farming; the earth was covered with bones. Then the two brothers settled down at Kurukshetra with no rival anywhere. {#p8 1.212}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=brahma,i10835_tilottama,shiva,indra light=i10835_tilottama}

The sages went to Brahma. He sent for Vishvakarma, the divine craftsman, and told him to make a woman who would captivate every heart. Vishvakarma gathered every beautiful thing into her, a tiny portion of every gem, and Brahma named her [Tilottama](i10835_tilottama). {#p9 1.213}

Before she left she walked round the assembly of the gods. Shiva, the epic says, wanted so much to keep looking at her that he grew a face on each side; and Indra grew a thousand eyes. Everyone thought the work as good as done. {#p10 1.213}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=i10312_sunda,i11181_upasunda,i10835_tilottama light=i10312_sunda,i11181_upasunda,i10835_tilottama}

The brothers were in the Vindhya hills, drinking, with women dancing for them, when Tilottama came by in red silk, picking wild flowers. They both got up at once. Sunda took her by the right hand. {#p11 1.214}

> She is my wife, and therefore your superior. {#q3 1.214}
> — Sunda

> She is my wife, and therefore your sister-in-law. {#q4 1.214}
> — Upasunda

*She is mine, not yours!* They forgot all their love for each other, took up their maces, and struck, each crying *I was first!*, and both fell dead in their blood, like two suns knocked out of the sky. The only death the boon allowed them, they gave each other. {#p12 1.214}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=narada,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=narada,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva}

*Therefore,* Narada said, *make some such arrangement that you may not quarrel with one another for the sake of Draupadi.* {#p13 1.214}

So, in front of him, the brothers made a rule. If any one of them, while Draupadi was alone with another brother, saw them together, he must go into the forest for twelve years and live there as a celibate. {#p14 1.214}

Narada was pleased with them, and went on his way. And because of that rule, the epic says, no quarrel ever arose between them. {#p15 1.214}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.214}
**A rule waiting to be broken.** The very next part of the epic is called *Arjuna's Exile in the Forest*. Rules in the Mahabharata are rarely made only to sit quietly on the page.
::
