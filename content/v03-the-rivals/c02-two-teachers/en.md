::scene{#s1 frame=sharadvan,i4789_janapadi,indra light=sharadvan}

The great sage Gautama had a son named [Sharadvan](sharadvan), who was born, the epic says, with arrows in his hand. He had no gift for the other sciences, only for weapons, and he won them by the same hard austerities by which other Brahmana students win the Vedas. {#p1 1.130}

His power frightened [Indra](indra) himself. So the king of the gods sent an apsara, [Janapadi](i4789_janapadi), to break his penance. She found him in his forest hermitage, bow and arrows at hand, and stood before him alone, in a single piece of cloth. {#p2 1.130}

Sharadvan's bow and arrows slipped from his hands and he shook all over. He did not yield; he had the strength of an ascetic, and he held firm. But his seed fell from him unknowing, and leaving his bow, his arrows and his deer-skin behind, he fled from her. The seed fell on a clump of heath and divided in two, and from it twins were born. {#p3 1.130}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=shantanu,kripa,kripi light=shantanu,kripa,kripi}

A soldier with King [Shantanu](shantanu), out hunting in those woods, came upon the two children lying beside a bow, arrows and a deer-skin, and guessed they were the children of some Brahmana skilled in arms. He brought them to the king. {#p4 1.130}

> Let these become my children. {#q1 1.130}
> — Shantanu

Shantanu took them home out of pity, performed the rites for them and raised them, and named them for the pity that had saved them: [Kripa](kripa) and [Kripi](kripi). {#p5 1.130}

Their father learnt by his inner sight where they were. He came to the king, told him who they were, and taught Kripa the four branches of the science of arms and all their secrets. Kripa became a master in his turn; and the hundred sons of Dhritarashtra, the Pandavas, the Yadavas and princes from many lands came to learn from him. {#p6 1.130}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.130}
**Shantanu again.** The king who found the twins is Bhishma's father, the same Shantanu who once met a goddess on a riverbank. Kripa, who will teach Shantanu's great-grandsons, was raised in Shantanu's own palace.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhishma,drona light=bhishma,drona}

But [Bhishma](bhishma) wanted something more for his grandsons: a teacher of energy and godlike might, a perfect master of arms. He found one in [Drona](drona), the son of Bharadvaja. How Drona came to Hastinapura is a longer story, and the epic goes back to tell it. {#p7 1.131}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bharadvaja,ghritachi,drona,i253_agnivesha light=bharadvaja,drona}

At the source of the Ganga lived a great sage, [Bharadvaja](bharadvaja). One day, going down to the river to bathe, he saw the apsara [Ghritachi](ghritachi) rising from the water, and her loose garment slipped. Desire overcame him and his seed fell, and he caught it in a vessel called a *drona*. From that vessel the child [Drona](drona) was born. {#p8 1.131}

Drona studied all the Vedas. From [Agnivesha](i253_agnivesha), a sage born of fire, who had himself been taught by Bharadvaja, he received the weapon of fire, the Agneya. {#p9 1.131}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=drona,drupada,prishata light=drona,drupada}

Bharadvaja had a friend, a king named [Prishata](prishata), and Prishata had a son, [Drupada](drupada). Every day the young prince came to Bharadvaja's hermitage to play with Drona and study with him. {#p10 1.131}

Then Prishata died and Drupada became king of the northern Panchalas. Bharadvaja too went to heaven, and Drona stayed on in his father's hermitage, giving himself to austerities. {#p11 1.131}

::aside{#a2 kind=versions src=1.131,1.133}
**Where they studied.** Here the boys grow up together at Bharadvaja's hermitage. When Drona tells the story himself, two sections later, he says they lived together as students at the hermitage of Agnivesha. The epic keeps both; either way they were friends from boyhood.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=drona,kripi,ashwatthama light=drona,kripi,ashwatthama}

At his father's bidding, and wanting a son, Drona married Kripi, the sister of Kripa, a woman always at her sacred fires and her vows. She bore him a son, and as soon as he was born the child cried out like the neighing of the divine horse [Ucchaihshravas](i11043_ucchaihshravas). A voice in the sky named him. {#p12 1.131}

> The voice of this child hath, like the neighing of a horse, been audible all around. The child shall, therefore, be known by the name of Aswatthaman. {#q2 1.131}
> — a voice from the sky

Drona was overjoyed with his son, [Ashwatthama](ashwatthama), and went on living in the hermitage, studying the science of arms. {#p13 1.131}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=drona,parashurama light=parashurama,drona}

Then Drona heard that [Parashurama](parashurama), the son of Jamadagni, the greatest of all who bear weapons, was giving all his wealth away to Brahmanas before going into the forest. Drona wanted his weapons, and his knowledge. He went with his disciples to the Mahendra mountains, bowed his head to the ground and worshipped Rama's feet. {#p14 1.131}

> Know me to have sprung from Bharadwaja, but not in any woman's womb! I am a Brahmana of high birth, Drona by name, come to thee with the desire of obtaining thy wealth. {#q3 1.131}
> — Drona

Parashurama had little left. His gold he had given away, and the whole earth to the edge of the sea he had given to Kashyapa. He had only his body and his weapons, he said; Drona could have either. {#p15 1.131}

> O son of Bhrigu, it behoveth thee to give me all thy weapons together with the mysteries of hurling and recalling them. {#q4 1.131}
> — Drona

*So be it,* said Parashurama, and gave him all of them: the whole science of arms with its rules and mysteries. Glad at heart, thinking himself richly repaid, Drona set out for the city of his old friend Drupada. {#p16 1.131}

::aside{#a3 kind=note src=1.131}
**Parashurama's pupils.** Parashurama is the warrior-sage who, the epic tells elsewhere, taught [Bhishma](bhishma) in his youth, and who will one day teach Karna too. Drona now carries his weapons into the Kuru court.
::

***

::scene{#s8 frame=drona,drupada light=drupada,drona}

He stood before the king of the Panchalas and said: *Know me for thy friend.* {#p17 1.132}

Drupada, drunk with the pride of wealth, could not bear to hear it. He frowned, his eyes went red, and he answered. {#p18 1.132}

> O thou of dull apprehension, great kings can never be friends with such luckless and indigent wights as thou! It is true there had been friendship between thee and me before, for we were then both equally circumstanced. But Time that impaireth everything in its course, impaireth friendship also. {#q5 1.132}
> — Drupada

Friendship can be only between equals, Drupada said: not between a poor man and a rich one, a learned man and an unlettered one, a hero and a coward, a king and one who is not a king. Their friendship had been for a purpose, and the purpose was gone. {#p19 1.132}

Drona was filled with wrath. He thought for a moment and made up his mind. Then he left the Panchala capital in haste and turned towards the city of the Kurus, the city named after the elephant. {#p20 1.132}
