::scene{#s1 frame=i239_agni,i11618_varuna light=i11618_varuna}

[Agni](i239_agni) thought of [Varuna](i11618_varuna), lord of the waters and guardian of one quarter of the sky, and Varuna appeared at once. {#p1 1.227}

> Give me without loss of time that bow and quiver, and that ape-bannered car also, which were obtained from king Soma. Partha will achieve a great task with Gandiva, and Vasudeva also with the discus! {#q1 1.227}
> — Agni

*Well, I am giving them,* said Varuna. {#p2 1.227}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,i11618_varuna light=arjuna}

First the bow, Gandiva. It could not be harmed by any weapon; it was worth a hundred thousand bows; gods and Gandharvas worshipped it. Then two quivers that would never be empty. {#p3 1.227}

Then a chariot. It had been made by Vishvakarma, the craftsman of the gods; it was the chariot from which Soma had once defeated the Danavas. Its horses were white as silver clouds, Gandharva-born, swift as the wind or the mind. Its wheels thundered, and it shone like an evening cloud lit by the setting sun. On its golden flagstaff sat a great ape, fierce as a lion or a tiger, that seemed ready to burn whatever it looked at. {#p4 1.227}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

[Arjuna](arjuna), in armour, sword at his side, his fingers in leather guards, walked round the chariot, bowed to the gods and climbed into it. He took up Gandiva, the first of bows, made by Brahma long ago, and was filled with joy. He bowed to Agni and strung it by force, and everyone who heard the sound of that stringing shook with fear. {#p5 1.227}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.227}
**The ape on the banner.** Later tradition says the ape is Hanuman, who promised his brother Bhima to sit on Arjuna's flag. Here the epic says only *a celestial ape*. Hanuman's meeting with Bhima comes later, in the forest years.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna,i239_agni,i11618_varuna light=krishna}

Then Agni gave [Krishna](krishna) a discus, a fiery weapon with an iron shaft at its centre, which became his favourite. {#p6 1.227}

> And, O Madhava, hurled by thee in battle at thy foes, this weapon will irresistibly slay the enemy and again come back into thy hands. {#q2 1.227}
> — Agni

And Varuna gave Krishna a mace named Kaumodaki, which roared like thunder when it was thrown. {#p7 1.227}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=krishna,arjuna,i239_agni light=i239_agni}

*Now we can fight all the gods and Asuras together,* they told Agni, *let alone the wielder of the thunderbolt fighting for his friend the Naga.* {#p8 1.227}

> Therefore, O lord, blaze thou forth as thou likest, surrounding this large forest on every side. We are quite able to help thee. {#q3 1.227}
> — Arjuna

Then Agni took on his most terrible form. He wrapped the whole forest in his seven flames, roaring like the clouds, as at the end of an age, and every creature in Khandava trembled. The burning forest shone like Mount Meru blazing in the sun. {#p9 1.227}
