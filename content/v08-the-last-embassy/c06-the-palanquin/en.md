::scene{#s1 frame=indra,nahusha light=indra}

> This is not the time for putting forth valour. Nahusha is stronger than I am. {#q1 5.15}
> — Indra

He would use policy instead, and Shachi must carry it out in secret. She went to [Nahusha](nahusha) and named her price. Indra had horses and elephants and chariots; she wanted him to have something new, a vehicle no god or Asura had ever had. {#p1 5.15}

> Let a number of highly dignified Rishis, united together, bear thee in a palanquin. {#q2 5.15}
> — Shachi

Nahusha was delighted. *He cannot be a feeble person who employeth Rishis for bearing him.* He harnessed the great saints to his car, and went about from world to world. {#p2 5.15}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=indra light=indra}

Brihaspati promised Shachi that Nahusha was already lost, and lit a sacrificial fire to find Indra. Agni searched mountains, forests, earth and sky in the blink of an eye, but would not enter the waters: water was where fire went out. {#p3 5.15}

> Fire rose from water, the military caste rose from the priestly caste; and iron had its origin in stone. The power of these which can penetrate all other things, hath no operation upon the sources from which they spring. {#q3 5.15}
> — Agni

Brihaspati praised him until he went in, and Agni found [Indra](indra) in the fibres of the lotus-stalk. The gods and Rishis came and praised Indra's old deeds, Namuchi and Shambara and Vala and Vritra, and he grew, little by little, back into his own form. Kubera, Yama, Soma and Varuna came; Indra gave Kubera the Yakshas and the world's wealth, Yama the Pitris, Varuna the waters, and Agni a share with him in the great sacrifices. {#p4 5.16}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=nahusha light=nahusha}

Then the sage Agastya arrived with the news. The Rishis carrying Nahusha, weary, had asked him whether certain Vedic hymns were authentic. He said they were not. {#p5 5.17}

> And incited by Untruth, he touched me on my head with his foot. {#q4 5.17}
> — Agastya

> For ten thousand years, thou shalt, in the form of an enormous snake, roam over the earth. When that period is full, thou mayst come back to heaven. {#q5 5.17}
> — Agastya

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.17}
The Pandavas met this serpent in the forest: Nahusha, coiled round Bhima, freed by Yudhishthira's answers. Volume VI, chapter 26 tells it.
::

::scene{#s4 frame=indra,shalya,yudhishthira light=shalya}

So Indra mounted Airavata and returned to heaven and his queen. That, said [Shalya](shalya), was how Indra too had suffered with his wife, and lived in hiding until his foes could be slain. {#p6 5.18}

> Similarly, O slayer of foes, thy enemies, Karna and Duryodhana and others of vicious souls will quickly be destroyed. {#q6 5.18}
> — Shalya

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) honoured him and reminded him of his promise: when he drove Karna's chariot, he must dampen Karna's spirits by praising Arjuna. *Let it be so.* Then Shalya took his leave of the Pandavas and went with his army to Duryodhana. {#p7 5.18}
