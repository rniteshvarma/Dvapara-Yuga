On the road into Virata's city, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) praised the goddess Durga in his mind: the daughter born to Yashoda in the cowherd Nanda's house, the sister of Vasudeva, who rose into the sky when Kansa dashed her against a stone; the slayer of Mahisha, whose home is the Vindhya; the one who rescues those sinking *like a cow in the mire*. {#p1 4.6}

The goddess showed herself to him. Victory would be his, she said, and the earth again, and she gave one more gift for the year ahead: {#p2 4.6}

> And through my grace neither the Kuru's spies, nor those that dwell in the country of the Matsyas, will succeed in recognising you all as long as ye reside in Virata's city! {#q1 4.6}
> — Durga

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=4.6}
This hymn to Durga is not in many manuscripts, and the Critical Edition prints it only in an appendix. Ganguli's text includes it, so it is told here.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,virata light=yudhishthira}

Then Yudhishthira tied his dice, gold set with lapis lazuli, in his cloth, held them under his arm, and walked into the court where King [Virata](virata) sat. He came *like the sun enveloped in dense clouds, or fire covered with ashes*, and the king saw it at once. {#p3 4.7}

> He cannot be a Brahmana. Methinks he is a man of men, and a lord of earth. {#q2 4.7}
> — Virata, to his counsellors

Yudhishthira said he was a Brahmana who had lost everything and had come to live by the king's service. Virata asked his name. {#p4 4.7}

> My name is Kanka, and I am a Brahmana belonging to the family known by the name of Vaiyaghra. I am skilled in casting dice, and formerly I was a friend of Yudhishthira. {#q3 4.7}
> — Yudhishthira

Virata offered to let him rule the Matsyas. Kanka asked for less: that he should never be drawn into quarrels over the dice with low men, and that whoever he beat should not keep what he had lost. The king promised more than he was asked. {#p5 4.7}

> Let the assembled subjects listen! Kanka is as much lord of this realm as I myself. {#q4 4.7}
> — Virata

He would ride in the king's own carriages, see into all the king's affairs, and find every door open. And no one found him out. {#p6 4.7}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,virata light=bhima}

Next came a man with *the playful gait of the lion*, dressed in black, carrying a cooking ladle, a spoon and an unsheathed dark sword without a spot on its blade. Virata looked at those lion's shoulders and wondered whether this was the king of the Gandharvas, or Indra himself. {#p7 4.8}

> O foremost of kings, I am a cook, Vallava by name. I am skilled in dressing dishes. {#q5 4.8}
> — Bhima

Virata did not believe that cooking was his trade. [Bhima](bhima) said that King Yudhishthira had always tasted his dishes, and that he was a wrestler too, with no equal in strength: he would fight lions and elephants for the king's entertainment. So Virata put him at the head of the kitchen, over all the cooks already there, and Bhima became the king's favourite, unrecognised by anyone. {#p8 4.8}
