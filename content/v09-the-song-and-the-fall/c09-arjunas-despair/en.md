[Duryodhana](duryodhana) went to [Drona](drona) and pointed out the Pandava army, drawn up by Drona's own pupil Dhrishtadyumna, full of warriors equal to Bhima and Arjuna; then he named his own, Drona, Bhishma, Karna, Kripa, Ashwatthama, Vikarna, Jayadratha. {#p1 6.25}

> Our army, therefore, protected by Bhishma, is insufficient. This force, however, of these (the Pandavas), protected by Bhima, is sufficient. {#q1 6.25}
> — Duryodhana

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=6.25}
**The Bhagavad Gita.** Sections 6.25–42 are the Gita, Krishna's teaching to Arjuna, in eighteen "lessons". Each lesson is a chapter here, told in condensed form with its key lines in Ganguli's words. The full text belongs in a companion volume. The Sanskrit word Ganguli renders "insufficient" can mean either more or less than enough; he reads it, from Duryodhana's alarm, as "less".
::

Let every man guard Bhishma, he said. Then [Bhishma](bhishma), to cheer him, gave a lion's roar and blew his conch, and the whole army answered with conchs, drums and horns. {#p2 6.25}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,arjuna,bhima,yudhishthira light=krishna}

On their great car with white horses, [Krishna](krishna) blew Panchajanya and [Arjuna](arjuna) Devadatta; [Bhima](bhima) blew the huge conch Paundra, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) Anantavijaya, Nakula Sughosha and Sahadeva Manipushpaka, and all the Pandava kings blew theirs, and the sound tore the hearts of Dhritarashtra's sons. {#p3 6.25}

As the first missiles began to fly, Arjuna raised his bow and said to Krishna: place my chariot between the two armies, so that I may see who stands there wanting battle. Krishna drove between them, before Bhishma and Drona and all the kings, and said: *Behold these assembled Kurus*. {#p4 6.25}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,krishna light=arjuna}

And Arjuna saw, in both armies, fathers and grandfathers, teachers, uncles, brothers, sons and grandsons, fathers-in-law and friends. Pity took hold of him. {#p5 6.25}

> Beholding these kinsmen, O Krishna, assembled together and eager for the fight, my limbs, become languid, and my mouth becomes dry. My body trembles, and my hair stands on end. Gandiva slips from my hand, and my skin burns. {#q2 6.25}
> — Arjuna

He wanted no victory, no kingdom, no pleasures, if the people for whose sake men want them stood here ready to die. {#p6 6.25}

> I wish not to slay these though they slay me, O slayer of Madhu, even for the sake of the sovereignty of the three worlds, what then for the sake of (this) earth? {#q3 6.25}
> — Arjuna

What joy could come from killing their own kin? When a race is destroyed its customs perish, sin overtakes it, its women are corrupted, its castes mix, and its ancestors fall from heaven without their offerings. Better that Dhritarashtra's sons should kill him unarmed and unresisting. {#p7 6.25}

> Having spoken thus on the field of battle, Arjuna, his mind troubled with grief, casting aside his bow and arrows, sat down on his car. {#q4 6.25}
> — Sanjaya
