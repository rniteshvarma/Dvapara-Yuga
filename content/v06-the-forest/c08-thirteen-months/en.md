::scene{#s1 frame=bhima,yudhishthira light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) had heard all of it, and could hold back no longer. {#p1 3.33}

> Like a weak offal-eating jackal snatching the prey from mighty lions, he hath snatched away our kingdom. {#q1 3.33}
> — Bhima

It was not taken by valour or strength but by crooked dice, he told [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), and because of Yudhishthira's own carelessness. Why should they suffer for the sake of a promise? Virtue that brings only pain is no virtue. Let them go and kill Duryodhana now. {#p2 3.33}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna light=yudhishthira}

Yudhishthira was silent for a moment. Then he admitted it all. {#p3 3.34}

> I sought to cast the dice desiring to snatch from Dhritarashtra's son his kingdom with the sovereignty. {#q2 3.34}
> — Yudhishthira

He had seen the dice falling Shakuni's way, he said, and could have stopped. But anger drives off patience. It was Draupadi who had saved them. And he had agreed to the second game's terms before all the Kurus. {#p4 3.34}

> For a respectable person, I think, even death itself is lighter than the acquisition of sovereignty by an act of transgression. {#q3 3.34}
> — Yudhishthira

When Bhima had wanted to burn his hands in the hall, he reminded him, Arjuna had stopped him. Wait, he said, as a farmer waits for his harvest. {#p5 3.34}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,yudhishthira,bhishma,drona,karna light=bhima}

Bhima did not stop. Yudhishthira was *like froth*, he said, kind like a Brahmana; how had he been born a Kshatriya? The Kurus would grow stronger every year, and thirteen years is a long time to live. They would surely be found in the year of hiding. And then he offered a way out. {#p6 3.35}

> We have already lived in the woods full thirteen months. Regard them, O king, for their length as thirteen years. {#q4 3.35}
> — Bhima

A month may stand in for a year, he said, as a pot-herb stands in for the Soma plant at a sacrifice. And if that was a sin, it could be atoned for by feeding a bull. {#p7 3.35}

Yudhishthira thought for a long moment, and then gave the real reason. Bhima's plan relied on courage alone. On the other side would be Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna, Ashwatthama, Bhurishravas and Shalya, masters of every weapon, and all the kings the Pandavas had humbled, now bound to Duryodhana by his gifts. Bhishma, Drona and Kripa loved the Pandavas as much as the Kauravas, but they would give their lives to repay the king who kept them. And there was Karna, in his impenetrable armour. {#p8 3.36}

> O Vrikodara, I cannot sleep thinking of the lightness of hand of that Suta's son, who, I regard, is the foremost of all wielders of the bow! {#q5 3.36}
> — Yudhishthira

Bhima heard it and fell silent, afraid. {#p9 3.36}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=vyasa,yudhishthira,arjuna light=vyasa}

Just then [Vyasa](vyasa) arrived. He knew the fear in Yudhishthira's heart, he said, and he had come to drive it out. The time would come when Arjuna would kill all his enemies. Vyasa taught Yudhishthira a secret knowledge called *Pratismriti*, to be passed on to Arjuna, who should then go to Indra, Rudra, Varuna, Kubera and Yama for their weapons. Then Vyasa vanished. {#p10 3.36}

The Pandavas left the Dvaita woods and went back to the Kamyaka forest on the Sarasvati. {#p11 3.36}
