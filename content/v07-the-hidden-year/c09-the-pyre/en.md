::scene{#s1 frame=upakichakas,draupadi,virata light=upakichakas}

Kichaka's kinsmen came and wailed over him, terrified at the sight of a body lying *like a tortoise dragged to dry ground from the water*. As they carried him out to burn him, they saw [Draupadi](draupadi) leaning against a pillar nearby. {#p1 4.23}

> Let this unchaste woman be slain for whom Kichaka hath himself lost his life. {#q1 4.23}
> — the Kichakas

Or better, they said, burn her with the man who had wanted her. They asked [Virata](virata), and the king, who knew how strong the Suta clan was, gave his consent. [The Upakichakas](upakichakas) seized her, tied her to the bier, and set off for the cremation ground. {#p2 4.23}

She cried out for her husbands by their secret names. {#p3 4.23}

> Oh, let Jaya, and Jayanta, and Vijaya and Jayatsena, and Jayadvala listen to my words. The Sutas are taking me away. {#q2 4.23}
> — Draupadi

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima,upakichakas,draupadi light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) heard her and sprang from his bed. *I have heard, O Sairindhri the words thou hast spoken.* He changed his clothes, left the palace by a side way, climbed a wall by way of a tree, and ran to the burning ground. There he pulled up a tree as tall as a palmyra, ten vyamas long, with a withered top, put it on his shoulder, and rushed at them like Yama with his mace. Banyans and peepals fell in clusters in his path. {#p4 4.23}

> Lo, the powerful Gandharva cometh hither, filled with rage, and with an upraised tree in hand. Let Sairindhri, therefore, from whom this danger of ours hath arisen, be set free. {#q3 4.23}
> — the Sutas

They let her go and ran for the city. With that tree Bhima killed a hundred and five of them, and with Kichaka the dead were a hundred and six. He untied her and comforted her. {#p5 4.23}

> Thus, O timid one, are they slain that wrong thee without cause. Return, O Krishna, to the city. Thou hast no longer any fear; I myself will go to the Virata's kitchen by another route. {#q4 4.23}
> — Bhima

***

::scene{#s3 frame=virata,sudeshna light=virata}

The townspeople ran to the king. The Gandharvas had killed all the Sutas, they said, and the Sairindhri was coming back; with her beauty, and her powerful Gandharvas, and men being what they are, the whole kingdom was in danger. Virata ordered the Kichakas burned together on one great pyre. Then, afraid, he told [Sudeshna](sudeshna) to send the Sairindhri away, wherever she liked, because he dared not say it to her himself. *A woman, however, cannot offend.* {#p6 4.24}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=draupadi,bhima,arjuna,uttara_f light=draupadi}

Draupadi washed herself and her clothes and walked back into the city *like a doe frightened by a tiger*. People ran from her; some shut their eyes. At the kitchen door Bhima stood like a huge elephant, and she spoke to him in words only they would understand. {#p7 4.24}

> I bow unto that prince of the Gandharvas, who hath rescued me. {#q5 4.24}
> — Draupadi

At the dancing hall, [Arjuna](arjuna) was teaching Virata's daughters, and the girls ran out to her: by good luck she was safe, by good luck the Sutas were dead. Brihannala asked how it had happened. {#p8 4.24}

> O blessed Vrihannala, always passing thy days happily in the apartments of the girls, what concern hast thou with Sairindhri's fate to say? {#q6 4.24}
> — Draupadi

Brihannala answered that Brihannala too had sorrows beyond compare. *But no one can completely read another's heart.* {#p9 4.24}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=sudeshna,draupadi light=sudeshna}

Sudeshna gave her the king's message: go wherever you like; you are young and beautiful, men desire you, and the Gandharvas are terrible in their anger. {#p10 4.24}

> O beauteous lady, let the king suffer me to live here for only thirteen days more. {#q7 4.24}
> — Draupadi

Then, she said, the Gandharvas would take her away themselves, and do what would please Virata, and the king would be the better for it. {#p11 4.24}
