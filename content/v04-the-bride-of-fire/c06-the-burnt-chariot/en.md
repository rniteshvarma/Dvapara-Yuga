::scene{#s1 frame=vyasa,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,kunti light=vyasa}

The brothers agreed to go to Panchala. But before they left Ekachakra, [Vyasa](vyasa) came to see them once more, and told them a story. {#p1 1.171}

There was once a sage's daughter, he said, beautiful and blameless, who could not find a husband because of her deeds in an earlier life. She did penance until [Shiva](shiva) appeared and offered her a boon, and she asked him for a husband with every virtue. She asked again, and again, five times in all. {#p2 1.171}

> Thou hast, O girl, said full five times, 'Give me (a) husband.' Thou shalt, therefore, in another life have five husbands! {#q1 1.171}
> — Shiva

That girl, Vyasa told them, had been born again in Drupada's line. Krishna, Drupada's daughter, was meant to be the wife of all of them. They should go to the Panchala capital. Then he blessed them and went away. {#p3 1.171}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.171,1.193}
**Told before it happens.** The epic has Vyasa tell the Pandavas about Draupadi's past life before they have even seen her. When the question of five husbands comes, it will not come out of nowhere.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,i2128_chitraratha light=arjuna,i2128_chitraratha}

They walked north, day and night, with their mother in front, and came at last to the Ganga. [Arjuna](arjuna) went ahead with a torch to light the way and guard them. {#p4 1.172}

That night the king of the Gandharvas was bathing in the river with his wives. He heard them coming and drew his bow. The twilight, he said, belonged to Yakshas, Gandharvas and Rakshasas; men who came near the water at such an hour were killed. He was Angaraparna, friend of Kubera, and this was his forest. {#p5 1.172}

Arjuna was not impressed. {#p6 1.172}

> Blockhead, whether it be day, night, or twilight, who can bar others from the ocean, the sides of the Himalayas, and this river? {#q2 1.172}
> — Arjuna

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,i2128_chitraratha,i6139_kumbhinasi,yudhishthira light=arjuna,i6139_kumbhinasi}

The Gandharva shot arrows at them like snakes. Arjuna turned them all aside with his torch and shield, and then answered with a celestial weapon: the weapon of fire, which had passed from Brihaspati to Bharadvaja, from him to Agnivesha, from him to Drona, and from Drona to Arjuna. It burned the Gandharva's chariot to nothing. {#p7 1.172}

The Gandharva fell from it senseless, head first. Arjuna caught him by his garlanded hair and dragged him towards his brothers. Then the Gandharva's wife, [Kumbhinasi](i6139_kumbhinasi), ran to Yudhishthira and begged him to spare her husband. {#p8 1.172}

> O slayer of foes, O child, who would slay a foe who hath been vanquished in fight, who hath been deprived of fame, who is protected by a woman, and who hath no prowess? {#q3 1.172}
> — Yudhishthira

*Keep thou thy life, O Gandharva!* said Arjuna. {#p9 1.172}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=i2128_chitraratha,arjuna light=i2128_chitraratha}

The Gandharva gave up his old name. He had been called Angaraparna, *blazing chariot*; now his chariot was burnt, and he would be [Chitraratha](i2128_chitraratha). He offered Arjuna friendship and gifts: the Gandharva science of vision, which lets a man see whatever he wishes to see, and a hundred Gandharva horses for each brother, swift as thought, that never tire. {#p10 1.172}

Arjuna would not take gifts for sparing a life. So they made an exchange between friends: the horses for Arjuna's weapon of fire. *Let our friendship last for ever,* said Arjuna. {#p11 1.172}

Then Arjuna asked him why he had scolded them. The Gandharva said they were travelling without wives and without a priest walking before them. A king, he told them, should have a learned priest; a king without a Brahmana to guide him wins no land by courage or birth alone. {#p12 1.172}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=i2128_chitraratha,arjuna,tapati,samvarana,kuru,vasishtha light=tapati,samvarana}

He had called Arjuna *son of Tapati*, and Arjuna asked why. So the Gandharva told him. [Tapati](tapati) was the daughter of the Sun. King [Samvarana](samvarana) saw her on a mountainside and fell in love, and with the help of his priest [Vasishtha](vasishtha) he won her. Their son was [Kuru](kuru), the founder of Arjuna's line. {#p13 1.173 1.176}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.176,1.184}
**A night of stories.** The Gandharva goes on talking for many sections: about Vasishtha, his rivalry with Vishvamitra over the wish-granting cow, the curse of King Kalmashapada, and the sages Parashara and Aurva. They are old tales of priests and kings, told to show the Pandavas why a king needs a good priest. We pass over them here; they stand on their own in the epic.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=dhaumya,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,kunti light=dhaumya}

*Who should be our priest?* Arjuna asked. The Gandharva sent them to a shrine called Utkochaka nearby, where [Dhaumya](dhaumya), the younger brother of the sage Devala, lived in penance. {#p14 1.185}

Arjuna gave the Gandharva his weapon of fire, and asked him to keep the horses until they were needed. They parted as friends. The Pandavas went to Utkochaka, and Dhaumya received them with wild fruits and roots and agreed to be their priest. {#p15 1.185}

With Dhaumya beside them, the epic says, they felt their kingdom as good as won back, and the princess of Panchala as good as theirs. {#p16 1.185}
