[Bhishma](bhishma) smiled: he would not fight from a chariot against a man on foot. Let Parashurama mount a chariot and put on mail. {#p1 5.182}

> The Earth, O Bhishma, is my car, and Vedas, like good steeds, are the animals that carry me! The wind is my car-driver. {#q1 5.182}
> — Parashurama

His armour was the mothers of the Vedas, he said. Then Bhishma saw him on a chariot made by his will, beautiful as a city, with Akritavrana driving. Bhishma got down, laid aside his bow, walked to his teacher and asked his blessing. Parashurama was pleased: had Bhishma not done that, he would have cursed him; but he could not wish him victory, since he meant to win himself. {#p2 5.182}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,parashurama light=bhishma}

Parashurama struck first, with nine hundred and sixty arrows. Bhishma said he did not strike at the Vedas in Parashurama's body, or his Brahmanhood, or his austerities, but at the Kshatriya he had chosen to become, and cut a horn off his bow. Blood ran down Parashurama *like the Sumeru mountain with streams of liquid metal rolling down its breast*. When a hundred of Bhishma's arrows left him reeling, Bhishma stopped of his own accord. {#p3 5.182}

> Oh, fie on battle! Fie on Kshatriya practices! {#q2 5.182}
> — Bhishma

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhishma,parashurama,amba light=bhishma}

Day after day they fought. Wind weapon against Guhyaka, fire against water. Parashurama pierced Bhishma's chest and he fainted, and Amba and the ascetics shouted for joy; Bhishma came back and struck Parashurama down with an arrow *with Death's self sitting at its head*, and the whole world cried out as if the sun had fallen. The arrows of both met so thick in the sky that they caught fire from their own friction and fell as ash. {#p4 5.183}

Parashurama hurled a blazing lance; Bhishma cut it in three. Twelve more darts like the suns at the end of the world; Bhishma burned them. His chariot was smashed to pieces, and both men bled. {#p5 5.184}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,ganga,parashurama light=ganga}

Then Bhishma's charioteer fell under Parashurama's arrows and died, and while Bhishma mourned him an arrow went through his chest and he fell. As he fell, eight Brahmanas shining like the sun caught him in their arms and held him above the ground: *Do not fear*. When he rose, he saw his mother [Ganga](ganga) standing in his chariot, holding the reins. He begged her to go, and drove himself. {#p6 5.185}

He struck Parashurama down on his knees; clouds rained blood, Rahu swallowed the sun, the earth shook. Parashurama rose again, beside himself with rage. And so it went on, day after day. {#p7 5.185}

> And it lasted for three and twenty days together. {#q3 5.185}
