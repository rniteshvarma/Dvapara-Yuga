[Krishna](krishna) wanted the Pandavas to prosper, he said, and Dhritarashtra and his many sons too, and had always counselled peace. But how could there be no enmity, when Dhritarashtra and his sons were so covetous? And why did [Sanjaya](sanjaya) reproach Yudhishthira, as if he knew right and wrong better than either of them? {#p1 5.29}

Some say the next world is won by work, some by knowledge alone, he went on. But knowing what food is does not end hunger. The gods themselves flourish by work: the wind blows, the sun rises sleepless, the earth carries her burden, the rivers run, Indra rains. {#p2 5.29}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,sanjaya,draupadi light=draupadi}

A king's duty, Krishna said, is to protect his people and punish those who take what is not theirs. Indra invented weapons for that. Dhritarashtra had seized what lawfully belonged to Pandu's son. {#p3 5.29}

> A thief who steals wealth unseen and one who forcibly seizes the same, in open day-light, are both to be condemned, O Sanjaya. {#q1 5.29}
> — Krishna

And where had Sanjaya's law been in the dicing hall, when Draupadi was dragged in weeping before her fathers-in-law, and no one but Vidura spoke for her? When Karna told her to choose another husband, *a wordy arrow, sharp, cutting all hopes*, which buried itself in Arjuna's heart? {#p4 5.29}

> Thou didst not, O Sanjaya, then say what law and morality were, but now thou comest to instruct the son of Pandu! {#q2 5.29}
> — Krishna

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna light=krishna}

He would go to the Kurus himself, Krishna said, to try for peace without harm to the Pandava cause. If they would not listen, Arjuna and Bhima would burn them. {#p5 5.29}

> Duryodhana is a big tree of evil passions; Karna is its trunk; Sakuni is its branches; Dussasana forms its abundant blossoms and fruits; (while) the wise king Dhritarashtra is its roots. Yudhishthira is a big tree of righteousness; Arjuna is its trunk; and Bhima is its branches; the sons of Madri are its abundant flowers and fruits; and its roots are myself and religion and religious men. {#q3 5.29}
> — Krishna

> King Dhritarashtra with his sons constitutes a forest, while, O Sanjaya, the sons of Pandu are its tigers. Do not, oh, cut down the forest with its tigers, and let not the tigers be driven away from the forest. {#q4 5.29}
> — Krishna

The tiger out of the wood is easily killed; the wood without its tiger is easily cut. The forest protects the tiger, and the tiger the forest. *The Dhritarashtras are as creepers, while, O Sanjaya, the Pandavas are Sala trees.* {#p6 5.29}
