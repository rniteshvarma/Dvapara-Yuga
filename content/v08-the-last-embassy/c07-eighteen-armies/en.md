::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,satyaki,drupada,virata light=yudhishthira}

The armies came. [Satyaki](satyaki) brought an akshauhini to [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), and it vanished into his host *as doth a small river when it enters the sea*. Dhrishtaketu of the Chedis brought one, Jayatsena of Magadha one, Pandya from the southern coast came with troops of every kind, and [Drupada](drupada) and [Virata](virata) brought theirs. Seven akshauhinis gathered for the sons of Pandu. {#p1 5.19}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,bhagadatta,shalya,kritavarma,jayadratha light=duryodhana}

To [Duryodhana](duryodhana) came [Bhagadatta](bhagadatta) with Chinas and Kiratas *like figures of gold*, Bhurishravas and [Shalya](shalya), [Kritavarma](kritavarma) with the Bhojas, Andhakas and Kukuras, [Jayadratha](jayadratha) with the men of Sindhu-Sauvira, Sudakshina of the Kambojas with Yavanas and Shakas, Nila of Mahishmati, the two kings of Avanti, and the five Kekaya brothers. Eleven akshauhinis in all. There was no room in Hastinapura even for the generals; the army spread over the land of the five rivers, Kurujangala, the Rohitaka forest, the banks of the Ganga and the hills along the Yamuna. {#p2 5.19}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,bhishma,vidura,duryodhana light=dhritarashtra}

Drupada's priest saw it all as he came. In the court, before [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), [Bhishma](bhishma), [Vidura](vidura) and all Duryodhana's generals, he spoke. Dhritarashtra and Pandu were sons of one father; their shares should be equal. Dhritarashtra's sons had taken everything, tried to kill the Pandavas, robbed them by deceit, insulted their wife. Yet the Pandavas wanted only peace and their own share. They had seven akshauhinis now, and Satyaki, Bhima and the twins, and Arjuna, and Krishna. {#p3 5.20}

> Therefore, I ask you to give back what should be given, as dictated by morality and compact. Do not let the opportunity pass! {#q1 5.20}
> — Drupada's priest

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,karna,dhritarashtra,sanjaya light=karna}

Bhishma said it was true, though sharply put, *the reason, I suppose, being that thou art a Brahmana*: the Pandavas were entitled to their father's property, and no one could stand against Arjuna. [Karna](karna) broke in angrily. Everyone knew the facts. Yudhishthira had lost at dice and gone to the forest by agreement; now, relying on the Matsyas and Panchalas, he ignored it. {#p4 5.21}

> O learned man, Duryodhana would not yield even a single foot of land if thou appealest to his fears, but if justice requires, he would give up the whole earth even to a foe. {#q2 5.21}
> — Karna

> What is the use of thy talking, O Radha's son? Thou shouldst remember that occasion when Pritha's son, single-handed, overpowered in battle six car-warriors. {#q3 5.21}
> — Bhishma

Dhritarashtra calmed Bhishma, rebuked Karna, and sent the priest home: he would send [Sanjaya](sanjaya) to the Pandavas himself. {#p5 5.21}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=dhritarashtra,sanjaya light=sanjaya}

He told Sanjaya what he feared. He had never found a single fault in the Pandavas. No one hated them but his own dull-headed son and the meaner Karna. He spoke of Arjuna's Gandiva, of Bhima equal to ten thousand elephants, of the twins like hawks, of Krishna who had killed Shishupala in a moment. {#p6 5.22}

> My heart is trembling with fear upon learning that the two Krishnas, are seated on the selfsame car. {#q4 5.22}
> — Dhritarashtra

But most of all he feared Yudhishthira's anger, because he knew it was just. Go to Upaplavya, he told Sanjaya, ask after them all, tell Krishna that Dhritarashtra wants peace, and say nothing that might provoke a war. {#p7 5.22}
