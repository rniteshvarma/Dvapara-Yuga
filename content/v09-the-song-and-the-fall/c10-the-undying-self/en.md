[Krishna](krishna) looked at him, sitting there with his eyes full of tears. Where had this weakness come from, at such a moment, unworthy of a man of noble birth? Shake it off and stand up. {#p1 6.26}

How could he shoot arrows at Bhishma and Drona, [Arjuna](arjuna) asked, who deserved his worship? Better to live on alms than enjoy pleasures stained with their blood. He did not know which was better, to conquer or be conquered. {#p2 6.26}

> I am thy disciple. O, instruct me, I seek thy aid. {#q1 6.26}
> — Arjuna

Then he said *I will not fight*, and fell silent. And Krishna, between the two armies, began. {#p3 6.26}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,arjuna light=krishna}

> Thou mournest those that deserve not to be mourned. {#q2 6.26}
> — Krishna

The truly wise grieve neither for the dead nor for the living. There was never a time when Krishna was not, or Arjuna, or these kings, nor will there be a time when they cease to be. As the embodied one passes through childhood, youth and age in this body, so it passes to another body. Heat and cold, pleasure and pain come and go; bear them. {#p4 6.26}

> It is never born, nor doth it ever die; nor, having existed, will it exist no more. {#q3 6.26}
> — Krishna

> As a man, casting off robes that are worn out, putteth on others that are new, so the Embodied (soul), casting off bodies that are worn out, entereth other bodies that are new. {#q4 6.26}
> — Krishna

> Weapons cleave it not, fire consumeth it not; the waters do not drench it, nor doth the wind waste it. {#q5 6.26}
> — Krishna

And even if he thought it was always being born and always dying, there was still nothing to grieve: death is certain for the born, and birth for the dead. {#p5 6.26}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna,arjuna light=arjuna}

Then look to your own duty, Krishna said. For a Kshatriya there is nothing better than a righteous battle, and it had come of itself, *an open gate of heaven*. Turn from it and men would speak of his infamy for ever, and to an honoured man infamy is worse than death. Slain, he would reach heaven; victorious, he would enjoy the earth. Treat pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat alike, and fight. {#p6 6.26}

That was the teaching of Sankhya; now hear Yoga. {#p7 6.26}

> Thy concern is with work only, but not with the fruit (of work). Let not the fruit be thy motive for work; nor let thy inclination be for inaction. {#q6 6.26}
> — Krishna

Evenness of mind in success and failure is yoga; *devotion is only cleverness in action*. {#p8 6.26}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna,arjuna light=krishna}

How, Arjuna asked, does a man of steady mind speak, sit and move? When he casts off every desire of the heart and is content in himself, Krishna said; when he is unshaken in calamity and without craving in pleasure, free of attachment, fear and anger; when he draws in his senses from their objects *as the tortoise its limbs*. {#p9 6.26}

> From attachment springeth wrath; from wrath ariseth want of discrimination; from want of discrimination, loss of memory; from loss of memory, loss of understanding; and from loss of understanding (he) is utterly ruined. {#q7 6.26}
> — Krishna

The self-restrained man is awake when it is night for all creatures. Desires enter him as rivers enter the ocean, which is always filling and never overflows; such a man finds peace. That, said Krishna, is the divine state. {#p10 6.26}
