No one dared answer. Then [Parashurama](parashurama), Jamadagni's son, turned to [Duryodhana](duryodhana) with a story. {#p1 5.96}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.96}
The three stories the sages tell in the Kuru court, Dambhodbhava (5.96), Matali's search (5.97–105) and Galava (5.106–123), are told here in outline; fuller tellings belong in a companion volume of tales.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=parashurama light=parashurama}

There was once a king named Dambhodbhava who ruled the whole earth. Every morning he asked the Brahmanas and Kshatriyas whether anyone, of any order, was his equal in battle. At last some angry Brahmanas told him: there are two, Nara and Narayana, doing penance on Gandhamadana. He marched there with all his army, and found two ascetics, thin with hunger, their veins standing out. They welcomed him with fruit and water. *Wrath and covetousness have no place in this retreat*, they said; *seek battle elsewhere*. He would not stop asking. {#p2 5.96}

> Nara, then, O Bharata, taking up a handful of grass-blades, said, 'Desirous of battle as thou art, come, O Kshatriya, and fight!' {#q1 5.96}

Dambhodbhava's arrows covered the sky; Nara's grass-blades turned them all aside, and then, unstoppable, cut off the eyes, ears and noses of his warriors. When the king saw the whole sky white with grass, he fell at the Rishi's feet. Nara sent him home to rule humbly and never again despise anyone. {#p3 5.96}

::scene{#s3 frame=parashurama,arjuna,krishna,duryodhana light=parashurama}

> They that were Nara and Narayana in days of yore are now Arjuna and Kesava. {#q2 5.96}
> — Parashurama

[Arjuna](arjuna)'s bow carried weapons that bring on the eight passions, so that men struck by them weep and laugh and vomit and run mad; and his friend was [Krishna](krishna), who was greater still. Lay aside your pride, said Parashurama, and make peace. {#p4 5.96}
