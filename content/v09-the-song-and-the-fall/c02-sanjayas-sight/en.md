As the two armies stood east and west, [Vyasa](vyasa) came privately to [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), who sat grieving over his sons. Their hour had come, he said; they would kill one another. Do not grieve. {#p1 6.2}

> O king, if thou wish to see them (fighting) in battle, I will, O son, grant thee vision. {#q1 6.2}
> — Vyasa

> O best of regenerate Rishi, I like not to behold the slaughter of kinsmen. I shall, however, through thy potency hear of this battle minutely. {#q2 6.2}
> — Dhritarashtra

So Vyasa gave the boon to [Sanjaya](sanjaya) instead. Nothing in the battle would be hidden from him, seen or unseen, by day or by night, even what men only thought; weapons would not cut him nor effort tire him, and he would come out of the war alive. And as for victory, *it is there where righteousness is*. {#p2 6.2}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vyasa,dhritarashtra light=vyasa}

Then Vyasa told of the omens. Vultures and herons gathered in the treetops, looking down on the field. Headless trunks covered the sun at dawn and dusk. The images of the gods laughed, trembled, sweated and vomited blood; drums sounded unstruck; chariots moved with no horses. Arundhati had put Vasishtha behind her; cows gave blood for milk; rivers ran backwards; the moon and the sun were eclipsed in the same month, on the thirteenth day. {#p3 6.2 6.3}

Dhritarashtra said it was all ordained; if the kings died in battle as Kshatriyas, they would reach the regions of heroes. Vyasa sat in yoga a moment, then spoke again. {#p4 6.3}

> O king, Death himself hath been born in the shape of thy son. {#q3 6.3}
> — Vyasa

It was Time that destroyed the world and Time that made it, he said. Let the king show his sons the way of righteousness; let the Pandavas have their kingdom and the Kauravas have peace. {#p5 6.3}

> Man, however, in what concerns his own interests, is deprived of judgment. {#q4 6.3}
> — Dhritarashtra

He knew all that, the king said, but he was an ordinary man, and his sons would not obey him. What were the signs of victory? A bright fire bending to the right, birds wheeling right, deep-voiced conchs, cheerful warriors whose garlands did not fade, Vyasa answered; and he added that victory by negotiation was best, by division middling, by battle worst. {#p6 6.3}

> Victory is uncertain. It depends on chance. Even they that become victorious have to sustain loss. {#q5 6.3}
> — Vyasa

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,sanjaya light=sanjaya}

Vyasa left, and Dhritarashtra sat in silence, sighing. Then he asked Sanjaya: these kings were ready to die for the earth; what was the earth, that they wanted it so? Tell me of the lands they came from. {#p7 6.4}

> He that hath the Earth, hath the entire universe with its mobile and immobile population. It is for this that longing for (the possession of the) Earth, kings slay one another. {#q6 6.4}
> — Sanjaya
