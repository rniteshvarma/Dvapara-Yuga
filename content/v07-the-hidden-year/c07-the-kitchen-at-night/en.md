[Draupadi](draupadi) went to her room, bathed, and washed her body and her clothes, and wept, and thought. *What am I to do? Whither shall I go?* There was only one who could do what her heart was set on. In the night she rose from her bed and went to the kitchen, where [Bhima](bhima) lay asleep, breathing hard like a lion. She put her arms round him and woke him *as a lioness awaketh a sleeping lion in a trackless forest*. {#p1 4.17}

> Arise, arise! Why dost thou, O Bhimasena, lie down as one dead? Surely, he that is not dead, never suffereth a wicked wretch that hath disgraced his wife, to live. {#q1 4.17}
> — Draupadi

Bhima sat up. She looked pale and thin, he said; she must tell him everything, pleasant or painful, and then go back to bed before anyone woke. {#p2 4.17}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=draupadi,yudhishthira light=yudhishthira}

> What grief hath she not who hath Yudhishthira for her husband? {#q2 4.18}
> — Draupadi

She counted her humiliations: dragged into the hall by the Pratikamin and called a slave; seized by Jayadratha in the forest; and now kicked by Kichaka in front of the Matsya king, who asked her every day to be his wife. *Censure thou that elder brother of thine addicted to execrable dice.* {#p3 4.18}

She remembered what [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) had been at Indraprastha: followed by ten thousand elephants with golden garlands; feeding guests day and night from a kitchen with a hundred thousand maidservants; giving a thousand nishkas a day; keeping eighty-eight thousand Snatakas; caring for the blind, the old and the orphaned of his kingdom. Now he was a servant in another man's court, flattering him, and called himself Kanka. {#p4 4.18}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=draupadi,bhima,arjuna,sahadeva,nakula light=arjuna}

Then she spoke of the others. Bhima himself, the king's cook, sent in to fight elephants while the women laughed, and lions and buffaloes in the inner rooms while she nearly fainted, and the queen teasing her that the Sairindhri must be in love with the handsome cook. {#p5 4.19}

[Arjuna](arjuna), who had beaten gods and men from a single chariot, now taught dancing to Virata's daughter, with conch bracelets on the wrists the bowstring had scarred, and braids ending in unsightly curls. He lived in the women's rooms *like fire hid in a well*. {#p6 4.19}

[Sahadeva](sahadeva), the youngest, slept on calf-skins among the cowherds. She could not sleep for thinking of him. When they left for the forest, [Kunti](kunti) had held him and given him into her care. {#p7 4.19}

> Sahadeva is bashful and sweet-speeched, and virtuous. He is also my favourite child. Therefore, O Yajnaseni, tend him in the forest day and night. {#q3 4.19}
> — Kunti, as Draupadi remembered

And [Nakula](nakula), at the sight of whom armies had fled, now ran horses up and down in front of the king. {#p8 4.19}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=draupadi,bhima light=draupadi}

She was under Sudeshna's orders now, walking before and behind her as others once walked before and behind Draupadi. She who had never pounded unguents for anyone but Kunti now pounded sandal paste for others, and was afraid of what the king would say of it. Then she held out her hands to him, and showed him the corns on them. {#p9 4.20}

Prosperity and adversity turn like a wheel, she said; she lived only in the hope that it would turn again. Then she cried silently, looking at him. {#p10 4.20}

Bhima took her hands, covered his face with them, and wept. {#p11 4.20}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=bhima,draupadi light=bhima}

> Fie on the might of my arms and fie on the Gandiva of Falguni, inasmuch as thy hands, red before, now become covered with corns. {#q4 4.21}
> — Bhima

He would have made a slaughter of Virata's court, he said, if Yudhishthira had not stopped him with a look. She must not say these things to Yudhishthira: he would kill himself, and Arjuna and the twins would follow, and so would he. Sukanya had followed Chyavana into the forest, Sita had followed Rama, Lopamudra Agastya, Savitri Satyavan into Yama's world. *Do thou spend a short while more that is measured by even a half month.* Then she would be a queen again. {#p12 4.21}

She did not blame Yudhishthira, she said; she wept from grief. But the work of the hour must be done. Kichaka spoke to her every day; he laughed at her Gandharvas and said he would kill a hundred thousand of them. He was the king's chief ally in war, loved by the king and queen, and he took what he wanted from others. If he saw her again, he would outrage her. {#p13 4.21}

> Do thou, therefore, smash this lustful wight even like an earthen pot dashed upon a stone. {#q5 4.21}
> — Draupadi

If the next day's sun shone on Kichaka, she said, she would drink poison. She would never yield to him. Then she hid her face in Bhima's chest and wept, and he held her and comforted her and wiped her tears. And thinking of Kichaka, he licked the corners of his mouth. {#p14 4.21}
