[Susharma](susharma), king of the Trigartas, had been beaten many times by Kichaka and the Matsyas. Now he saw his chance. {#p1 4.30}

> My kingdom hath many a time been forcibly invaded by the king of the Matsyas. {#q1 4.30}
> — Susharma

With Kichaka dead, Virata had lost his pride and his refuge. Let them invade, Susharma said, and take his gems and villages, or carry off his cattle by the thousand. [Karna](karna) agreed: the moment was good. As for the Pandavas, they had no wealth or strength left; they were gone for good, or dead. {#p2 4.30}

So [Duryodhana](duryodhana) made the plan. Susharma and the Trigartas would go first, and fall on Virata's cowherds by surprise. The Kauravas would follow the next day in two divisions and take thousands more cattle. Susharma set out on the seventh day of the dark fortnight, and on the eighth the Kauravas began seizing the herds. {#p3 4.30}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=virata,yudhishthira,bhima,nakula,sahadeva light=virata}

By now the Pandavas had completed their year of hiding, and since Kichaka's death [Virata](virata) had come to rest his hopes on them. A herdsman rushed into the court: the Trigartas had beaten the cowherds and were driving off the king's cattle by hundreds and thousands. {#p4 4.31}

The Matsyas armed. Virata's brother Shatanika put on a coat of mail of adamantine steel, Madiraksha one plated with gold, Virata one with a hundred suns and a hundred eyes; his eldest son, Shankha, one of burnished steel. Then the king thought of his servants. {#p5 4.31}

> Bearing such martial forms and possessed of arms resembling the trunk of mighty elephants, I can never persuade myself that they cannot fight. {#q2 4.31}
> — Virata

So Kanka, Vallava, Tantripala and Damagranthi, as the king called Nakula here, were given chariots, armour and weapons, and [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), [Bhima](bhima), [Nakula](nakula) and [Sahadeva](sahadeva) rode out behind Virata, with eight thousand chariots, a thousand elephants and sixty thousand horses, following the tracks of the cattle. {#p6 4.31}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=virata,susharma light=susharma}

They caught the Trigartas when the sun had passed noon, and fought on into the evening, chariots against chariots, elephants against elephants, until the dust hid everything and the birds fell out of the sky. Neither side could break the other. Shatanika killed a hundred and Vishalaksha four hundred, and they drove into the heart of the Trigarta host. {#p7 4.32}

Virata destroyed five hundred chariots and came at last upon Susharma in his golden chariot. The two kings rushed at each other *like two bulls in a cow-pen*, and shot at each other until the dust hid both armies from each other. {#p8 4.32}
