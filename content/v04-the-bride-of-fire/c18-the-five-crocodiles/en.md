Arjuna went south, to the holy waters on the shore of the southern ocean. There were five of them, named for sages, Agastya, Saubhadra, Pauloma, Karandhama and Bharadvaja, and every ascetic in the place avoided them. Arjuna asked why. {#p1 1.218}

> There dwell in these waters five large crocodiles which take away the ascetics that may happen to bathe in them. {#q1 1.218}
> — the ascetics

They begged him not to go. He went straight to the water called Saubhadra and plunged in. {#p2 1.218}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,i11589_varga light=i11589_varga,arjuna}

A great crocodile seized him by the leg. Arjuna, the strongest of men, seized it in turn and dragged it, struggling, onto the shore; and on the shore it became a beautiful woman, shining with ornaments. {#p3 1.218}

Her name was [Varga](i11589_varga), she told him, an apsara, a favourite of Kubera. She and four friends, Saurabheyi, Samichi, Budbuda and Lata, had once come upon a young Brahmana meditating alone in a forest, and set out to distract him with songs and smiles. He never once looked at them with desire. He looked at them in anger. {#p4 1.218}

> Becoming crocodiles, range ye the waters for a hundred years. {#q2 1.218}
> — the Brahmana

***

::scene{#s3 frame=i11589_varga,narada light=i11589_varga}

They begged his pardon, Varga said, and he softened. A hundred years it must be, for he had never told a lie even in jest; but at the end of it a great man would drag them out of the water, and they would be themselves again, and the waters would afterwards be called the *Nari-tirthas*, the women's sacred waters, and cleanse sins. {#p5 1.219}

As they went away wondering who that man would be, they met [Narada](narada), who told them to go to the five waters by the southern ocean: Dhananjaya, the son of Pandu, would soon free them. {#p6 1.219}

*Now I am free,* said Varga. *But my four friends are still in the other waters.* {#p7 1.219}

Arjuna freed them all. They rose from the waters in their own forms again, and he let them go where they wished. {#p8 1.219}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.218,1.135}
**Arjuna and crocodiles.** This is the second time the epic sets Arjuna against a crocodile. The first was in the Ganga, when one seized his teacher Drona by the thigh. Ulupi's boon, that the creatures of the water would be at his mercy, comes only a little before this.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna,chitrangada_m,babhruvahana light=babhruvahana}

Then Arjuna wanted to see Chitrangada again, and went back to Manipura. He found the son he had fathered on the throne, a boy called [Babhruvahana](babhruvahana). He saw Chitrangada once more, and went on to the shrine of Gokarna. {#p9 1.219}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=arjuna,krishna light=arjuna,krishna}

He visited all the holy places on the western coast and came at last to Prabhasa. [Krishna](krishna) heard he was there and came to see his friend. They embraced; the epic says they were the ancient sages Nara and Narayana. Arjuna told him everything, and Krishna said: *This is as it should be.* {#p10 1.220}

They spent some days on the Raivataka hill, which Krishna had had decorated for him, watching actors and dancers. Arjuna fell asleep on a splendid bed telling Krishna about the rivers and mountains he had seen, and woke to music. Then they rode to Dvaraka in a golden chariot. The whole city came out to see the son of Kunti, the Bhojas, Vrishnis and Andhakas, and he stayed with Krishna in his palace for many days. {#p11 1.220}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.220}
**Nara and Narayana.** The epic says here, almost in passing, what it will return to again and again: that Arjuna and Krishna are the twin sages Nara and Narayana, born again as men.
::
