::scene{#s1 frame=sanjaya,yudhishthira light=sanjaya}

[Sanjaya](sanjaya) came to Upaplavya, bowed to [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), and brought the old king's greetings: to Bhima, to Arjuna, to the twins, and to Draupadi, *she who never swerves from the path of truth*. {#p1 5.23}

Yudhishthira was glad to see him; it was like seeing the king himself. He asked after everyone in turn: Bhishma, Dhritarashtra and his sons, Bahlika, Somadatta, Drona and his son, Kripa, Yuyutsu, the old ladies, the kitchen maids and the children. Had Duryodhana taken back the gifts Yudhishthira had made to the Brahmanas? Did the Kurus remember Arjuna's arrows, Bhima's charge, the twins' conquests? Did they remember Dvaitavana, where they had been freed by the very men they came to mock? {#p2 5.23}

> It is not by a single good deed, O Sanjaya, that happiness can here be attained, when by all our endeavours we are unable to win over the son of Dhritarashtra! {#q1 5.23}
> — Yudhishthira

***

::scene{#s2 frame=sanjaya light=sanjaya}

They were all well, said Sanjaya. There were good old men around Duryodhana, and wicked ones. Duryodhana would never take back gifts from Brahmanas. And the old king grieved at heart; he had learned from the Brahmanas that strife within a family is the worst of sins. {#p3 5.24}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=sanjaya,yudhishthira,krishna,satyaki,drupada,dhrishtadyumna light=sanjaya}

Before them all, the Pandavas, [Krishna](krishna), [Satyaki](satyaki), Virata, [Drupada](drupada) and [Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna), Sanjaya gave his message. Dhritarashtra wanted peace. The sons of Pritha were too good for a base deed. {#p4 5.25}

> If you committed a sinful act, it would be a blot on your fair name, as a drop of collyrium on a white cloth. {#q2 5.25}
> — Sanjaya

Who could beat the Pandavas, with Krishna and Satyaki and Dhrishtadyumna? And who could beat the Kurus, with Drona and Bhishma, Ashwatthama, Shalya, Kripa and Karna? {#p5 5.25}

> If you, ye sons of Pritha, chastise the Kurus, by defeating and slaying all your foes,—that subsequent life of yours would be equivalent to death, for what, in sooth, is life after having killed all your kinsfolk? {#q3 5.25}
> — Sanjaya

*Therefore it is, that I do not see any good either in victory or in defeat.* He bowed to Krishna and to the old Panchala king, with joined hands. {#p6 5.25}
