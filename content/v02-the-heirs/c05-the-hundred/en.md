::scene{#s1 frame=gandhari,vyasa light=gandhari,vyasa}

One day [Vyasa](vyasa) came to [Gandhari](gandhari)'s house, worn out with hunger and travel. She looked after him with such care that he offered her a boon, and she asked for a hundred sons, each as strong and as gifted as her husband. He granted it. {#p1 1.115}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=1.110,1.115}
**The second boon.** In the last chapter the epic said Gandhari's hundred sons were Shiva's gift. Here it tells them as Vyasa's. It keeps both, and so do we.
::

Gandhari conceived. But the months passed, and the child did not come. For two whole years she carried it, and grieved. {#p2 1.115}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=gandhari,kunti,yudhishthira light=gandhari}

Then she heard that [Kunti](kunti) had given birth to a son, splendid as the morning sun. {#p3 1.115}

Out of her mind with grief and impatience, and without her husband knowing, Gandhari struck her own womb with great force. What came out, after two years, was not a child but a hard mass of flesh, like a ball of iron. She was about to throw it away. {#p4 1.115}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.115}
**A story told out of order.** Kunti's son, born before Gandhari's, is [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira), the eldest of the Pandavas. The epic tells of the hundred first, and only afterwards goes back to tell how Pandu's sons were born in the forest. We follow its order.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=gandhari,vyasa light=vyasa}

Vyasa knew at once, and was there in a moment. *What hast thou done?* he asked. Gandhari did not try to hide what she felt. {#p5 1.115}

> Having heard that Kunti had brought forth a son like unto Surya in splendour, I struck in grief at my womb. Thou hadst, O Rishi, granted me the boon that I should have a hundred sons, but here is only a ball of flesh for those hundred sons! {#q1 1.115}
> — Gandhari

> Daughter of Suvala, it is even so. But my words can never be futile. I have not spoken an untruth even in jest. {#q2 1.115}
> — Vyasa

He told them to bring a hundred pots filled with clarified butter and set them in a hidden place, and to sprinkle cool water on the ball of flesh. As the water fell, the mass divided into pieces, each about the size of a thumb, and each piece was put into a pot. Vyasa told Gandhari to open them after two more years. Then he went away to the Himalayas to his austerities. {#p6 1.115}

::scene{#s4 frame=gandhari,vyasa,duhshala light=duhshala,gandhari}

There was one piece more than a hundred. While Vyasa was dividing the flesh, Gandhari had been wishing silently for a daughter, younger than all her sons, to love as women love their daughters and their daughters' children. When Vyasa had counted a hundred, he told her: *Here are thy hundred sons. I did not speak aught unto thee that was false.* The last piece, he said, would be the daughter she wished for, and he put it in a pot of its own. She was [Duhshala](duhshala). {#p7 1.116}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=duryodhana,dhritarashtra,bhima light=duryodhana}

In time the first of them was born: [Duryodhana](duryodhana). On the very same day, far away, [Bhima](bhima) of the mighty arms was born. {#p8 1.115}

As soon as Duryodhana was born, he began to cry and bray like an ass. Asses and vultures, jackals and crows cried out in answer. Fierce winds blew, and fires broke out on every side. {#p9 1.115}

::scene{#s6 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura,bhishma,duryodhana light=dhritarashtra,vidura}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) was afraid. He gathered [Bhishma](bhishma), [Vidura](vidura), the Kurus and many Brahmins, and asked them plainly: Yudhishthira, the eldest of the princes, would inherit the kingdom by right of birth, and he had nothing to say against it. But would his own son, born after him, become king? {#p10 1.115}

As he spoke, the jackals howled again. The Brahmins and Vidura answered him. {#p11 1.115}

> O king, O bull among men, when these frightful omens are noticeable at the birth of thy eldest son, it is evident that he shall be the exterminator of thy race. The prosperity of all dependeth on his abandonment. Calamity there must be in keeping him. {#q3 1.115}
> — Vidura and the Brahmins

Give him up, they said; he would still have ninety-nine sons. A man may be given up for the sake of a family, a family for a village, a village for a country, and the earth itself for the sake of the soul. {#p12 1.115}

Dhritarashtra loved his son. He did not have the heart to do it. {#p13 1.115}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=dhritarashtra,gandhari,yuyutsu,sughada light=yuyutsu}

Within a month a full hundred sons were born to Dhritarashtra, and a daughter beyond the hundred. {#p14 1.115}

And there was one more son. During the long year of Gandhari's pregnancy, a Vaishya maid had waited on Dhritarashtra, and he had a son by her, intelligent and strong, named [Yuyutsu](yuyutsu). {#p15 1.115}

::aside{#a3 kind=note src=1.115}
**Yuyutsu's mother.** The epic calls her only a Vaishya maid. Later tellings name her [Sughada](sughada), and so does our map. Remember Yuyutsu: on the eve of the great war, he will make a choice none of his brothers makes.
::

So Dhritarashtra had a hundred and two children. Pandu, far away in the forest, would have five sons; yet he lived under a sage's curse, and the epic says his sons were fathered by the gods. How that came to be is the next part of the story. {#p16 1.115}
