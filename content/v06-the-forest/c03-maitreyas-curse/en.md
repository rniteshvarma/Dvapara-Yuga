::scene{#s1 frame=vyasa,dhritarashtra light=vyasa}

[Vyasa](vyasa) did not mince words with [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra). The Pandavas had been cheated, and in the thirteenth year they would remember it all. Restrain your son, he said. Or send him alone into the forest to live with the Pandavas; perhaps he would learn to love them. But it is said a man's nature does not leave him until he dies. {#p1 3.8}

The king said he had never wanted the game; fate had dragged him into it. He knew everything, and still he could not give up his son. {#p2 3.9}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vyasa,dhritarashtra,pandu light=vyasa}

So Vyasa told him a story. Once Surabhi, the mother of all cows, was weeping in heaven, and Indra asked why. She showed him a farmer on earth beating one of her sons, a thin, weak bullock who could hardly pull the plough. *But you have thousands of children*, said Indra. {#p3 3.9}

> Although I have a thousand offspring, yet my affections flow equally towards all! But, O Sakra, I feel greater compassion for one that is weak and innocent! {#q1 3.9}
> — Surabhi

And Indra sent down rain, so the farmer had to stop. {#p4 3.9}

> Thou art possessed of a hundred and one sons, but Pandu hath only five. {#q2 3.9}
> — Vyasa

They were his sons too, said Vyasa, as much as Dhritarashtra was. Let Duryodhana make peace with them. {#p5 3.9}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana,dhritarashtra light=duryodhana}

Dhritarashtra asked Vyasa to speak to Duryodhana himself. Vyasa said the sage Maitreya was coming, fresh from seeing the Pandavas, and would advise the prince. And if he was not obeyed, he would curse him. Then Vyasa left. {#p6 3.10}

Maitreya arrived, tired from the road, and the king and his son received him with honour. He had seen Yudhishthira in the Kamyaka forest, he said, in deer-skin and matted hair, and he had heard what was done in this court, *even like the acts of wretched outcasts*. He turned to [Duryodhana](duryodhana) and spoke to him gently. Do not quarrel with the Pandavas. They are as strong as ten thousand elephants. Bhima has killed Hidimba and Jarasandha, and now the Rakshasa Kirmira too. Make peace. {#p7 3.10}

Duryodhana slapped his thigh, smiled, and scratched the ground with his foot, and said nothing. {#p8 3.10}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=duryodhana,bhima,dhritarashtra light=duryodhana}

Maitreya's eyes went red. He touched water and cursed him. {#p9 3.10}

> In the great war which shall spring out of the wrongs perpetrated by thee, the mighty Bhima shall smash that thigh of thine with a stroke of his mace! {#q3 3.10}
> — Maitreya

Dhritarashtra begged him to take it back. Maitreya said that if Duryodhana made peace, the curse would not take effect; otherwise it must be as he had said. {#p10 3.10}

The king asked how Kirmira had died. *I shall not speak again unto thee*, said Maitreya, *for my words are not regarded by thy son. Vidura will tell you.* And he left. {#p11 3.10}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.10}
**The thigh, twice.** In the dice hall Bhima swore to break the thigh that Duryodhana had bared to Draupadi. Now a sage curses the same thigh, for a smaller insult. The epic often settles a man's fate twice: once by a human vow, and once by a word from heaven.
::
