When the night passed, the kings cried *Array! Array!*, and with conchs and drums, neighing horses, rattling wheels and the shouts of men, the din was enormous. At sunrise the two armies stood fully visible, their chariots in rows like cities, their gold-decked banners in thousands. Ten great kings stood each at the head of an army: Shakuni, Shalya, Jayadratha, Vinda and Anuvinda, the Kekayas, Sudakshina, Shrutayudha of Kalinga, Jayatsena, Brihadbala and Kritavarma. The eleventh, Duryodhana's own, stood in front of them all, and at its head was [Bhishma](bhishma). {#p1 6.16}

> With his white head-gear, white umbrella, and white mail, O monarch, we beheld Bhishma of unfailing prowess look like the risen moon. {#q1 6.16}
> — Sanjaya

The Srinjayas and Dhrishtadyumna trembled at the sight of him, like small animals before a yawning lion. The two armies faced each other like two oceans at the end of an age. {#p2 6.16}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,drona light=bhishma}

It was as Vyasa had said. The seven planets blazed, the sun seemed split in two, jackals and crows cried from every side. And every morning, the grandsire and [Drona](drona) rose and said, with concentrated minds, *Victory to the sons of Pandu*, and then went out and fought for Duryodhana as they had promised. {#p3 6.17}

Bhishma called the kings together. This broad door was open to them into heaven; Nabhaga, Yayati, Mandhatri, Nahusha and Nriga had gone through it. {#p4 6.17}

> To die of disease at home is sin for a Kshatriya. The death he meets with in battle is his eternal duty. {#q2 6.17}
> — Bhishma

Only [Karna](karna), with his friends and kin, laid down his arms for Bhishma's sake. {#p5 6.17}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhishma,ashwatthama,drona,duryodhana,jayadratha,bhagadatta light=bhishma}

Bhishma's palm-tree banner with its five stars shone like the sun. [Ashwatthama](ashwatthama) went at the head of all the divisions under his lion-tail banner; Drona's banner was a golden altar with a water-pot and a bow; Duryodhana's an elephant worked in gems; [Jayadratha](jayadratha)'s a silver boar. The king of Kalinga came with sixty thousand chariots; [Bhagadatta](bhagadatta) rode out on his elephant like the wielder of the thunderbolt. The whole Kuru array had elephants for its body, kings for its head and horses for its wings, and faced every way, *ready to spring*. {#p6 6.17}

Duhshasana, Vikarna and Duryodhana's other brothers guarded Bhishma from behind with twenty thousand chariots, and twelve warrior races swore to guard him with their lives. {#p7 6.18}

> And the ten and one Akshauhinis of thy son, O Bharata, looked, O mighty king, like Ganga separated from Yamuna. {#q3 6.18}
> — Sanjaya
