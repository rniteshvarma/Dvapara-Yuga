All this time Yudhishthira's great fear had been [Karna](karna), whose armour no weapon could pierce. Indra had promised to take that fear away. {#p1 3.298}

One night, as the twelfth year was ending, Karna dreamed that a Brahmana came to him, handsome and shining. It was [Surya](surya), the sun. Indra was coming, he warned, disguised as a Brahmana, to beg Karna's earrings and armour for the sake of the Pandavas. Karna must not give them. {#p2 3.298}

Karna refused. All the world knew his vow, he said: he would give his life itself to a Brahmana who asked. If Indra came begging for the Pandavas, it would bring Karna fame and Indra shame. {#p3 3.298}

> Fame keepeth people alive in this world even like a mother, while infamy killeth men even though they may move about with bodies undestroyed. {#q1 3.298}
> — Karna

***

::scene{#s2 frame=surya,karna light=surya}

> The fame of one that is dead is like a garland of flowers around the neck of a corpse. {#q2 3.299}
> — Surya

There was a secret behind all this, Surya said, which even the gods must not tell; Karna would understand it in time. But if he must give the armour, let him ask for something in return: Indra's own infallible dart, which kills hundreds and comes back to the hand. Then he vanished. In the morning, Karna told the sun his dream, and the sun smiled and said, *It is even so!* {#p4 3.299 3.300}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=kunti,surya,karna light=kunti}

And what was the secret? Long ago, a fierce Brahmana stayed as a guest in the house of King Kuntibhoja, and the king's adopted daughter Pritha, [Kunti](kunti), served him so well that he gave her a mantra by which she could call any god to her. Curious, she called the Sun, and he would not go away without giving her a son. The child was born with armour on his body and earrings made of nectar. {#p5 3.301 3.304 3.306}

Kunti was unmarried and afraid. She laid him in a basket coated with wax and set it on the river Ashva at night, weeping. {#p6 3.306}

The basket drifted from the Ashva into the Charmanvati, into the Yamuna, into the Ganga, and came to the land of Anga, where the charioteer Adhiratha and his wife Radha, who had no son, took it out of the water. They called the child Vasusena. And Kunti learned through spies that her son was growing up as a charioteer's son among the Angas. {#p7 3.306 3.307}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=3.301,3.306}
**The same story, told twice.** The Adi Parva told Kunti's secret at Karna's birth and named the guest as Durvasa. Here the Vana Parva tells it again, at greater length, just before the armour is lost, and leaves the sage unnamed.
::

***

::scene{#s4 frame=indra,karna light=indra}

So when [Indra](indra) came to Karna disguised as a Brahmana, at midday, as Karna came up out of the water and worshipped the sun, when Brahmanas came to ask him for gifts, Karna knew him. *Give me!* said the Brahmana. Karna offered him land, women, cattle, the whole earth. The Brahmana wanted only the armour and earrings. {#p8 3.307 3.308}

Then Karna said, smiling, that he had recognised him: it was not right for the king of the gods to ask a mortal for a gift; rather he should give one. Indra said Surya must have told him. *Except the thunder-bolt alone, tell me what it is that thou desirest to have!* {#p9 3.308}

Karna asked for the dart. Indra gave it on a condition: in Karna's hand it would kill only one enemy, and then return to Indra. And that one, the one Karna wanted to kill, was protected by Krishna. {#p10 3.308}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=karna,indra light=karna}

Then Karna took a blade and cut the armour from his own body, and cut the earrings from his ears, and gave them to Indra still wet with blood. He did not change his expression. He smiled. The gods beat their drums and showered flowers on him; and the text says this is how he came to be called Karna. {#p11 3.308}

And Indra smiled and thought that the business of the sons of Pandu was done, and went back to heaven. {#p12 3.308}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=3.308}
**The dart that kills once.** Karna now has a weapon that will kill one enemy, whoever it is. He means it for Arjuna. Watch where it goes.
::
