::scene{#s1 frame=bhima,yudhishthira light=bhima}

Duryodhana's old attendants came to [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) in tears. The Gandharvas were taking Duryodhana away, they said, and Duhshasana and the princes in chains, and the royal women. Follow them! {#p1 3.240}

[Bhima](bhima) laughed. What they would have had to do with a whole army, someone else had done for them while they sat idle. The wretch had come here to look at them in their misery, and now everyone could see his disgrace. {#p2 3.240}

*This is not time for cruel words*, said Yudhishthira. {#p3 3.240}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=yudhishthira}

Kinsmen quarrel, Yudhishthira said; that happens. But no family lets a stranger insult its honour. The Gandharva king knew the Pandavas were living here, and had seized their kinsman and the women of their house anyway. {#p4 3.241}

> If any stranger seeketh to insult the honour of a family, they that are good never tolerate such insult coming from the stranger. {#q1 3.241}
> — Yudhishthira

Arm yourselves, he told his brothers, and take the Kauravas' own chariots. Even a common Kshatriya protects whoever comes to him for refuge. If he had not been in the middle of a sacrifice, he would have run himself. Try peace first; then light skirmishing; and only if that fails, fight in earnest. {#p5 3.241}

> If the Gandharvas do not set the Dhartarashtras free peacefully, the Earth shall this day drink the blood of the king of the Gandharvas! {#q2 3.241}
> — Arjuna

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=3.241}
**"We are a hundred and five."** A famous saying attributed to Yudhishthira here, that among themselves the Pandavas and Kauravas are five and a hundred, but against outsiders a hundred and five, is a later summary of this scene. Ganguli's text gives the idea in his words about family honour, not in that form.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,chitrasena_k light=arjuna}

The Pandavas asked the Gandharvas to let the Kauravas go. The Gandharvas laughed. So [Arjuna](arjuna) fought them, with his weapons of fire and sun and moon, a net of arrows above and crescent arrows below. Chitrasena came at him with a mace, then made himself invisible, and Arjuna found him by sound alone. {#p6 3.243}

Then the Gandharva king showed himself. {#p7 3.243}

> Behold in me thy friend battling with thee! {#q3 3.243}
> — Chitrasena

It was Chitrasena, who had taught Arjuna music and dance in heaven. Arjuna withdrew his weapons, and they sat in their chariots and asked after each other's health. {#p8 3.243}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna,yudhishthira,duryodhana,indra light=arjuna}

Why have you done this? asked Arjuna. Chitrasena told him. He had known Duryodhana's purpose without leaving his home: to come here in his splendour and mock the Pandavas and Draupadi in their misery. And [Indra](indra) had sent him to bring Duryodhana back in chains, and to protect Arjuna, his friend and pupil. {#p9 3.244}

*Set Suyodhana free, at the command of king Yudhishthira the just, for he is our brother!* said Arjuna. Chitrasena said the king did not yet know why the wretch had come; let him hear it first. {#p10 3.244}

So they went to Yudhishthira and told him everything. And he set all the Kauravas free, and thanked the Gandharvas for not killing his cousin. Indra came himself and brought the dead Gandharvas back to life with nectar. {#p11 3.244}

> O child, never again do such a rash act. O Bharata, a rash wight never cometh by happiness. {#q4 3.244}
> — Yudhishthira

*Return home with all thy brothers*, he told [Duryodhana](duryodhana). *Do not be cheerless.* And Duryodhana bowed to him and went, his heart rent in two with shame, like a man with no life in him. {#p12 3.244}
