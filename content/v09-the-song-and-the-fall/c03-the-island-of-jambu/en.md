So [Sanjaya](sanjaya) described the earth to [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra). Everything is made of the five elements, space, air, fire, water and earth. The island of Jambu, called Sudarshana, is round as a wheel, and its image is seen in the disc of the moon: two parts like a peepul tree, two like a great hare. Six mountain ranges cross it from the eastern ocean to the western, and in the middle stands golden Meru, where the gods gather. {#p1 6.5 6.6}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=6.5}
Sections 6.5–12 are a description of the world: its mountains and *varshas*, the Northern Kurus, the rivers and peoples of Bharata, the four ages, and the seven island-continents ringed by oceans of salt, ghee, curds and wine. It is told here in outline; the full account belongs in the companion volume.
::

He told of the lands north of Meru, where the Northern Kurus live among trees that bear whatever fruit is wished for; of Ramanaka and Hiranmaya and the other varshas, where men live for thousands of years. {#p2 6.7 6.8}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=dhritarashtra,sanjaya,duryodhana light=sanjaya}

Tell me of Bharata's land, Dhritarashtra said, which my son covets so, and the sons of Pandu too. {#p3 6.9}

> The sons of Pandu are not covetous about this country. On the other hand, it is Duryodhana that is covetous. {#q1 6.9}
> — Sanjaya

Then he named its mountains and its rivers, the Ganga, Sindhu and Sarasvati and hundreds more, and its peoples by the hundred. In this land, he said, the four ages turn: Krita, Treta, Dvapara and Kali, each shorter in life and strength than the last. {#p4 6.9 6.10}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=sanjaya light=sanjaya}

Beyond Jambu, he said, lie six more island-continents, Shaka, Kusha, Shalmali, Krauncha and the rest, each twice the size of the one before, each ringed by its own ocean, of salt water, of ghee, of curds, of wine. He told the sizes of Rahu, the moon and the sun. And then he fell silent. {#p5 6.11 6.12}
