::scene{#s1 frame=shantanu,satyavati light=shantanu,satyavati}

Four years after his son came home, [Shantanu](shantanu) was walking in the woods along the Yamuna when a sweet scent reached him on the air. He could not tell where it came from, and he wandered this way and that to find it. At the river he found a black-eyed girl of heavenly beauty, the daughter of a fisherman, standing by a boat. {#p1 1.100}

He asked her who she was, and whose daughter, and what she was doing there. {#p2 1.100}

> Blest be thou! I am the daughter of the chief of the fishermen. At his command, I am engaged for religious merit, in rowing passengers across this river in my boat. {#q1 1.100}
> — Satyavati

The king wanted her for his wife the moment he saw her. He went straight to her father to ask for her hand. {#p3 1.100}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=shantanu,dasharaja,satyavati light=dasharaja}

The [chief of the fishermen](dasharaja) did not refuse. A daughter must be given to a husband, he said, and he could never hope to find her a better one. But there was a promise he had long cherished in his heart, and the king would have to make it first. {#p4 1.100}

> O king, what I ask of thee is this: the son born of this maiden shall be installed by thee on thy throne and none else shall thou make thy successor. {#q2 1.100}
> — the chief of the fishermen

Shantanu felt no inclination to grant it. Though desire burned in him like fire, he gave no promise and went home to Hastinapura. There he could think of nothing but the fisherman's daughter, and he sank into a silent grief. {#p5 1.100}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=shantanu,bhishma light=bhishma,shantanu}

[Devavrata](bhishma) saw it. His father no longer rode out, spoke to no one, and had grown pale and thin. *All is prosperity with thee,* he said to him; *all chiefs obey thee; then how is it that thou grievest thus?* {#p6 1.100}

Shantanu told him a truth, though not all of it. He worried, he said, about how fragile a life is. Devavrata was his only son, and always at the practice of arms; if he fell in battle, the line of the Bharatas would end. {#p7 1.100}

> Truly thou alone art to me as a century of sons. I do not, therefore, desire to wed again. {#q3 1.100}
> — Shantanu

*The wise say that he that hath one son hath no son,* the king added. He did not say a word about the girl on the Yamuna. {#p8 1.100}

Devavrata thought it over, and went to his father's old minister. The minister told him the rest: the girl, whom he called Gandhavati, *the sweet-scented*, and the promise her father demanded. {#p9 1.100}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,dasharaja,satyavati light=bhishma,dasharaja}

So Devavrata went to the fisher-chief himself, with a company of old and honoured Kshatriya chiefs as witnesses, and asked for the girl on his father's behalf. {#p10 1.100}

The chief received him with every honour, and then spoke frankly. Even Indra would regret turning down such a match, he said. The girl's real father, a great man as virtuous as Devavrata himself, had often said that only Shantanu was worthy of her; and he had refused even the celestial sage Asita, who had asked for her more than once. There was only one objection, and Devavrata was it. No one, not even an Asura or a Gandharva, was safe with a rival like him. {#p11 1.100}

Devavrata answered at once, so that every chief could hear. {#p12 1.100}

> O foremost of truthful men, listen to the vow I utter! The man has not been or will not be born, who will have the courage to take such a vow! I shall accomplish all that thou demandest! The son that may be born of this maiden shall be our king. {#q4 1.100}
> — Devavrata

It was not enough. The fisher-chief had no doubt that Devavrata would keep his word, he said. But what about the children Devavrata might have one day? {#p13 1.100}

Then Devavrata made the second vow, the one the epic remembers. {#p14 1.100}

> Ye kings, I have already relinquished my right to the throne, I shall now settle the matter of my children. O fisherman, from this day I adopt the vow of Brahmacharya (study and meditation in celibacy). If I die sonless, I shall yet attain to regions of perennial bliss in heaven! {#q5 1.100}
> — Devavrata

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.99,1.100}
**The curse comes true.** In chapter 2, Vasishtha told the Vasu Dyu that on earth he would obey his father, take no wife and have no children. Here, in a fisherman's house on the Yamuna, the prince takes all of it on himself, freely.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=bhishma light=bhishma}

The fisher-chief's hair stood on end with joy. *I bestow my daughter!* he said. {#p15 1.100}

From the sky the Apsaras, the gods and the sages rained flowers on Devavrata's head, and they cried out a new name for him: *This one is Bhishma*: *the terrible*, the one who had taken a terrible vow. The kings who had witnessed it said the same, and the name never left him. {#p16 1.100}

Then he turned to the girl he had won for his father. {#p17 1.100}

> O mother, ascend this chariot, and let us go unto our house. {#q6 1.100}
> — Bhishma

***

::scene{#s6 frame=shantanu,bhishma,satyavati light=shantanu,bhishma,satyavati}

Bhishma helped her into his chariot and drove her to Hastinapura, and told his father everything that had happened. {#p18 1.100}

Shantanu was overjoyed, and he gave his son the greatest gift he had to give: the power to choose the hour of his own death. {#p19 1.100}

> Death shall never come to thee as long as thou desirest to live. Truly death shall approach thee, O sinless one, having first obtained thy command. {#q7 1.100}
> — Shantanu

The girl from the ferry was [Satyavati](satyavati). Her sons would be kings, as her father had demanded, and Bhishma would stand beside their throne for the rest of his long life. But where had she come from? The epic tells that story much earlier, in its long first book, and it begins with a fish. {#p20 1.100 1.63}
