::scene{#s1 frame=nakula,bhishma,dhritarashtra,drona,kripa,vidura,duryodhana light=nakula}

[Nakula](nakula) went to Hastinapura and formally invited [Bhishma](bhishma), [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) and the elders, and they came with joyful hearts, with Brahmanas walking in front. {#p1 2.34}

Kings came from every land with costly gifts: [Duryodhana](duryodhana) and all his brothers, [Shakuni](shakuni), [Karna](karna), [Shalya](shalya), [Bhurishravas](bhurishravas), [Ashwatthama](ashwatthama), [Kripa](kripa), [Drona](drona), Jayadratha, [Drupada](drupada) and his sons, [Bhagadatta](bhagadatta) with the tribes of the sea-marshes, Virata, and [Shishupala](shishupala) with his son. From the Vrishnis came [Balarama](balarama), [Pradyumna](pradyumna), [Samba](samba) and many more. {#p2 2.34}

They were lodged in white mansions like the peaks of Kailasa, with golden lattices, rows of pearls, carpets and garlands, and looked on Yudhishthira, among his priests, like the gods in heaven. {#p3 2.34}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,duhshasana,ashwatthama,sanjaya,bhishma,drona,kripa,vidura,duryodhana,krishna light=yudhishthira}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) bowed to his grandfather and his teacher and gave everyone work. [Duhshasana](duhshasana) was put in charge of the food; Ashwatthama attended the Brahmanas; [Sanjaya](sanjaya) offered return-honours to the kings; Bhishma and Drona oversaw everything done and left undone; Kripa kept the jewels and gold and the gifts to Brahmanas; [Vidura](vidura) was the paymaster. And Duryodhana received the tribute the kings brought. {#p4 2.35}

[Krishna](krishna), the epic says, chose for himself the task of washing the Brahmanas' feet. {#p5 2.35}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,duryodhana light=yudhishthira}

No one came with tribute of less than a thousand. Every king brought jewels, each proud to think his gift would help complete the sacrifice. The mansions rose so high their roofs touched the chariots of the gods who came to watch. {#p6 2.35}

Yudhishthira, as if rivalling Varuna in wealth, began the Rajasuya with its six fires. Everyone was fed to the full with rice and every kind of food; the gods were satisfied with the offerings and the Brahmanas with gifts. {#p7 2.35}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.35,2.50}
**The man at the door.** Of all the jobs at the Rajasuya, Duryodhana's was to receive the tribute. Every jewel the kings of the earth gave Yudhishthira passed through his hands. He will describe them later, one by one, to his father.
::
