::scene{#s1 frame=duryodhana,shakuni light=duryodhana}

[Duryodhana](duryodhana) stayed on in Maya's hall after the sacrifice, and with [Shakuni](shakuni) he walked slowly through all of it, seeing designs he had never seen in Hastinapura. {#p1 2.47}

One day he came to a crystal floor, took it for a pool, and drew up his clothes. When he found his mistake he wandered on, ashamed. A little later he came to a real pool of clear water, set with crystal lotuses, took it for solid floor, and fell in with all his clothes on. {#p2 2.47}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=duryodhana,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) laughed aloud, and so did the palace servants. Yudhishthira had dry clothes brought for him at once. But Bhima and [Arjuna](arjuna) and the twins all laughed, and Duryodhana, who had never learned to bear an insult, hid his face and would not look at them. {#p3 2.47}

Then he lifted his clothes again to cross a dry floor he thought was water, and they laughed again. He walked into a closed crystal door thinking it open, and struck his head and stood there dizzy. He pushed with both hands at an open door that looked closed, and fell through it. {#p4 2.47}

Later he told his father all of it. The twins had come running and held him up, and Sahadeva had said, *as if with a smile*, *This, O king, is the door. Go this way!* And Draupadi, with the other women, had joined in the laughter. {#p5 2.49}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=2.49}
**The famous line.** Many retellings give Draupadi a cruel joke here: *the son of a blind man is blind too.* Ganguli has no such words. Draupadi's laughter is mentioned once, in Duryodhana's own account of the day, among the laughter of the other women. The joke is a later addition, and it has done her a great deal of harm.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana,shakuni light=duryodhana}

He rode home in silence. Shakuni spoke to him again and again before he answered. {#p6 2.47}

> I, being filled with jealousy and burning day and night, am being dried up like a shallow tank in the summer season. {#q1 2.47}
> — Duryodhana

He had seen the kings of the earth bring tribute to Yudhishthira *like tribute-paying Vaisyas*. He had seen Shishupala killed and no one lift a hand. He had tried before to destroy the Pandavas, and they had grown out of every attempt *like the lotus from within a pool of water*. He would throw himself into fire, or take poison, or drown. He could not live. {#p7 2.47}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=shakuni,duryodhana,yudhishthira light=shakuni}

Shakuni told him not to be jealous: the Pandavas had earned what they had, and Duryodhana had allies too, Drona, Karna, Kripa, Shakuni's own brothers. Then Duryodhana said he would fight them, and Shakuni said that no one, not even the gods, could beat those brothers in battle. {#p8 2.48}

But he knew another way. {#p9 2.48}

> The son of Kunti is very fond of dice-play although he doth not know how to play. That king if asked to play, is ill able to refuse. {#q2 2.48}
> — Shakuni

There was no one on earth, or in the three worlds, who could equal Shakuni at dice. Only, he said, the king had to agree. Let Duryodhana ask his father. {#p10 2.48}

*You ask him*, said Duryodhana. *I shall not be able to.* {#p11 2.48}
