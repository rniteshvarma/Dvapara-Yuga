::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,duhshasana,draupadi light=duhshasana}

The Pandavas took off their royal robes one by one and put on deer-skins. {#p1 2.76}

[Duhshasana](duhshasana) danced among the Kurus. The sovereignty of Duryodhana had begun, he crowed; the sons of Pandu had gone to everlasting hell. They were sesame seeds with no oil in them. He told [Draupadi](draupadi) to choose a husband from among the Kurus while she could. And he danced around Bhima crying, *O cow! O cow!* {#p2 2.76}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima,duryodhana,duhshasana,arjuna,karna light=bhima}

As they left the hall, [Duryodhana](duryodhana) mimicked Bhima's lion-like walk. Bhima half turned. {#p3 2.76}

> I will slay this wretched Duryodhana in battle with my mace, and prostrating him on the ground I will place my foot on his head. {#q1 2.76}
> — Bhima

He would drink Duhshasana's blood like a lion, he said; Arjuna would kill Karna, and Sahadeva Shakuni. [Arjuna](arjuna) said quietly that great men's resolutions are not known by words. {#p4 2.76}

> On the fourteenth year from this day, they shall see what happeneth. {#q2 2.76}
> — Arjuna

But he took Karna as his own: the Himalayas might move and the sun lose its light, but that vow would stand, if in the fourteenth year Duryodhana did not give back their kingdom. [Sahadeva](sahadeva) swore to kill Shakuni, and [Nakula](nakula) to kill every son of Dhritarashtra who had spoken harshly to Draupadi. {#p5 2.76}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,vidura,kunti light=vidura}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) bade farewell to everyone by name. No one could answer him for shame. [Vidura](vidura) said that [Kunti](kunti), old and delicate, should not go into the forest; she would stay in his house. And he blessed them: a man beaten by foul means need not grieve over it. *We hope to see thee return.* {#p6 2.77}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kunti,draupadi,sahadeva light=kunti}

Draupadi went to Kunti to take leave. *Keep thy eye on my child Sahadeva*, said Kunti. Then Draupadi went out, weeping, in one blood-stained cloth, with her hair loose. {#p7 2.78}

Kunti followed her, and saw her sons in deer-skins, heads bowed with shame, their enemies laughing around them. She held them all and wept. {#p8 2.78}

> Alas I have brought you forth. All this must be due to my ill fortune. {#q3 2.78}
> — Kunti

Pandu was lucky, she said, and Madri was lucky, to have died before this. She called on Krishna, and on Pandu, and begged Sahadeva to stay with her. The sons comforted her and went, and Vidura led her slowly to his house. {#p9 2.78}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura,dhaumya light=vidura}

Dhritarashtra sent for Vidura and asked anxiously how they had gone. {#p10 2.79}

Yudhishthira had covered his face, said Vidura, so that his angry eyes would not burn the people. Bhima went stretching out his great arms. Arjuna scattered sand behind him, as easily as he would scatter arrows. Sahadeva had smeared his face so no one would know him; Nakula had covered himself with dust so as not to steal the hearts of women along the road. And [Dhaumya](dhaumya) walked in front with kusha grass, singing the Sama hymns for the dead. {#p11 2.79}

Draupadi went in her one stained cloth, weeping, with her hair loose. That meant, said Vidura, that in the fourteenth year the wives of the men who had done this would enter Hastinapura the same way: widowed, hair unbound, after offering water to their dead. {#p12 2.79}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=2.79,2.78}
**The unbound hair.** Many retellings have Draupadi swear never to tie up her hair until she can wash it in Duhshasana's blood. Ganguli's Sabha Parva has no such vow. Her loose hair is a sign, and Vidura reads it as a prophecy about the Kaurava widows.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=narada,drona,duryodhana,dhrishtadyumna light=narada}

Then [Narada](narada) appeared in the assembly. {#p13 2.79}

> On the fourteenth year hence, the Kauravas, in consequence of Duryodhana's fault, will all be destroyed by the might of Bhima and Arjuna. {#q4 2.79}
> — Narada

Duryodhana, Karna and Shakuni turned to [Drona](drona) and offered him the kingdom. Drona said he would stand by them, since they had come to him, though the Pandavas could not be killed. And he spoke of [Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna), born from the fire with bow and armour to kill him. {#p14 2.79}

> This thy happiness will last for but a moment, even as in winter the shadow of the top of the palm tree resteth (for a short time) at its base. {#q5 2.79}
> — Drona

***

::scene{#s7 frame=dhritarashtra,sanjaya,draupadi light=dhritarashtra}

Dhritarashtra could not rest. [Sanjaya](sanjaya) asked why he grieved, now that he had the whole earth. When destruction comes, said Sanjaya, it does not come with a raised club; it makes a man see evil as good. {#p15 2.80}

> O Sanjaya, the glances of the distressed daughter of Drupada might consume the whole earth. Can it be possible that even a single son of mine will live? {#q6 2.80}
> — Dhritarashtra

He remembered the women wailing, the Brahmanas refusing to light their evening fires, the chariots catching fire by themselves. He remembered Vidura's advice: make peace with the sons of Pandu. {#p16 2.80}

> And I did not accept this counsel, moved by affection for my son. {#q7 2.80}
> — Dhritarashtra
