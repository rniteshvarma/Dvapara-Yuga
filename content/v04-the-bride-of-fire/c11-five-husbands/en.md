::scene{#s1 frame=dhrishtadyumna,drupada light=dhrishtadyumna}

[Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna) told his father everything. The youth who strung the bow, red-eyed, in a deer-skin, and his brother who fought with a tree; the potter's house, and the lady there like a flame; how Krishna had divided the alms and eaten last; how she had slept at their feet. And how they had talked. {#p1 1.195}

> The talk of those heroes indicated them to be neither Vaisyas nor Sudras, nor Brahmanas. Without doubt, O monarch, they are bulls amongst Kshatriyas, their discourse having been on military subjects. {#q1 1.195}
> — Dhrishtadyumna

The Pandavas had escaped the house of lac, he said. These must be the sons of Pritha, in disguise. {#p2 1.195}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=drupada,yudhishthira,bhima light=yudhishthira}

[Drupada](drupada) sent his priest to find out. The priest told them how Drupada had loved Pandu as his own self, and had always hoped to give his daughter to Arjuna. {#p3 1.195}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) had [Bhima](bhima) wash the priest's feet, and answered him carefully. Drupada had set a price for his daughter, and the price had been paid; there was nothing more to ask about the winner's race or family. No weakling and no one of low birth could have strung that bow. The king should not grieve over what could not be undone. {#p4 1.195}

Then a messenger came: the wedding feast was ready. {#p5 1.195}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drupada,dhrishtadyumna,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=drupada}

Drupada was still not sure who they were, so he set a test. He had everything laid out that a wedding of each of the four orders might need: fruits and garlands, cattle and seed and ploughs, the tools of every craft, and also armour, shields, swords, chariots, horses, bows and arrows. {#p6 1.196}

The brothers came in their deer-skins, ate, and then walked past everything else to look at the weapons. Drupada and his son saw, and were glad. {#p7 1.196}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=drupada,yudhishthira light=drupada,yudhishthira}

Drupada asked Yudhishthira plainly whether they were Kshatriyas, Brahmanas, or gods in disguise. *Truth becometh monarchs better than sacrifices and dedications of tanks.* {#p8 1.197}

> We are Kshatriyas, O king, and sons of the illustrious Pandu. {#q2 1.197}
> — Yudhishthira

*Thy daughter, O monarch, hath like a lotus been transferred only from one lake into another.* Drupada's eyes rolled with joy, and for a while he could not speak. Then he asked how they had escaped Varanavata, heard the whole story, cursed Dhritarashtra, and swore to put Yudhishthira back on his father's throne. {#p9 1.197}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=drupada,yudhishthira,arjuna,draupadi light=drupada,yudhishthira}

Then Drupada said: let Arjuna take my daughter's hand today. *I also shall have to marry,* said Yudhishthira. Then take her yourself, said Drupada, or give her to whichever brother you choose. {#p10 1.197}

> Thy daughter, O king, shall be the common wife of us all! Even thus it hath been ordered, O monarch, by our mother. {#q3 1.197}
> — Yudhishthira

> O scion of Kuru's race, it hath been directed that one man may have many wives. But it hath never been heard that one woman may have many husbands! {#q4 1.197}
> — Drupada

> O monarch, morality is subtle. We do not know its course. Let us follow the way trodden by the illustrious ones of former ages. {#q5 1.197}
> — Yudhishthira

His tongue had never told a lie, he said, and his heart had never turned to sin; his mother had commanded it and his heart approved. Drupada told him to talk it over with Kunti and Dhrishtadyumna. And just then [Vyasa](vyasa) arrived. {#p11 1.197}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=vyasa,drupada,dhrishtadyumna,yudhishthira,kunti light=vyasa}

Vyasa asked each of them what they thought. {#p12 1.198}

Drupada said it was a sin, against custom and the Vedas; he had never seen it done. Dhrishtadyumna asked how an elder brother of good character could go to his younger brother's wife. Yudhishthira said he had heard of Jatila of the line of Gotama, who married seven sages, and a sage's daughter who married ten brothers, the Prachetas; and that obeying one's mother is the first of duties. Kunti said only that she was afraid her word would become a lie. {#p13 1.198}

Vyasa told Kunti she would be saved from untruth: this was eternal virtue. But the reason he would tell to Drupada alone. And he took the king by the hand and led him away into a private room. {#p14 1.198}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=vyasa,drupada,indra,shiva light=indra,shiva}

In that room, Vyasa told Drupada a story. {#p15 1.199}

Once [Indra](indra), following a golden lotus up the Ganga to its source, found a weeping woman whose tears turned into golden lotuses as they fell. She led him to a young man playing dice with a girl on a Himalayan peak. Indra told the young man that he, Indra, was lord of the universe. The young man, who was [Shiva](shiva), only smiled, and Indra stood frozen. {#p16 1.199}

Shiva had him roll aside a great stone, and beneath it, in a cave, lay four others like him: four Indras of earlier ages, humbled for their pride, and Shiva ordered Indra in to join them. Trembling, Indra begged to send a portion of himself instead, to be the fifth. All five, Shiva decreed, would be born as men, do great and terrible deeds, and so win back the heaven of Indra; and the weeping woman, who was the goddess Shri, would be their common wife on earth. {#p17 1.199}

Those Indras, Vyasa told Drupada, are the sons of Pandu, and Arjuna is the portion of Indra himself. And the goddess is your daughter. {#p18 1.199}

***

::scene{#s8 frame=drupada,vyasa,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva}

Then Vyasa gave Drupada divine sight, and the king saw the Pandavas as they truly were: in celestial bodies, with golden crowns and garlands, radiant as fire, each like Indra himself. He looked at his daughter and saw that she was a fitting wife for them. He touched Vyasa's feet. {#p19 1.199}

Then Vyasa told him the other story, the one he had told the Pandavas at Ekachakra: of the girl in an earlier life who asked Shiva five times for a husband. *Having listened to all this, O king Drupada, do what thou desirest.* {#p20 1.199}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.199,1.171}
**Two reasons, both given.** Vyasa offers Drupada two explanations: the five Indras and the goddess Shri, and the girl who asked five times. The epic does not choose between them; it gives both, and lets them stand together.
::
