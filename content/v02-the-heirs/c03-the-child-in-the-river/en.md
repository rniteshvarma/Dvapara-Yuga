::scene{#s1 frame=shura,kunti,kuntibhoja,vasudeva light=kunti}

Among the Yadavas there was a chief named [Shura](shura), the father of [Vasudeva](vasudeva). He also had a daughter, Pritha, whose beauty had no rival on earth. Long before, Shura had promised his first-born child to his cousin and friend [Kuntibhoja](kuntibhoja), who had no children of his own, and Shura kept his word. So Pritha grew up in Kuntibhoja's house, and that is why the world knows her as [Kunti](kunti). {#p1 1.111}

In her adoptive father's house, Kunti looked after the Brahmins and the other guests. {#p2 1.111}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti,durvasa light=durvasa,kunti}

Once a guest came who was feared everywhere: [Durvasa](durvasa), a Brahmin of terrible temper and rigid vows. Kunti served him so well that he was pleased with her. And looking into the future with his inner sight, he saw a time of trouble coming for her. {#p3 1.111}

So he gave her a mantra, a formula of invocation by which she could call any god she chose. {#p4 1.111}

> Those celestials that thou shall summon by this Mantra shall certainly approach thee and give thee children. {#q1 1.111}
> — Durvasa

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.111}
**The trouble Durvasa saw.** The epic says plainly what it was: a curse that would fall on her future husband, for killing a deer. We will come to it in its place.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=kunti,surya light=surya,kunti}

Kunti was young, and she was curious. While still unmarried, she spoke the mantra and called on the Sun. {#p5 1.111}

At once she saw him coming toward her, blazing, the god who sees everything in the world. She was overcome with amazement. {#p6 1.111}

> Here I am, O black-eyed girl! Tell me what I am to do for thee. {#q2 1.111}
> — Surya

Kunti was frightened. A Brahmin had given her the mantra as a boon, she said, and she had only called him to see if it worked. She begged his pardon. But [Surya](surya) told her that he knew of Durvasa's gift, and that his coming could not be in vain. If she sent him away with nothing, the fault would be hers. {#p7 1.111}

He said many things to calm her, but she would not consent, out of modesty and fear of her family. At last he told her that, for his sake, it would be no sin; and the Sun had his way. {#p8 1.111}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kunti,surya,karna light=karna}

At once a son was born, wearing armour that was part of his body, his face shining with a pair of earrings. He was beautiful as a child of the gods, and he would be the first of all warriors. Then the Sun restored Kunti's maidenhood and returned to heaven. {#p9 1.111}

Kunti looked at her son with sorrow and thought hard about what to do. Out of fear of her family, she decided to hide the proof of her folly. She cast the child into the water. {#p10 1.111}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=karna,adhiratha,radha light=adhiratha,radha,karna}

He was found. A charioteer, the husband of [Radha](radha), took the child out of the water, and he and his wife raised him as their own son. They named him Vasusena, *born with wealth*, because he was born with armour and earrings. {#p11 1.111}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.111}
**Adhiratha.** In this passage Ganguli names the foster-father only as *the husband of Radha, of the Suta caste*. Elsewhere the epic calls him [Adhiratha](adhiratha). His caste is the charioteers', the Sutas, and that is why the boy will be called *Suta's son* for the rest of his life.
::

The boy grew strong and mastered every weapon. Every morning he worshipped the Sun until the sun's rays warmed his back, and during those hours there was nothing on earth he would refuse to a Brahmin who asked. {#p12 1.111}

::scene{#s6 frame=karna,indra,arjuna light=indra,karna}

That generosity would cost him. The epic tells, here, in a few lines, what it will tell at length much later: that [Indra](indra), wanting to help his own son Arjuna, came to him disguised as a Brahmin and asked for his armour, and that Vasusena cut it from his body and gave it with joined hands. Indra was so moved that he gave him a dart in return, which could kill any one enemy he chose. {#p13 1.111}

And because he cut off his own armour, Vasusena came to be called [Karna](karna), *the one who cut away*. {#p14 1.111}

Kunti, meanwhile, went on living in her father's house as a maiden. No one knew. {#p15 1.111 1.112}
