When Sanjaya had gone, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) turned to [Krishna](krishna). *O thou that art devoted to friends, the time hath come for friends to show their friendship.* Dhritarashtra wanted peace without giving back the kingdom. He had asked for five villages, and been refused even those. {#p1 5.72}

He spoke of poverty: how avarice destroys intelligence, and with it shame, virtue and prosperity; how kinsmen shun a poor man *as birds avoid a tree that beareth neither flower nor fruits*; how a man born to wealth and robbed of it suffers more than one who was always poor. {#p2 5.72}

And he spoke of war. A Kshatriya kills a Kshatriya, *fishes live on fish; a dog preyeth upon a dog*. A coward may kill a hero; the gentle die and the wicked escape; the victor loses those he loves. And the dead always leave someone to remember. {#p3 5.72}

> Enmity, O Kesava, is never neutralised by enmity; on the other hand, it is fomented by enmity, like fire fed by clarified butter. {#q1 5.72}
> — Yudhishthira

He did not want to give up the kingdom, nor to see his race destroyed. Peace, even through humiliation, was best; and only when peace failed would war come, as it does with dogs: first the wagging of tails, then the bark, then the circling, the bared teeth, and the fight. What did Krishna think? {#p4 5.72}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,yudhishthira light=krishna}

> I will go to the court of the Kurus for the sake of both of you. If without sacrificing your interests I can obtain peace, O king, an act of great religious merit will be mine, productive of great fruits. {#q2 5.72}
> — Krishna

Yudhishthira did not want him to go: all the Kshatriyas of the world were gathered there for Duryodhana, and if any harm came to Krishna, nothing would give them joy. Krishna said that if they did him any harm, he would consume the Kurus; and even if he failed, they would at least be free of blame before all the kings. {#p5 5.72}

> Whether peace is to be established by (apparent) sin or by any other means, O Kesava, speak such words as may prove beneficial to us. {#q3 5.72}
> — Yudhishthira
