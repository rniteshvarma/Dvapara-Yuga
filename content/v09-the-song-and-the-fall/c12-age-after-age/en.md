This yoga, [Krishna](krishna) said, he had taught long ago to Vivasvat the sun, who taught it to Manu, who taught it to Ikshvaku; the royal sages knew it, and in time it was lost. Now he had told it again to Arjuna, his devotee and friend. {#p1 6.28}

But you were born later, [Arjuna](arjuna) said; how could you have taught it to Vivasvat? {#p2 6.28}

Many births of mine have passed, and of yours, Krishna said. I know them all; you do not. Though unborn and unchanging, lord of all creatures, I come into being by my own power. {#p3 6.28}

> Whenever, O Bharata, loss of piety and the rise of impiety occurreth, on those occasions do I create myself. For the protection of the righteous and for the destruction of the evil doers, for the sake of establishing Piety, I am born age after age. {#q1 6.28}
> — Krishna

> In whatsoever manner men come to me, in the selfsame manner do I accept them. {#q2 6.28}
> — Krishna

Even the wise are confused about what is action and what is inaction. The wise man sees inaction in action and action in inaction; his works are burned up in the fire of knowledge. Content with whatever comes, beyond the pairs of opposites, unjealous, the same in success and failure, he is not bound though he acts. {#p4 6.28}

For him the whole act is Brahman: the ladle, the offering, the fire, the one who offers, and the goal. There are many sacrifices: of wealth, of austerity, of study, of breath held and released. But the sacrifice of knowledge is greater than any, and knowledge is learned by bowing, by asking and by service, from those who have seen the truth. {#p5 6.28}

> As a blazing fire, O Arjuna, reduceth fuel to ashes, so doth the fire of knowledge reduce all actions to ashes. {#q3 6.28}
> — Krishna

Even the worst of sinners can cross over on the raft of knowledge. But for the doubter there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness. So, said Krishna, cut this doubt born of ignorance with the sword of knowledge, take to yoga, and stand up. {#p6 6.28}
