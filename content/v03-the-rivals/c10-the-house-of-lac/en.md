::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,kunti,bhishma,dhritarashtra,drona,kripa,vidura light=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,kunti}

The Pandavas touched the feet of [Bhishma](bhishma), [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), [Drona](drona), [Kripa](kripa), [Vidura](vidura) and the other elders, embraced their equals, took leave of the women of the house and even of the children, and set out for Varanavata. Vidura and many of the citizens followed them a long way. {#p1 1.147}

Some of the citizens said aloud what they all thought. Dhritarashtra did not see with an even eye; he could not bear his brother's sons. How could Bhishma allow it? They would leave the city and go wherever Yudhishthira went. {#p2 1.147}

> The king is our father, worthy of regard, our spiritual guide, and our superior. To carry out with unsuspicious hearts whatever he biddeth, is indeed, our duty. {#q1 1.147}
> — Yudhishthira

He sent them home with his thanks, and they went. {#p3 1.147}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vidura,yudhishthira,kunti light=vidura,yudhishthira}

Then [Vidura](vidura) spoke to [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) in a foreign tongue, the speech of the Mlecchas, which only the two of them understood. {#p4 1.147}

> He that knoweth that there are sharp weapons capable of cutting the body though not made of steel, and understandeth also the means of warding them off, can never be injured by foes. {#q2 1.147}
> — Vidura

He went on in riddles. Neither the eater of straw and wood nor the drier of dew burns those who live in a hole in the deep woods. A man given a weapon not made of steel by his enemies can escape the fire by making his home like a jackal's burrow, with many ways out. By wandering a man learns the paths, and by the stars he finds his direction. {#p5 1.147}

*I have understood thee,* said Yudhishthira, and Vidura walked round them in farewell and went home. {#p6 1.147}

When they were alone, [Kunti](kunti) asked what Vidura had said. Yudhishthira told her: the house they would live in at Varanavata was built to burn, and they would not be left without a way out. {#p7 1.147}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,i8787_purochana light=yudhishthira,i8787_purochana}

They set out on the eighth day of the month of Phalguna, under the star Rohini. The people of Varanavata came out in thousands to meet them, crying *Victory!*, and the Pandavas visited the homes of every order in the town, Brahmanas, officials, Sutas, Vaishyas and even Shudras. [Purochana](i8787_purochana) received them with every comfort. {#p8 1.148}

After ten nights he showed them their new house, which he called *the Blessed Home*. The epic calls it what it was: *the cursed house*. {#p9 1.148}

Yudhishthira walked through it, smelt fat and butter and lac, and turned to [Bhima](bhima). *This house is truly built of inflammable materials!* Hemp, resin, heath, straw and bamboo, all soaked in butter; and Purochana waiting to burn them when they trusted him. {#p10 1.148}

Bhima wanted to go back to their first lodgings. Yudhishthira said no. {#p11 1.148}

> If Purochana findeth from our countenances that we have fathomed designs, acting with haste he may suddenly burn us to death. {#q3 1.148}
> — Yudhishthira

If they ran, Duryodhana, with the rank, the allies and the treasury they lacked, would hunt them down with spies. Better to stay, seem to trust, go hunting by day and learn every path in the forest, and dig a tunnel from their own room in secret. {#p12 1.148}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,vidura light=yudhishthira}

A friend of Vidura's came to them secretly, a skilled miner. He gave them proof that Vidura had sent him: he knew of the words in the foreign tongue. Purochana, he told them, would set fire to the door of their house on the fourteenth night of the dark fortnight. {#p13 1.149}

Yudhishthira trusted him. The miner dug a large tunnel, with its mouth in the middle of the house, level with the floor and covered with planks, because Purochana kept constant watch at the door. The Pandavas slept with their weapons ready and went hunting by day, looking content and trusting while they were neither. No one else knew: not the citizens, not Purochana. Only the miner. {#p14 1.149}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,kunti,i8787_purochana light=bhima,kunti}

They lived there a full year, and Purochana grew pleased with himself. Yudhishthira saw it and told his brothers the time had come. They would set fire to the house themselves, leave Purochana to burn in it, and the six of them would escape unseen. {#p15 1.150}

One night Kunti held a feast for Brahmanas. Many women came, ate and drank, and went home. And there came too, as if driven by fate, a Nishada woman looking for food, with her five sons. They drank wine until they were senseless and lay down to sleep in the house. {#p16 1.150}

That night a violent wind blew. When everyone was asleep, Bhima set fire to the house where Purochana slept, then to the door of the house of lac, and then to every side. When it was burning, the Pandavas and their mother went down into the tunnel. {#p17 1.150}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=bhima,kunti,yudhishthira,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=bhima}

The roar and heat of the fire woke the town. The people gathered round the burning house and wept. Purochana had built it on Duryodhana's orders to kill his master's own kin, they said; shame on Dhritarashtra's partial heart. And the wretch had burnt in it himself, as fate would have it. {#p18 1.150}

The Pandavas came out of the tunnel and fled into the dark. But they were sleepy and afraid, and could not go fast. So Bhima took them all: his mother on his shoulders, the twins on his hips, Yudhishthira and Arjuna in his arms. And he went on through the forest, breaking the trees with his chest and pressing the earth down as he went. {#p19 1.150}

***

::scene{#s7 frame=vidura,yudhishthira,kunti light=vidura}

Vidura had sent another trusted man into those woods. He found the Pandavas at a river, measuring its depth, and showed them a boat on the Ganga with engines and flags, built to ride wind and wave. He proved himself, too, with Vidura's riddle of the fire and the hole in the forest. {#p20 1.151}

> O son of Kunti, thou shalt surely defeat in battle Karna, and Duryodhana with his brothers, and Sakuni. {#q4 1.151}
> — Vidura's message

He took them across the Ganga, saw them safe on the far bank, said *Victory* to them, and went back. The Pandavas sent a message to Vidura with him, and went on in haste and in secret. {#p21 1.151}

***

::scene{#s8 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura,bhishma light=dhritarashtra,vidura}

In the morning the townspeople put out the fire. They saw that the house had been made of lac, and that Purochana had burned in it, and said aloud that Duryodhana had done this with his father's knowledge. They searched the ashes, and found the Nishada woman and her five sons, burnt to death. The miner, clearing the ashes, covered the mouth of his tunnel so that no one saw it. {#p22 1.152}

They sent word to Hastinapura that the Pandavas were dead. Dhritarashtra wept. *King Pandu, my brother of great fame, hath, indeed, died today,* he said, and ordered the funeral rites for the princes and their mother. The whole family offered water to the dead and cried out their names: *O Yudhishthira! O Bhima! O Phalguna! O the twins! O Kunti!* {#p23 1.152}

The citizens wept too. Vidura did not weep much, because he knew the truth. {#p24 1.152}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.152,1.150}
**The six in the ashes.** The six bodies that let the town believe the Pandavas and their mother had burned were a Nishada woman and her five sons, who had come to the feast for food. The epic calls her *innocent* and says no more. Her name is not recorded.
::

***

::scene{#s9 frame=bhima,kunti,yudhishthira,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=bhima,yudhishthira}

On the far bank the six went south, finding their way in the dark by the stars, until they came, worn out, to a dense forest. They were thirsty and could hardly keep their eyes open. {#p25 1.152}

> What can be more painful than this? We are now in the deep woods. We know not which side is which, nor can we proceed much further. {#q5 1.152}
> — Yudhishthira

*Thou alone amongst us art strong and swift as the wind,* he said to Bhima. So Bhima took his mother and his brothers on his body again and went on into the forest. {#p26 1.152}
