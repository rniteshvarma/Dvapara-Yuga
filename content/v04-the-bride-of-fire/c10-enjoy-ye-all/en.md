::scene{#s1 frame=kunti,draupadi,bhima,arjuna light=kunti,draupadi}

The brothers came to their mother and told her they had brought home the day's alms: the daughter of Yajnasena. [Kunti](kunti) was inside, and did not see. {#p1 1.193}

> Enjoy ye all (what ye have obtained). {#q1 1.193}
> — Kunti

The next moment she saw [Draupadi](draupadi), and said, *Oh, what have I said?* {#p2 1.193}

She took the girl by the hand and went to Yudhishthira. She had spoken without knowing, she said; how could her words not become untrue, and how could sin not touch the king's daughter, and how could the girl not be made unhappy? {#p3 1.193}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna light=yudhishthira,arjuna}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) thought for a moment and said to Arjuna: *By thee hath Yajnaseni been won.* He should light the fire and marry her. {#p4 1.193}

> O king, do not make me a participator in sin. Thy behest is not conformable to virtue. {#q2 1.193}
> — Arjuna

The eldest should marry first, Arjuna said, then Bhima, then he himself, then Nakula, then Sahadeva. All of them, and the princess too, would do whatever Yudhishthira decided was right. {#p5 1.193}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi light=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi}

Then the brothers all looked at Draupadi, and she looked at all of them, and they looked at one another and sat down, and could think of nothing but her. {#p6 1.193}

Yudhishthira saw what was in his brothers' faces. He remembered what Vyasa had told them, and he was afraid of what it would do to them if they quarrelled over her. {#p7 1.193}

> The auspicious Draupadi shall be the common wife of us all. {#q3 1.193}
> — Yudhishthira

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.193,1.171}
**Not settled yet.** Yudhishthira's word decides it among the brothers, but not for the world. Drupada will be horrified when he hears, and it will take Vyasa himself, in the chapters ahead, to explain why five husbands is not a sin for Draupadi.
::

::scene{#s4 frame=krishna,balarama,yudhishthira,kunti light=krishna,yudhishthira}

That evening [Krishna](krishna) came to the potter's house with [Balarama](balarama). He went to Yudhishthira, touched his feet, and said, *I am Krishna.* Balarama did the same, and both of them touched the feet of Kunti, their father's sister. {#p8 1.193}

Yudhishthira asked how he had found them, living in disguise. {#p9 1.193}

> O king, fire, even if it is covered, can be known. Who else among men than the Pandavas could exhibit such might? {#q4 1.193}
> — Krishna

It was good fortune that they had escaped the fire, he said, and that Duryodhana had failed. Then, so that no king would recognise them through him, he left quickly. {#p10 1.193}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=dhrishtadyumna,kunti,draupadi,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,yudhishthira light=dhrishtadyumna,draupadi}

Someone else had followed them home. [Dhrishtadyumna](dhrishtadyumna), sending away his attendants, hid himself in the potter's house to see who these men were. {#p11 1.194}

That evening the brothers came in from begging and gave everything to Yudhishthira, and Kunti told Draupadi what to do with it: a portion for the gods and for Brahmanas and guests, then half of what remained for Bhima, who ate a great deal, and the other half in six parts, for the four brothers, for Kunti and for herself. The king's daughter did it cheerfully, and they ate. {#p12 1.194}

Sahadeva spread a bed of kusha grass on the floor, and each brother laid his deer-skin on it, heads to the south. Kunti lay along the line of their heads, and Draupadi along the line of their feet. She did not grieve, the epic says, or think less of them. {#p13 1.194}

They talked into the night, and they did not talk like beggars. They talked of celestial chariots, weapons and elephants, swords, arrows and battle-axes. And in his hiding place the prince of Panchala listened to every word. {#p14 1.194}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=drupada,dhrishtadyumna,arjuna light=drupada}

In the morning Dhrishtadyumna hurried to his father. [Drupada](drupada) had been miserable all night, not knowing who had taken his daughter. {#p15 1.194}

Who had won her? he asked. Had some low-born man put his foot on his head? Had his wreath of flowers been thrown on a cremation ground? He would not grieve, he would be overjoyed, if she had gone to Arjuna. {#p16 1.194}

> O, are the sons of that foremost of Kurus, Vichitravirya's son alive? Was it Partha (Arjuna) that took up the bow and shot the mark? {#q5 1.194}
> — Drupada
