::scene{#s1 frame=kunti,dharma,yudhishthira light=dharma,yudhishthira}

It was a year after [Gandhari](gandhari) had conceived when [Kunti](kunti) made her offerings and spoke the mantra Durvasa had taught her. The god of justice came to her in a chariot as bright as the sun. Smiling, he asked her what he should give her. Smiling in turn, she said: *Thou must even give me offspring.* {#p1 1.123}

So she bore a son to [Dharma](dharma), a son devoted to the good of every living thing. He was born at noon, at the hour called Abhijit, on an auspicious day, and as soon as he was born a voice spoke from the sky. {#p2 1.123}

> This child shall be the best of men, the foremost of those that are virtuous. Endued with great prowess and truthful in speech, he shall certainly be the ruler of the earth. And this first child of Pandu shall be known by the name of Yudhishthira. {#q1 1.123}
> — a voice from the sky

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti,vayu,bhima light=vayu,bhima}

Then [Pandu](pandu) said to his wife that a Kshatriya without strength is no Kshatriya, and asked her for a son of great strength. So Kunti called on [Vayu](vayu), the god of the wind, and he came to her riding on a deer. She asked him, smiling shyly, for a child with great strength and great limbs, able to humble anyone's pride. {#p3 1.123}

So [Bhima](bhima) was born, of the mighty arms and fierce prowess, and again the voice spoke: *This child shall be the foremost of all endued with strength.* {#p4 1.123}

Something remarkable happened after he was born. One day Kunti, sitting with the baby asleep in her lap, was startled by a tiger and leapt up, forgetting the child. He fell from her lap onto the mountainside below. The rock he fell on shattered into a hundred pieces, and the baby was not hurt at all. Pandu looked on in wonder. {#p5 1.123}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.123,1.115}
**Two births on one day.** The epic says again that Bhima was born on the very day [Duryodhana](duryodhana) was born. The two will be rivals all their lives.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=pandu,indra light=pandu,indra}

Then Pandu wanted a son greater still, a son whose fame would fill the world, and he thought of [Indra](indra), the king of the gods. To win Indra's favour he took counsel with the sages and set Kunti a vow for a full year, while he himself stood on one leg from morning to evening, rapt in meditation. {#p6 1.123}

After a long time Indra came, and promised him a son who would be famous in the three worlds, the friend of the good and the destroyer of the wicked. Pandu told Kunti that her vow had borne fruit. {#p7 1.123}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kunti,indra,arjuna light=arjuna,indra}

So Kunti called on Indra, and [Arjuna](arjuna) was born. As soon as he was born, a voice deep as thunder filled the sky, so that every creature on the mountain heard it, and it told what he would do. {#p8 1.123}

> This child of thine, O Kunti, will be equal unto Kartavirya in energy and Siva in prowess. Invincible like Sakra himself he will spread thy fame far and wide. {#q2 1.123}
> — a voice from the sky

The voice went on. He would feed the fire-god Agni with the whole Khandava forest; with his brothers he would perform three great sacrifices; he would please Shiva in battle and receive from him the weapon called Pashupata; at Indra's command he would destroy the Nivatakavacha demons, the enemies of the gods; and he would win every celestial weapon and restore the fortunes of his race. {#p9 1.123}

Kunti heard it all lying in her room. Drums sounded across the sky, flowers fell from invisible hands, and the gods and sages, the Gandharvas and the Apsaras, the Nagas and the Adityas and the Rudras gathered on the mountain to sing and dance in honour of the child. Only the great sages saw them there; and their love for Pandu's sons grew all the more. {#p10 1.123}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=kunti,pandu light=kunti}

Pandu wanted still more sons. But this time Kunti refused. The wise, she reminded him, did not approve a fourth birth of this kind even in distress, and he knew the law as well as she did. Why was he asking, forgetting it in his longing for children? {#p11 1.123}

So Pandu had three sons: Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma; Bhima, the son of the Wind; and Arjuna, the son of Indra. His second wife had none. {#p12 1.123 1.124}
