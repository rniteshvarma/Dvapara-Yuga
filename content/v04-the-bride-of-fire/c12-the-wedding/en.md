::scene{#s1 frame=drupada,vyasa light=drupada}

[Drupada](drupada) gave way. {#p1 1.200}

> The knot of destiny cannot be untied. Nothing in this world is the result of our own acts. {#q1 1.200}
> — Drupada

What he had arranged for one bridegroom, he said, had ended in many. The god himself had given the boon, and knew its right and wrong; when Shiva had ordained it, no sin could fall on Drupada. *Let these with happy hearts take, as ordained, the hand of Krishna with the rites.* {#p2 1.200}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vyasa,yudhishthira,draupadi,dhaumya light=yudhishthira,draupadi}

That very day, said [Vyasa](vyasa), the moon was in the constellation Pushya; it was auspicious. Yudhishthira should marry first. {#p3 1.200}

The palace was hung with jewels and strewn with lotuses, and lined with troops; friends, ministers, Brahmanas and citizens took their places. [Draupadi](draupadi) was brought out, bathed and decked in jewels and pearls. The Pandavas came in one after another, bathed, perfumed, in ear-rings and fine robes, with their priest [Dhaumya](dhaumya) walking before them, bright as fire. {#p4 1.200}

Dhaumya lit the sacred fire and poured the butter, and joined [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) and Draupadi; they walked round the fire hand in hand. Then, day by day, each of the brothers in turn took her hand. {#p5 1.200}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.200}
**A wonder, as the epic tells it.** Vaisampayana adds something the sage Narada told him: that after each day's wedding, the princess became a maiden again. The epic reports it as a marvel, on Narada's word.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=drupada,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=drupada}

Then Drupada gave them gifts, with the sacred fire as witness: a hundred chariots with golden standards, each drawn by four horses with golden bridles; a hundred elephants like mountains with golden peaks; a hundred young serving-women; and wealth, robes and ornaments for each of them. {#p6 1.200}

And the Pandavas lived happily in Drupada's capital, like so many Indras, with Draupadi like a second Shri. Drupada, allied with them now, was afraid of nothing, not even of the gods. {#p7 1.200 1.201}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kunti,draupadi light=kunti,draupadi}

The women of Drupada's household came to [Kunti](kunti), told her their names and bowed at her feet. And Draupadi, in red silk, with the wedding thread still on her wrist, came and stood before her mother-in-law with folded hands. {#p8 1.201}

Kunti blessed her. {#p9 1.201}

> Be thou unto thy husband as Sachi unto Indra, Swaha unto Vibhavasu, Rohini unto Soma, Damayanti unto Nala, Bhadra unto Vaisravana, Arundhati unto Vasishtha, Lakshmi unto Narayana! {#q2 1.201}
> — Kunti

She wished her heroic, long-lived children, and the queenship of the Kuru country with Yudhishthira, and husbands who would conquer the earth and give it away at the horse sacrifice. {#p10 1.201}

> And, O daughter-in-law, as I rejoice today beholding thee attired in red silk, so shall I rejoice again, when, O accomplished one, I behold thee become the mother of a son! {#q3 1.201}
> — Kunti

***

::scene{#s5 frame=krishna,yudhishthira light=krishna}

When the weddings were over, [Krishna](krishna) sent his cousins gifts: gold ornaments set with pearls and lapis, robes from many countries, soft blankets and hides, beds, carpets and carriages, vessels set with gems, serving-women by the thousand, trained elephants from Madra, horses in fine harness, and gold coins in heaps beyond counting. Yudhishthira accepted them all with joy, to please him. {#p11 1.201}
