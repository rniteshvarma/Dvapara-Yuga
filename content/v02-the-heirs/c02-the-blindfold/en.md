::scene{#s1 frame=bhishma,vidura,dhritarashtra,pandu light=bhishma,vidura}

The line of the Kurus had been saved once. [Bhishma](bhishma) meant to make sure it never came so close to ending again. He called [Vidura](vidura) to him and told him what he had heard: there were three princesses worthy of the Kurus. One was the daughter of a Yadava chief, one the daughter of Subala of Gandhara, and one a princess of Madra. Bhishma thought they should seek all three. What did Vidura think? {#p1 1.110}

> Thou art our father and thou art our mother, too. Thou art our respected spiritual instructor. Therefore, do thou what may be best for us in thy eyes. {#q1 1.110}
> — Vidura

***

::scene{#s2 frame=gandhari,subala,shiva light=gandhari,shiva}

Soon afterwards Bhishma heard from the Brahmins that [Gandhari](gandhari), the gentle daughter of [Subala](subala), had worshipped [Shiva](shiva), and had been granted a boon: she would have a hundred sons. {#p2 1.110}

Bhishma sent messengers to the king of Gandhara. Subala hesitated, because the bridegroom was blind. But he weighed the blood of the Kurus, their fame and their conduct, and he agreed to give his daughter to [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra). {#p3 1.110}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=gandhari,dhritarashtra light=gandhari}

Gandhari heard that the man she was to marry was blind, and that her parents had consented. And out of love and respect for her future husband, she bound her own eyes. {#p4 1.110}

The epic says it in a single sentence and moves on. It does not say here how long she kept the cloth there. The rest of the story shows her still blindfolded, decades later. {#p5 1.110}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=shakuni,gandhari,dhritarashtra,bhishma light=shakuni,gandhari}

Her brother [Shakuni](shakuni), the son of Subala, brought her to the Kurus, young and beautiful, and formally gave her to Dhritarashtra. The wedding was celebrated with great splendour under Bhishma's direction. Then Shakuni, having given his sister away with many fine robes, and having received Bhishma's honours, went home to his own city. {#p6 1.110}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=1.110}
**Shakuni, as the epic first shows him.** This is Shakuni's first appearance in the story: a prince who escorts his sister to her wedding, is honoured by Bhishma, and returns home. A popular story, best known from television, says that the Kurus later threw Gandhari's family into prison and starved them, and that Shakuni's long revenge began there. That story is not in Vyasa's Mahabharata. We will tell it, clearly marked as a later telling, in Shakuni's own volume.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=gandhari,dhritarashtra,bhishma,satyavati light=gandhari}

Gandhari won the hearts of all the Kurus with her conduct and her care. She was devoted to her husband and respectful to her elders, and so chaste, the text says, that she never even spoke of any man except her husband and her elders. {#p7 1.110}

::aside{#a2 kind=versions src=1.110,1.115}
**Whose boon?** Here the epic says Gandhari's hundred sons were Shiva's gift. A few sections later it tells how she served Vyasa when he came to her tired and hungry, and how *he* granted her a hundred sons. The Mahabharata keeps both, and we will meet the second in its place.
::

The other two princesses Bhishma had named were still to come: the Yadava girl, and the princess of Madra. Both would marry Pandu. But the first of them, before she ever came to Hastinapura, had already done something no one at court knew about. {#p8 1.110 1.111}
