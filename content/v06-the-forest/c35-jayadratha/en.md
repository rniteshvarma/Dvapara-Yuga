::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi,dhaumya light=draupadi}

One day the brothers went hunting in four directions to feed the Brahmanas, and left [Draupadi](draupadi) at the hermitage, in the care of the sage Trinabindu and of [Dhaumya](dhaumya). {#p1 3.262}

That day [Jayadratha](jayadratha), king of Sindhu, son of [Vriddhakshatra](vriddhakshatra), came through the Kamyaka forest in his finest clothes, with many princes, on his way to the land of the Shalvas to take a wife. He saw Draupadi standing at the door of the hermitage, *like lightning illuminating masses of dark clouds*, and wanted her. *I have no need to marry if I can secure this exquisitely beautiful creature.* {#p2 3.262}

He sent the prince Kotika to ask who she was. {#p3 3.262}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=draupadi light=draupadi}

Kotika asked whether she was a goddess, a Yakshi or an Apsara, and named all the kings with him: the king of Trigarta, the prince of the Pulindas, and Jayadratha with his six thousand chariots. {#p4 3.263}

Draupadi let go of the kadamba branch she was holding and put her silk in order. It was not proper to speak to him alone, she said, but there was no one else. She was Drupada's daughter, Krishna, wife of the five Pandavas, who had gone hunting; Yudhishthira to the east, Bhima south, Arjuna west, the twins north. Let the kings alight and wait, and the Pandavas would welcome them. Then she went inside. {#p5 3.264}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=jayadratha,draupadi light=jayadratha}

Jayadratha said that since he had seen her, other women seemed like monkeys to him. He went into the hermitage with six men, *like a wolf entering the den of a lion*. Draupadi greeted him as a guest, offered water for his feet, and promised the king's hunt for his breakfast. {#p6 3.265}

*Come now and ride my chariot*, said Jayadratha. A woman of sense does not stay with a husband who is poor. Leave the Pandavas and be queen of Sindhu and Sauvira. {#p7 3.265}

> Speak not thus again! Art thou not ashamed? Be on thy guard! {#q1 3.265}
> — Draupadi

And she began to keep him talking, waiting for her husbands to come back. {#p8 3.265}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=draupadi,jayadratha,arjuna,bhima,krishna light=draupadi}

She told him he was kicking a sleeping lion to pull the hair from its face. He would run when he saw Bhima angry. Like the bamboo and the plantain, which bear fruit only to die, he would lay hands on her to his own destruction. {#p9 3.266}

> Even Indra himself cannot abduct her for whose protection Krishna and Arjuna would together follow, riding in the same chariot. {#q2 3.266}
> — Draupadi

*Defile me not by your touch!* she said, and called for Dhaumya. Jayadratha seized her by her upper garment. She pushed him, and he fell to the ground like a tree cut from its roots. He seized her again, and dragged her, gasping, to his chariot. She bowed to Dhaumya's feet as she went. {#p10 3.266}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=dhaumya,jayadratha light=dhaumya}

Dhaumya stood in front of Jayadratha. {#p11 3.266}

> Thou canst not carry her off without having vanquished those great warriors. {#q3 3.266}
> — Dhaumya

And the old priest walked into the middle of Jayadratha's soldiers and followed the chariot on foot. {#p12 3.266}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.262}
**The brother-in-law.** Jayadratha is married to Duhshala, the only daughter of Dhritarashtra and Gandhari. The man who carries off Draupadi is the husband of the Kauravas' sister, and so also a kinsman of the Pandavas.
::
