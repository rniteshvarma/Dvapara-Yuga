::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,kunti light=kunti}

At Ekachakra the Pandavas lived in the Brahmana's house and begged for their food, and the townspeople came to love them. Every evening they brought Kunti what they had been given, and she divided it: half for herself and four of her sons, and the other half for [Bhima](bhima) alone. {#p1 1.159}

One day the others were out begging and Bhima was at home with his mother, when they heard a terrible wailing from inside the Brahmana's rooms. [Kunti](kunti) could not bear it. {#p2 1.159}

> O son, I always think of the good I should do to this Brahmana, like what they do that live happily in others' abodes! {#q1 1.159}
> — Kunti

*Find out what is wrong,* Bhima said, *and I will put it right, however hard it is.* {#p3 1.159}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti light=kunti}

Kunti went in, quick as a cow running to her tethered calf. The Brahmana sat with his wife, his son and his daughter, all in despair, and he was speaking. {#p4 1.159}

> Oh, fie on this earthly life which is hollow as the reed and so fruitless after all which is based on sorrow and hath no freedom, and which hath misery for its lot! {#q2 1.159}
> — the Brahmana

He had wanted to leave this place long ago, he told his wife, but she would not go; she had been born here. Now one of them must die, and he could give up none of them: not her, his partner in every good deed; not his little son; not his daughter, placed in his keeping for a husband. He loved them equally. If he went himself, they could not live without him. Better they all died together. {#p5 1.159}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=kunti light=kunti}

His wife would not have it. She would go. She had borne him a son and a daughter; her debt to him was paid. He could look after the children, and she could not; a widow with a daughter would be at the mercy of every unworthy suitor, and men circle a woman who has lost her husband *as birds seek with avidity for meat that hath been thrown away.* And a Rakshasa, she said, might not even kill a woman. {#p6 1.160}

The Brahmana held her, and the two of them wept in silence. {#p7 1.160}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kunti light=kunti}

Then their daughter spoke. They must lose her one day anyway, to a husband, she said; let them lose her now and save everyone. *Cross the stream of your difficulties by means of my poor self, as if I were a raft.* If her father died, her little brother would die too, and the offerings to the ancestors would stop. Let her go. {#p8 1.161}

All three wept together. And then the little boy, his eyes wide, went from one to the other. {#p9 1.161}

> Weep not, O father, nor thou, O mother, nor thou O sister! {#q3 1.161}
> — the Brahmana's son

He picked up a blade of grass and said, delighted: *With this will I slay the Rakshasa who eateth human beings!* And in spite of everything, they smiled. {#p10 1.161}

That was when Kunti came forward. {#p11 1.161}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=kunti,i1119_baka light=kunti,i1119_baka}

The Brahmana told her the cause. Near the town lived a Rakshasa named [Baka](i1119_baka), the lord of the country. He protected it from every other enemy, and his price was his food: a cartload of rice, two buffaloes, and the man who brought them. Every household took its turn, once in many years; any family that refused, he ate entire. The king, in his city of Vetrakiya, did nothing. {#p12 1.162}

Today the turn was theirs. The Brahmana had no money to buy a man, and could not give up one of his own. So he would take his whole family to the Rakshasa, and let him eat them all at once. {#p13 1.162}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=kunti,bhima light=kunti,bhima}

> Grieve not at all, O Brahmana, on account of this danger. I see a way by which to rescue thee from that Rakshasa. {#q4 1.163}
> — Kunti

He had one son and one daughter, she said. She had five sons. Let one of them go. {#p14 1.163}

The Brahmana refused. He would never let a guest, a Brahmana as he thought, die for him; better to die himself. {#p15 1.163}

> As regards myself, no son of mine would be less dear to me even if I had a hundred instead of the five I have. {#q5 1.163}
> — Kunti

But this son, she said, the Rakshasa could not kill. He was strong and skilled in mantras, and she had seen him kill many huge Rakshasas. Only the Brahmana must tell no one. {#p16 1.163}

The Brahmana and his wife heard her as if she had given them nectar. Kunti took the Brahmana to Bhima and asked him to do it. *So be it,* said Bhima. {#p17 1.163}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.163,1.159}
**The family without names.** The epic never names the Brahmana, his wife, his daughter or the little boy with the blade of grass. Each of them offers to die for the others; they are some of the most quietly brave people in the whole story.
