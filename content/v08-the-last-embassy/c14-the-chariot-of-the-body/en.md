[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) could not stop listening. Tell me what a sleepless man should do, he said, burning with anxiety; tell me what is in Ajatashatru's mind. *Apprehending future evils. I look back only on my previous guilt.* {#p1 5.34}

> Even if unasked, one should speak truly, whether his words be good or bad, hateful or pleasing, unto him whose defeat one doth not wish. {#q1 5.34}
> — Vidura

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vidura light=vidura}

Before an act, said [Vidura](vidura), weigh the doer, the deed and its purpose. Master yourself first, then your counsellors, then your enemies. {#p2 5.34}

> One's body, O king, is one's car; the soul within is the driver; and the senses are its steeds. {#q2 5.34}
> — Vidura

Well-trained horses carry the wise man pleasantly through the journey of life; unbroken ones drag an unskilled driver to ruin. One's self is one's friend, and one's own foe. Desire and anger break through wisdom *just as a large fish breaks through a net of thin cords*. And the sinless burn with the sinful, *as fuel that is wet burneth with that which is dry*. {#p3 5.34}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=vidura,dhritarashtra light=vidura}

Hardest of all is to control speech. {#p4 5.34}

> A forest pierced by arrows, or cut down by hatchets may again grow, but one's heart wounded and censured by ill-spoken words never recovereth. {#q3 5.34}
> — Vidura

When destruction is near, he said, wrong looks like right, and sticks fast to the heart. That was what had happened to the king's sons in their hatred of the Pandavas. Let Yudhishthira rule the earth: he had suffered all this out of kindness, to protect the king's good name. {#p5 5.34}
