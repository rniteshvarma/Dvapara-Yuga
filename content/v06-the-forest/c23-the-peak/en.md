::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna light=yudhishthira}

Four years had passed in the forests, [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) told his brothers, and Arjuna had promised to come back to the White Mountain in the fifth. So they went up into the Gandhamadana again, carried by no one this time, on foot through its groves of mango and pomegranate and plantain, to the hermitage of the royal sage Arshtishena, a man worn to the bone by austerity. {#p1 3.157}

He welcomed them and told them to stay with him until Arjuna came. But they must not go higher, he said: above this point lived Kubera's Yakshas and Rakshasas, and the peak was forbidden to men. {#p2 3.158}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=draupadi,bhima light=draupadi}

One day the wind blew flowers of five colours down from the summit. [Draupadi](draupadi) spoke to [Bhima](bhima). In the Khandava forest, she said, Arjuna had beaten the Gandharvas and Nagas and Indra himself. Bhima was as strong. {#p3 3.159}

> O Bhima, I have for long cherished this thought in my mind,—that protected by the might of thy arms, I shall see that summit. {#q1 3.159}
> — Draupadi

Bhima took it as a rebuke, *like a high-mettled bull that hath been struck*. He took his bow, sword and mace, climbed the one narrow path to the top, saw Kubera's golden palaces, and blew his conch. {#p4 3.159}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima light=bhima}

Yakshas and Rakshasas poured out at him with maces and spears. He cut them down with arrows and broke them with his mace until they fled south. Then Kubera's friend, the Rakshasa Maniman, came at him alone. *How will ye say unto that lord of wealth, that numbers have been defeated by a single mortal?* {#p5 3.159}

They fought with mace and iron club and dart. At last Bhima leapt into the air and hurled his mace like Indra's thunderbolt, and it crushed Maniman and fell to the ground. {#p6 3.159}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,bhima light=yudhishthira}

Hearing the noise from the caves, Yudhishthira left Draupadi with Arshtishena and climbed up with the others. He found Bhima among the fallen Yakshas, and this time he was angry. {#p7 3.160}

> Either it be through rashness, or through ignorance, thou hast, O Bhima, committed a sinful act. {#q2 3.160}
> — Yudhishthira

Living as a hermit, he said, Bhima had killed without cause, and offended the gods. *If thou seekest my good, never again commit such a deed.* {#p8 3.160}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=bhima,yudhishthira light=bhima}

Then Kubera himself came, on a cloud-coloured chariot drawn by golden horses, with a thousand Yakshas behind him. The Pandavas bowed with joined hands, expecting his anger. Bhima stood, bow in hand, ready to fight him. {#p9 3.160}

But the lord of wealth was pleased. These Yakshas had already been killed by Destiny, he told Yudhishthira; Bhima was only the instrument. And Bhima had done Kubera a kindness. {#p10 3.160}

> O Vrikodara, to-day I have been freed from a terrible curse. {#q3 3.160}
> — Kubera

Long ago, he said, on his way to a gathering of the gods, his friend Maniman, out of sheer arrogance, had fouled the head of the sage Agastya as he stood in penance by the Yamuna. Agastya had cursed them: Maniman and Kubera's army would die at the hands of a mortal, and Kubera would be freed from the sin only when he saw that mortal. {#p11 3.160}

So Kubera gave Yudhishthira counsel on patience, timing and courage, warned Bhima against rashness, and told them to stay on his mountain in peace, guarded by his Yakshas, until Arjuna came. {#p12 3.161}
