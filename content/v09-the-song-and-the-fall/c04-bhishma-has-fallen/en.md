::scene{#s1 frame=sanjaya,dhritarashtra,bhishma light=sanjaya}

Then, says Vaishampayana, [Sanjaya](sanjaya) came quickly from the battlefield and rushed into the court in grief, to [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) sitting deep in thought. {#p1 6.13}

> Bhishma, the son of Santanu and the grandsire of the Bharatas, hath been slain. {#q1 6.13}
> — Sanjaya

[Bhishma](bhishma), on whose strength Duryodhana had played the dice, who had beaten all the kings at Kashi on a single chariot and fought Parashurama to a standstill, lay on a bed of arrows, killed by Shikhandi. For ten days he had guarded the army and killed ten thousand warriors a day. Now he lay on the bare ground *like a (mighty) tree broken by the wind*, because of the king's evil counsel. {#p2 6.13}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=6.13}
From here the book is told looking back. The war has already run ten days; Bhishma has fallen; and Sanjaya, with Vyasa's sight, now tells the blind king everything from the first morning. The chapters that follow are his account.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=dhritarashtra light=dhritarashtra}

Dhritarashtra asked question after question. How had Shikhandi killed his father, who was like Indra? Who stood at Bhishma's wheels, who guarded his back, who left him? How could he fall with Drona and Kripa and Ashwatthama near him? {#p3 6.14}

> Hard as my heart is, surely it must be made of adamant, for it breaketh not on hearing the death of that tiger among men, viz., Bhishma! {#q2 6.14}
> — Dhritarashtra

Neither weapons nor courage nor austerity frees a man from death, he said. The Pandavas wanted sovereignty even at the cost of their grandfather's life, and his own sons had offered him up as a sacrifice. Who had played in that gambling-house where the board was made of men and horses and elephants and the dice were arrows, and the stakes were their lives? Tell me everything. {#p4 6.14}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=sanjaya,duryodhana,duhshasana,shikhandi light=sanjaya}

Sanjaya would not let him blame Duryodhana alone: the man who comes to evil through his own conduct should not put it on others. Then he bowed to Vyasa, who had given him sight beyond the eyes and hearing from afar, knowledge of others' hearts and of past and future, and began. {#p5 6.15}

When the armies were drawn up, [Duryodhana](duryodhana) told [Duhshasana](duhshasana) that nothing mattered more than protecting Bhishma. Bhishma had said he would not kill [Shikhandi](shikhandi), who had been a woman; so every warrior must be set to kill Shikhandi, and Bhishma guarded on every side. {#p6 6.15}

> Let us not, therefore, cause Bhishma to be slain by Sikhandin like the lion slain by the jackal. {#q3 6.15}
> — Duryodhana
