Why, [Duryodhana](duryodhana) asked, would Bhishma not kill Shikhandi, even if Shikhandi came at him with weapons raised? [Bhishma](bhishma) told him a story, before all the kings. {#p1 5.174}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,amba,ambika,ambalika,satyavati light=bhishma}

After Shantanu died, Bhishma had set his brother Chitrangada on the throne, and after Chitrangada, young Vichitravirya. Looking for wives for him, he heard that the three daughters of the king of Kashi, [Amba](amba), [Ambika](ambika) and [Ambalika](ambalika), were to choose husbands, and that every king on earth had been invited. He went alone on a single chariot, took the three girls up into it, and told the kings: *Bhishma, the son of Shantanu, is carrying these maidens away by force. Rescue them if you can.* He beat them all, cut down their banners, and brought the girls home to [Satyavati](satyavati) for his brother. {#p2 5.174}

As the wedding came near, Amba, the eldest, spoke to him in great shyness. {#p3 5.175}

> The ruler of the Salwas was before this mentally chosen by me as my lord. {#q1 5.175}
> — Amba

He had asked for her secretly, she said, without her father knowing. How could Bhishma, born in Kuru's race, keep in his house a girl who loved another? So Bhishma put the matter before his mother, the counsellors and the priests, and let her go, with old Brahmanas and her nurse to escort her. {#p4 5.175 5.176}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.174}
Volume I, chapter 7 tells this abduction from the first book, where Amba's going is a single sentence. Here, in the fifth book, Bhishma tells the rest himself.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=amba light=amba}

She came to Shalva, the king of Saubha, and said, *I have come, expecting you*. He laughed. {#p5 5.176}

> I no longer desire thee that was forcibly ravished by Bhishma. {#q2 5.176}
> — Shalva

She had gone with Bhishma cheerfully, he said; how could a king who laid down laws for others take in a woman meant for another man? She begged him. She had not gone cheerfully; she had wept the whole way. She swore by touching her own head that she had never thought of any husband but him. He cast her off *like a snake casting off his slough*. {#p6 5.176}

> Cast off, O king, by thee, whithersoever I may go, the righteous will be my protectors, for truth is indestructible! {#q3 5.176}
> — Amba

*Go, go*, Shalva kept saying; *I am afraid of Bhishma; you are Bhishma's capture*. {#p7 5.176}

> Thus addressed by Salwa destitute of foresight, that maiden issued out of his city sorrowfully and wailing like a she-osprey. {#q4 5.176}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=amba light=amba}

Outside the city she thought: no woman on earth was as wretched as she. She could not go back to Hastinapura, which had let her go. Whom should she blame? Herself, for not leaping from Bhishma's chariot in the fight? Bhishma? Her father? {#p8 5.177}

> Cursed be Bhishma! Cursed be my own wretched father of foolish understanding, who had arranged prowess to be my dower. {#q5 5.177}
> — Amba

But the cause of it all was Bhishma, she decided, and her vengeance should fall on him, by austerity or by battle. Yet what king would dare face Bhishma? She went to a hermitage of ascetics and told them everything. An old Brahmana there, Shaikhavatya, asked what ascetics could do for her. Let me live in the forest, she said, and practise the hardest austerities. {#p9 5.177}
