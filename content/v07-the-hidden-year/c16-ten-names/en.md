But where were they, asked [Uttara](uttar): Arjuna, Yudhishthira, Bhima and the twins, who had lost their kingdom at dice and were heard of no more? And where was Draupadi, the jewel among women, who had followed them into the forest? {#p1 4.44}

> I am Arjuna, called also Partha. Thy father's courtier is Yudhishthira and thy father's cook Vallava is Bhimasena, the groom of horses is Nakula, and Sahadeva is in the cow-pen. And know thou that the Sairindhri is Draupadi, for whose sake the Kichakas have been slain. {#q1 4.44}
> — Arjuna

Uttara would believe it if he could give Partha's ten names, and say why he bore each one. {#p2 4.44}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

> They are Arjuna, Falguna, Jishnu, Kiritin, Swetavahana, Vibhatsu, Vijaya, Krishna, Savyasachin and Dhananjaya. {#q2 4.44}
> — Arjuna

*Dhananjaya*, because he had conquered every country and lived among their wealth. *Vijaya*, because he never came back from battle unvictorious. *Swetavahana*, for the white horses in golden armour yoked to his chariot. *Falguna*, because he was born on the slopes of the Himavat under the star Uttara Phalguni. *Kiritin*, for the diadem Indra set on his head when he fought the Danavas. *Vibhatsu*, because he had never done a hateful deed in battle. *Savyasachin*, because either hand could draw the Gandiva. *Arjuna*, for the rare, pure colour of his skin and his stainless deeds. *Jishnu*, because he could not be held down, and was the son of Indra. {#p3 4.44}

> And Krishna, my tenth appellation, was given to me by my father out of affection towards his black-skinned boy of great purity. {#q3 4.44}
> — Arjuna

Then Uttara came close and bowed to him. *My name is Bhuminjaya, and I am also called Uttara.* He asked pardon for what he had said in ignorance; his fear was gone. {#p4 4.44}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,uttar light=arjuna}

Which part of the army should he drive to? asked Uttara. Arjuna was pleased with him. He told him to tie the quivers to the chariot and take a gold-adorned sword, and Uttara came down from the tree with the weapons. The chariot would be his citadel, Arjuna said: its passages the streets, his arms the ramparts, his bowstring the engines of war, the clatter of its wheels the kettledrums of the city. {#p5 4.45}

One thing still puzzled the prince: how could a man with such marks of fortune have lost his manhood? {#p6 4.45}

> I tell thee truly that I am only observing this vow for a whole year agreeable to the behest of my elder brother. {#q4 4.45}
> — Arjuna

Then Uttara was ready to fight the gods themselves. He had been trained to drive, he said, and would be to Arjuna what Daruka was to Krishna, and Matali to Indra. {#p7 4.45}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

Arjuna took the conch bracelets off his arms and put on gloves worked with gold. He tied back his black curling hair with a white cloth. Then he sat facing east, purified himself, and called his weapons to mind, and they all came. {#p8 4.45}

> We are here, O illustrious one. We are thy servants, O son of Indra. {#q5 4.45}
> — the weapons

*Dwell ye all in my memory*, he told them. Then he strung the Gandiva and twanged it, and the sound was like two great bulls colliding: the earth shook, a wind rose, meteors fell, birds faltered in the sky, and the Kurus knew who was drawing that bow. {#p9 4.45}

Uttara still feared: one man against so many. Arjuna laughed. Who had stood beside him against the Gandharvas at the cattle stations, against gods and Danavas at Khandava, against the Nivatakavachas, against the kings at Draupadi's svayamvara? {#p10 4.45}
