Was there anything more? asked [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra). There was, said [Vidura](vidura), but not from him. The ancient sage Sanatsujata, forever young and forever celibate, who taught that there is no death, could answer the king's doubts. Why would Vidura not say it himself? {#p1 5.41}

> I am born in the Sudra order and, therefore, do not venture to say more than what I have already said. {#q1 5.41}
> — Vidura

So Vidura thought of the sage, and Sanatsujata appeared. Vidura received him with due rites and asked him to free the king from his sorrows. {#p2 5.41}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.41}
Sanatsujata's teaching (5.41–46) is a philosophical dialogue on death, knowledge and the self, read by later tradition alongside the Bhagavad Gita. It is told here only in outline; a fuller telling belongs in a companion volume.
::

***

::scene{#s2 frame=dhritarashtra light=dhritarashtra}

Dhritarashtra asked him in private: some say there is no death, and yet the gods and Asuras practise austerities to escape it. Which is true? {#p3 5.42}

> Some say, death is avertable by particular acts; others' opinion there is no death; thou hast asked me which of these is true. {#q2 5.42}
> — Sanatsujata

Both are true, said the sage. {#p4 5.42}

> I say that ignorance is Death, and so the absence of ignorance (Knowledge) is immortality. {#q3 5.42}
> — Sanatsujata

Death does not devour creatures like a tiger, he said. The desire for enjoyment kills first, and lust and anger follow. Those who conquer them pass beyond death. {#p5 5.42}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura light=dhritarashtra}

Through the rest of the night the king questioned him: on silence and what it is, on the Vedas and whether knowing them saves a man from sin, on the true Brahmana, on the practice of celibacy, on the Brahman that the Vedas cannot reach. {#p6 5.43 5.44 5.45}

> Subtler than the subtle, of excellent eyes capable of looking into both the past and the future, Brahman is awake in every creature. {#q4 5.46}
> — Sanatsujata

So, with Vidura and Sanatsujata, the king passed that night. And in the morning the princes gathered in the hall to hear what Sanjaya had brought. {#p7 5.47}
