::scene{#s1 frame=drona,arjuna light=drona,arjuna}

Drona kept his word to Arjuna. He taught him to fight on horseback, from the back of an elephant, from a chariot and on foot; with the mace, the sword, the lance, the spear and the dart; and how to fight many men at once. Word of the teacher spread, and kings and princes came to him by the thousand. {#p1 1.134}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=ekalavya,hiranyadhanu,drona light=ekalavya}

Among them came a prince named [Ekalavya](ekalavya), the son of [Hiranyadhanu](hiranyadhanu), king of the Nishadas, the forest people. [Drona](drona) would not take him as a pupil in archery. The epic gives the reason plainly: he was a Nishada, and he might in time surpass all of Drona's high-born pupils. {#p2 1.134}

Ekalavya bowed his head and touched Drona's feet, and went away into the forest. There he made an image of Drona out of clay, and worshipped it as if it were his teacher, and practised before it every day with the strictest discipline. Because of his reverence and his single purpose, the three arts of the archer, fitting the arrow, aiming and loosing, became easy for him. {#p3 1.134}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.134}
**Who the Nishadas were.** Ganguli glosses the word as *the lowest of the mixed orders*, which was the view of the text and its age. In the epic they are the people of the forests and hills, hunters with their own kings. Ekalavya is a king's son.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,ekalavya light=ekalavya}

One day the Kuru and Pandava princes, with Drona's leave, rode out in their chariots to hunt. A servant followed with the gear and a dog. The dog ran on alone through the woods and came upon the Nishada prince, dark, dusty, dressed in black, with his hair matted, and it began to bark. {#p4 1.134}

Ekalavya, wanting to show how fast his hand was, shot seven arrows into the dog's mouth before it could close it. The dog ran back to the Pandavas with the seven arrows in its mouth. {#p5 1.134}

They were amazed, and ashamed of their own skill, and went looking for the archer. They found him shooting, without pause. *Who art thou, and whose son?* they asked. {#p6 1.134}

> Ye heroes, I am the son of Hiranyadhanus, king of the Nishadas. Know me also for a pupil of Drona, labouring for the mastery of the art of arms. {#q1 1.134}
> — Ekalavya

***

::scene{#s4 frame=arjuna,drona light=arjuna}

They went home and told Drona everything. [Arjuna](arjuna) could not stop thinking about it. He went to Drona alone, trusting in his teacher's love. {#p7 1.134}

> Thou hadst lovingly told me, clasping me, to thy bosom, that no pupil of thine should be equal to me. Why then is there a pupil of thine, the mighty son of the Nishada king, superior to me? {#q2 1.134}
> — Arjuna

***

::scene{#s5 frame=drona,arjuna,ekalavya light=drona,ekalavya}

Drona thought for a moment and decided what to do. He took Arjuna and went into the forest. {#p8 1.134}

They found Ekalavya as before, covered in dust, in rags, bow in hand, shooting without pause. When he saw Drona coming he went forward to meet him, touched his feet and lay down on the ground before him. He named himself Drona's pupil and stood with folded hands, waiting. {#p9 1.134}

> If, O hero, thou art really my pupil, give me then my fees. {#q3 1.134}
> — Drona

Ekalavya was overjoyed. *What shall I give? Command me,* he said; there was nothing he would not give his teacher. {#p10 1.134}

> O Ekalavya, if thou art really intent on making me a gift, I should like then to have the thumb of thy right hand. {#q4 1.134}
> — Drona

***

::scene{#s6 frame=ekalavya,drona,arjuna light=ekalavya}

The epic calls them *these cruel words*. Ekalavya, true to his word, with a cheerful face and an untroubled heart, cut off his thumb without a moment's pause and gave it to Drona. {#p11 1.134}

When he took up his bow again and shot with the fingers he had left, his old lightness of hand was gone. {#p12 1.134}

And Arjuna was happy. The fever of his jealousy had left him. {#p13 1.134}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.134}
**The epic does not look away.** Vyasa's text makes no excuse here. It gives Drona's reason in a single clause, calls his request cruel, gives Ekalavya the grace of the scene, and records Arjuna's relief without praising it. Readers have argued about this page ever since; the text itself only tells it.
::

***

::scene{#s7 frame=duryodhana,bhima,ashwatthama,nakula,sahadeva,yudhishthira,arjuna light=arjuna}

So Drona's pupils grew. [Duryodhana](duryodhana) and [Bhima](bhima) became masters of the mace, and were always jealous of each other. [Ashwatthama](ashwatthama) excelled everyone in the secrets of the science of arms. The twins, [Nakula](nakula) and [Sahadeva](sahadeva), were the best with the sword. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) was the best in a chariot. {#p14 1.134}

But Arjuna surpassed them all in everything: in understanding, in resource, in strength and in perseverance. His fame spread to the edge of the sea. Of all the princes he alone became an *atiratha*, a chariot-warrior able, Ganguli explains, to fight sixty thousand at once. {#p15 1.134}

And the sons of Dhritarashtra, seeing Bhima's strength and Arjuna's skill, burned with jealousy. {#p16 1.134}
