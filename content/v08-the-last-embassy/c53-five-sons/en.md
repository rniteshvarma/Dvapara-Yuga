When Krishna had gone, [Vidura](vidura) came to [Kunti](kunti) in grief. He had cried himself hoarse and Duryodhana would not listen; Yudhishthira, strong as he was, still waited at Upaplavya like a weak man, for love of his kin; the old king was drunk on pride in his sons. When Krishna came back without peace, there would be war. He could not sleep by day or night. {#p1 5.144}

Kunti sighed, and thought. *Fie on wealth*, for which kinsmen would kill kinsmen. Yet if her sons did not fight, they would have poverty and shame. Drona would never fight his pupils willingly; Bhishma surely loved the Pandavas. Only Karna, deluded and very powerful, hated them, and that burned her. She would go to him and tell him the truth. {#p2 5.144}

She remembered the boon [Durvasa](durvasa) had given her in her father's house, and how she had called down [Surya](surya) while still a girl, *from excess of curiosity and from folly*. Why should the son she carried then not listen to her now, when her words would do his brothers good? {#p3 5.144}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=karna,kunti light=karna}

She went to the bank of the Ganga. [Karna](karna) stood facing east with his arms raised, chanting the Vedic hymns, and she waited behind him in the heat until she wilted *like a faded garland of lotuses*, and at last stood in the shade of his upper cloth. He prayed until the sun burned his back; then he turned, saw her, and bowed with joined hands. {#p4 5.144}

> I am Karna, son of Radha and Adhiratha. For what, O lady, hast thou come here? Tell me what I am to do for thee? {#q1 5.145}
> — Karna

> Thou art Kunti's son, and not Radha's. Nor is Adhiratha thy father. Thou, O Karna, art not born in the Suta order. {#q2 5.145}
> — Kunti

She had borne him as a girl in Kuntibhoja's palace, begotten by Surya, born with earrings and a coat of mail. It was not right that he should serve Dhritarashtra's son without knowing his brothers. Let the Kurus see Karna and Arjuna united, named together like Balarama and Krishna. Let not the name *Suta's son* cling to him; he was a Partha. {#p5 5.145}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.145}
Karna no longer had the earrings and the mail by this time. He had cut them from his body and given them to Indra, disguised as a Brahmana, near the end of the forest years; Volume VI, chapter 40 tells it.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=surya,karna,kunti light=surya}

Then a voice came out of the disc of the sun, from very far away, with a father's love. What Pritha said was true; let Karna do as his mother asked. {#p6 5.146}

Karna did not waver. {#p7 5.146}

> O mother, I was abandoned by thee as soon as I was born. This great injury, involving risk to life itself, that thou didst me, hath been destructive of my achievements and fame. {#q3 5.146}
> — Karna

If he was a Kshatriya, she had robbed him of every rite a Kshatriya should have. What enemy could have done worse? She had never sought his good before; she sought her own now. And who would not say he went over to the Pandavas out of fear of Arjuna and Krishna? Duryodhana's people treated him as the Vasus treat Indra; he was the boat in which they meant to cross the ocean of battle. How could he leave them now? {#p8 5.146}

Yet her coming would not be wasted. He would not kill Yudhishthira, or Bhima, or the twins, though he could. He would fight only Arjuna. {#p9 5.146}

> O famous lady, the number of thy sons will never be less than five. Five it will always be,—either with me, or with Arjuna, and myself slain. {#q4 5.146}
> — Karna

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kunti,karna light=kunti}

Kunti, trembling with grief, embraced the son who would not be moved. Destiny was all, she said; but let him remember, when the arrows flew, the promise he had given for four of his brothers. {#p10 5.146}

> And having told all this, Pritha also addressed Karna, saying, 'Blessed be thou, and let health be thine.' And Karna replied unto her, saying, 'Be it so!' And they then left the spot, wending in different directions. {#q5 5.146}
