The ascetics argued among themselves. Send her back to her father; ask Shalva again; no, he had refused her. Go home, they told her: a woman in distress has her father for protector, and the forest is hard, and kings might court a girl alone in the woods. She would not go. Her family would scorn her now; she wanted to do penance, so that no such grief would follow her into another life. {#p1 5.178}

Then the royal sage Hotravahana came to the hermitage, and heard her story, and trembled, and took her on his lap: he was her mother's father. Don't go home, he said. Go to [Parashurama](parashurama), Jamadagni's son, on Mahendra mountain; he is my friend, and he will kill Bhishma if Bhishma does not obey him. Parashurama's companion Akritavrana arrived just then, and said Parashurama would be there the next morning. {#p2 5.178}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=amba,parashurama light=parashurama}

Did she want Shalva made to marry her, Akritavrana asked, or Bhishma beaten? If Bhishma had never carried her to Hastinapura, Shalva would have taken her, he said; let her vengeance fall on Bhishma. That had been her own wish, she answered. {#p3 5.179}

In the morning Parashurama came, in matted locks and deerskin, with bow, sword and battle-axe. Amba touched his feet and wept. He looked at her youth and her delicacy and sat a long time in silence, full of pity. Then he said he would send word to Bhishma; if Bhishma did not obey, he would burn him in battle. {#p4 5.179}

> Slay that Bhishma, O thou of mighty arms, for whose sake, O tiger of Bhrigu's race, overwhelmed with such distress, I suffer such poignant misery! {#q1 5.179}
> — Amba

***

::scene{#s3 frame=parashurama,amba light=parashurama}

> I do not, on any account, take up arms now except for the sake of those that are conversant with the Vedas. {#q2 5.180}
> — Parashurama

At his word, he said, Bhishma would put her feet on his head. But she wanted Bhishma dead. And Akritavrana reminded him of his old vow: to kill whoever, having beaten all the Kshatriyas of the earth, grew proud; and never to abandon anyone who came to him in fear. Bhishma had done exactly that. Parashurama remembered. He would try conciliation first, he said; if Bhishma refused, he would kill him. With Amba and the ascetics he went to Kurukshetra and camped on the Sarasvati. {#p5 5.180}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhishma,parashurama,ganga light=bhishma}

Bhishma came to him gladly, with a cow at the head of his train, and worshipped him. Parashurama told him: you carried off this girl and let her go, and no one will marry her now; take her yourself, at my command. Bhishma said he could not give her to his brother, who had heard her say she was Shalva's. {#p6 5.181}

> As regards myself, even this is my firm vow that I cannot abandon Kshatriya practices from fear or pity, or avarice of wealth, or lust! {#q3 5.181}
> — Bhishma

Parashurama said he would kill him that very day with his counsellors. Bhishma reminded him that he had been his pupil as a child; Parashurama said all the more reason to obey. Then Bhishma said a teacher who does not know right from wrong may be given up. He would not kill a Brahmana, but a Brahmana who takes up arms like a Kshatriya may be fought. {#p7 5.181}

> As regards thyself, thou hast consumed only heaps of straw! {#q4 5.181}
> — Bhishma

When Parashurama had killed all the Kshatriyas, there had been no Bhishma yet. Parashurama laughed: let Ganga see her son dead on that plain, food for the vultures. Bhishma dressed all in white, with a white bow and white umbrella, drove out to Kurukshetra and blew his conch. Then [Ganga](ganga) herself appeared and begged him not to fight his teacher, a Brahmana; she went to Parashurama and begged him too. *Make Bhishma desist*, said Parashurama; *he does not do my will*. {#p8 5.181}
