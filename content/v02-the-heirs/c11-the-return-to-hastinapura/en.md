::scene{#s1 frame=kunti,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva light=kunti}

The sages of the mountain consulted among themselves. Pandu had given up his kingdom and come to live among them, they said, and he had gone to heaven leaving his wife and young sons in their care. Their duty now was to take them home. {#p1 1.126}

So they set out that very hour for Hastinapura, with Pandu's sons in front, and [Kunti](kunti), and the two bodies. Kunti had never known hardship in her life, but the long road seemed short to her. Before long they came to the land of the Kurus and stood at the main gate of the city, and the sages sent word to the king. {#p2 1.126}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,dhritarashtra,vidura,satyavati,gandhari,somadatta,duryodhana light=bhishma,dhritarashtra}

The news that thousands of sages had arrived spread through Hastinapura, and soon after sunrise the whole city came out to see them, Kshatriyas and Brahmins, Vaishyas and Shudras, with their wives and children, all quiet and full of reverence. [Bhishma](bhishma) came, and [Somadatta](somadatta) of the Bahlika line, and [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), and [Vidura](vidura); the venerable [Satyavati](satyavati), Pandu's mother the princess of Kosala, and [Gandhari](gandhari) with the women of the palace; and the hundred sons of Dhritarashtra, decked in their ornaments. {#p3 1.126}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.126,1.119}
**"The princess of Kosala" again.** Ganguli uses this name here, and when Pandu speaks of *the princess of Kosala (my mother)*, for [Ambalika](ambalika), Pandu's mother. In Volume I it was used for her sister Ambika. Both sisters had it from their mother's side.
::

Bhishma honoured the sages with water for their feet and the customary offerings. Then the eldest of them, with matted hair and a deerskin about his loins, rose to speak. {#p4 1.126}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,dharma,vayu,indra,ashvins light=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva}

He told them how Pandu had gone to the mountain of a hundred peaks to live as an ascetic, and how, for some purpose of the gods, sons had been born to him there. This was Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma; this Bhima, the son of the Wind, the mightiest of men; this Dhananjaya, Arjuna, the son of Indra, who would humble every archer in the world; and these the twins, sons of the Ashvins and of Madri. Living rightly in the forest, Pandu had revived his grandfather's line. {#p5 1.126}

> Steadily adhering to the path of the virtuous and the wise, and leaving behind him these children, Pandu departed hence seventeen days ago. {#q1 1.126}
> — the eldest of the sages

He told them that Madri had gone onto the pyre with him. Here were what remained of their bodies; here were their children and their mother. Let them be received with honour, and let the rites be done. {#p6 1.126}

Then, before the eyes of the whole city, the sages and the Guhyakas who were with them vanished like mist in the sky, and the people went home full of wonder. {#p7 1.126}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura,pandu,madri light=vidura,dhritarashtra}

Dhritarashtra told Vidura to perform the funeral rites of Pandu and Madri as befitted a king: to give away cattle, cloth, gems and wealth to everyone who asked, and to let Kunti perform Madri's rites as she wished. *Lament not for the sinless Pandu,* he said. *He was a worthy king and hath left behind him five heroic sons equal unto the celestials themselves.* {#p8 1.127}

With Bhishma's counsel Vidura chose a sacred place on the banks of the Ganga. The priests carried the sacred fire before the procession; the bodies, wrapped and adorned with flowers, were carried under a white umbrella with yak-tail fans and music, while people threw gems to the crowds. Thousands followed, weeping: *O prince, where dost thou go, leaving us behind?* Bhishma, Vidura and the Pandavas wept aloud. {#p9 1.127}

By the river they washed Pandu's body, anointed it with sandal paste and dressed it in white, so that he seemed only asleep. Then they set the bodies of the king and the queen on the pyre with lotuses and sandal and fragrant things, and lit it. {#p10 1.127}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=ambalika,kunti,bhishma,vidura,dhritarashtra light=ambalika,kunti}

When the flames rose, Pandu's mother cried out, *O my son, my son!*, and fell senseless to the ground. The people wailed for their king; even the birds and beasts, the text says, were moved by Kunti's grief. Bhishma and Vidura were inconsolable. {#p11 1.127}

Then they all offered water to the dead, and the people tried to comfort Pandu's sons. The Pandavas slept on the bare ground in mourning, and when the Brahmins and the citizens saw it, they gave up their own beds too. For twelve days the whole city, young and old, mourned with the weeping boys. {#p12 1.127}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,duryodhana,bhishma light=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,duryodhana}

So the five sons of Pandu came home to Hastinapura, to the palace where a hundred cousins of their own age were already growing up. The heirs of the Kurus were all under one roof now, and Bhishma, who had saved the line once, watched over all of them. What happened between those boys is the story of the next volume. {#p13 1.127}
