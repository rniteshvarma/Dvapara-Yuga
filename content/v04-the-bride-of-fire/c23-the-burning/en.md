::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,arjuna,i239_agni light=krishna,arjuna}

[Krishna](krishna) and [Arjuna](arjuna) took their chariots to opposite sides of the forest, and wherever any creature tried to escape, they were there. The two chariots, the epic says, seemed one, and the two warriors one man. {#p1 1.228}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=i239_agni light=i239_agni}

The epic does not soften what happened next. Creatures ran screaming in every direction, some with their limbs burnt, some scorched, some simply running in terror. {#p2 1.228}

> And some clasping their children and some their parents and brothers, died calmly without, from excess of affection, being able to abandon these that were dear to them. {#q1 1.228}
> — Vaisampayana

The pools boiled, and the fish and tortoises died in them. Birds that flew up out of the fire were cut down by Arjuna's arrows and fell back into it. The noise was like the uproar at the churning of the ocean, and the flames rose so high that the gods themselves were afraid, and went to [Indra](indra) asking whether the end of the world had come. {#p3 1.228}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.228,1.230}
**Told plainly.** The burning of Khandava is one of the hardest passages in the epic to read. Vyasa's text does not hide the cost: the creatures dying in the arms of their kin, the birds shot down, the boiling pools. It sets the scene beside the language of glory and leaves both standing. We have kept it as it is told.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=indra,arjuna light=indra}

Indra covered the sky with clouds and poured rain on the forest, but the heat dried the rain in the air before it could fall. He gathered more clouds, heavier ones, and the forest filled with smoke and flashes of lightning. {#p4 1.228}

Arjuna answered with his weapons. He covered the whole forest with arrows, like a fog over the moon, so that no rain could get through and no creature could get out. {#p5 1.229}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=takshaka,indra,arjuna,krishna,i239_agni light=takshaka}

[Takshaka](takshaka) himself was not there; he had gone to Kurukshetra. But his son Ashvasena was, and could find no way out through Arjuna's arrows. So his mother tried to save him by swallowing him, head first, and flew up while she was still swallowing his tail. {#p6 1.229}

Arjuna cut off her head with a single arrow. But Indra, to save his friend's son, sent a violent wind that knocked Arjuna senseless for a moment, and in that moment Ashvasena got away. {#p7 1.229}

Arjuna was furious at the trick. He and Agni and Krishna cursed the snake: *Never shalt thou be famous!* {#p8 1.229}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=1.229}
**The snake who got away.** Ashvasena does not forget. Years later, on the field of Kurukshetra, he will try to take his revenge on Arjuna. Takshaka, absent from his forest that day, is the serpent who will one day kill Arjuna's grandson Parikshit.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=indra,arjuna,krishna,skanda light=indra,arjuna,krishna}

Now Arjuna turned his arrows on Indra himself, and Indra on him. The winds roared, the oceans heaved, and the clouds poured thunder and lightning; Arjuna dispelled them with the weapon of the wind, and the sky cleared. Agni, unopposed, blazed up with all his flames, roaring through the universe. {#p9 1.229}

Then the Garuda birds swooped down at them, and Nagas spitting poison, and Asuras, Gandharvas, Yakshas and Rakshasas with iron-ball machines and catapults. Arjuna struck off their heads; Krishna cut them down with his discus. {#p10 1.229}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=indra,arjuna,krishna,skanda light=indra}

At last Indra himself came on his white elephant and hurled his thunderbolt. *These two are slain,* he told the gods. And the gods took up their weapons with him: Yama his mace, Kubera his club, Varuna his noose, Skanda his long lance, the Ashvins, Surya, Mitra, Tvashtri with a whole mountain, the Rudras and the Vasus and the Maruts. {#p11 1.229}

Krishna and Arjuna waited, bows in hand, and drove them back again and again, until the gods fled to Indra for protection. Indra, watching his son fight, was secretly pleased, the epic says, and attacked again to test him: with a rain of stones, and then a heavier one, and then a whole peak torn from Mount Mandara. Arjuna split the mountain into a thousand pieces with his arrows, and its fragments fell like stars into the forest. {#p12 1.229}
