Take me to the golden palmyra banner, said [Arjuna](arjuna): there the grandsire waits. But [Uttara](uttar), wounded, could drive no more. {#p1 4.61}

> O hero, I am no longer able to guide thy excellent steeds. My spirits droop and my mind is exceedingly bewildered. {#q1 4.61}
> — Uttara

The stench of fat and blood had taken his senses; the conchs and the Gandiva had taken his hearing and memory; he could not see when Arjuna took an arrow, set it or loosed it. Arjuna steadied him. He too had done wonders that day, he said; let him hold the reins once more. Then Arjuna told him what he would see: arrows playing like lightning among clouds, the Kurus arguing which hand he shot with, a river of blood with chariots for its eddies. From Rudra he had the Raudra weapon, from Varuna the Varuna, from Agni the Agneya, from the Wind the Vayavya, from Indra the thunderbolt. *Therefore, O Virata's son, let thy fears be dispelled.* {#p2 4.61}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,duhshasana,vikarna,k_duhsaha,k_vivimshati light=arjuna}

As they drove into Bhishma's division, Arjuna shot down the grandsire's standard at the root. Four sons of Dhritarashtra closed round him: [Duhshasana](duhshasana) struck Uttara with a crescent arrow and Arjuna in the chest, and Arjuna cut his bow and sent him off with five arrows in his chest; [Vikarna](vikarna), hit in the forehead, fell from his chariot; [Duhsaha](k_duhsaha) and [Vivimshati](k_vivimshati) lost their horses and were carried away. {#p3 4.61}

Then all the Kuru warriors came at once, and Arjuna covered them *as the mist covereth the mountains*. Chariot-fighters leapt from their cars and horsemen from their horses; footsoldiers ran. He seemed to dance on the field with his bow. {#p4 4.62}

> Indeed, so swift-handed was Arjuna that the spectators could not perceive any interval between his taking up an arrow, and fixing it on the bow-string, and letting it off by a stretch of the Gandiva. {#q2 4.62}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhishma,arjuna light=bhishma}

Then [Bhishma](bhishma) came, under his white umbrella *like unto a hill at sunrise*, blowing his conch to cheer Dhritarashtra's sons. Arjuna received him gladly. Bhishma's eight arrows struck the ape and the creatures on Arjuna's banner; Arjuna cut down Bhishma's umbrella with a javelin, struck his flagstaff, his horses and the two men guarding his flanks. They fought with the weapons of Prajapati and Indra, Agni and Rudra, Kubera, Varuna, Yama and Vayu, and the Kurus watched. {#p5 4.63}

> Bravo Partha of long arms! Bravo Bhishma! Indeed, this application of celestial weapons that is being witnessed in the combat between Bhishma and Partha is rare among human beings. {#q3 4.63}
> — all beings

In the sky the Gandharva Chitrasena said to Indra that no man could shoot such a weapon, and that no one could look at either of them, and Indra showered flowers on both. Then Bhishma struck at Arjuna's left side, and Arjuna, laughing, cut his bow and put ten arrows in his chest. The grandsire stood a long time leaning on the pole of his chariot, senseless, and his driver, remembering the rule for a warrior who has fainted, took him away. {#p6 4.63}
