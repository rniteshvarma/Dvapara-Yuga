::scene{#s1 frame=dhritarashtra,bhima light=bhima}

All of them together, said [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), were equal only to Bhima. {#p1 5.51}

> I pass all my nights in sleeplessness, breathing deep and hot sighs afraid of Vrikodara, O child, like an animal of any other species afraid of the lion. {#q1 5.51}
> — Dhritarashtra

[Bhima](bhima) never smiled, even in jest; he glanced sideways and spoke like thunder. Even as boys, his sons had always been ground down by him. He would break, but not bend. Already the king could see his eight-sided steel mace, adorned with gold, *uplifted like a Brahmana's curse*. Bhima had killed Jarasandha with his bare hands. And Bhishma, Drona and Kripa, who knew all this, would still stand in the van and die, because the king's sons were as much their grandsons and pupils as the Pandavas were. {#p2 5.51}

> It seems, O Sanjaya, that wisdom is incapable of dispelling woe; on the other hand, it is overwhelming woe that dispelleth wisdom. {#q2 5.51}
> — Dhritarashtra

***

::scene{#s2 frame=dhritarashtra,arjuna,krishna light=arjuna}

And [Arjuna](arjuna). Thirty-three years since Khandava, and no one had ever heard of his defeat. *We hear that the two Krishnas on the same car and the stringed Gandiva,—these three forces,—have been united together.* {#p3 5.52}

> O Sanjaya, the blazing thunderbolt falling on the head leaveth something undestroyed, but the arrows, O child, shot by Kiritin leave nothing undestroyed. {#q3 5.52}
> — Dhritarashtra

He feared Yudhishthira's anger most of all, a fire no fool should leap into like a moth. *Alas, I have behaved deceitfully towards him.* It was not right to fight them, he told the Kurus. {#p4 5.53}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=sanjaya,dhritarashtra light=sanjaya}

[Sanjaya](sanjaya) had no sympathy left. The king knew all this, and still followed his sons. {#p5 5.54}

> Hearing of the defeat of the Pandavas at dice, thou hadst, O king, laughed like a child, saying, 'This is won, this is acquired!' {#q4 5.54}
> — Sanjaya

The Kuru country was his father's kingdom; the whole earth beyond it the Pandavas had won for him. It was Partha who saved his sons from the Gandharvas. *Falguna is the foremost of all shooters; Gandiva is the foremost of all bows; Kesava is the foremost of all beings.* Let the king check his son; this lamenting, as if he were helpless, was useless. {#p6 5.54}
