::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,kunti,bhima light=yudhishthira,kunti}

The others came home with the day's alms, and [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) knew from Bhima's face alone that something was afoot. He asked his mother privately what it was. [Kunti](kunti) told him: at her command, Bhima would do a great deed for the Brahmana and free the town. {#p1 1.164}

> What rash act hast thou done, O mother! It is difficult of being performed and almost amounteth to suicide! {#q1 1.164}
> — Yudhishthira

How could she give up her own son for someone else's? Bhima, on whose arms they all slept safe at night, who had carried them out of the house of lac, whose strength kept Duryodhana and Shakuni awake: had her sorrows clouded her mind? {#p2 1.164}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti,yudhishthira,bhima light=kunti}

Kunti answered him calmly. She had not lost her reason. They had lived safe and honoured in this man's house; this was how to repay him. And she knew what Bhima was. She had seen him carry them all out of Varanavata, and kill Hidimba. When he was a baby he had fallen from her lap onto a mountain, and the rock had broken. {#p3 1.164}

> I have not acted in this from foolishness or ignorance or from motive of gain. I have deliberately resolved to do this virtuous deed. {#q2 1.164}
> — Kunti

A Kshatriya who helps a Brahmana, she said, wins the worlds of bliss; one who protects even a Shudra who comes to him for help is reborn among kings. Vyasa had told her so. {#p4 1.164}

Yudhishthira was satisfied. Bhima would come back, he said, since his mother had acted from compassion. But the Brahmana must keep it from the townspeople. {#p5 1.165}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,i1119_baka light=bhima}

In the morning Bhima took the Rakshasa's food and set out. When he came near the forest where [Baka](i1119_baka) lived, he sat down, began to eat the food himself, and called the Rakshasa loudly by name. {#p6 1.165}

Baka came out in a fury, huge, red-eyed, red-bearded and red-haired, his mouth stretching from ear to ear, the ground sinking under his feet. {#p7 1.165}

> Who is this fool, who desiring to go to the abode of Yama, eateth in my very sight the food intended for me? {#q3 1.165}
> — Baka

Bhima smiled and went on eating, with his face turned away. {#p8 1.165}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhima,i1119_baka light=bhima,i1119_baka}

Baka yelled and ran at him with both arms raised. Bhima glanced at him once and kept eating. The Rakshasa struck him on the back with both fists, hard; Bhima did not even look up. Baka tore up a tree to hit him again. And Bhima, at his leisure, finished every bit of the food, washed his hands, and stood up, cheerful, ready to fight. {#p9 1.165}

He caught the tree with his left hand. They fought with trees until there were no trees left. Then they grappled, and the earth shook. When the Rakshasa was tired, Bhima threw him down, put a knee in the middle of his back, took his neck in his right hand and his loincloth in his left, and bent him double until he broke. {#p10 1.165}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=bhima light=bhima}

Baka died with terrible cries, and his kinsmen came running out, terrified. Bhima made them a promise and made them keep it. {#p11 1.166}

> Do not ever again kill human beings. If ye kill men, ye will have to die even as Vaka. {#q4 1.166}
> — Bhima

*So be it,* they said, and from that day the Rakshasas of that country were peaceful towards men. Bhima dragged the body to one of the town gates, left it there, and went home unseen, and told Yudhishthira everything. {#p12 1.166}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=bhima,kunti light=kunti}

In the morning the townspeople found the Rakshasa lying at the gate, huge as a cliff and covered in blood, and their hair stood on end. Thousands came out to see. They worked out whose turn it had been, and went to the Brahmana. {#p13 1.166}

He kept his promise. A high-souled Brahmana skilled in mantras had found him weeping, he said, and had smiled and taken the food to the Rakshasa himself. {#p14 1.166}

The whole town rejoiced, and they founded a festival in which the worship of Brahmanas was the chief rite, in memory of the Brahmana who had saved them. The Pandavas went on living quietly at Ekachakra. {#p15 1.166 1.167}
