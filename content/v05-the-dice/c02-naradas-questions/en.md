::scene{#s1 frame=narada,yudhishthira light=narada}

While they sat in the new hall, the sage [Narada](narada) arrived: master of the Vedas and of logic, of war and of music, wanderer of every world, and, the epic adds, *ever desirous of humbling the celestials and Asuras by fomenting quarrels among them*. {#p1 2.5}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) rose with his brothers, bowed, and gave him a seat and the guest-offering. And Narada began asking him questions. {#p2 2.5}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=narada,yudhishthira light=narada,yudhishthira}

> Is the wealth thou art earning being spent on proper objects? Doth thy mind take pleasure in virtue? Art thou enjoying the pleasures of life? Doth not thy mind sink under their weight? {#q1 2.5}
> — Narada

He did not stop for a long time. Did Yudhishthira divide his time wisely between duty, wealth and pleasure? Were his ministers loyal, old and wise, and his counsels kept secret? Did he wake at the right hour and plan the next day in the small hours? Did he settle nothing alone, and nothing with too many? Did the farmers fear to approach him? Would he give a thousand fools for one learned man? Were his forts full of food, water and weapons? {#p3 2.5}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.5}
**A king's examination.** Narada's questions run on for a whole long section, a survey of everything a good king must attend to, from spies and treasuries to farmers' loans and the care of the old and the blind. We give only a taste. It reads like an inspection, and it is meant to.
::

Yudhishthira bowed at the sage's feet and said he would do all of it. {#p4 2.5}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=narada,yudhishthira,indra,i4395_harishchandra light=narada}

Then Yudhishthira asked Narada about the assembly halls he had seen in his wanderings, and Narada described them: the halls of Indra, of Yama, of Varuna, of Kubera and of Brahma, and who sat in each. {#p5 2.6 2.11}

Yudhishthira noticed something. In Indra's hall, among all the gods and sages, Narada had named only one human king: [Harishchandra](i4395_harishchandra). And he asked about his own father too: how had Narada met Pandu, among the dead? {#p6 2.12}

Harishchandra, said Narada, had conquered the whole earth and performed the great sacrifice called the Rajasuya, at which every king of the earth served, and he gave five times what anyone asked. Kings who perform the Rajasuya live with Indra. {#p7 2.12}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=narada,pandu,yudhishthira light=pandu}

And [Pandu](pandu) had seen Harishchandra's good fortune, and sent his son a message. {#p8 2.12}

> Thou shouldst tell Yudhishthira, O Rishi, that he can subjugate the whole Earth inasmuch as his brothers are all obedient to him. And having done this let him commence the grand sacrifice called Rajasuya. {#q2 2.12}
> — Pandu, as Narada reported him

If his son performed it, Pandu said, he too might reach the hall of Indra. {#p9 2.12}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=narada,yudhishthira light=narada}

Narada had one more thing to say before he left. {#p10 2.12}

> On the commencement of such a sacrifice a war may take place destroying the Kshatriyas and even furnishing occasion for the destruction of the whole Earth. {#q3 2.12}
> — Narada

*Reflecting upon all this, O king of kings, do what is for thy good.* And he went away to Dvaraka. Yudhishthira sat with his brothers and thought about the Rajasuya. {#p11 2.12}

::aside{#a2 kind=note src=2.12,2.5}
**The sage who stirs things up.** The epic introduced Narada as one who loves to set gods and Asuras quarrelling. Here he brings a dead father's wish and, in the same breath, a warning that it could destroy the world. Both turn out to be true.
::
