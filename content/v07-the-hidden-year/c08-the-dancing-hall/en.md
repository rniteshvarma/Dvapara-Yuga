[Bhima](bhima) told her what to do. Virata had built a dancing hall where the girls danced by day and went home at night, and in it stood a fine wooden bed. She must arrange to meet Kichaka there the next evening, and make sure no one saw them talking. {#p1 4.22}

> Even there I will make him see the spirits of his deceased grandsires. {#q1 4.22}
> — Bhima

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kichaka,draupadi light=draupadi}

In the morning [Kichaka](kichaka) came to the palace and found her. He had kicked her in front of the king, he said, and no one had protected her. Virata was king of the Matsyas in name only; he, the commander, was the real lord. He would give her a hundred nishkas, a hundred serving men and a hundred serving women, and chariots drawn by mules. {#p2 4.22}

[Draupadi](draupadi) set one condition: neither his friends nor his brothers must know, for fear of the Gandharvas. He agreed at once. Then, she said, he should come alone after dark to the dancing hall, where the girls danced by day: the Gandharvas did not know that place. {#p3 4.22}

The half day seemed a month long to her. Kichaka went home in delight, *not knowing that it was Death that had assumed the form of a Sairindhri*, and spent the day anointing himself and putting on garlands and jewels, and the day would not end. {#p4 4.22}

> And the beauty of Kichaka, who was about to forsake his beauty for ever, seemed to heighten, like the wick of a burning lamp about to expire. {#q2 4.22}

Draupadi went to the kitchen and told Bhima it was done. He was as glad of it, he said, as he had been at killing Hidimba. She asked him to kill Kichaka in secret, so as not to break the year. He promised to crush his head *as an elephant crusheth a vela fruit*. {#p5 4.22}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,kichaka light=kichaka}

Bhima went first, lay down on the bed in the dark, and waited *like a lion lying in wait for a deer*. Kichaka came, perfumed and garlanded, felt his way through the gloom of the hall, and found someone lying on the bed. He told her of all the riches he had set aside for her, and that women had suddenly begun to praise his looks. {#p6 4.22}

> It is well that thou art handsome, and it is well thou praisest thyself. I think, however, that thou hadst never before this such pleasurable touch! {#q3 4.22}
> — Bhima

Then he rose up laughing. {#p7 4.22}

> Thy sister, O wretch, shall today behold thee dragged by me to the ground, like a mighty elephant, huge as a mountain, dragged to the ground by a lion. {#q4 4.22}
> — Bhima

He seized Kichaka by his garlanded hair. Kichaka wrenched it free and caught Bhima's arms, and they fought in the dark *like the brothers Vali and Sugriva* of old, with nails and teeth, their arms cracking like splitting bamboo, until the building shook. Kichaka was strong: once he brought Bhima to the ground with his knees. But Bhima rose, threw him down and tossed him about the room like a storm tossing a tree, and Kichaka began to weaken. {#p8 4.22}

Bhima bound him in his arms, whirled him while he roared like a broken trumpet, took his throat and squeezed it, and drove his knees into his waist and killed him, *as one would slay a beast*. Then, still in rage, he pressed the arms and legs and head into the body until it was only a ball of flesh. {#p9 4.22}

> Slaying this wretch who intended to violate our wife,—this thorn in the side of Sairindhri, I am freed from the debt I owed to my brothers, and have attained perfect peace. {#q5 4.22}
> — Bhima

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhima,draupadi light=draupadi}

He lit a torch and showed Draupadi what was left. {#p10 4.22}

> Come princess of Panchala, and see what hath become of that lustful wretch! {#q6 4.22}
> — Bhima

Then he went back to the kitchen. Draupadi, her grief gone, called the keepers of the hall: let them come and see Kichaka, who had lusted after other men's wives, killed by her Gandharva husbands. They came by the thousand with torches, and stared at the body without arms or legs. *Where is his neck, and where are his legs?* It could only have been a Gandharva. {#p11 4.22}
