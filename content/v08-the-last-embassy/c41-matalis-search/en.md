Then the sage [Kanva](kanva) spoke. Only Brahma, Nara and Narayana, and Vishnu are eternal; everything else perishes. And one should never think *I am strong*, for there is always someone stronger. He told the story of Matali, Indra's charioteer. {#p1 5.97}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=narada light=narada}

Matali had a daughter, Gunakeshi, so beautiful that he could find no husband for her among gods or men. So he went down to the underworld, and on the way met [Narada](narada), who offered to guide him. Together they saw Varuna's realm and its weapons; Patala, where the gods drank the Amrita and left the rest, and where a blazing egg waits to burst into fire at the end of the world; Hiranyapura of the Daityas, whom Matali would not marry into; the land of the birds descended from Garuda; Rasatala, where Surabhi the mother of all cows lives; and Bhogavati, the city of the Nagas, under Vasuki. {#p2 5.98 5.99 5.100 5.101 5.102 5.103}

There Matali saw a young Naga and his heart went to him: Sumukha, of Airavata's line, grandson of Aryaka. But Sumukha's father had been eaten by Garuda, and Garuda had said he would come back in a month for the son. {#p3 5.103}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=indra,vishnu,garuda light=garuda}

So they took Sumukha to heaven. [Vishnu](vishnu), there with [Indra](indra), told Indra to give him the Amrita; Indra, wary of Garuda, gave him long life instead, and Sumukha married Gunakeshi. Then [Garuda](garuda) came storming in. Why had Indra taken away his food? He was as strong as Indra, and he carried Vishnu himself on his back; who was stronger? {#p4 5.104}

> Come now, bear thou the weight of this one right arm of mine. If thou canst bear even this, thy boast would be regarded as reasonable. {#q1 5.105}
> — Vishnu

Vishnu laid his arm on Garuda's shoulder, and Garuda fell senseless under a weight as great as the earth with all her mountains, shedding his feathers, and begged forgiveness. Vishnu put Sumukha on Garuda's breast with his toe, and from then on they were friends. {#p5 5.105}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=kanva,duryodhana,karna light=duryodhana}

So, said Kanva, Duryodhana lived only as long as he did not meet the Pandavas in battle. Duryodhana frowned, breathed hard, looked at Karna and laughed aloud, slapping his thigh. {#p6 5.105}

> I am, O great Rishi, precisely what the Creator hath made me. What is to be, must be. What also hath been ordained in my case must happen, I cannot act otherwise. What can these senseless declamations, therefore, avail? {#q2 5.105}
> — Duryodhana
