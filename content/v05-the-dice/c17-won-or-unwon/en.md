::scene{#s1 frame=draupadi,duhshasana,bhishma,drona,kripa light=draupadi}

*Wait a little*, said [Draupadi](draupadi). There was a duty she had not yet done. Dragged by this man, she had lost her senses; now she bowed to the elders of the Kurus. {#p1 2.68}

Then she fell to the ground and wept. Only once had kings ever seen her, at her svayamvara; even the wind and the sun had not seen her in her rooms. Now the Pandavas let this wretch drag her, and the Kurus let him do it to their daughter-in-law. Was she a slave, or not? {#p2 2.68}

> Ye kings, I desire ye to answer whether ye regard me as won or unwon. I will accept your verdict whatever it be. {#q1 2.68}
> — Draupadi

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhishma,yudhishthira,draupadi light=bhishma}

[Bhishma](bhishma) said again that dharma is subtle, and then said something harder. {#p3 2.68}

> What in this world a strong man calls morality is regarded as such by others, however otherwise it may really be; but what a weak man calls morality is scarcely regarded as such even if it be the highest morality. {#q2 2.68}
> — Bhishma

He could not answer her, he said. But he knew this: the Kurus had become slaves of greed and folly, and their race would soon be destroyed. Drona and the others sat with their heads down like dead men. Yudhishthira, he said, should be the one to say. {#p4 2.68}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=duryodhana,bhima,yudhishthira,arjuna light=duryodhana}

The kings said nothing, out of fear of [Duryodhana](duryodhana). He smiled a little. Let her husbands answer, he said. Let Bhima and Arjuna and the twins declare that Yudhishthira is not their lord; then she is free. Or let Yudhishthira himself say whether he is her lord. The kings applauded and winked at one another. {#p5 2.69}

[Bhima](bhima) lifted his sandal-smeared arms. {#p6 2.69}

> He is the lord of all our religious and ascetic merits, the lord of even our lives. If he regardeth himself as won, we too have all been won. {#q3 2.69}
> — Bhima

If it were not for that, he said, no mortal who had touched Draupadi's hair would have left alive. Only Yudhishthira's word and Arjuna's pleading held him back. *Forbear, O Bhima*, said Bhishma, Drona and Vidura. *Everything is possible with thee.* {#p7 2.69}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=karna,draupadi,bhima light=karna}

[Karna](karna) spoke to Draupadi. A slave, a son and a wife own nothing, he said; she was the wife of a slave. She should go and serve in Dhritarashtra's household. {#p8 2.70}

> O handsome one, select thou another husband now,—one who will not make thee a slave by gambling. {#q4 2.70}
> — Karna

Bhima breathed hard. He could not be angry with the son of a Suta, he told Yudhishthira; they were slaves. But would anyone have dared say this to him, if Yudhishthira had not staked her? {#p9 2.70}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=duryodhana,bhima,draupadi light=duryodhana,bhima}

Then Duryodhana, to please Karna and insult Bhima, bared his left thigh and showed it to Draupadi. {#p10 2.70}

> Let not Vrikodara attain to the regions, obtained by his ancestors, if he doth not break that thigh of thine in the great conflict. {#q5 2.70}
> — Bhima

Sparks flew from Bhima like fire from the cracks of a burning tree. {#p11 2.70}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=vidura,arjuna,duryodhana light=vidura,arjuna}

[Vidura](vidura) gave his judgment again. If Yudhishthira had staked her before he lost himself, she would have been his to lose. {#p12 2.70}

> If, however a person staketh anything at a time when he himself is incapable of holding any wealth, to win it is very like obtaining wealth in a dream. {#q6 2.70}
> — Vidura

Duryodhana repeated his offer: let the brothers say Yudhishthira was not their master. [Arjuna](arjuna) answered. {#p13 2.70}

> This illustrious son of Kunti, king Yudhishthira the just, was certainly our master before he began to play. But having lost himself, let all the Kauravas judge whose master he could be after that. {#q7 2.70}
> — Arjuna

***

::scene{#s7 frame=dhritarashtra,gandhari,vidura,bhishma,drona,kripa light=dhritarashtra}

At that moment a jackal howled in the fire-chamber of Dhritarashtra's palace. Asses brayed in answer, and birds of ill omen cried on every side. Bhishma, Drona and Kripa cried *Svasti! Svasti!* [Gandhari](gandhari) and Vidura went to the king and told him what the sounds meant. {#p14 2.70}

[Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra) turned on his son. *Thou wicked-minded Duryodhana, thou wretch, destruction hath all ready overtaken thee.* {#p15 2.70}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.65,2.70}
**Never answered.** Draupadi asks her question four times. Vidura and Vikarna answer that she was not won, and Arjuna's reply comes close. The elders never answer at all. The question is settled not by a ruling but by a jackal, and a frightened old king.
::
