::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,arjuna light=krishna}

As the Kuru army came on, [Krishna](krishna) spoke to [Arjuna](arjuna) for his good: purify yourself, and on the eve of battle sing your hymn to Durga for the defeat of the enemy. Arjuna got down from his chariot and, with joined hands, praised the goddess. {#p1 6.23}

He called her by her many names: leader of yogis, one with Brahman, dweller in the forest of Mandara; Kali, Mahakali, the dark and the tawny; bearer of the peacock banner, the spear, the sword and shield; younger sister of the chief of cowherds, born in the race of Nanda; Uma, Shakambhari, slayer of Kaitabha; the Vedas, Svaha and Svadha, Sarasvati and Savitri; the sleep from which there is no waking; the light that makes the sun and the moon shine. {#p2 6.23}

> O great goddess, let victory always attend me through thy grace on the field of battle. {#q1 6.23}
> — Arjuna

***

::scene{#s2 frame=arjuna,krishna light=arjuna}

Durga, who is always kind to men, appeared in the sky before Govinda. {#p3 6.23}

> Within a short time thou shalt conquer thy foes, O Pandava. O invincible one, thou hast Narayana (again) for aiding thee. {#q2 6.23}
> — the goddess

No enemy could defeat him, she said, not even the wielder of the thunderbolt. Then she vanished, and Arjuna, counting himself already successful, mounted his chariot again, and he and Krishna blew their celestial conchs. Whoever recites this hymn at dawn, Sanjaya said, need fear no Yaksha, Rakshasa or Pishacha, nor snakes nor kings, and will win in every dispute. {#p4 6.23}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=6.23}
Many manuscripts do not have this hymn, and the critical edition of the Mahabharata prints it in an appendix rather than in the main text. Ganguli's text includes it, and so do we.
::

***

::scene{#s3 frame=sanjaya,dhritarashtra light=sanjaya}

All this, Sanjaya said, he knew by Vyasa's grace. The king's sons, caught in the net of death, did not know that those two were Nara and Narayana. Vyasa and Narada, Kanva and Parashurama had all tried to stop Duryodhana, and he had not listened. {#p5 6.23}

> There where righteousness is, there is Krishna; and there where Krishna is, there is victory. {#q3 6.23}
> — Sanjaya

Then the ranks met with a roar of conchs and drums and shouting men and trumpeting elephants. On both sides the warriors were cheerful, and the garlands of both armies smelled equally sweet. {#p6 6.24}
