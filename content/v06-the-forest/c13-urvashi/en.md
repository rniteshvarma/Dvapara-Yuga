::scene{#s1 frame=indra,urvashi,arjuna light=urvashi}

[Indra](indra) noticed that [Arjuna](arjuna) kept looking at [Urvashi](urvashi), and sent Chitrasena to her with a message: as Arjuna had learned weapons and music in heaven, let him learn from her the arts of a woman's company. {#p1 3.45}

Chitrasena praised Arjuna to her at length. Urvashi smiled and said she was already in love with him. *I shall gladly go to Arjuna.* {#p2 3.45}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=urvashi,arjuna light=urvashi}

She bathed and dressed in ornaments and garlands of heavenly scent. When the moon was up, a little flushed with wine, she went through the streets of heaven, and the Siddhas and Gandharvas thought her the most beautiful thing they had ever seen. She sent word at Arjuna's door, and was let in. {#p3 3.46}

When Arjuna saw her there at night, he was frightened. He shut his eyes out of modesty, and greeted her as one greets an elder. *I wait upon thee as thy servant.* {#p4 3.46}

Urvashi told him everything: Indra's festival, how Arjuna had gazed only at her, Chitrasena's message, her own desire. Arjuna covered his ears. {#p5 3.46}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=arjuna,urvashi,pururavas,kunti light=arjuna}

He had looked at her, he said, but for a reason. *Even this blooming lady is the mother of the Kaurava race.* For Urvashi was the wife of Pururavas, the ancestor of his whole line. {#p6 3.46}

The Apsaras are free, she said. The sons of Puru's line who come to heaven all enjoy them without sin. *I am burning with desire.* {#p7 3.46}

> As Kunti, or Madri, or Sachi, is to me, so art thou, the parent of my race, an object of reverence to me. {#q1 3.46}
> — Arjuna

He bowed his head and lay at her feet. *Thou deservest my worship as my own mother; and it behoveth thee to protect me as a son.* {#p8 3.46}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=urvashi,arjuna light=urvashi}

Urvashi trembled with rage and cursed him. {#p9 3.46}

> Thou shalt have to pass thy time among females unregarded, and as a dancer, and destitute of manhood and scorned as a eunuch. {#q2 3.46}
> — Urvashi

And she went home, her lips still shaking. {#p10 3.46}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=indra,arjuna light=indra}

Arjuna told Chitrasena, and Chitrasena told Indra. Indra called his son in private and smiled. *Pritha hath to-day become a truly blessed mother.* He had beaten even the sages in self-control. {#p11 3.46}

> The curse that Urvasi hath denounced on thee will be to thy benefit, O child, and stand thee in good stead. {#q3 3.46}
> — Indra

In the thirteenth year of the exile, when the Pandavas must live unrecognised, Arjuna would serve out the curse: one year as a dancer without manhood, and then it would end. So Arjuna stopped worrying about it, and went on enjoying heaven with Chitrasena. {#p12 3.46}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.46,3.44}
**The dance explained.** Now we know why Indra had Arjuna taught to dance. The curse and the training together make the disguise he will need for the hidden year.
::
