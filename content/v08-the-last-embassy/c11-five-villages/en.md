Sanjaya took his leave, hoping he had said nothing offensive. [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) told him he was the dearest of envoys, never harsh, never false; only Vidura was as welcome. {#p1 5.30}

Then he sent greetings to everyone in Hastinapura, one by one: to the Brahmanas and ascetics; to Drona and Ashwatthama; to Kripa, whose feet Sanjaya should touch; to Bhishma; to the blind king; even to the wicked Duryodhana and Duhshasana; to Bahlika, who wanted only peace; to Somadatta and his son, *my friend and is a brother to us*; to Yuyutsu, who never wanted this war; to Shakuni and Karna; to Vidura, *who is our father and mother and friend*; to the old women, the wives, the daughters, the servants, the lame and the blind whom Dhritarashtra fed. {#p2 5.30}

> O foremost of heroes among the Bharatas, either give me back my own Indraprastha or fight with me! {#q1 5.30}
> — Yudhishthira, to Duryodhana

***

::scene{#s2 frame=yudhishthira,sanjaya,duryodhana light=yudhishthira}

Tell Dhritarashtra, he said, that it was by his grace the Pandavas, as children, had a kingdom; let him not now be indifferent to them. Tell Bhishma that he had saved Shantanu's line once, and must do so again. And tell Duryodhana: the insults to Draupadi, the exile in deerskins, Duhshasana dragging her in front of Kunti, all of it they would forgive, because they did not want to see the Kurus slain. But they must have their share. {#p3 5.31}

> We are desirous of peace; give us even a single province of the empire. Give us even Kusasthala, Vrikasthala, Makandi, Varanavata, and for the fifth any other that thou likest. Even this will end the quarrel. O Suyodhana, give unto thy five brothers at least five villages. {#q2 5.31}
> — Yudhishthira

Let brothers follow brothers, he said, and fathers be with sons; let the Panchalas laugh with the Kurus. {#p4 5.31}

> O Sanjaya, I am equally capable of war and peace. {#q3 5.31}
> — Yudhishthira
