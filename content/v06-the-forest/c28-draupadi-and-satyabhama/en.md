::scene{#s1 frame=draupadi,satyabhama light=satyabhama}

While the men sat with the sages, [Draupadi](draupadi) and [Satyabhama](satyabhama) went into the hermitage, laughing, glad to see each other after so long, and talked about the Kurus and the Yadus. Then Satyabhama asked her something in private. {#p1 3.231}

How did she rule the sons of Pandu, those heroes like the guardians of the world, so that they were never angry with her and always did as she asked? Was it a vow, or a spell, or a drug, or a charm? *Tell me … by which, O Krishna, Krishna may ever be obedient to me.* {#p2 3.231}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=draupadi,satyabhama light=draupadi}

Draupadi said Satyabhama was asking about the ways of wicked women. A husband who finds his wife uses spells and drugs fears her like a snake in his bedroom. {#p3 3.231}

She had no spells. She served her husbands and their other wives, she said, without vanity or jealousy. She never ate or slept before them. She kept the house clean and served food on time; she did not laugh loudly or linger at the gate. She served Kunti herself, with food and drink and clothes, and never preferred herself to her. {#p4 3.231}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=draupadi,yudhishthira light=draupadi}

And then she told her something else. At Indraprastha, she said, eight thousand Brahmanas had been fed every day from gold plates, and eighty thousand householders, each with thirty serving-girls; Yudhishthira had a hundred thousand horses and a hundred thousand elephants. She had known the names and faces of all the serving-women, and what each of them did. {#p5 3.231}

> O blessed and illustrious lady, it was I alone amongst the Pandavas who knew the income and expenditure of the king and what their whole wealth was. {#q1 3.231}
> — Draupadi

The brothers had put the whole burden on her and gone about their virtue, and she had carried it day and night. *This, O Satyabhama, hath ever been my charm for making my husbands obedient to me!* {#p6 3.231}

Satyabhama bowed to her. *Among friends, conversations in jest arise naturally.* Forgive me. {#p7 3.231}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=satyabhama,draupadi,krishna,prativindhya,sutasoma,shrutakarma,shatanika,shrutasena light=satyabhama}

When Krishna was ready to leave, Satyabhama embraced Draupadi. Do not grieve or lie awake, she said; you will see the earth ruled by your husbands again, and the Kuru women who laughed at you on your way to exile weeping in their turn. {#p8 3.233}

Your sons are well, she said: [Prativindhya](prativindhya), [Sutasoma](sutasoma), [Shrutakarma](shrutakarma), [Shatanika](shatanika) and [Shrutasena](shrutasena), all skilled in arms, all happy at Dvaraka with Abhimanyu. Then she climbed into Krishna's chariot, and they drove away. {#p9 3.233}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.231}
**More than a lesson in obedience.** This conversation is often quoted as Draupadi's teaching on a wife's duty, and it is that. But listen to the end of it: she ran the household and the treasury of the greatest court on earth, and was the only one who knew what the king owned.
::
