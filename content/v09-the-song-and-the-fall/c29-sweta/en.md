::scene{#s1 frame=bhishma,abhimanyu light=abhimanyu}

Late in the morning, Durmukha, Kritavarma, Kripa, Shalya and Vivimshati closed round [Bhishma](bhishma), and under their guard his palm-tree banner went gliding through the Chedis, the Kashis, the Karushas and the Panchalas. With straight broad-headed arrows he cut off heads, yokes and banners, and seemed to dance on his chariot. {#p1 6.47}

Then [Abhimanyu](abhimanyu) came at him on his chariot with tawny horses, under a golden banner like a *karnikara* tree. He pierced Kritavarma, Shalya and his great-grandfather; he cut down the head of Durmukha's charioteer and Kripa's golden bow, seeming to dance as he did it. {#p2 6.47}

> And in consequence of Abhimanyu's sureness of aim, all the car-warriors headed by Bhishma regarded him to be possessed of the capacity of Dhananjaya himself. {#q1 6.47}
> — Sanjaya

With nine arrows he cut down Bhishma's silver palm-tree banner, and the people shouted, and Bhima roared to cheer the boy on. Then Bhishma brought out his celestial weapons and covered Abhimanyu with thousands of arrows, and ten Pandava warriors rushed to protect him; Bhishma cut down Bhima's lion banner. {#p3 6.47}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=uttar,shalya light=shalya}

[Uttara](uttar), Virata's son, charged [Shalya](shalya) on an elephant with its trunk raised. The elephant set its foot on the yoke of Shalya's chariot and killed his four horses; Shalya hurled an iron dart that went through Uttara's armour, and he fell senseless from the elephant's neck, the hook and lance slipping from his hands. Shalya leapt down with his sword and cut off the elephant's trunk, and it shrieked and died. {#p4 6.47}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=6.47}
This is the prince who drove out with Arjuna as his charioteer to recover Virata's cattle, in Volume VII. The text says only that he fell senseless here, but from this point it speaks of him as slain, and his brother takes up his cause.
::

Seeing his brother Uttara slain, Sweta, the Pandava commander, blazed up *like fire with clarified butter*. He drove at Shalya, and seven Kuru warriors surrounded him; he cut their bows twice over with seven arrows, cut down their seven darts in the air, struck Shalya's son Rukmaratha senseless, and pressed on toward Shalya until the Kurus cried out in alarm. {#p5 6.47}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhishma light=bhishma}

Sweta's slaughter was so great that the Kurus left Bhishma alone on the field and drew back out of bowshot, Sanjaya said; he himself had fled with them, and that was why he was alive to tell it. Bhishma alone stood like Meru. If Sweta had not held him, Bhishma could have burned the whole Pandava army in a day. {#p6 6.48}

Sweta cut Bhishma's bow into ten pieces, and with an iron arrow brought down the palm tree from the top of his banner, and Duryodhana's men thought Bhishma was dead. *Either Sweta dies today or Bhishma*, Duryodhana said, and sent everyone to protect the grandsire. Bhishma killed Sweta's horses and his charioteer; Sweta hurled a golden dart like the rod of Death, and Bhishma cut it into nine pieces. Then Sweta, beside himself, took a mace and smashed Bhishma's chariot to dust; Bhishma had leapt down just in time. {#p7 6.48}

> O, Bhishma, O thou of mighty arms, strive without losing a moment. Even this is the hour fixed by the Creator of the Universe for success over this one. {#q2 6.48}
> — a voice from the sky

Bhishma mounted another chariot, held off Bhima, Satyaki, Abhimanyu and the rest, and went for Sweta alone. He fixed on his string an arrow charged with the force of the Brahma weapon. It went through Sweta's armour and body and into the earth. {#p8 6.48}

> Like the Sun when speedily retiring to his western chambers taking along with him the rays of light, even thus that shaft passed out of Sweta's body, bearing away with itself his life. {#q3 6.48}
> — Sanjaya

Sweta fell like a mountain peak. Duhshasana danced on the field to the sound of conchs and drums, and the Pandava warriors, Shikhandi at their head, trembled. {#p9 6.48}
