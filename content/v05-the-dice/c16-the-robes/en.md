::scene{#s1 frame=bhima,yudhishthira,arjuna,sahadeva light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) turned on his eldest brother. Gamblers keep loose women in their houses, he said, and even they do not stake them. The wealth, the kingdom, the brothers, Yudhishthira himself: all that Bhima had borne, because Yudhishthira was their lord. But not this. {#p1 2.67}

> It is for her sake, O king, that my anger falleth on thee. I shall burn those hands of thine. Sahadeva, bring some fire. {#q1 2.67}
> — Bhima

[Arjuna](arjuna) stopped him. Bhima had never spoken like that before; that was what the enemy wanted. The king had been challenged and had played, against his will, by the custom of Kshatriyas. Bhima answered that if he had not known that, he would have held those hands in the fire himself. {#p2 2.67}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=vikarna,draupadi,bhishma,dhritarashtra,vidura,drona,kripa light=vikarna}

Then one of Dhritarashtra's own sons stood up. [Vikarna](vikarna) asked the kings to answer Draupadi's question. Why did Bhishma and Dhritarashtra say nothing, or Vidura, or Drona, or Kripa? Let every king say which side he was on. {#p3 2.67}

No one answered him, good or ill. Vikarna rubbed his hands and sighed like a snake, and then said he would give his own answer. Hunting, drinking, dice and women are the four vices of kings, and nothing done in their grip carries authority. Yudhishthira had staked her in the middle of a game, urged on by cheats. She was the wife of all five brothers, not his alone. He had lost himself before he staked her. And it was Shakuni who had named her as the stake. {#p4 2.67}

> Reflecting upon all these circumstances, I regard Draupadi as not won. {#q2 2.67}
> — Vikarna

The hall roared its approval of Vikarna and its contempt for Shakuni. {#p5 2.67}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=karna,vikarna,draupadi,duhshasana light=karna}

[Karna](karna) rose, shaking with anger. Vikarna's rage would burn him up, he said, as a fire burns the stick it comes from. He was a boy talking like an old man. Yudhishthira had staked everything he owned, and Draupadi was part of it; the Pandavas themselves had heard her named and agreed. {#p6 2.67}

> The gods have ordained only one husband for one woman. This Draupadi, however, hath many husbands. Therefore, certain it is that she is an unchaste woman. {#q3 2.67}
> — Karna

So there was nothing strange, he said, in bringing her here in a single cloth, or even in uncovering her. *O Dussasana, this Vikarna speaking words of (apparent) wisdom is but a boy. Take off the robes of the Pandavas as also the attire of Draupadi.* {#p7 2.67}

The Pandavas took off their upper garments and threw them down, and sat. Then [Duhshasana](duhshasana) took hold of Draupadi's cloth in front of everyone and began to pull it off her. {#p8 2.67}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=draupadi,krishna,duhshasana light=draupadi,krishna}

[Draupadi](draupadi) covered her face and cried out to Krishna: *O Govinda, O thou who dwellest in Dwaraka, O Krishna … seest thou not that the Kauravas are humiliating me?* {#p9 2.67}

And as each cloth was pulled away, another of the same kind appeared beneath it, and then another, until there were hundreds of robes of every colour, and a heap of cloth lay on the floor of the hall. A great noise rose from the kings, praising Draupadi and cursing the son of Dhritarashtra. At last Duhshasana, tired and ashamed, sat down. {#p10 2.67}

::aside{#a1 kind=versions src=2.67}
**Who covered her?** Ganguli's text says it twice in a row: that Krishna, hearing her, left his seat and came on foot, *and* that *the illustrious Dharma, remaining unseen*, covered her. Her prayer to Krishna is missing from the oldest manuscripts, and the Critical Edition prints the miracle without naming anyone. Popular tradition gives it all to Krishna.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=bhima,duhshasana light=bhima}

Then Bhima, his lips trembling, squeezed his hands together and swore, in front of all the kings. {#p11 2.67}

> Tearing open in battle, by sheer force, the breast of this wretch, this wicked-minded scoundrel of the Bharata race, if I do not drink his life-blood, let me not obtain the region of my ancestors. {#q4 2.67}
> — Bhima

Everyone who heard it felt their hair stand on end. {#p12 2.67}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=vidura,karna,duhshasana,draupadi light=vidura}

The hall cried *Fie!* on Dhritarashtra. [Vidura](vidura) raised his hands for silence. Draupadi was weeping, he said, and they had not answered her; Vikarna had, and so should they. {#p13 2.67}

> Knowing the rules of morality, and having attended an assembly, he that doth not answer a query that is put, incurreth half the demerit that attacheth to a lie. {#q5 2.67}
> — Vidura

He told them how the demon king Prahlada was once asked to judge between his own son and a sage's son, and was told by Kashyapa what befalls a judge who stays silent or lies. Prahlada judged against his own son, and the sage's son, moved, gave the boy back his life. {#p14 2.67}

The kings heard this and answered not a word. Only Karna spoke, to Duhshasana. *Take away this serving-woman Krishna into the inner apartments.* And Duhshasana began to drag her again. {#p15 2.67}
