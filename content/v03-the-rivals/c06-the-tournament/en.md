::scene{#s1 frame=drona,dhritarashtra,kripa,somadatta,bahlika,bhishma,vyasa,vidura light=drona,dhritarashtra}

When the princes had learnt all he had to teach, [Drona](drona) went to [Dhritarashtra](dhritarashtra), with [Kripa](kripa), [Somadatta](somadatta), [Bahlika](bahlika), [Bhishma](bhishma), [Vyasa](vyasa) and [Vidura](vidura) present, and said that the king's children had finished their education. With his leave, let them show what they could do. {#p1 1.136}

The blind king was glad, and said something else as well. {#p2 1.136}

> Grief arising from my own blindness maketh me envy those who, blessed with sight, will behold my children's prowess in arm. {#q1 1.136}
> — Dhritarashtra

He told Vidura to do whatever Drona asked. Drona measured out a level piece of ground, clear of trees and bushes and supplied with wells and springs, chose a day when the stars were favourable, and made offerings there before the citizens, called together by proclamation. The king's builders raised a great stage hung with every kind of weapon, and a separate hall for the women; the citizens built platforms, and the wealthy pitched tall tents all round. {#p3 1.136}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=dhritarashtra,gandhari,kunti,bhishma,kripa light=gandhari,kunti}

On the day, the king came with his ministers, Bhishma and Kripa walking ahead, into a theatre of gold hung with strings of pearls and lapis. [Gandhari](gandhari) and [Kunti](kunti) and the other women of the household climbed to their places in splendid clothes, like goddesses climbing Mount Meru. Brahmanas, Kshatriyas and all the people came running from the city, and the noise of trumpets, drums and voices was like the sea in a storm. {#p4 1.136}

Then Drona came in with his son, all in white: white robes, white sacred thread, white hair, white beard, white garlands, white sandal paste. The epic says it was as though the moon had risen with the planet Mars beside him. {#p5 1.136}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,duryodhana light=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,duryodhana}

The princes came in by order of age, Yudhishthira first, with their finger-guards, bows and quivers. They shot on horseback at targets with arrows marked with their own names. Some spectators ducked, afraid of stray arrows; others watched without fear. Hundreds and thousands cried out *Well done! Well done!* Then the princes took up sword and shield and ranged the lists, and the people marvelled at their grace and their steady grip. {#p6 1.136}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=bhima,duryodhana,ashwatthama,drona,vidura light=bhima,duryodhana}

Then [Bhima](bhima) and [Duryodhana](duryodhana) came into the arena, maces in hand, both of them glad of the chance, like two single-peaked mountains. They roared like two bull elephants fighting over a cow, and circled the lists. {#p7 1.136}

Vidura told Dhritarashtra, Kunti and Gandhari everything that was happening. {#p8 1.136}

The crowd split in two. Some shouted for the Kuru king, some for Bhima, and the uproar rose like a troubled sea. Drona saw the danger, and sent his son between them. {#p9 1.137}

> Restrain both these mighty warriors so proficient in arms. Let not the ire of the assembly be provoked by this combat of Bhima and Duryodhana. {#q2 1.137}
> — Drona

[Ashwatthama](ashwatthama) stepped between them with their maces raised, and parted them. {#p10 1.137}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.137,1.134}
**A game that was not a game.** The epic has already told us that Duryodhana and Bhima were the two masters of the mace, *always jealous of each other*. The first time they meet in public, the teacher has to stop it.
::

***

::scene{#s5 frame=drona,arjuna,kunti light=arjuna}

Drona stopped the music, and spoke in a voice like thunder. {#p11 1.137}

> Behold ye now that Partha who is dearer to me than my own son, the master of all arms, the son of Indra himself. {#q3 1.137}
> — Drona

[Arjuna](arjuna) came in, in golden mail, with his finger-guard, his full quiver and his bow, like an evening cloud lit by the setting sun and a rainbow and lightning. Conches sounded everywhere, and the crowd shouted: *This is the graceful son of Kunti! This is the son of Indra! This is the protector of the Kurus!* {#p12 1.137}

When Kunti heard it, her tears, the epic says, mixed with the milk of her breast and wet her bosom. {#p13 1.137}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=dhritarashtra,vidura light=dhritarashtra}

Dhritarashtra heard the roar and asked Vidura what it was. Vidura told him: Arjuna, the son of Pandu and Pritha, had entered the lists in his armour. {#p14 1.137}

> O thou of soul so great, by the three fires sprung from Pritha who is even like the sacred fuel, I have, indeed, been blessed, favoured and protected! {#q4 1.137}
> — Dhritarashtra

***

::scene{#s7 frame=arjuna light=arjuna}

When the crowd had grown a little calmer, Arjuna began. With the weapon of fire he made fire, with the weapon of Varuna water, with the weapon of the wind air, with the weapon of Parjanya clouds. With one weapon he made land and with another mountains, and with the weapon of vanishing he made them all disappear. {#p15 1.137}

Now he seemed tall, now short; now on the yoke of his chariot, now in the chariot, now on the ground. He shot five arrows at once, as if they were one, into the mouth of a moving iron boar, and twenty-one into the hollow of a cow's horn swinging on a rope. He showed his skill with the sword, the bow and the mace, walking round the lists in circles. {#p16 1.137}

***

::scene{#s8 frame=drona,yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,duryodhana,ashwatthama light=drona,duryodhana}

The show was nearly over. The crowd's excitement had cooled and the instruments had fallen silent. Then, from the gate, came the sound of a man slapping his arms, a challenge, loud as thunder. {#p17 1.137}

Were the mountains splitting? people asked. Was the earth breaking open, or was the sky full of storm clouds? Every eye turned to the gate. {#p18 1.137}

Drona stood surrounded by the five sons of Pritha, like the moon with the five stars of the constellation Hasta. Duryodhana leapt up, mace in hand, and his hundred brothers gathered round him, with Ashwatthama among them, like Indra with the gods on the day of battle with the demons. {#p19 1.137}
