In the afternoon [Krishna](krishna) went to his father's sister, [Kunti](kunti). She put her arms round his neck and wept for her sons. {#p1 5.90}

They had been woken all their lives by conchs and drums and flutes and the hymns of Brahmanas; how could they sleep in forests full of the cries of beasts? She asked after each one. Yudhishthira, *of complexion like that of pure gold*. Bhima, who had the strength of ten thousand elephants and held his anger in for his brother's sake. Arjuna, who shot five hundred arrows at one stretch. Sahadeva, who served his brothers and her so humbly. And Nakula: {#p2 5.90}

> Behold, O hero, I am alive today, even I, who could know peace by losing sight of Nakula for the short space of time taken up by a wink of the eye. {#q1 5.90}
> — Kunti

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=5.90}
The sense of Kunti's words is that once she *could not* bear to lose sight of Nakula for the blink of an eye; Ganguli's English here reads oddly.
::

> More than all my sons, O Janardana, is the daughter of Drupada dear to me. {#q2 5.90}
> — Kunti

Nothing had grieved her like Draupadi dragged into the hall in a single garment. Of all who were there, it was Vidura she honoured. {#p3 5.90}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=kunti,kuntibhoja light=kunti}

She blamed no one, she said, but her own father, who had given her away as a child to his friend Kuntibhoja *like a wealthy man giving away a sum of money in gift*. On the night of Arjuna's birth a voice had told her he would conquer the world. Fourteen years she had not seen her sons. {#p4 5.90}

> Practically, O Janardana, my children are all dead to me and I am dead to them. {#q3 5.90}
> — Kunti

***

::scene{#s3 frame=kunti,krishna light=krishna}

Then she gave Krishna her message to her sons. To Yudhishthira: his virtue was dwindling; *fie to them that live by dependence on others*. {#p5 5.90}

> Thou must also say unto Dhananjaya and the ever-ready Vrikodara that—The time for that event is come in view of which a Kshatriya woman bringeth forth a son. {#q4 5.90}
> — Kunti

To the twins: win by prowess what pleases a Kshatriya's heart. And to Arjuna: *Tread thou the path that may be pointed out to thee by Draupadi.* {#p6 5.90}

Krishna comforted her. She was like a lotus moved from one great lake to another; her sons were heroes, and heroes take no pleasure in the middle way. {#p7 5.90}

> The wise always delight in extremes. {#q5 5.90}
> — Krishna

She would soon see them lords of the world. Kunti, steadied, told him to do what was right, without guile. Then he walked round her and went to Duryodhana's palace. {#p8 5.90}
