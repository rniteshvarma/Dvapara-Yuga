# Dvapara Yuga — The Volumes (Phase 2)

**Goal:** a reader who knows nothing can arrive at the site and read the whole Mahabharata, from King Shantanu on the banks of the Ganga to the Pandavas climbing the mountains toward heaven, as a set of beautifully made volumes. Later, companion volumes tell the side stories: Karna, Shakuni, Amba, Ekalavya, and the many tales almost nobody knows. All of it is in English first, then Hindi, Telugu and the other Indian languages, and every line can be traced back to the text.

Phase 1 built the **map**: who everyone is and how they are joined. Phase 2 builds the **story**: what they did. The two are one site. The map becomes the illustration of the book, and every character in the book opens onto the map.

---

## 1. The idea in one line

> **Read the epic, and watch it happen on the map.**

As you read a chapter, the living lineage beside the text answers. When Bhishma takes his vow, the map flies to him, lights the thread to Satyavati, and dims the throne he gives up. No other Mahabharata resource can do this, because no other has the map. That makes it the heart of the project, ahead of any amount of artwork.

## 2. What we write, and where it comes from

### 2.1 Our own retelling, grounded line by line in the text

| Source | Status | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **K. M. Ganguli's English translation** (1883–96) | Public domain | The backbone. Our retelling follows it, and every paragraph cites its book and section. Key lines are quoted verbatim, marked as quotes. |
| **M. N. Dutt's translation** (1895–1905) | Public domain | A second reading where Ganguli is unclear. |
| **Puranic Encyclopaedia** (Vettam Mani) | Reference | Cross-checking names and relations, as in Phase 1. Never copied. |
| **The Sanskrit text** (Critical Edition, via GRETIL) | Text is ancient; check the e-text's terms before republishing verses | A few famous shlokas, with our own translation. |
| **Andhra Mahabharatam** (Nannaya, Tikkana, Errapragada), Kannada Pampa Bharata, Tamil Villiputturar | Public domain classics | "Versions differ" notes, especially in the Telugu, Kannada and Tamil editions. |
| C. Rajagopalachari, Debroy, van Buitenen, Pattanaik, TV serials | **Under copyright** | Not used as text. At most named as "a popular retelling says…". |

**Why our own words, not Ganguli's:** Ganguli is accurate but Victorian and very long (about 1.8 million words). A reader needs a retelling of perhaps 120,000–180,000 words for the main story. We write that, and keep Ganguli one click away beside every paragraph.

### 2.2 Three labels on everything (this matters most for the side stories)

Accuracy is the brand. Every chapter, scene and claim carries one of these labels:

1. **The epic.** In Vyasa's Mahabharata (Ganguli / Critical Edition), with a section reference.
2. **Later tradition.** From the Harivamsha, the Puranas, or a regional classic, with that source named.
3. **Folk and modern telling.** Widely believed but not in any classical text. We tell it, and say so plainly.

> **Shakuni is the important example.** The story most people know is this: Gandhari's family is imprisoned and starved by the Kurus, Shakuni survives on his family's rations, and he makes dice from his father's bones and takes revenge. That story is **not in Vyasa's Mahabharata**. It comes from later folk telling and was made famous by television. In the epic itself, Shakuni is Gandhari's brother, the son of Subala of Gandhara, and is said to be born of Dvapara, the losing throw of the dice (Book 1). His motives there are loyalty to Duryodhana and ambition.
>
> The Shakuni volume can tell **both** stories, clearly labelled. Done that way, it becomes the most trusted account of Shakuni anywhere. Telling the folk version as if it were the epic would undo what Phase 1 built.
>
> The same care applies to Karna's wife Vrishali, Barbarika, Iravan's marriage, and many famous "facts". The site already has `TRADITION` labels and the "Versions differ" panel, and these volumes reuse both.

## 3. The main saga: thirteen volumes

From Shantanu to the ascent. Each volume has about 8–14 chapters, each a 6–10 minute read.

| # | Volume | Covers | Books (parvas) |
|---|---|---|---|

| II | **The Heirs** | Gandhari's marriage, Kunti's boon and Karna set adrift, Pandu's curse, the births of the Pandavas and the hundred, Pandu's death | Adi |
| III | **The Rivals** | Bhima poisoned, Kripa and Drona, Drona and Drupada, Ekalavya, the tournament where Karna becomes king of Anga, the house of lac | Adi |
| IV | **The Bride of Fire** | Hidimba, Baka, Draupadi's birth and svayamvara, five husbands, Indraprastha, Arjuna's exile (Ulupi, Chitrangada, Subhadra), the Khandava fire | Adi |
| V | **The Dice** | The Maya hall, Jarasandha, the Rajasuya and Shishupala, Duryodhana humiliated, the game, Draupadi's question, exile | Sabha |
| VI | **The Forest** | Kirmira, Shiva as the hunter, Arjuna in heaven and Urvashi's curse, Bhima meets Hanuman, Jayadratha, Karna gives away his armour, the Yaksha's questions | Vana |
| VII | **The Hidden Year** | Virata's court, Kichaka, the cattle raid, Brihannala, Uttara weds Abhimanyu | Virata |
| VIII | **The Last Embassy** | Arjuna chooses Krishna and Duryodhana the army, Krishna's peace mission, Krishna and Karna, Kunti and Karna, Amba reborn as Shikhandi | Udyoga |
| IX | **The Song and the Fall** | The Bhagavad Gita, days 1–10, Bhishma on the bed of arrows | Bhishma |
| X | **Drona's War** | Days 11–15: the wheel formation and Abhimanyu, Jayadratha, Ghatotkacha and the spent Shakti, "Ashvatthama is dead", Drona's end | Drona |
| XI | **Karna** | Days 16–18: Duhshasana, the sunken wheel, Shalya, Shakuni's end, Duryodhana in the lake, the mace duel | Karna, Shalya |
| XII | **Night and Grief** | The night raid, Ashvatthama's curse, the women's lament, Gandhari's curse on Krishna, Karna revealed, Bhishma's last teaching, the horse sacrifice | Sauptika → Ashvamedhika |

The longest books (Shanti and Anushasana, mostly Bhishma's teachings) are told in condensed form. Their full content can become a later companion volume.

### Companion volumes (Phase 3, same format)

- **Karna:** the whole life in one arc, every encounter, every gift.
- **Shakuni and Gandhara:** the epic's Shakuni and the revenge legend, side by side, labelled.
- **Amba / Shikhandi:** three lives of one vow.
- **Drona and Ashvatthama**, **Bhishma**, **Ekalavya**, **Ghatotkacha**, **Krishna** (from the Harivamsha), **Kunti**.
- **Tales told within the epic:** Nala and Damayanti, Savitri, Shakuntala, Yayati, Rama's story as told to Yudhishthira, the churning of the ocean, Garuda. Most readers have never met these. They are already on the map as islands.
- **Folk and regional Mahabharatas:** Barbarika, Iravan and Koovagam, the Tamil Draupadi cult. Clearly labelled.

## 4. The reading experience

### 4.1 Layout: the book and the map side by side

```
┌──────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┐
│  VOLUME I · THE VOW              │                                      │
│  Chapter 4 — The Fisher's Price  │        the living map, framed        │
│                                  │        on Shantanu, Satyavati        │
│  Shantanu could not sleep…       │        and Devavrata                 │
│                                  │                                      │
│  ❝ I will never marry… ❞     1.100│        ◉ ── gold thread lights ──◉   │
│                                  │                                      │
│  [Bhishma] [Satyavati] cast chips│                                      │
│                                  │   (or a scene painting, which        │
│  ⇄ Versions differ               │    gives way back to the map)        │
└──────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Scroll drives the scene.** Each passage carries a small cue (`frame`, `light`, `moment`), and the map moves as you read. The engine already has everything this needs: `frameIds`, `highlightSet`, story-moment arcs and the dynasty focus.
- **Names are doors.** Every name in the text opens that person's card. Every profile gains a "Read their story" list of the chapters they appear in.
- **Margin notes, not footnotes.** The section reference (`1.100`) sits beside its paragraph, and a click shows Ganguli's own words.
- **On a phone:** the map or painting is a short band at the top that changes with the scene, and the text runs beneath. On a phone, reading comes first.
- **Remembered progress, bookmarks and "continue reading"**, saved in the browser.
- **A chapter opening** with a painting, the volume and chapter title in English and Devanagari, and a one-line "previously".

### 4.2 Visuals: what to use, in order of value

| Layer | What | Cost | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The map itself** | Camera moves, threads lighting, arcs between characters, the war drawn as days | Built already | **The core.** Every chapter uses it. |
| **2. Public-domain art** | Ravi Varma, the Razmnama, Kangra and Pahari pages, Kalighat | Free, with credit | For famous scenes where a real painting exists. Authentic and sourced. |
| **3. Chapter paintings** | One opening painting per chapter, plus 2–4 scene paintings per volume (≈ 150 for the main saga) | AI generation (cents each, plus a lot of review) **or** a commissioned artist | See §4.3. |
| **4. Living paintings** | Gentle depth parallax, drifting light and dust over a painting, the way the map already breathes | Low: CSS/WebGL layers from one image | Gives motion without the cost of animation. Recommended. |
| **5. Narration** | Each chapter read aloud, in every language | Text-to-speech: ElevenLabs, Google, or AI4Bharat's open Indic voices | Phase 2.3. Huge for reach in India. Many people would rather listen. |
| **6. Animation / video** | Animated scenes | High (studio, or AI video with heavy review) | Later, for a few signature moments only (the dice, the Gita, the ascent). |

### 4.3 Illustration: AI or an artist?

- **AI-generated** paintings are affordable at this scale. The hard parts are keeping each character's face and dress consistent across 150 images, and getting the details right: the ape on Arjuna's banner, Krishna dark-skinned, Draupadi dark-complexioned and lotus-eyed as the text describes her. The process would be:
  1. Fix one style, close to the Ravi Varma portraits you liked.
  2. Make a reference sheet for each main character, with the descriptions and attributes taken from the text and our details data.
  3. Generate, then review every image against a checklist.
  4. Label each one *"illustration, AI-assisted"*.
- **A commissioned artist** costs more, but gives a signature look nobody can copy, and is easier to defend as "a source of truth".
- **A good middle path:** an artist draws the style guide and the main character sheets, and AI-assisted images follow those sheets, with the artist or you reviewing them.

Note: the image generator already connected to this workspace has 0 credits. It would need topping up for style tests.

## 5. Languages: built in from day one

### 5.1 Order

English → **Hindi** → **Telugu** → Tamil, Kannada, Marathi, Bengali, Malayalam, Gujarati.

### 5.2 How the content is stored so translation stays aligned

Every chapter is a file of **blocks with stable ids**. A translation is the same file with the same ids in another language:

```
content/
  volumes/01-the-vow/04-the-fishers-price/
    en.md      ← the source
    hi.md      ← same block ids, Hindi text
    te.md      ← same block ids, Telugu text
    chapter.yaml   ← cast, map cues, sources, labels (shared by all languages)
```

```md
::scene{id=s3 frame="shantanu,satyavati,bhishma" light="bhishma"}

Shantanu could not sleep. {#p12 src=1.100}

> I will never marry, and no son of mine will claim this throne. {#q3 src=1.100 quote}
```

- When the English changes, every translation of that block is flagged *"needs review"*.
- The map cues, cast and sources are written once and shared by all languages.

### 5.3 Names in every script

The data already holds every name in Devanagari. A transliteration library, such as the MIT-licensed `@indic-transliteration/sanscript`, can turn those into Telugu, Tamil, Kannada and other scripts automatically. A native reviewer then corrects the names that have their own local forms. Searching in any language then finds anyone: typing कर्ण, కర్ణుడు or Karna leads to the same person.

### 5.4 Translation workflow

1. **Draft:** AI translation (Claude can do this well), using a fixed glossary per language for words like *dharma*, *kshatriya*, *svayamvara* and *niyoga*.
2. **Review:** a native-speaking reviewer for each language. A chapter is published only once reviewed.
3. **Regional differences:** where the Telugu, Kannada or Tamil classical retelling differs from Vyasa, that edition gets a "Versions differ" note. Readers in those languages know those versions.

### 5.5 Type

- The site already uses **Tiro Devanagari**. The same family exists for **Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, Bangla and Gurmukhi**, all on Google Fonts, so every language keeps the same look.
- Each script loads only when needed.
- Indic scripts get more line height and are never letter-spaced (we already learned this with the Devanagari conjuncts).

## 6. Becoming the source of truth

| Need | How |
|---|---|
| **Found on search** | Chapters and profiles are pre-rendered to real HTML pages, with their own addresses (`/hi/volume/1/4`), language tags (hreflang), a sitemap and a share image per chapter. The map loads after the text, so pages are fast. |
| **Trusted** | Section references on every paragraph, the three labels, "Versions differ", a public corrections form (the "Report an error" feature already exists), and a visible changelog. |
| **Checked automatically** | Extend the Phase 1 audit. Every name in a chapter must exist on the map, every family claim must match the graph, every paragraph must have a source, and every quote must match Ganguli's text. |
| **Online** | The site is local-only today. Going public needs hosting (Vercel, Cloudflare Pages or Netlify), a domain, and later a small backend for the corrections queue. |

### Technical direction

The map stays exactly as it is, a React and WebGL component. For the reading side, the best fit is **Astro** around the existing app. It has content collections, built-in multi-language routing, pre-rendered pages for search engines, and React "islands" that let the map live inside a chapter page. The alternative is to keep Vite and add a pre-rendering step. That's less change now, but more work for every language later.
