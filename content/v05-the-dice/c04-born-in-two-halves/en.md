::scene{#s1 frame=krishna,yudhishthira light=krishna}

[Krishna](krishna) agreed with Arjuna. No one ever became immortal by refusing to fight, he said. But a wise man does not attack a powerful enemy head-on at the head of his army; better to enter the enemy's house in secret and strike at the man himself. {#p1 2.17}

*Who is this Jarasandha,* [Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) asked, *that he has touched you and not been burnt like a moth?* So Krishna told him. {#p2 2.17}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=brihadratha,i1945_chandakaushika light=brihadratha}

There was a king of Magadha named [Brihadratha](brihadratha), splendid and powerful, who married twin daughters of the king of Kashi and promised them in secret that he would love them equally. But his youth passed, and no son was born, whatever rites he performed. {#p3 2.17}

One day he heard that the sage [Chandakaushika](i1945_chandakaushika) had come to his city and was sitting under a mango tree. The king went to him with both his wives, and when the sage offered him a boon, Brihadratha wept. He was going into the forest, he said. What use was a kingdom, or a boon, to a man with no son? {#p4 2.17}

The sage sat in meditation under the tree, and a mango fell into his lap, ripe and whole, untouched by any bird. He said mantras over it and gave it to the king. *Return, O king, thy wish is fulfilled.* {#p5 2.17}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=brihadratha,jarasandha light=brihadratha}

Brihadratha remembered his promise to love his wives equally, and gave the one fruit to both of them. They cut it in two and each ate half, and both conceived. {#p6 2.17}

And each gave birth to half a child: one eye, one arm, one leg, half a belly, half a face. The two queens trembled at the sight, and in their grief they abandoned the two halves. The midwives wrapped them up, took them out by the back door, and left them at a crossroads. {#p7 2.17}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=jara,jarasandha,brihadratha light=jara}

A Rakshasi named [Jara](jara), who lived on flesh and blood, found them there, and to carry them more easily she put the two halves together. They joined, and became one sturdy child, as hard as a thunderbolt, too heavy for her to lift. The baby put his copper-red fists in his mouth and roared like a rain cloud. {#p8 2.17}

The whole palace came running, the king and the two queens with their breasts full of milk. Jara looked at them, and at the king who had wanted a son so badly, and did not have it in her to kill the child. She took human form and gave him back. {#p9 2.17}

> O Vrihadratha, this is thy child. Given to thee by me, O, take it. It hath been born of both thy wives by virtue of the command of the great Brahmana. {#q1 2.17}
> — Jara

***

::scene{#s5 frame=jara,brihadratha,jarasandha light=jara,jarasandha}

She was a household goddess, she told the king. Anyone who painted her picture on the walls of his house, surrounded by children, would prosper; and there was a picture of her in his palace, worshipped every day with flowers and incense. She had only been repaying the worship. {#p10 2.18}

So the king named the child for her: *Jarasandha*, joined by Jara, and held a festival in her honour. And the boy grew like a fire fed with butter. {#p11 2.18}

***

::scene{#s6 frame=i1945_chandakaushika,brihadratha,jarasandha,shiva light=jarasandha}

When Chandakaushika came again, he foretold what the boy would be: no king would equal him; weapons, even the gods', would not hurt him; he would outshine every crowned head; and he would see Shiva himself with his own eyes. Brihadratha put Jarasandha on the throne and went to the forest with his two wives. {#p12 2.19}

Jarasandha brought many kings under his sway. And when Krishna killed Kamsa, Jarasandha whirled his mace ninety-nine times in his city of Girivraja and hurled it all the way to Mathura, ninety-nine yojanas away. It fell near the city, at a place still called Gadavasana, *where the mace lies*. {#p13 2.19}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=2.17,2.24}
**A body in two halves.** The child who was joined together at a crossroads will, in the end, be undone the same way. Keep it in mind for the wrestling match.
::
