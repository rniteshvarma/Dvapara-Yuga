You praise giving up action, [Arjuna](arjuna) said, and then you praise doing it. Tell me plainly which is better. {#p1 6.29}

Both lead to freedom, [Krishna](krishna) said, but doing is better than giving up. The true renouncer is the one who neither hates nor desires. Only fools say Sankhya and Yoga are different; whoever stands firmly in one reaches the fruit of both. {#p2 6.29}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=krishna,arjuna light=krishna}

The man of yoga who knows the truth thinks *I do nothing at all*, even while he sees, hears, touches, smells, eats, walks, sleeps and breathes: it is only the senses moving among their objects. {#p3 6.29}

> He who renouncing attachment engageth in actions, resigning them to Brahma, is not touched by sin as the lotus-leaf (is not touched) by water. {#q1 6.29}
> — Krishna

The self-controlled one dwells at ease in the city of nine gates, neither acting nor making others act. The Lord takes no one's sin or merit; knowledge is hidden by ignorance, and that is why creatures are deluded. {#p4 6.29}

> Those, who are wise cast an equal eye on a Brahmana endued with learning and modesty, on a cow, an elephant, a dog, and a chandala. {#q2 6.29}
> — Krishna

***

::scene{#s3 frame=krishna light=krishna}

Pleasures born of contact with the senses are wombs of sorrow; they have a beginning and an end, and the wise do not delight in them. Whoever can bear the surge of desire and anger here, before the body falls, is a yogi and is happy. {#p5 6.29}

Shutting out the outside world, the gaze between the brows, the breaths made even, senses, mind and understanding held, free of desire, fear and anger: such a sage is free already. And knowing Krishna as the enjoyer of all sacrifice, lord of the worlds and friend of all creatures, he finds peace. {#p6 6.29}
