::scene{#s1 frame=kunti,hidimbi light=kunti,hidimbi}

[Kunti](kunti) looked at the stranger in wonder and asked her gently who she was. A goddess of the woods? An apsara? {#p1 1.156}

[Hidimbi](hidimbi) told her the whole truth. This forest belonged to a Rakshasa, Hidimba, and she was his sister. He had sent her to kill them all. But when she saw Kunti's son, the god of love had taken hold of her, and she had chosen him, in her heart, as her husband. Now her brother had come to kill them, and *my husband* had dragged him away. *Behold now that couple,* she said, *man and Rakshasa, engaged in combat.* {#p2 1.156}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima,i4487_hidimba,arjuna,yudhishthira,nakula,sahadeva light=bhima,i4487_hidimba,arjuna}

The brothers leapt up and saw the two of them in a cloud of dust, like two cliffs in a mist. [Arjuna](arjuna) thought Bhima looked hard pressed, and offered, smiling, to kill the Rakshasa himself. {#p3 1.156}

> Look on this encounter, O brother, like a stranger. Fear not for the result. Having come within the reach of my arms, he shall not escape with life. {#q1 1.156}
> — Bhima

But the east was reddening, Arjuna warned him, and Rakshasas grow stronger at twilight. He should stop playing and finish it. {#p4 1.156}

Bhima, roused to fury, called up the strength his father the wind puts forth at the end of the world. He lifted the Rakshasa high, whirled him round a hundred times, and dashed him to the ground, and broke him across the middle. The dying yell filled the forest like the boom of a wet drum. {#p5 1.156}

Arjuna thought there must be a town nearby. They should go before Duryodhana could trace them. So they set off, and Hidimbi followed. {#p6 1.156}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=bhima,hidimbi,yudhishthira light=bhima,yudhishthira}

Bhima turned on her. Rakshasas take revenge through tricks no one can see through, he said; she should go the way her brother had gone. {#p7 1.157}

> O Bhima, O tiger among men, however enraged, do not slay a woman. {#q2 1.157}
> — Yudhishthira

Hidimbi bowed to Kunti and Yudhishthira with folded hands. She had left her people and the ways of her race for Bhima, she said; if he or Kunti cast her off, she would not live. Let her take him with her; she would always bring him back. Whenever they thought of her she would come, and carry them over any country, and save them from any danger. {#p8 1.157}

***

::scene{#s4 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,hidimbi light=hidimbi,bhima}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) agreed, on terms. Every day, when Bhima had bathed and said his prayers, she could have him until sunset; and every night she must bring him back. {#p9 1.157}

> Truly do I make this engagement with thee that I will stay with thee, O thou of slender waist, until thou obtainest a son. {#q3 1.157}
> — Bhima

*So be it,* she said, and took him up and flew. She took him to mountain peaks and the haunts of the gods, to lotus lakes and river islands, Himalayan bowers and shores of gold and pearl, to the banks of Lake Manasarovara; and she took the loveliest forms, and sang to him, and made him happy. {#p10 1.157}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=ghatotkacha,hidimbi,bhima light=ghatotkacha}

In time she bore him a son. He had fierce eyes, a huge mouth, ears like arrows, copper-brown lips, sharp teeth and a great roar, and though he was born of a man, there was nothing human in his face. He grew to a youth in the hour of his birth, for Rakshasa women, the epic says, give birth the same day they conceive. He soon mastered every weapon. {#p11 1.157}

He bowed to his mother and touched his father's feet. His head was bald, like a water-pot, a *ghata*, and so they named him [Ghatotkacha](ghatotkacha). He loved the Pandavas, and became almost one of them. {#p12 1.157}

Then Hidimbi knew her time with her husband was over. She took leave of the Pandavas and went where she would. Ghatotkacha promised his father he would come whenever he was needed, and went away to the north. {#p13 1.157}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=1.157}
**Made for one purpose.** The epic adds, already, why Ghatotkacha was born: Indra created him as a match for Karna, because of the dart Indra had given Karna, which would surely kill whoever it was thrown at. That story is a long way off.
::

***

::scene{#s6 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,kunti,vyasa light=vyasa}

The Pandavas wandered from forest to forest, through the lands of the Matsyas, the Trigartas and the Panchalas, with matted hair, dressed in bark and skins like ascetics. Sometimes they carried their mother; sometimes they went in disguise. And they studied the Vedas and the sciences of law and statecraft as they went. {#p14 1.158}

Then they met their grandfather, [Vyasa](vyasa). {#p15 1.158}

> Do not grieve for what hath befallen you. Know that all this is for your happiness. {#q4 1.158}
> — Vyasa

He loved Dhritarashtra's sons and them equally, he said; but people always feel more for those in trouble and those who are young. He led them to the town of Ekachakra, comforted Kunti with the promise that Yudhishthira would one day rule the earth, and lodged them in a Brahmana's house. *Wait here for me,* he said, and went away. {#p16 1.158}
