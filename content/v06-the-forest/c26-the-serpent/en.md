::scene{#s1 frame=yudhishthira,bhima,arjuna,nakula,sahadeva,draupadi,ghatotkacha light=yudhishthira}

When they left Kubera's mountain, the Pandavas went back the way they had come: to Badari, through the land of Suvahu, king of the Kiratas, where their charioteers and cooks were waiting, and there they let [Ghatotkacha](ghatotkacha) go home. Then they lived a year in the forest of Vishakhayupa near the Yamuna, hunting. In that forest Bhima met the serpent. {#p1 3.176}

***

::scene{#s2 frame=bhima,nahusha light=bhima}

[Bhima](bhima) was roaming the mountains, killing boars and tearing up trees and shouting until the lions left their dens, when he came on a serpent filling a whole cave, yellow and spotted, with a copper mouth like a cavern. It seized him at once and coiled around him, and Bhima, who had the strength of ten thousand elephants, could not move. {#p2 3.177}

*Who art thou?* he asked. *How hast thou been able to overpower me?* {#p3 3.178}

The serpent told him. He was Bhima's own ancestor, [Nahusha](nahusha), son of [Ayu](ayu). Once he had ruled the three worlds, and in his pride he had made Brahmanas carry his chair; and when his foot touched the sage Agastya, Agastya cursed him to become a snake. Any creature that came within his reach at the sixth part of the day was his to eat, by a boon. He would be freed only when someone answered his questions. {#p4 3.178}

***

::scene{#s3 frame=yudhishthira,nahusha,bhima light=yudhishthira}

[Yudhishthira](yudhishthira) saw evil omens and went looking, and found his brother in the serpent's coils. He offered the serpent other food. No, said Nahusha; but answer my questions, and I will let him go. {#p5 3.179}

> O Yudhishthira, say—Who is a Brahmana and what should be known? {#q1 3.179}
> — Nahusha

He in whom truth, charity, forgiveness, good conduct, kindness and mercy are seen is a Brahmana, said Yudhishthira; and what should be known is the supreme Brahman. But those virtues are found in Shudras too, said the serpent. {#p6 3.179}

> And a Sudra is not a Sudra by birth alone—nor a Brahmana is Brahmana by birth alone. {#q2 3.179}
> — Yudhishthira

***

::scene{#s4 frame=nahusha,yudhishthira,bhima light=yudhishthira}

They talked a long time, about birth and conduct and the soul and what leads to heaven, and the serpent was satisfied. Nahusha let Bhima go. He shed his serpent body, took his heavenly form again, and went back to heaven. {#p7 3.180}

Yudhishthira went home with Bhima and Dhaumya and told the Brahmanas everything, and they all scolded Bhima for his rashness. {#p8 3.180}

***

::scene{#s5 frame=yudhishthira,arjuna light=yudhishthira}

The rains came, then autumn, with its clear skies and lotus-filled pools. In the twelfth year of their exile the Pandavas went back to the Dvaita lake and the Sarasvati, and spent the holy full-moon night of Kartika there among the sages. Then, with Arjuna once more among them, they went back to the Kamyaka forest. {#p9 3.176 3.181}

::aside{#a1 kind=note src=3.179}
**Birth or conduct?** Yudhishthira's answer, that caste is shown by conduct and not by birth alone, is one of the most quoted passages in the epic. The epic gives it to the eldest Pandava, in a test he must pass to save his brother.
::
